# Pension Sonnenhof (Sun Courtyard Guesthouse) — Show Bible

## Premise & Setting
Set in **Leipzig**, **Pension Sonnenhof** (*Sun Courtyard Guesthouse*) is an eight-room neighbourhood guesthouse built around a quiet paved courtyard with a four-table morning bakery-café. `klara` (Klara) begins her five-day trial week at the front desk alongside sceptical veteran handyman `werner` (Werner), warm morning baker `miriam` (Miriam), and anxious owner `martin` (Martin), who constantly loses his glasses on his own forehead.

## Cast & Register Relationships
- **`klara` (Klara / Klara, f, mid-20s):** Resourceful newcomer at the front desk. Uses polite `Sie` with guests (`helga`, `lukas`, `moritz`) and owner `martin`, and casual `du` with colleagues `miriam` and `werner`.
- **`werner` (Werner / Werner, m, late 40s):** Veteran handyman who trusts his paper ledger and brass keyring over tablets; gruff exterior, warm heart.
- **`miriam` (Miriam / Miriam, f, early 30s):** Morning café baker; immediately warm and informal with colleagues, famous for cinnamon rolls and Friday lemon cake.
- **`martin` (Martin / Martin, m, late 50s):** Formal, polite owner obsessed with five-star reviews and punctuality; uses polite first-name + `Sie` (*Hamburger-Sie*) with staff and regulars.
- **`helga` (Helga / Helga, f, 60s):** Retired piano teacher in Room 4 whose dark-blue suitcase is swapped on Monday's train.
- **`lukas` (Lukas / Lukas, m, 30s):** Soft-spoken acoustic architect in the third-floor attic room, secretly hoping to reconcile with his brother.
- **`moritz` (Moritz / Moritz, m, late 30s):** `lukas`'s estranged older brother who works in municipal contracts at City Hall.

## Running Jokes & Recurring Places
- The front door only opens smoothly if you lift the handle first.
- Room 4's old buzzer wire always rings twice on its own.
- `martin` searches for his reading glasses while wearing them on his forehead.
- Key places: Front desk, four-table café corner, Leipzig courtyard under the shade tree, third-floor attic room, brick boiler cellar, and historic Lindengasse.

## Season 1 Episode Arc (6 × 8 Scenes)
1. **`s01e01` — Der blaue Koffer (*The Blue Suitcase*):** Klara arrives for her first morning shift and Werner shows her the trick to the front door and his paper ledger. ... Cliffhanger: Late at night the front bell rings, and Klara and Werner find Helga's real suitcase on the step with an anonymous note.
2. **`s01e02` — Frühstück für acht (*Breakfast for Eight*):** On Tuesday morning Helga happily recovers her own suitcase while the unclaimed bag stays locked away. ... Cliffhanger: Checking the evening ledger, Martin turns pale: he booked two separate guests into Room 7 on a sold-out night.
3. **`s01e03` — Zimmer sieben doppelt (*Room Seven Twice*):** Martin panics over the double-booked room while Werner unlocks the third-floor staircase. ... Cliffhanger: Coming up from the cellar, Werner delivers bad news: the hot-water boiler valve just cracked for the night.
4. **`s01e04` — Kein warmes Wasser (*No Hot Water*):** At dawn Werner draws Klara a street map to the old hardware shop across the bridge before guests wake up. ... Cliffhanger: In the courtyard Helga describes the quiet younger brother listening from the stairs just as Lukas walks out and hears her.
5. **`s01e05` — Freitag um zwölf (*Friday at Noon*):** Lukas explains how he accidentally swapped suitcases with Helga on Monday's train and panicked at the gate. ... Cliffhanger: As Moritz steps into the courtyard outside, Martin opens the landlord's letter inside and turns pale at a sale notice.
6. **`s01e06` — Die Laternen im Hof (*The Courtyard Lanterns*):** Klara brings tea to the courtyard table as Lukas uses her three simple sentences to break the ice with Moritz. ... Cliffhanger: Fetching spare café chairs from the cellar, Werner uncovers a locked wooden crate stamped 'Room 9' and a ninth brass key.

## Localization & Cultural Notes
- German street: Lindengasse. Shoe size 44. Klara, Miriam, and Werner use casual `du` with one another, while owner Martin uses polite first-name + `Sie` (*Hamburger-Sie*) with staff/regulars and everyone uses polite `Sie` with guests.
