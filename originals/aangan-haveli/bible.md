# आँगन हवेली (Courtyard Haveli) — Show Bible

## Premise & Setting
Set on quiet **Champa Lane** (*चंपा गली*) in **Jaipur**, **आँगन हवेली** (*Courtyard Haveli*) is an eight-room neighbourhood guesthouse built around a sandstone-paved inner courtyard with a four-table morning bakery-café on the ground floor. Resourceful newcomer `kavya` (काव्या) starts her five-day trial week at the front desk alongside dry-humoured veteran handyman `pratap` (प्रताप), cheerful morning baker `diya` (दीया), and anxious owner `mohan` (मोहन), who loses his reading glasses on his own forehead three times a day.

## Cast & Register Relationships (`तू / तुम / आप`)
- **`kavya` (काव्या / Kavya, f, mid-20s):** Quick-thinking newcomer at the front desk. Uses polite `आप` + honorific `जी` (`सुधा जी`, `मोहन जी`, `प्रताप जी`, `कबीर जी`) with guests, her boss, and her senior colleague, while immediately sharing warm colleague `तुम` with `diya`.
- **`pratap` (प्रताप / Pratap, m, late 40s):** Fifteen years keeping the haveli running; trusts his big paper register (`काग़ज़ का रजिस्टर`), pencil, and brass keyring over any tablet. Uses friendly `तुम` with younger colleagues `kavya` and `diya`, and polite `आप` with guests and `mohan`.
- **`diya` (दीया / Diya, f, early 30s):** Runs the four-table breakfast corner; immediately invites `kavya` to drop stiff formality and speak on first-name `तुम` terms. Famous for warm morning buns, mint-and-ginger tea, and Friday lemon cake.
- **`mohan` (मोहन / Mohan, m, late 50s):** Polite, formal owner perpetually worried about five-star online reviews and budgets; addresses staff warmly (`आप` / `तुम`) and guests with ceremonious `आप`.
- **`sudha` (सुधा / Sudha, f, 60s):** Calm, witty retired piano teacher checking into Room 4 after arriving at Jaipur Junction by morning train with a swapped dark-blue suitcase. (Taught piano on Champa Lane twenty-five years ago; plays the forgotten walnut upright piano in the ground-floor glass sunroom when Room 9 opens in Season 2.)
- **`kabir` (कबीर / Kabir, m, 30s):** Soft-spoken acoustic architect staying in the quiet third-floor attic study (`बरसाती कमरा`), studying quiet traditional courtyards and hoping to reconcile with his older brother.
- **`dev` (देव / Dev, m, late 30s):** `kabir`'s estranged older brother, who works in heritage and municipal contracts at Jaipur City Hall (`नगर निगम`).

## Running Jokes & Recurring Places
- The heavy wooden front door only opens smoothly if you lift the handle slightly first (`पहले हैंडल ऊपर उठाइए, फिर धक्का दीजिए`).
- Room 4's old desk buzzer wire always rings twice on its own, even when the room is empty.
- `mohan` hunts frantically through his pockets for his reading glasses (`चश्मा`) while wearing them right on his forehead (`माथे पर`).
- **Key Places:** Front desk with eight brass keys, four-table café corner, sandstone courtyard under the old neem shade tree with fresh herb pots, sunny third-floor timber-beamed attic study (`बरसाती कमरा`), basement hot-water boiler room (`तहख़ाना`), forgotten ground-floor glass sunroom (`कमरा नौ`) housing Aunt's walnut upright piano behind the hallway bookcase, and historic Champa Lane in Jaipur.

## Season 1 Episode Arc (6 × 8 Scenes)
1. **`s01e01` — नीला सूटकेस (*The Blue Suitcase*):** `kavya` arrives ten minutes early for her first morning shift and `pratap` shows her the front-door handle trick and his paper register; `diya` welcomes her with warm buns, `mohan` finds his glasses on his forehead, and retired music teacher `sudha` checks into Room 4—only to discover her dark-blue suitcase was swapped at Jaipur Junction for an identical bag holding men's brown shoes and a wooden toy train. Cliffhanger: At nine at night the gate bell rings once, and `kavya` and `pratap` find `sudha`'s real suitcase on the stone step with an unsigned note: *"गलती के लिए माफ़ कीजिए। कृपया मेरा नीला बैग शुक्रवार तक रखिए।"*
2. **`s01e02` — आठ लोगों का नाश्ता (*Breakfast for Eight*):** On Tuesday morning `sudha` recovers her suitcase and red ribbon while the mystery bag stays locked in the closet; when the dairy van breaks down two and a half kilometres outside town twenty minutes before breakfast, `kavya` sprints to the corner shop with five hundred rupees, accidentally grabs sea-salt butter that `diya` turns into a triumph with scrambled eggs and courtyard herbs, and answers a nervous phone call from the mystery owner promising to come Friday at noon. Cliffhanger: On Wednesday evening `mohan` realises he booked two separate guests into Room 7 without his glasses on a rainy, sold-out night.
3. **`s01e03` — कमरा सात दो बार (*Room Seven Twice*):** With thirty-five minutes before the eight o'clock train arrival, `kavya`, `pratap`, `diya`, and `mohan` transform the quiet third-floor timber-beamed attic study into a cosy room at half price; soaked traveller `kabir` loves the quiet courtyard room, but leaves his brown leather notebook of courtyard measurements on the counter, convincing everyone he is a secret Jaipur Travel Guide inspector—just as the heating pipe clanks and `pratap` reports the basement hot-water boiler valve has cracked before morning showers.
4. **`s01e04` — गरम पानी नहीं है (*No Hot Water*):** At six forty-five on Thursday morning `pratap` sketches `kavya` street directions across the old stone bridge to buy two twenty-millimetre brass boiler valves while `diya` and `mohan` stall early risers with hot cinnamon rolls; once the water is hot again, `kabir` laughs and reveals he is an acoustic architect studying quiet havelis, right as `sudha` recognises the old Champa Lane tag on the wooden toy train from twenty-five years ago.
5. **`s01e05` — शुक्रवार दोपहर बारह बजे (*Friday at Noon*):** `kabir` confesses he accidentally swapped suitcases on Monday's train and left the Friday postcard for his estranged older brother `dev`; `sudha` remembers teaching both boys piano on Friday afternoons, `diya` bakes their childhood lemon cake, and `kavya` coaches `kabir` on three simple opening sentences—just as `dev` arrives at the gate at noon and `mohan` opens a letter putting the haveli's lease up for sale.
6. **`s01e06` — आँगन की लालटेनें (*The Courtyard Lanterns*):** `kabir` and `dev` reconcile warmly over tea and the wooden toy train in the courtyard while `dev` spots a Jaipur heritage protection clause on page two of the lease letter; `mohan` offers `kavya` the permanent front-desk job, and down in the cellar `pratap` uncovers a dusty cedar chest stencilled **"कमरा ९"** (*Room 9*) in an eight-room haveli.

## Localization & Spoken Register Notes
- **Everyday Spoken Hindi (`बोलचाल की हिंदी`):** Natural home-and-shop Hindi in Devanagari—neither stiff Sanskritised `शुद्ध हिंदी` (`भ्रमणभाष`, `स्वागतकक्ष`) nor ornate literary Urdu. Everyday words (`रिसेप्शन`, `कमरा`, `चाबी`, `चाय`, `नाश्ता`, `फ़ोन`, `रसीद`, `सूटकेस`, `ट्रेन`, `स्टेशन`) sit naturally inside idiomatic Hindi grammar, postpositions, and verb constructions (`उठा लेना`, `बंद कर देना`, `पता चलना`).
- **Currency & Times:** Prices in realistic Jaipur rupees (`पाँच सौ रुपये`, `तीन सौ अस्सी रुपये`, `एक सौ बीस रुपये` change in `s01e02`), spoken Hindi clock times and fractions (`साढ़े सात बजे`, `ढाई किलोमीटर`), and zero numerals in spoken lines (all numbers written out in Devanagari words).
- **Multi-Season Continuity (Seasons 2–5):** Preserves the aunt's walnut upright piano in the forgotten ground-floor glass garden room (`कमरा नौ`) behind the hallway bookcase so `sudha`'s piano teaching, the unfinished *Courtyard Waltz*, and Seasons 2–5 plotlines align seamlessly.
