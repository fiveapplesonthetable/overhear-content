# Official Season 1 trailer — Japanese taster

The directory `s01e01` is the first catalogue lesson slot. **The source is an official Season 1 trailer, not a verified Episode 1 scene.** The public episode title, introduction and source label make that distinction explicit. No full episode or full trailer is represented as reviewed or published.

The canonical `sources/the-naked-director/s01/e01.ja.srt` is a **partial Japanese transcript** containing only the selected continuous request. It is not a Japanese caption track, an English backtranslation, or a claim to a complete native transcript. Its single cue retains source-relative trailer times and is bound by SHA-256 in `scenes.json` coverage. Timing identifies the excerpt; no source word timings have been invented.

Three short clauses are enough for this bounded taster. The `format: taster` recipe preserves introduction, complete request, a walkthrough of every original line, an unchanged complete recap and an English send-off. The new closing `またね。` is lesson dialogue, not claimed to occur in the source. No extra plot or padding was added to reach a full-lesson line count.

Japanese display text has no inserted spaces. Every annotation has an explicit half-open codepoint span and a reviewed kana reading. Stress is null because Japanese pitch accent was not reviewed. In the first clause, `で` is the connecting form of copula `だ`; `いたく` is a desire form of `いる`, not `痛く`. In the request, `出演させて` is a causative form of `出演する`, and `ん` is explanatory `の`, not negation. Actor, permission-giver and speaker remain distinct in the meanings.

Original show video, derived clips and source audio remain private and are used only for verification or conditioning. Public native dialogue must be newly generated Japanese speech that passes the production audio and alignment checks. There is no `sourceAudio` field in the candidate script.

Ordinary-lemma histories are individually sourced to Shogakukan dictionaries on Kotobank, with Shueisha’s own katakana dictionary for the English-derived compound. Some records document attested history rather than an unproved prehistoric derivation. Disputed origins of `です` and `から` stay explicitly uncertain. No invented generic kanji etymologies or pronunciation stress claims are included.
