## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Let's go to the meeting.' The opening word is an informal invitation to do something together. The next word is a shortened everyday form meaning to or for. Here it gives the destination. The last word means meeting; its final nasal sound is part of the word, not a separate ending to skip.
- 'I'm not going to be at that meeting. But thanks.' The speaker places a negative at the start and repeats it at the end for emphasis. This is common in conversational Brazilian Portuguese. The last word is a casual way to say thanks here; it does not mean that he accepts the invitation.
- 'Aren't you going to the meeting?' There is no extra question word. The questioning voice asks the employee to confirm his refusal. The opening word refers to the person being addressed, but it takes a third-person verb form. That is why the form for going differs from the earlier one used by the speaker about himself.
- 'It's a marketing meeting. I'm in finance.' The first sentence names the kind of meeting. In the second, the employee identifies his department. Literally, he says that he is from the finance department. The joined word before the department combines a linking word with the masculine article. His department is the reason he gives for declining.
