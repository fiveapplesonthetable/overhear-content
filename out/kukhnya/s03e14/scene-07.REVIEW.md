## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Listen, why did you tell Katya everything?' Wondering why Max tells Katya everything cuts straight through the tension between Barinov and Max.
- 'I thought that Katya is so beautiful and good.' Saying that he thought that Katya is so beautiful and good is how Max sets the tone with Barinov.
- 'What one hundred thousand, three hundred is my price!' Max declares that what one hundred thousand three hundred is his price as the tension in the room peaks. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'Fine, we meet in an hour in the back courtyard.' Around the conversation and meat, Barinov sets a calm, everyday tone with Max.
- 'He pocketed ten thousand too!' With the kitchen and meat at stake, Barinov reacts without holding back. Russian puts the key word right up front so his emotion lands on the first beat.
- 'Hello, Timur, hi, how do I settle a conflict?' Wondering how he settles a conflict is how Max puts Barinov on the spot. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'How do we settle conflicts among ourselves?' Here wondering how they settle conflicts among themselves shows Timur looking for the real truth.
- 'Well, there are always two options here.' Timur tells Barinov that there are always two options here without dressing it up.
- 'The second option is really costly.' Because the second option is really costly, Timur treats the matter as settled.
- 'There you have to set a generous table and treat the guests.' Explaining that there Barinov has to set a generous table and treat the guests lays the cards on the table for Barinov.
- 'Got it, do you think I should bring shashlik right away?' Max asks whether got it do Barinov thinks he should bring shashlik right away, refusing to let the point slide. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
- 'Yes, bring good meat and good wine.' Even though Barinov should bring good meat and good wine, Timur keeps a steady face in front of Barinov.
- 'Wait, I wanted everything to go peacefully.' Pointing out that he wanted everything to go peacefully gives Barinov a sobering reality check.
- 'I want to offer you a good dish with good wine.' Wanting to offer Barinov a good dish with good wine moves the conversation forward without extra drama. Even in the middle of the drama, he keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
