## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I always thought that you were simply an idiot.' Once he always thought that Elena was simply an idiot, Barinov can finally tell Elena what he means. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
- 'Wait, you are not just an idiot.' Adding that Barinov is not just an idiot fills in the missing piece for Barinov.
- 'Why the hell did you decide to do that.' Elena tells Barinov that why the hell did Barinov decides to do without dressing it up. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
- 'Chef, I just thought it would help.' Since he just thought it would help, Barinov does not waste words on excuses.
- 'Good afternoon once again.' Around the plan and conversation, Nastya sets a calm, everyday tone with Barinov.
- 'How is your mood today?' Here wondering how Barinov's mood today is shows Elena looking for the real truth. In Russian, she does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'Do you have many guests?' By asking whether Elena has many guests, Barinov presses Elena for a straight answer. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'It is a message from Elena.' Explaining that it is a message from Elena lays the cards on the table for Barinov.
- 'Wait, what is that?' With the kitchen and moment hanging in the air, Elena puts Barinov on the spot. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'Do not worry, Chef.' Claiming that do not worry sounds confident, even if Elena has doubts. Notice how he sticks to the formal "you", keeping a professional line between them.
- 'I will delete the whole thing right now.' Pointing out that she will delete the whole thing right now gives Barinov a sobering reality check.
- 'We have to reply something to her.' Elena explains that they have to reply something, keeping her voice calm and direct. There is no "you" needed in the Russian here: one short word states the rule for everyone in the room.
- 'Write it down like this.' Writing it down like this is how Barinov lays out the situation for Elena.
