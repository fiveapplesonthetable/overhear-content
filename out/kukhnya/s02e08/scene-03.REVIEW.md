## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'That you beat up my husband.' Once that Nastya beats up his husband, Kostya can finally tell Nastya what he means. Notice how he sticks to the formal "you", keeping a professional line between them.
- 'Mom, first of all, it was not Kostya's fault.' Adding that Mom first of all it was not Kostya fault fills in the missing piece for Kostya.
- 'And secondly, he feels ashamed about it anyway.' Kostya tells Nastya that secondly he feels ashamed about it anyway without dressing it up.
- 'Tell her, Kostya.' Between the table and the kitchen, Nastya gives Kostya a quick, grounded response.
- 'Tell her that you are ashamed.' Noting that Nastya should tell her that Nastya is ashamed keeps Kostya's side of the story simple and clear.
- 'I am ashamed.' Nastya mentions that she is ashamed so Kostya sees what is at stake.
- 'Nastya, may I go back to work now?' Wondering if may he goes back to work now shows Kostya caught off guard. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'Come visit us at our house on Saturday.' Because Kostya should come visit them at their house on Saturday, Nastya treats the matter as settled.
- 'And let us put an end to this foolish conflict.' Here proposing that they put an end to this foolish conflict sets up the next turn in their exchange.
- 'Why do the two of you not come visit Kostya and me instead.' Claiming that why do the two of Kostya not come visit Kostya and her instead sounds confident, even if Kostya has doubts. Even in the middle of the drama, she keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
- 'Nastya, darling, you come visit us, and I will drop by another time.' Pointing out that darling Nastya comes visit them and he will drop by another time gives Nastya a sobering reality check.
- 'Here is how it is going to be.' Nastya explains that here is how it is going to be, keeping her voice calm and direct.
- 'I am afraid that will be very hard to explain to Dad.' Realizing that he is afraid that will be very hard to explain to dad changes how Kostya handles the situation. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
