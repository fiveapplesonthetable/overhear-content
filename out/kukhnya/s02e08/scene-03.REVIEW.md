## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'That you beat up my husband.' Kostya jumps in promptly facing Nastya, bringing things back to earth after the buildup.
- 'Mom, first of all, it was not Kostya's fault.' Underscoring the mum in the exchange, Nastya centers the remark on "vo-pervykh" ("first of all") without any extra fuss.
- 'And secondly, he feels ashamed about it anyway.' Kostya responds evenly in the exchange and brings in "vo-vtorykh" ("second") to make the meaning unmistakable.
- 'Tell her, Kostya.' Nastya steps in honestly during the talk, refusing to let the moment slip past.
- 'Tell her that you are ashamed.' Kostya answers pointedly right here and leans on "skazhi" ("say", a form of "skazat") to give the remark its edge.
- 'I am ashamed.' Pushing back promptly before Kostya, Nastya links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
- 'Nastya, may I go back to work now?' Kostya challenges eagerly before Nastya can change the subject.
- 'Come visit us at our house on Saturday.' Nastya cuts in frankly on the spot and lands on "subbotu" ("Saturday", built on "subbota") to keep the statement grounded.
- 'And let us put an end to this foolish conflict.' Turning to the conflict to Nastya, Kostya lands on "konflikt" ("conflict") as the key everyday word here.
- 'Why do the two of you not come visit Kostya and me instead.' Nastya steps in honestly toward Kostya, testing Kostya's reaction in the middle of the exchange.
- 'Nastya, darling, you come visit us, and I will drop by another time.' Kostya follows up briskly beside Nastya and uses "dorogaya" ("expensive", from "dorogoy") to make the point stick.
- 'Here is how it is going to be.' Following up warmly beside Kostya, Nastya links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
- 'I am afraid that will be very hard to explain to Dad.' Kostya counters calmly with a smile, before Nastya can change the subject.
