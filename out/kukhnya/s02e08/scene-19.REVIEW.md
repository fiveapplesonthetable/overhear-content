## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Then that is his own business.' Talking about the matter beside Nastya, Kostya reaches for the Russian word "delo" ("matter") right at the heart of the line.
- 'And there is no need to yell at Nastya.' Leaning in naturally right here, Nastya leans on "nado" ("it is necessary, one needs to"), which carries the whole predicate in Russian without needing "it is".
- 'Nastya, Kostya, please stop.' Max speaks up plainly across the room, shifting the mood of the conversation in a single beat.
- 'Who do you think you are?' Reacting warmly in this beat, Nastya links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
- 'Come on, let us step outside.' Weighing in evenly around Nastya, spoken Russian compresses the whole thought into just 3 words, skipping the extra helper words English needs.
- 'What do you think you are doing?' Nastya asks firmly while looking straight at Kostya here.
- 'And what will she do for you?' Holding firm plainly with Nastya, Kostya puts the question word "chto" right at the start so Nastya hears the focus immediately.
- 'Love you more for it?' Looking over directly with Kostya, Nastya keeps normal statement word order here, turning the line into a question for Kostya purely through rising pitch.
- 'Those are her principles, right?' Kostya presses promptly before Nastya can change the subject.
- 'I respect them, and you ought to respect them too.' Nastya follows up boldly in this beat and centers the thought on "dolzhny" ("must, should", from "dolzhen") without wasting a word.
- 'You will not have to beg me for long.' Reacting candidly on the spot, the "-sya" ending on "pridetsya" ("will have to") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'Dad, we do not drink alcohol.' Nastya cuts in steadily around Kostya, so Kostya knows she is not just making small talk.
- 'Want some cognac?' Pointing to the cognac before Nastya, Kostya lands on "konyak" ("cognac, brandy") as the key everyday word here.
