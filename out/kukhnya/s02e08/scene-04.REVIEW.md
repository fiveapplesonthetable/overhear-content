## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Good afternoon, Viktor Petrovich.' Vika gently delivers this daytime greeting without hesitation using "dobryy den" ("good afternoon") — literally "good day", the standard Russian greeting from late morning through afternoon.
- 'You have already seen it, huh?' Following up directly in front of Barinov, Vika keeps normal statement word order here, turning the line into a question for Barinov purely through rising pitch.
- 'Well, how do you like it?' Barinov tests firmly why things suddenly took this turn between them.
- 'Better than in real life.' Mentioning the life in the exchange, Vika reaches for the Russian word "zhizni" ("life", built from "zhizn") right at the center of the line.
- 'And in this picture even better still.' Barinov speaks dryly in front of Vika and centers the remark on "luchshe" ("better") without any extra fuss.
- 'I see, thank you very much.' Vika directly voices this word of thanks while facing Barinov using "spasibo" ("thank you") — the everyday word for "thank you", which grew out of the old blessing "spasi Bog" ("God save you").
- 'Well, you know how it is.' Pushing back frankly before Vika, spoken Russian compresses the whole thought into just 2 words, skipping the extra helper words English needs.
- 'I was actually asking you about the interview itself.' Pointing to the interview around Barinov, Vika drops in "voobshche" ("in general, altogether") to sum up the whole situation at once.
- 'Oh, I never read that book at all.' Barinov responds wryly with a shrug, watching Vika's face to see how the news lands.
- 'And you are quite right not to.' Vika replies evenly in front of Barinov and puts the emphasis on "pravilno" ("right") in the middle of the talk.
- 'What I meant to say was.' Pressing on naturally to Vika, spoken Russian compresses the whole thought into just 3 words, skipping the extra helper words English needs.
- 'And when it comes to hairstyles, he is a bit off.' Vika steps in bluntly toward Barinov, testing Barinov's reaction in the middle of the exchange.
- 'Do not listen to me.' Barinov counters readily in the exchange and reaches for the Russian word "slushayte" ("listen", built from "slushat") right at the center of the line.
