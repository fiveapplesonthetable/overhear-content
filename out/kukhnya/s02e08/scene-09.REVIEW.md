## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I am going to lose this bet.' By being going to lose this bet, Barinov keeps his side of the story grounded.
- 'Well, what did you want?' Asking what Barinov wants is how Max puts Barinov on the spot.
- 'You only beat me last time because you caught me off guard.' Barinov tells Max that Max only beat him last time because Max caught him off guard without dressing it up.
- 'Well, you could put it that way.' Since Barinov could put it that way, Max does not waste words on excuses. Russian packs the whole idea of what is allowed or needed into one impersonal word at the front.
- 'Wait, I did not quite get that.' Mentioning that he did not quite get moves the talk in a new direction.
- 'Come on, throw a punch, I will show you how to block it.' Max mentions that Barinov should come on throw a punch he will show Barinov how to block it so Barinov sees what is at stake. Starting with "davay" is the everyday Russian way to say "come on, let us do this".
- 'Oh, well in that case, here I go.' Because in that case here he goes, Barinov treats the matter as settled.
- 'I am so sorry, please forgive me.' Even though he is so sorry please forgive him, Max keeps a steady face in front of Barinov.
- 'I think he just broke your nose.' Pointing out that he thinks he just broke Max's nose gives Max a sobering reality check. Notice how he sticks to the formal "you", keeping a professional line between them.
- 'Chef, good afternoon.' Between the truth and the shift, Max gives Barinov a quick, grounded response.
- 'What I wanted to tell you is...' Questioning whether what he wanted to tell Max is is how Barinov tests Max's story.
- 'Did you add me as a friend?' By checking whether Barinov adds him as a friend, Max presses Barinov for a straight answer. Even in the middle of the drama, he keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
- 'I am about to add you to the meat grinder.' Mentioning that he is about to add Max to the meat grinder moves the talk in a new direction. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
