## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Vika, why are you looking so strangely?' "Smotrish" is "you are looking", from "smotret", which Barinov used in "smotri za Alisoy", "watch Alisa". "Tak stranno" is "so strangely". Max can see something is up.
- 'Max, I must tell you an important thing.' "Ya dolzhna" is "I must", with -na because Vika is a woman. A man says "dolzhen". "Vazhnuyu veshch" is "an important thing".
- 'Are you angry again because of yesterday's silly joke?' "Serdishsya" is "you're angry", with -sya, as in the boy's "papa serditsya". "Iz-za" is "because of", for a cause you don't like. Max guesses wrong.
- 'No, I am pregnant with your child.' "Beremenna ot tebya", "pregnant from you", comes back from the last scene. Vika says it in four words: "Net, ya beremenna ot tebya".
- 'Are you absolutely sure that the baby is mine?' "Uverena" is "sure", with -a for a woman. "Moy" is "mine", masculine to match "rebyonok", "the baby". "Absolyutno" is easy to catch.
- 'I have not been with anyone since you.' "U menya nikogo ne bylo" is "I had nobody". Russian uses two negatives here, "nikogo" and "ne". "Posle tebya" is "after you". Vika answers calmly.
- 'But we are completely young people!' "Lyudi", "people", is the irregular plural of "chelovek", "person". "Sovsem" is "completely", the word in Max's "sovsem ne prav", "completely wrong". "Molodye" is "young".
- 'Just do not worry so much, Max.' "Ne perezhivay" is "don't worry", from "perezhivat", the verb Lyova used about the evening service. "Tolko" here is "just". "Tak silno" is "so much". Vika calms him down.
- 'What on earth should we do with this now?' "Chto zhe" is "what on earth": the little "zhe" adds force. "Nam delat", "for us to do", is "should we do". "S etim" is "with this".
- 'I do not need anything at all from you.' "Mne ot tebya nichego ne nuzhno" is "I need nothing from you", with two negatives, "nichego" and "ne". "Sovershenno" is "completely". Vika wants nothing from him.
- 'Then why did you decide to tell me the truth?' "Reshila" is "you decided", with -la for Vika, the same verb as "reshili", "solved". "Zachem" is "what for". "Pravdu" is "the truth".
- 'This is just so that you would know.' "Dlya togo, chtoby" is "in order that". After "chtoby", "znat" takes its past form "znal", even though nothing is in the past. "Prosto" is "just".
