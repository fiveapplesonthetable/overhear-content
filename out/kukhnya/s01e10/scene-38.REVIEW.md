## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Maxim, did you think that I would get scared?' She calls him "Maksim", in full. "Ispugayus" is "I will get scared", the verb in Kostya's "On ispugalsya svadby". After "chto", Russian keeps the future, where English says "would".
- 'I simply made you a beautiful proposal.' Russians "make a proposal", "sdelat predlozhenie", to propose marriage. "Predlozhenie" is also the grammar word for "sentence". "Prosto" is "simply".
- 'You wanted to test my courage?' "Proverit" is "to test", "to check", heard in Barinov's "Snachala ya proveryu rabotu nashikh povarov". "Smelost", "courage", comes from "smely", "brave".
- 'I am ready to create a strong family with you.' "Sozdat" is "to create", a grand word. "Krepkaya semya" is "a strong family"; "krepkiy" also describes strong tea. "S toboy" is "with you".
- 'Are you sure of your words?' "Uveren v svoikh slovakh" is "sure in your own words". Russian uses "svoy" for things that belong to the person in the sentence. Vika asks for proof.
- 'I am absolutely confident in every word of mine!' "V kazhdom svoyom slove" is "in every own word": "kazhdy", "svoy" and "slovo" all change after "v". "Absolyutno uveren" is "absolutely sure".
- 'Then listen to my honest answer in front of everyone.' "Pri vsekh" is "in front of everyone", literally "at all". "Chestny otvet" is "honest answer"; "chestno" was in Kostya's "pogovori s ney chestno".
- 'I agree to become your wife!' "Soglasna" is "I agree", with -a because Vika is a woman. "Stat tvoey zhenoy" is "to become your wife": after "stat", "zhena" becomes "zhenoy". Vika says yes.
- 'Do you really agree?' "Pravda", "truth", works here as "really": "ty pravda soglasna?", "you truly agree?". Max asks again.
- 'Yes, I accept your unexpected proposal.' "Prinimayu" is "I accept", the verb behind Tamara's "tvoya stavka prinyata", "your bet is accepted". "Neozhidannoe", "unexpected", is "not awaited", from "zhdat".
- 'Now we are bride and groom!' "Zhenikh i nevesta" is "groom and bride", the fixed pair for an engaged couple, with the groom first. There is no word for "are": "now we groom and bride".
- 'We will see how you handle this, Maxim!' Vika's "Posmotrim" is "we will see". "Spravishsya" is "you will cope", as in Louis's "Ya odin segodnya ne spravlyus". "S etim" is "with this".
- 'I am ready for all the difficulties of our wedding!' "Gotov k" is "ready for": "gotov ko vsem trudnostyam". "Trudnosti" comes from "trudny", "difficult", as in Barinov's "spravitsya gorazdo trudnee". Max sounds very sure.
