## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Now you are in deep trouble, lad.' "Popal", literally "hit", as in hitting a target, here means "you've landed in it". "Paren" is "lad", a casual word. "Vot eto" adds "now that's".
- 'Chef, believe me, it just happened accidentally.' "Poverte" is "believe me", the polite command. "Sluchayno" is "by accident", from "sluchay", "chance". "Tak poluchilos" is "it just turned out that way".
- 'You had to think with your head in advance.' "Nado bylo", "it was necessary", means "you should have". "Dumat golovoy", "think with the head": "golova" becomes "golovoy". "Zaranee" is "in advance".
- 'What am I to do now?' "Chto zhe" is "what on earth". "Mne delat", "to me to do", is the Russian for "am I to do". "Teper" is "now". Max asks for help.
- 'Their family will not leave you alone now.' "Ne otstanet ot tebya", literally "won't fall behind you", is "won't leave you alone". "Ikh", "their", never changes its ending. "Semya" is "family".
- 'Do you know this family so well?' "Znaete" is "you know", polite form. "Etu semyu" is "this family": "eta semya" becomes "etu semyu" as the thing known. "Tak khorosho" is "so well".
- 'I myself used to live in this family.' "Zhil", "lived", is the past of "zhit". "Ranshe", "earlier", gives the "used to". "V etoy seme" is "in this family". Barinov says he was once part of it.
- 'And did they drive you out of the house?' "Vygnali", "drove out", is the verb Kostya used in "vygnat s kukhni". "Iz doma" is "out of the house": "dom" becomes "doma" after "iz". Max guesses at the story.
- 'They took all strength from me.' "Zabrali u menya", literally "took at me", is how Russian says "took from me". "Vse sily" is "all strength", and "sily" is plural. Barinov sounds worn out.
- 'Maybe I should just leave for Voronezh?' "Uekhat" is "to leave" by vehicle, the verb in Barinov's "plita uekhala", "the stove has gone off". "V Voronezh" is "to Voronezh". Max wants to go back there.
- 'You will not go anywhere now.' "Nikuda ne poedesh" is "you won't go anywhere", with two negatives, "nikuda" and "ne". "Poedesh" is by vehicle. Barinov says it flatly.
- 'But I am very afraid of such responsibility.' "Boyus" is "I'm afraid", and after it "takaya otvetstvennost" becomes "takoy otvetstvennosti". "Otvetstvennost", "responsibility", shares a root with "otvet", "answer".
- 'A man is obliged to answer for his actions.' "Obyazan" is "obliged", a strong word. "Otvechat za" is "answer for". "Svoi postupki" is "his own actions": "svoi" means "one's own".
