## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'What is happening here? Where is our stove?' "Proiskhodit" is "is happening", the same word as before. "Gde nasha plita" has no "is" at all. Vika has found an empty place in the kitchen.
- 'Our stove has now gone for diagnostics.' The stove "uekhala", "drove away", the word for leaving by vehicle. "Diagnostika" is a borrowed technical word. Barinov sounds like a doctor and a car mechanic at once.
- 'Its main handle simply broke down.' "Ruchka", "handle", is a small form of "ruka", "hand". "Slomalas" has the -sya, "broke itself". "U neyo", "at her", is how Russian says "its" for the stove.
- 'Why am I finding out about this last?' "Posledney", "last", has a feminine ending, because Vika is speaking about herself. "Uznayu" is "I find out", the word Kostya used for Max.
- 'It did not have time to leave you a note!' "Uspela" is "had time", a single word in Russian, with -la because the stove is "she". "Zapiska" is a note. Barinov's sarcasm is dry, and it's a bad idea.
- 'But the stove asked you not to worry.' "Prosila" is the same "asked" as in the invented goodbye message. "Ne volnovatsya" is "not to worry". Barinov keeps up the joke with a straight face.
- 'Stop clowning around and return it immediately!' Angry as she is, Vika gives both commands the polite -ite ending: "prekratite", "vernite". "Payasnichat" is built on "payats", "clown". "Eyo", "her", stands for the stove, "plita", which is feminine.
- 'The repairmen promise to return everything very soon.' "Mastera", "masters", is the word for craftsmen and repairmen. "Obeshchayut" is "promise", the word Louis used about his cake. Each lie needs another.
- 'On what should the cooks prepare hot food?' "Goryachee" is "hot" with a neuter ending, and alone it means "hot dishes". "Na chyom" is the cook's "on what" again.
- 'We have a small electric burner.' "U nas est", "at us there is", is the pattern for having a thing. "Elektricheskaya" is easy to recognise. Barinov's answer to the problem is a toy.
- 'This is a top-class restaurant, not a canteen!' Russian says "and not" with "a ne": "restoran, a ne stolovaya". A "stolovaya" is the plain cafeteria of a school or factory, and "stol" is a table. "Vysshego klassa", "of the highest class", comes after the noun.
- 'I will call Dmitry Nagiyev right now.' You phone "to" someone in Russian, so both names take the "to" ending: "Dmitriyu Nagievu". "Pozvonyu" is a future, one finished call. Adding "zhe" to "seychas" makes it "this very minute".
- 'No need to call anyone, I will sort it out.' "Nikomu", "to nobody", sits with "ne nado" as a double negative. "Razberus", "I will sort it out", comes from "razbirat", "take apart". We know he won't.
