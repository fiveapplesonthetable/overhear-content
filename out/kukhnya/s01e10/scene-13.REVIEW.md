## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Kostya, quickly speak, what is the matter!' "V chyom delo", literally "in what is the matter", is a phrase you've heard a few times now. "Govori" is "speak", said to one person. Vika is done with polite questions.
- 'Vika, please, stop scaring me!' "Perestan" is "stop", said to one person. "Pugat", "to scare", is the same family as "ispugalsya", "got scared". Kostya begs.
- 'Or I will transfer Nastya to another shift!' "Perevedu", "I will transfer", has pere-, "across", on the front. Nastya becomes "Nastyu", because she's the one being moved. "V druguyu smenu" is "to another shift".
- 'All right, I will tell you everything right now!' "Skazhu" is "I will tell", the future of "skazat". "Pryamo seychas" is "straight now", "right now". Kostya gives in after one sentence from Vika.
- 'I am listening attentively to your confession.' "Priznanie", "confession", comes from "priznatsya", the "priznalas" Barinov used about Vika. "Vnimatelno" is "attentively". Her tone is formal and dry.
- 'Max found out your secret from Alisa.' "Uznal", "found out", can also mean "recognised". "Ot Alisy" is "from Alisa": after "ot", Alisa becomes "Alisy". "Sekret" is easy.
- 'What secret did that fool find out?' "Kakoy eshchyo sekret" is "what still secret", "what other secret". "Durak" is "fool", blunt, though Vika says it with some affection.
- 'He thinks that you love him!' "Lyubish ego" is "you love him": "ego" is both "him" and "it", the same word used for the chocolate. "Dumaet" is "thinks".
- 'And why did he start hiding now?' "Stal pryatatsya" is "became hiding", what Russians say for "started to hide". "Stal" is from "stat", the same as "stanet legche", "it will become easier".
- 'He got scared of a wedding and little kids!' "Ispugalsya" is back with its -sya. After it, "svadba" becomes "svadby" and "deti" becomes "detey": being afraid of something takes the "of" ending.
- 'Poor cowardly Maxik, got scared of kids.' "Maksik" is a pet form of Max, made by adding -ik. It can be loving or mocking, and here it is both. "Truslivy" is "cowardly", from "trus", "coward".
- 'Thank you for not touching Nastya's shift!' Word for word, "thank you that you don't touch": Russian links the reason with "chto". In "smenu Nasti" the owner comes after the thing, and "Nastya" changes to "Nasti" to mean "Nastya's".
