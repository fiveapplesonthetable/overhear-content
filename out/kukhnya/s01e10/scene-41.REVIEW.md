## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Vitya, come quickly here to the table!' Nagiyev uses the friendly "Vitya", not "Viktor". "Idi syuda" is "go to here", the Russian for "come here". "K stolu" is "to the table".
- 'What happened, Dmitry Vladimirovich, what is the matter?' Barinov uses the first name and father's name. "V chyom delo" is "in what is the matter", the question Barinov asked Vika in the last episode.
- 'Vitya, tell me straight, what is this?' "Chego" is a casual form of "chto", "what", and "eto chego?" has no verb: "this what?". "Skazhi pryamo" is "say straight".
- 'What? What is this on the plate?' Barinov repeats the question back. "Na tarelke" is "on the plate", from "tarelka". "Vot eto" is "this here".
- 'This is real fresh Russian caviar.' "Samaya nastoyashchaya" is "the most real"; "samaya" here means "the very". "Svezhaya russkaya ikra" is "fresh Russian caviar".
- 'And very expensive and very good caviar!' The line opens with "prichyom", "and what's more". "Dorogaya" is "expensive" and "khoroshaya" is "good". Barinov piles on the adjectives.
- 'Vitya, are you a complete fool or pretending?' "Durak" is "fool", Vika's word for Max: "etot durak". "Pritvoryaeshsya" is "you are pretending", with -sya on the end.
- 'In what sense a fool, I do not understand you!' "V kakom smysle" is "in what sense", a casual "what do you mean". "Ne ponimayu vas" uses the polite "vas". Barinov plays offended.
- 'I am asking you, what is this?!' "Ya tebya sprashivayu" is "I you am asking". The question "vot eto chego?" comes back word for word, louder. Nagiyev is not letting go.
- 'The guests asked for black caviar for the holiday table.' "K prazdnichnomu stolu" is "to the holiday table"; "prazdnichny" was the word for the cake, "prazdnichny tort". "Prosili" is "asked for".
- 'But the color of this caviar is strange and suspicious.' "Tsvet u etoy ikry" is "the colour at this caviar". "Podozritelny", "suspicious", comes up in Senya's "nichego podozritelnogo".
- 'Special lighting in the hall changes all shades.' "Svet" is "light", and it also means "world", as in Alisa's "na svete". "Menyaet" is "changes", from Fedya's "menyatsya". "Ottenki" are "shades".
- 'Do not lie to me, tell the whole truth now!' "Ne vri" is "don't lie", from "vrat", as in Tanya's "A vrat ty tak i ne nauchilsya". "Vsyu pravdu" is "the whole truth".
