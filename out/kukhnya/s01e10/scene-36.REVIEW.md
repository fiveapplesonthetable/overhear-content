## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello, Vika! I will sit at the corner table.' "Zdravstvuy" is the long hello for one person you know. "Syadu" is "I will sit down". Russians sit "za" a table, "behind" it: "za uglovoy stolik", as in Kostya's "Gost za pyatym stolikom".
- 'Hi, Tanya! What happened?' "Privet" is the informal hello. "Chto sluchilos?" is "what happened?", the usual question when something seems wrong. Vika can see it in her sister's face.
- 'Why didn't you tell me anything at all?' Russian has "skazat", "to say", and "rasskazat", "to tell", for news or a story, as in Max's "Mne tolko chto rasskazali samuyu bezumnuyu novost". "Nichego ne rasskazala" is the double negative.
- 'Tanya, I don't understand your strange questions.' After "ne ponimayu", "voprosy" can turn into "voprosov": "tvoikh strannykh voprosov". "Strannye" is "strange". Vika has no idea what Tanya means.
- 'You should have immediately called your own sister!' "Dolzhna byla" is "had to", "should have": "dolzhna" plus "byla", "was". "Rodnoy sestre" is "to your own sister"; "rodnoy" means "related by blood", as in Vika's "eyo rodnoy papa".
- 'Explain to me calmly, what happened at all?' "Obyasni" is "explain", from Barinov's "Tanya, podozhdi, ya vsyo obyasnyu!". "Spokoyno" is "calmly". "Proizoshlo" is "happened".
- 'You are pregnant, and I find out about it last!' This is Vika's complaint about the stove, word for word: "Pochemu ya uznayu ob etom posledney?". "Beremenna" is "pregnant". Now it is Tanya who finds out last.
- 'Where did you get such nonsense at all?' "S chego ty vzyala" is "from what did you take", meaning "what makes you think so". "Glupost", "nonsense", comes from "glupy", "stupid".
- 'Chef Vitya told me about this personally!' "Lichno" is "personally", Barinov's word in "Ya lichno otvyoz eyo v servis". The person comes last: "lichno skazal shef Vitya", "personally said chef Vitya". Tanya names her source.
- 'Viktor again made up this insane story!' "Pridumal" is "made up". "Bezumnaya", "insane", is "bez" plus "um", "without mind", heard in Max's "bezumnuyu novost". "Opyat" is "again".
- 'So, you are not expecting a baby at all?' "Znachit" is "so", literally "it means". "Zhdat rebyonka" is "to expect a baby", the phrase in Max's plan to wait for the baby together.
- 'Of course not, it is simply not true!' "Nepravda" is "untruth": "ne" plus "pravda". "Konechno net" is "of course not". Vika denies it in the plainest way.
- 'Then you need to urgently speak with Viktor.' "Pogovorit s Viktorom" is "to talk with Viktor", the verb in Vika's "Nam nado seryozno pogovorit". "Srochno" is "urgently". "Tebe nuzhno" is "to you it is needed".
