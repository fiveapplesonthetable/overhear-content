## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello, Vika! I sat down at the corner table.' Tanya says "zdravstvuy", the casual form of the long hello. "Sat down" ends in -la, since she's a woman. "Behind the little corner table" is how a diner sits. She is friendly, for now.
- 'Hello, Tanya! Why did you come so urgently?' "Privet" is the informal hello. "Came" has the woman's -la. "Urgently" is "srochno", which began the whole banquet. Vika is polite, and she is already wary.
- 'Why didn't you tell me anything at all?' Russian has "say" and "tell", and this is the second one, for telling a story or giving news. "Nothing not" carries the double negative. Tanya sounds hurt.
- 'Tanya, I don't understand your strange questions.' "Of your strange questions" is the "of" form, because "not understand" asks for it. Vika is honestly puzzled. She has no idea what has been said.
- 'You should have immediately called your own sister!' "Must" with a past "were" makes "should have". "Own sister" uses the word "rodnoy", for a sister by blood. Tanya's complaint is a family complaint.
- 'Explain to me calmly, what happened at all?' "Explain" is the command form of the word the chef used to Tanya. "Happened" is a close relative of "what is happening". Vika is calm, because she has nothing to hide.
- 'You are pregnant, and I find out about it last!' "Find out" is the same as in Vika's earlier complaint. "Last" ends in -ey for a woman. Tanya's anger is clear, and so is her hurt.
- 'Where did you get such nonsense at all?' Russian says "from what did you take", meaning "what made you think so". "Nonsense" covers foolishness. Vika is angry, though not yet at her sister.
- 'Chef Vitya told me about this personally!' "Personally" is the one the chef used about the stove. The doer of the sentence comes last: "to me about this said chef Vitya". Tanya names her source.
- 'Viktor again made up this insane story!' "Made up" means inventing. "Insane" is "without mind". Vika knows who is behind it. She uses his proper name, Viktor, because she is angry.
- 'So, you are not expecting a baby at all?' "So" means "it means". "Wait for a baby" is "expecting", as before. Tanya checks the facts, and her tone changes.
- 'Of course not, this is simply an insolent lie!' "Lie" is related to the word "to lie". "Insolent" is a strong word, and it applies to the chef. Vika denies it in the plainest possible way.
- 'Then you need to urgently speak with Viktor.' "Then" starts the line. "Speak with" has the little "po" for a proper chat. Tanya tells her sister what to do. It's a natural move for an older sister.
