## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Let us start dyeing this pike caviar.' "Davay nachnyom", "let's begin", is how Russians start a job together. "Krasit" is "to dye" or "to paint", and it shares a root with "kraska", "paint". "Shchuchya ikra" is "pike caviar".
- 'Do you want to make it black?' "Sdelat eyo chyornoy" is "to make it black". After "sdelat", "chyornaya" turns into "chyornoy". Senya checks that he has understood the plan.
- 'Yes, we will dye all of this caviar.' "Pokrasim" is "we will dye": "krasit" with po- in front, for a job that gets finished. "Vsyu etu ikru" is "all this caviar". Barinov does not hesitate.
- 'It turns out a real pike Easter.' At Easter, "Paskha", Russians dye eggs, so dyed caviar becomes a "pike Easter". "Paskha" is also the name of a sweet curd dessert. "Poluchaetsya" is "it turns out".
- 'Bring black food dye here.' "Nesi" is "carry" or "bring", said to one person. "Syuda" is "to here", for movement; "zdes" is "here" for staying put. "Krasitel" is the dye itself.
- 'I found an excellent dye on the shelf.' "Nashyol" is "found", heard in Barinov's question about the knife, "Gde ty ego nashyol?". "Na polke" is "on the shelf", from "polka".
- 'Add the dye slowly and carefully.' "Dobavlyay" is "keep adding", for a step that takes time. "Medlenno i akkuratno" is "slowly and carefully". Barinov wants the colour to go in evenly.
- 'The caviar is already becoming completely black.' "Stanovitsya" is "becomes", a change still happening. After it the colour takes the same -oy ending as before: "sovsem chyornoy". Senya reports the progress.
- 'Now stir all the caviar well with a spoon.' "Pomeshay" is "give it a stir", from "meshat", the word Louis used for the chocolate: "Meshay ravnomerno". "Lozhkoy" is "with a spoon", from "lozhka".
- 'Now it looks like expensive black caviar.' "Pokhozha na" is "looks like", literally "similar onto". After "na", "dorogaya chyornaya ikra" becomes "doroguyu chyornuyu ikru". "Dorogoy" means both "expensive" and "dear".
- 'Not a single guest of the restaurant will notice anything.' "Ni odin gost", "nichego", "ne zametit": three negatives, and Russian needs all of them. Barinov said much the same about the cake: "chtoby gosti nichego ne zametili".
- 'Chef, you are simply a culinary genius.' "Geniy" is "genius" and "kulinarny" is "culinary", both easy to recognise. "Prosto" is "simply". Senya flatters his boss.
- 'No one must know this secret.' "Nikto ne dolzhen" is "nobody not must". "Dolzhen" also means "owe": Fedya told Barinov "Vy mne eshchyo pyat tysyach dolzhny", "you still owe me five thousand". "Znat" is "to know".
