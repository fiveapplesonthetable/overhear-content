## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Let us start dyeing this pike caviar.' "Let's begin" is the pair you know. "Dye" comes from the term for "paint". Barinov gives the order cheerfully. It's an odd way to cook.
- 'Do you want to turn it into black?' "Turn into" is a "change-over" word. Senya checks that he has understood. His voice shows a mixture of awe and doubt.
- 'Yes, we will dye all of this caviar.' The prefix "po-" in "will dye" gives the sense of "all the way through". "All of this" shows "all" in the ending. Barinov is decisive.
- 'This turns out to be a real pike Easter.' Russians dye eggs for Easter, so dyed caviar is a "pike Easter". It is a joke on the festival and also the name of a cheese dessert. Senya has a sense of humour.
- 'Bring black food dye here.' "Carry" stands for carrying in your hands. "To here" is the word that goes with "here". Barinov gives commands the way he gives everything, loudly.
- 'I found an excellent dye on the shelf.' "Found" is the past of "find". "On the shelf" has the ending for "on". Senya is resourceful. The kitchen has everything, including things it shouldn't.
- 'Add the dye slowly and carefully.' "Add" is an ongoing command, "keep adding". "Carefully" also means "neatly". Barinov teaches him the craft of forgery.
- 'The caviar is already becoming completely black.' "Becoming" is the on-going form of "become". "Completely" echoes "completely not right". Senya reports the progress with delight.
- 'Now stir all the caviar well with a spoon.' "Stir" has the "po-" of "a little while". "With a spoon" shows "with" in its ending. Barinov supervises, and the work is slow.
- 'It looks just like expensive black caviar.' "Just like" is "straight as". "Expensive" is the "dear" word. Senya is amazed at his own work, and he sounds like an artist.
- 'Not a single guest of the restaurant will notice anything.' It's a triple negative: "not one", "nothing", "not". Russian wants all of them. Barinov is full of confidence, and that is a bad sign.
- 'You are simply a genius of culinary secrets, chef.' "Genius" is the same as in English. "Of culinary secrets" has the "of" endings. Senya flatters his boss, and the boss accepts it.
- 'No one must know this secret.' "Nobody must" is "nobody not must", with the "must" word from the loan scene. "Know" is the plain form. Barinov closes the scene with his rule, and we know it won't hold.
