## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Sorry, mom, I was very late today.' "Izvini" is a bare command, "excuse me", and it is the casual one. His "was late" ends in -l because he is a boy. Russians sometimes say "strongly" where English says "very".
- 'Why did you come home so late?' The Russian for "home", as in "come home", is one little word that already carries the "to". So there's no separate word for it. His mother's question is more worry than anger.
- 'I walked for a long time with my friends.' The word he uses means to stroll or hang about, not to walk somewhere. "With my friends" has the usual ending for "with" on the last word. It's a boy's story, and it's probably true.
- 'You got lucky that dad is sleeping.' Russian doesn't say "you got lucky". It says "to you it was lucky", which turns you into the person who just happens to benefit. She is on his side.
- 'Is dad really angry with me?' The little word at the start is "really?", a bit of doubt. And Russians are angry "on" someone, with the -sya ending that makes it "angers himself". The boy is worried, which is natural.
- 'Dad lost all our money again.' The verb is "play" with a prefix that makes it "play away", so you lose by playing. That fits a gambler. And "money" is plural in Russian, so "all" has the plural form too.
- 'Did he play with our money again?' Russians say they play "on" money, meaning stakes. So the whole phrase is "played on our money". The boy already knows the answer, but he asks it anyway.
- 'Yes, he placed bets on football.' Russians "make" a bet, like making a decision. A bet is a "stake", something you put down. The mother says it plainly. She has said it before, many times.
- 'And his favorite team lost again?' "Team" is a feminine word, so "favorite" has the feminine ending. The question is short and weary. It's the sort of conversation a family has when it has had it before.
- 'Yes, his favorite Spartak lost again.' Spartak is a Moscow football club, and the chef in this show loves it too. This time the club is masculine, so "favorite" turns masculine. The mother names the team as if it were an enemy.
- 'Now do we have no money at all?' It's "at us entirely no of money". After "no" in this sense, the thing that's missing takes an "of" ending. So there is no "have" and no "any", just that "of".
- 'It is alright, son, we will cope with everything.' "Nothing" is Russian for "never mind" here. "Sonny" is an affectionate cut-down of "son". And the last word is "cope", the same one Louis used in the pastry kitchen, now in the plural.
- 'Tomorrow I will find myself a good job.' The boy says "find to myself", where English says "find myself". It's his answer to the family's trouble, and he says it with a boy's confidence. He is trying to be the man of the house.
