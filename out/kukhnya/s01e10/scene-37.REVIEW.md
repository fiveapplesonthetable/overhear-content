## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Vitya, why did you lie to Tanya about me?' "Deceived" is what Kostya used about never deceiving Vika. "About me" uses the same small word as "about the chef". Vika uses the friendly "Vitya", which makes it sound pointed.
- 'Chef, I can explain everything in detail now.' "In detail" grows out of a word for a thing described fully. "Can" and "explain" go with "everything". Max steps in, though no one has asked him.
- 'You, boy, picked the wrong girl today!' "Boy" is "malchik", the little-boy word, and it is an insult here. "Not that one" means "the wrong one". Vika is furious, and she scolds in a cold, clear voice.
- 'And now we will put on a real fun show.' "Arrange" stands for staging something, the same as the chef's scandal. "Cheerful" is a good adjective for a show. Max has decided to play along.
- 'Hello, Tatyana, let's get acquainted, I am Maxim.' The name "Tatyana" is the full form of Tanya, and "Maksim" the full form of Max. Using them is formal. "Get acquainted" has the -sya, "acquaint ourselves".
- 'Max, stop this silly circus immediately!' "Stop" is the command you've heard. "Circus" in Russian means a farce, just as in English. Vika is not amused, and she says so.
- 'I am the father of your future wonderful nephew!' "Future" is easy to guess. "Nephew" is the masculine of the "niece" word. Max invents a role for himself, and he enjoys it. Vika is horrified.
- 'You are behaving like a complete madman!' "Lead yourself" is "behave". "Madman" is built from "went from the mind", the phrase Senya used. Vika picks the same words as the cook, and that's no accident.
- 'We are now the closest family members.' "Closest" and "kin" are two words you have heard separately. Max puts them together. He is making a speech, and he overdoes it.
- 'I want to say something important to you all.' "Koe-chto" means "something in particular". The "koe-" prefix works like the "-to" ending. Max prepares his audience for his announcement.
- 'Vika, marry me right now!' A woman "goes out behind a husband": "vykhodi za menya zamuzh". The last word is "za" plus "muzh", "behind-husband". It is the Russian way to say a woman marries. Max says it loudly.
- 'You are simply insolently and openly bluffing now!' "Boldly" and "openly" are two adverbs. "Bluff" is a borrowed word with a Russian ending. Vika sees through him at once. She refuses to be rattled.
- 'I am speaking completely seriously in front of your sister!' "In front of" is the small word "pered". "Seriously" is the one used by the chef. Max insists, and he is committed to the show.
