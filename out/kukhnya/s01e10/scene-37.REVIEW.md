## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Vitya, why did you lie to Tanya about me?' "Obmanul" is "deceived", "lied to", as in Kostya's "ya nikogda tebya ne obmanyvayu". "Pro menya" is a casual "about me". "Vitya" is the short, friendly form of Viktor.
- 'Chef, I can explain everything.' "Mogu vsyo obyasnit" is "I can explain everything". Barinov said nearly the same to Tanya in the last episode: "ya vsyo obyasnyu". This time it is Max who says it, to Barinov.
- 'You, boy, picked the wrong girl today!' "Malchik", "boy", is a put-down here. "Ne tu devushku" is "not that girl", the Russian for "the wrong girl". "Vybral" is "chose".
- 'And now we will put on a real show.' "Ustroim" is "we will put on", the verb in Barinov's "Ty ustroil nastoyashchiy bolshoy skandal". "Shou" is borrowed from English. "A seychas" is "and now".
- 'Hello, Tatyana, let's get acquainted, I am Maxim.' Max uses the full names, which sound formal: the full form of Tanya, and "Maksim" for himself. "Davayte znakomitsya" is "let's get acquainted", with -sya: "acquaint ourselves".
- 'Max, stop this silly circus immediately!' "Prekrati" is "stop", and "nemedlenno" is "immediately"; both were in Vika's "Prekratite payasnichat i nemedlenno vernite eyo!". "Tsirk" is "circus", a farce.
- 'I am the father of your future nephew!' "Otets" is "father". "Plemyannik", "nephew", is the partner of "plemyannitsa", "niece", which is what Vika calls Alisa. "Budushchego" is "future".
- 'You are behaving like a complete madman!' "Vedyosh sebya" is "you lead yourself", "you behave", as in Vika's "on stranno sebya vedyot". "Sumasshedshiy", "madman", comes from Senya's "s uma soshyol", "went off his mind".
- 'We are family now, after all.' "Rodstvenniki" is "relatives", from Max's "Neuzheli shef — tvoy blizkiy rodstvennik?". "My zhe teper" is "we after all now". Max greets Tanya as family.
- 'I want to say something important to you all.' "Koe-chto" is "something", a particular thing the speaker has in mind. "Vsem vam" is "to all of you", and "vazhnoe" is "important".
- 'Vika, marry me right now!' A woman "goes out behind a husband": "vykhodi za menya zamuzh". The last word is "za" plus "muzh", "behind-husband". It is the Russian way to say a woman marries. Max says it loudly.
- 'You are just bluffing!' "Blefuesh" is "you're bluffing": the English "bluff" with a Russian ending. "Zhe" adds "come on". "Prosto" is "just". Vika sees through him.
- 'I am speaking completely seriously in front of your sister!' "Pered tvoey sestroy" is "in front of your sister": after "pered", "sestra" becomes "sestroy". "Sovershenno seryozno" is "completely seriously". Max insists.
