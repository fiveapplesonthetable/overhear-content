## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Max, we need to talk seriously!' "Nam nado", "to us it's necessary", is the casual "we must", a cousin of "nuzhno". "Seryozno" is "seriously". Vika has had enough waiting.
- 'Hey, Vika! Glad to see you.' "Privet" is the casual hello. "Rad", "glad", ends bare because Max is a man; Vika would say "rada". His smile is as false as the greeting.
- 'Oh, Kostya is calling, sorry!' "Zvonit" is "rings" or "calls", the phone word. "Izvini" is the casual "sorry" the boy used in the first scene. Max waves his phone as he says it.
- 'Hello, Kostya, speak louder!' "Allo" is the phone hello. "Gromche", "louder", is "gromko", "loudly", in its "more" form. Max shouts into a phone that has not rung, and Vika is watching.
- 'Hard to hear, I will come over right now!' "Plokho slyshno", "badly audible", is what Russians say for "I can hardly hear". "Podoydu" is the come-over word Barinov used with Kostya. Max edges toward the door.
- 'Max, wait, do not leave!' "Ne ukhodi" is "don't go away": the u- on the front means "away". Its opposite is pri-, "towards", so "ukhodit" and "prikhodit" are leave and arrive. Vika isn't fooled.
- 'Vika, this is a very urgent matter!' "Srochnoe delo" is "urgent matter": "srochno" from the first episode scenes, and "delo" from "v chyom delo". "Srochnoe" ends in -oe to match "delo".
- 'Kostya, why did you call?' "Chego" is a casual way to ask "why", more relaxed than "pochemu". "Zvonil" is "called", with the man's -l. It's a clumsy question.
- 'What do you mean, I did not call you.' "V smysle", "in the sense", is the casual Russian for "what do you mean". "Ya tebe ne zvonil" is "I to you didn't ring": you ring "to" someone.
- 'Pretend that you need help!' Russian says "sdelay vid", "make a look", for "pretend". "Pomoshch", "help", is a she-word, so it's "nuzhna" with -a. Max whispers it.
- 'Oh, you are hiding from Vika?' "Pryachesh-sya" is "you hide yourself", from the same word as "spryatalas" in the hide-and-seek game. "Ot Viki" is "from Vika": after "ot", Vika becomes "Viki".
- 'Yes, play along with me, please!' "Podygray", "play along", is "igray", "play", with pod-, "under": play under me, back me up. "Mne" is "to me".
- 'All right, let us go to the storeroom.' "Nu davay" is "well, okay". "Poshli" is the casual "let's go", literally "went". "Na sklad" is "to the storeroom", and "sklad" stays as it is after "na" when you're heading there.
- 'Thank you, friend, you saved me!' "Spas", "saved", is from the same family as "spasibo", the one that began as "God save". "Drug" is "friend". It was a close thing.
