## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello, chef, how are your affairs today?' Russians ask "how are your affairs", in the plural, where we say "how are you". It's polite and friendly. Max has no idea what he's in for.
- 'Max, do not ask me foolish questions.' Russians "set" a question, like setting a task, so the word means "ask" in this sense. The questions take an "of" ending after the "don't". Barinov is short with him, as usual.
- 'Did you lose money on bets again?' Max uses the Russian for "lost" that means lost in a game, as the mother did in the first scene. He has guessed, and he's right. "Bets" has the ending for "on".
- 'I would gladly drink vodka right now.' A small word makes "would", so the sentence reads "I would drank". "With pleasure" is Russian for "gladly". Barinov sounds a man who wants the day to be over.
- 'What else unpleasant has happened to you?' "Unpleasant" is literally "not pleasant", and it takes the "of" ending after "what else". "At you happened" is the same pattern as the first scene of the episode, nearly. Max is concerned.
- 'The matter is not at all about my bets.' Russians say "the matter is in" something, and then the thing. So "the matter is not in my bets". It's a handy way to say "the point is". The chef won't be rushed.
- 'Then what is the real problem?' "In what" is the same pair as in "what is the matter". Max asks it calmly and carefully. The term for "real" is the one meaning genuine, which he has heard from Barinov himself.
- 'I have important news for you.' Here Russian does add the little word for "there is": "at me there is for you important news". It stresses that he has something. Barinov says "you" the casual way.
- 'What news, chef, tell me quickly.' "News" takes the feminine ending in the question. "Speak" here is the polite plural command, because Max is addressing the chef. The question is eager, and the chef takes his time.
- 'Aunt Vika said that she loves you.' Barinov repeats what his daughter said, "Aunt Vika", and keeps her way of saying it. There's no "she" in the Russian, only "said that loves you". It's the news, and it lands with a bump.
- 'What? That simply cannot be!' After "not", what Russian calls "this" takes its "of" form. It's a small oddity in Russian. "Can be" also means "maybe", which is why the line reads like a doubt as much as a denial.
- 'She herself confessed this to me.' The "herself" gives weight to it. "Confessed" stands for admitting something, and it ends in the woman's -las. Barinov says it as if he had been there.
- 'Chef, are you definitely not joking right now?' Max uses "definitely" and the polite "you" together, which is how you question your boss. The word for "joke" is the one Barinov used about the girl's mother. He is a happy man.
