## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello, chef, how are your affairs today?' Russians ask "kak vashi dela", "how are your affairs", in the plural, where we say "how are you". With a friend it's "kak dela". Max has no idea what he's in for.
- 'Max, do not ask me foolish questions.' Russians "zadavat", "set", a question, like setting a task. After "ne", "glupye voprosy" becomes "glupykh voprosov". Barinov is short with him, as usual.
- 'Did you lose money on bets again?' "Proigrali" is the losing-in-a-game word the mother used in the first scene. "Na stavkakh" is "on bets", with -akh after "na". Max has guessed, and he's right.
- 'I would gladly drink vodka right now.' "By" makes "would", and it goes with the past: "ya by vypil", "I would drank". "S udovolstviem", "with pleasure", is Russian for "gladly". "Vodki" is "some vodka".
- 'What else unpleasant has happened to you?' "Nepriyatnogo" is "not pleasant" with the "of" ending after "chto eshchyo". "U vas sluchilos" is "at you happened". Max says "vas", politely.
- 'The matter is not at all about my bets.' Russians say "delo v", "the matter is in", and then the thing. So "delo ne v moikh stavkakh" is "the matter is not in my bets". "Vovse" is "at all".
- 'Then what is the real problem?' "A v chyom", "and in what", picks up the "delo v" from the chef's line. "Nastoyashchaya" is "real", the same word as in "true friend". "Problema" is easy.
- 'I have important news for you.' Here Russian does add "est", "there is": "u menya est dlya tebya", "at me there is for you". It stresses that he has something. Barinov says "tebya", the casual "you".
- 'What news, chef, tell me quickly.' "Kakaya", "what", ends in -aya because "novost" is a she-word. "Govorite" is the polite "speak", with -te. "Skoree" is "quicker".
- 'Aunt Vika said that she loves you.' Barinov keeps his daughter's way of saying it, "tyotya Vika". There's no "she" in the Russian, only "skazala, chto lyubit tebya", "said that loves you".
- 'What? That simply cannot be!' After "ne", "eto" becomes "etogo": "etogo ne mozhet byt", a set phrase. And "mozhet byt" on its own means "maybe".
- 'She herself confessed this to me.' "Sama" is "herself", and it gives the claim weight. "Priznalas", "confessed", ends in the woman's -las. "V etom" is "in this": Russians confess "in" something.
- 'Chef, are you definitely not joking right now?' Max keeps the polite "vy" with his boss: in "vy ne shutite", -ite is the polite ending. "Tochno" makes it "for sure", and "seychas" pins it to right now.
