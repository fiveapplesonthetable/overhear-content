## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Taste this appetizer, gentlemen.' "Zakuska" is the little dish you eat alongside a drink, and "gospoda" is an old word for "gentlemen" that came back into use. It sounds slightly grand, which suits the caviar.
- 'Listen, this is wonderful caviar!' Russians begin with "slushayte", "listen", when they have something to say. "Chudesnaya", "wonderful", comes from "chudo", "miracle". The guest is warming up.
- 'You can immediately sense that it is natural.' The Russian is "srazu chuvstvuetsya", "at once it is felt": the -sya makes the feeling come by itself. "Naturalnaya" is "natural". The guest trusts his palate.
- 'Yes, I only ever ate such caviar in Astrakhan.' Astrakhan is a southern river city known for its caviar, so this is high praise. "El" is "ate". Word for word it is "such only in Astrakhan ate".
- 'In Moscow you rarely encounter such quality.' "Vstretish" is "you will meet", with "you" meaning "anyone". "Redko" is "rarely". "V Moskve" is "in Moscow": "Moskva" becomes "Moskve" after "v".
- 'Yes, delicious, very delicious!' "Vkusno" without the adjective ending means "it is tasty", and Russians shout it at the table. The repetition is genuine enthusiasm. The second guest cannot get enough.
- 'The head chef of this restaurant is a true master.' "Shef-povar" joins "chef" and "cook" into a title. "Master" is the word for the repairmen in the stove story: "Mastera obeshchayut". The guest praises the chef.
- 'And the fresh bread goes with it perfectly.' "Podkhodit" is "suits", from the walking-up verb of Fedya's "podoydi". "Svezhiy khleb" is "fresh bread". "Otlichno" is "excellently".
- 'We will definitely order another portion.' "Obyazatelno" is "without fail", heard in Max's promise of a big wedding. "Zakazhem" is "we will order", from "zakaz", "order". "Portsiya" is "portion".
- 'I am ready to come here every day.' "Prikhodit" is "to come", in the form for coming again and again. "Gotov" is "ready". "Kazhdy den" is "every day".
- 'Pass on our sincere gratitude to the chef.' "Peredayte" is "pass on", as in Kostya's "ya peredam vashi slova". "Iskrennyuyu blagodarnost" is "sincere gratitude", a longer, formal "thanks". "Shefu" is "to the chef".
- 'This evening was a glorious success!' The Russian is "succeeded to glory", an idiom for "went wonderfully". "Succeeded" grows from the word for "luck". The guest closes the scene with a flourish.
