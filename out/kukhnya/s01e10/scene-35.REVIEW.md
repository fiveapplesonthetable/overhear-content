## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Taste this appetizer, gentlemen.' "Zakuska" is the little dish you eat alongside a drink, and "gospoda" is an old word for "gentlemen" that came back into use. It sounds slightly grand, which suits the caviar.
- 'Listen, this is wonderful caviar!' Russians begin with "listen" when they have something to say. "Chudesnaya" grows out of what Russian calls "miracle", the same root as in the chef's culinary miracle. The guest is warming up.
- 'You can immediately sense that it is natural.' The Russian is "immediately it is felt", with the -sya doing the passive job. So the feeling comes by itself. The man is sure of his palate, and the dye is invisible.
- 'Yes, I only ever ate such caviar in Astrakhan.' Astrakhan is a southern river city known for its caviar, so this is high praise. "Ate" is "el". Word for word it's "such only in Astrakhan ate". He remembers it like a holiday.
- 'In Moscow you rarely encounter such quality.' Russian says "you" for "one" or "people": "rarely you will meet". "Meet" is the verb for running into something. The guest speaks as a connoisseur.
- 'Yes, delicious, very delicious!' "Vkusno" without the adjective ending means "it is tasty", and Russians shout it at the table. The repetition is genuine enthusiasm. The second guest cannot get enough.
- 'The head chef of this restaurant is a true master.' "Shef-povar" joins "chef" and "cook" together, like a title. "Master" is the same word as the repairmen in the stove story. The guest praises Barinov, who is not there.
- 'And the fresh bread goes with it perfectly.' "Goes with" is the walking-up word again, which also means "suits". "Bread" is "khleb". The guests talk about bread now, which is the kind of detail people mention when everything is fine.
- 'We will definitely order another portion.' "Obyazatelno" means "without fail", and it comes from the same root as "obliged". The guest has decided. This is a bill that someone else will have to explain.
- 'I am ready to come here every day.' "Come" is in a form that means "come regularly". "Ready" is "gotov". The guest says it with great warmth, which is the highest praise a restaurant can get.
- 'Pass on our sincere gratitude to the chef.' "Pass on" is the same "give across" you've heard. "Sincere" is a warm word, and "gratitude" is longer than "thanks". It's a formal, old-fashioned sentence, and the guests mean it.
- 'This evening was a glorious success!' The Russian is "succeeded to glory", an idiom for "went wonderfully". "Succeeded" grows from the word for "luck". The guest closes the scene with a flourish.
