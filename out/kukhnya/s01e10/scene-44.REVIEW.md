## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Chef, why are you shouting so loudly?' "Krichite" is "you shout", polite, from the verb in Fedya's "pochemu ty tak krichish?". "Tak gromko" is "so loudly". "Vy pochemu" puts "you" first.
- 'Quickly get out of here and close the door!' "Uydi" is "go away", with u-, "off". "Otsyuda" is "from here". "Zakroy dver" was in Barinov's "Zakhodi v komnatu i zakroy dver", the opposite order.
- 'I just brought clean chef aprons.' Notice "prinyos", "brought": a past tense with no -l at all, one of the irregular ones. "Povarskie" is the adjective from "povar", "cook", and "chistye" is the word in Vika's "chistuyu pravdu", "pure truth".
- 'I do not need any aprons right now, runt!' "Nikakie... ne nuzhny" is "not any... needed", the double negative. "Oguzok" is Barinov's old nickname for Max, a cheap cut of beef.
- 'Did Spartak really lose this match again?' "Neuzheli" is "can it really be", from Barinov's own question to the guests. "Proigral" is "lost". "Opyat" is "again".
- 'They conceded a goal in the last minute!' "Propustili gol" is "let a goal through", football talk for "conceded". "Na posledney minute" is "in the last minute", with "na", "on".
- 'Chef, it is only an ordinary sports game.' "Vsego lish" is "only", two small words that mean the same. "Sportivnaya igra" is "sports game". Max says the wrong thing.
- 'For me football is all my life!' All my life is "vsya moya zhizn", and "dlya menya" is "for me". The dash stands where English has "is".
- 'But guests in the hall are waiting for hot dishes.' "Zhdut goryachie blyuda" is "wait hot dishes", with no "for". "Goryachie" is "hot", heard in "goryachiy tsekh", the hot kitchen. Max tries to bring him back to work.
- 'Let Lyova run the hot kitchen himself!' "Pust" is "let", as in Barinov's "Pust zaydyot ko mne v kabinet". "Komanduet" is "commands". "V goryachem tsekhe" is "in the hot shop"; "tsekh" is a workshop.
- 'Lyova is very anxious without your strict instructions.' "Volnuetsya" is "worries", with -sya. "Ukazaniy", "instructions", comes from "ukazat", "to point out". "Strogikh" is "strict".
- 'Fine, go peel potatoes and do not bother me!' "Chistit" is "to peel" and also "to clean", as in Barinov's "On budet chistit i rezat". "Ne meshay" is "don't bother", the other meaning of "meshat".
- 'Right away, chef, I am already running back to the kitchen.' Max answers with "slushayus", "yes, sir". "Begu" is "I am running", as in his "U menya tozhe vsyo khorosho, begu". "Obratno" is "back".
