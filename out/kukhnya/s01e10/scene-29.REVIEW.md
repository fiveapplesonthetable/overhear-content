## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Here is the goods, as you ordered, chef.' "Goods" is singular again in the Russian, so "here is the goods". "As you ordered" uses the order word. Senya is proud of himself. He says it with a flourish.
- 'Open the box and show me the caviar.' Both orders are bare commands. "Open" is the one you heard in the previous scene. Barinov is eager, and he can't wait.
- 'This is excellent pike caviar, five kilograms.' "Pike" turns into a describing word, "pike's", for the caviar. "Kilograms" here has its full ending. Senya states the facts, and the facts are not what the chef wanted.
- 'It looks very beautiful and appetizing.' "Appetizing" is made from "appetite". "Looks" is the "appear" word. Barinov is half pleased, because he's not looking at the colour yet.
- 'The price is only two hundred rubles per kilogram.' "Only" is made from "all", so "in all two hundred rubles". "Per" is the small word for "for". Senya has done the sums and thinks he's a hero.
- 'This is indeed a very cheap price.' "Cheap" is the opposite of "dear". The word for "indeed" is the same one used by Barinov at the tasting. He is pleased with the price, but only a little.
- 'We saved the restaurant a lot of money.' Russian has two words for "save". This one means "keep", as in not spending. The other means "rescue". Senya speaks as if he had done the restaurant a favour.
- 'But the color of this caviar is too yellow.' "Too" is back from the little hotplate. "Colour" and "yellow" are both short. Barinov spots the flaw at last, and the "but" says it.
- 'Pike caviar is always yellow in color.' "Is usually" is the habit word, "happens to be". "Of yellow colour" is the way Russian describes a quality. Senya defends the pike, and also his own purchase.
- 'And the guests expect expensive black caviar.' "Expect" is "wait", with no "for". "Expensive" is the "dear" word. Barinov names the gap between the box and the menu.
- 'What are we to do with this color?' The little "zhe" is back. "With this colour" has the "with" ending. Senya asks his chef, as a good cook asks. He has no plan of his own.
- 'I have got a clever plan.' "Appeared at me" is how Russians say "I've had an idea". "Cunning" was used of the rat. Barinov grins, and the audience knows trouble is coming.
- 'I am ready to help you in this business.' "Ready" is the one from "ready to play". "Help" is the root of the Russian for assistant. "In this matter" means "business" or "matter". Senya is in.
