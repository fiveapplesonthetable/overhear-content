## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Oguzok, have you completely lost your mind?' Notice how Russian expresses 'Rump' and 'lost your mind' with crisp inflection rather than helper words. Max answers right away.
- 'Chef, wait, what happened?' Max raises the pitch on 'happened' to turn the phrase into a direct spoken question.
- 'You ruined my dream with your pâté!' Spoken Russian keeps this brisk, leaning on 'you' and finishing on 'pâté'.
- 'Vitya, what exactly is the matter here?' Spoken Russian keeps this brisk, leaning on 'proper name' and finishing on 'business'.
- 'Why is this guy cooking for the Michelin inspector?' Eleonora anchors the line on 'why', while 'Michelin' completes the thought.
- 'I appointed him temporarily, gave him a chance.' The '-l' ending on the verb meaning 'to give' places the action in the masculine past tense.
- 'I was the one who gave you a chance to work in a prestigious hotel!' The meaning hinges on 'this' working together with 'hotel' in spoken Russian. Chef Barinov answers right away.
- 'Without me your hotel would have become a station flophouse!' The '-l' ending on the verb meaning 'to become' places the action in the masculine past tense.
- 'Do you think you are here because you are a great chef?' Eleonora brings out the contrast between 'you' and 'think' in a few short words.
- 'When you were left without a job, Katya came to me.' Eleonora addresses the listener with the informal singular 'you' alongside 'Katya'.
- 'She cried that nobody would hire her dad anywhere.' Eleonora brings out the contrast between 'she' and 'were crying' in a few short words.
- 'I simply felt sorry for you back then.' Eleonora addresses the listener with the informal singular 'you' alongside 'felt sorry for'.
