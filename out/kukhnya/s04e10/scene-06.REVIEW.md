## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'An ordinary cook could not have done this.' Putting the short word for 'not' right in front of the action word is all Russian needs to make a sentence negative, with no extra helper words like 'do' or 'did'.
- 'He is not an ordinary cook.' Fedya repeats the exact adjective phrase with 'not' to build dramatic tension.
- 'He is Sergeant Arseny Meat-Grinder.' The compound noun for 'meat-grinder' combines the roots for 'meat' and 'chop'.
- 'He and I served together.' Instead of saying 'he and I', Russian says 'we with him' plus a plural verb.
- 'And I do not care at all.' Here Nagiyev anchors the thought on 'at all' and finishes on 'to all', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'Aren't you supposed to be businessmen?' The particle 'zhe' reminds the villain of his own earlier claim.
- 'Or who are you after all?' Short direct questions keep the pacing brisk.
- 'And here I am, villains!' The word order 'and here is I' is the natural Russian way to announce a dramatic entrance.
- 'Senya, you came to save us!' Here Fedya anchors the thought on 'arrived' and finishes on 'to save', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'It is nothing, anyone would have acted the same way.' The adjective 'any' stands alone as a pronoun meaning 'anyone'.
- 'I simply did my best for you, guys.' Senya pairs 'simply, just' with 'guys, team' directly, since spoken Russian skips English filler words like 'the', 'a', and 'is'.
- 'Thank you, our savior!' Fedya uses a polite everyday Russian courtesy expression that sets the tone of the conversation right away.
