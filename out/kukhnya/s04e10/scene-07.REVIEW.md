## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Brother, you are simply the best.' The slang word for 'bro' pairs with the superlative adjective 'best'.
- 'You are celebrating too early!' The adverb 'early' precedes the reflexive verb 'to rejoice'.
- 'Now the little cook will get what is coming to him.' The diminutive noun for 'little cook/kitchen boy' is used mockingly by the villain.
- 'The beef is a bit tough today.' The suffix '-ovat-' on an adjective means 'somewhat' or 'a bit too tough'.
- 'I love olives with the pit.' Spitting out a bullet like an olive pit, Barinov uses the instrumental singular after 'with'.
- 'And is that all you are capable of?' The short-form adjective 'capable' takes the preposition 'on' plus the accusative.
- 'Viktoriya Sergeyevna, I told you so.' Combining 'I' with the particle 'zhe' and 'told' means 'I told you so'.
- 'This chandelier should have been replaced long ago.' The impersonal verb 'it is worth' pairs with the perfective infinitive 'to replace'.
- 'I am too young for all this.' Inverting the famous Hollywood cop cliché, fifty-year-old Barinov claims he is 'too young' using the short masculine adjective.
- 'Too young for such nonsense.' After the preposition 'for', the noun phrase takes the genitive case.
- 'We defeated all the enemies!' The animate plural object 'all enemies' takes the genitive-accusative ending '-ov'.
- 'Excellent work, Sergeant Senya!' Barinov salutes Senya in his daydream with a warm compliment.
