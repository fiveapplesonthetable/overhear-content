## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Viktor Petrovich, the guest refused the dish.' The reflexive verb 'to refuse' always takes the preposition 'from' plus the genitive case.
- 'What did this guest not like?' In Russian, 'what did he not like?' is phrased as 'what did not please to this guest?'.
- 'He has a severe allergy to salt.' Medical allergies in Russian take the preposition 'on' plus the accusative case ('allergy on salt').
- 'He says this is not a restaurant, but a dive.' The colloquial noun comes from 'running in' for a quick bite — a cheap diner or dive.
- 'Have you completely lost your minds in there?' Nagiyev rushes into the kitchen in a panic.
- 'The Emperor has been waiting half his life for his order!' Since the alien only lives seven hours, waiting twenty minutes really is half his life!
- 'I am telling jokes in front of him.' In Russian, 'anekdot' means a funny short joke, not a personal anecdote.
- 'Who cooked this dish today?' Even with a plural group of cooks, the question word 'who' always takes masculine singular verb agreement.
- 'Listen, in a minute the dish will be ready.' Time expressions for 'in a minute' use the preposition 'through/across' plus the accusative.
- 'The Emperor is displeased, he will destroy us!' The perfective future verb means to annihilate or destroy completely.
- 'By the way, where is my clone?' The real Barinov suddenly walks in and asks where his duplicate is.
- 'He was supposed to replace me.' Combining 'must' with past 'was' means 'was supposed to'.
- 'He has already replaced you, chef!' Barinov wakes up with a start as his own clone grins back at him.
