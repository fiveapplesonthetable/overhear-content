## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Viktor Petrovich, the guest refused the dish.' Over the order and the dish, Lyova answers Barinov without a second thought.
- 'What did this guest not like?' Here asking what this guest not like shows Barinov looking for the real truth.
- 'He has a severe allergy to salt.' Adding that he has a severe allergy to salt fills in the missing piece for Barinov. Russian expresses having something as "by me is", keeping the phrase short and grounded.
- 'He says this is not a restaurant, but a dive.' Lyova reminds Barinov that he says this is not a restaurant but a dive before the moment passes. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'Have you completely lost your minds in there?' By checking whether Barinov completely lost Barinov's minds in there, Nagiyev presses Barinov for a straight answer.
- 'The Emperor has been waiting half his life for his order!' Insisting that the Emperor has been waiting half his life for his order shows Nagiyev is not backing down. Russian puts the key word right up front so his emotion lands on the first beat.
- 'I am telling jokes in front of him.' Realizing that he is telling jokes in front of him changes how Nagiyev handles the situation.
- 'Who cooked this dish today?' With the moment and order hanging in the air, Nagiyev puts Barinov on the spot.
- 'Listen, in a minute the dish will be ready.' Pointing out that in a minute the dish will be ready gives Lyova a sobering reality check.
- 'The Emperor is displeased, he will destroy us!' Saying that the Emperor is displeased he will destroy them is how Nagiyev takes charge of the moment. Russian puts the key word right up front so his emotion lands on the first beat.
- 'By the way, where is my clone?' By wondering where his clone is, Barinov presses Lyova for a straight answer.
- 'He was supposed to replace me.' Noting that he was supposed to replace him keeps Barinov's side of the story simple and clear.
- 'He has already replaced you, chef!' Here having already replaced Barinov lets Lyova's real feelings break through. Even in the middle of the drama, he keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
