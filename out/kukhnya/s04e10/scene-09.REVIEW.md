## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Ugh, Fedya, how vulgar you are!' The noun denotes a person who tells tacky, vulgar jokes.
- 'Check Kostya's browser history on his laptop.' Modern tech terms like 'browser' and 'laptop' decline just like regular Russian masculine nouns.
- 'I already checked his laptop.' In everyday Russian, 'already' flows straight into 'laptop' without extra linking words.
- 'There is nothing bad on there.' Nastya centers this short reply on 'bad', letting a few direct words carry the whole meaning.
- 'That means he simply deleted it.' Notice how Russian puts 'simply, just' and 'to delete, to remove' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'I will stay out in the corridor for a bit.' Adding the prefix 'po-' to 'will be' means 'I will stay for a short while'.
- 'I will wait until the trailer finishes.' In Russian, clauses with 'until' ('poka') include the negative particle 'ne' before the future verb.
- 'What, is everything really that bad?' Three simple words ask whether the movie preview is really that terrible.
- 'A question has come up for me here.' Literally 'a question stood up/arose', this is a standard phrase for 'I have a question'.
- 'A nightmare, this is aimed at fools.' Here Elena anchors the thought on 'nightmare' and finishes on 'fools', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'You are simply out of the loop.' Literally 'in the theme', this slang phrase means to be 'in the know' or 'hip to it'.
- 'It is impossible to watch such a movie.' Elena pairs 'cinema, movies' with 'to look, watch' directly, since spoken Russian skips English filler words like 'the', 'a', and 'is'.
