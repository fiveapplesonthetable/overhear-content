## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Comb the entire restaurant quickly!' Literally 'comb through', this figurative command means to search a building thoroughly.
- 'Did I say something funny?' Indefinite pronouns like 'something' are followed by neuter singular adjectives.
- 'Well yes, very funny.' The henchman chuckles at the literal meaning of the verb.
- 'I pictured a giant hairbrush.' The noun for 'comb/hairbrush' shares the exact same root as the verb 'to comb a building'.
- 'Just try arguing with me!' Notice how Russian puts 'only' and 'to argue' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'Find him for me quickly!' Here Nagiyev anchors the thought on 'quickly' and finishes on 'find', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'Do not be afraid of a sharp knife.' In everyday Russian, 'fear' flows straight into 'knife' without extra linking words.
- 'Instead, be afraid of an ordinary fork!' In Russian kitchen folklore, a fork is joked about as deadlier than a knife.
- 'One strike, four holes.' Notice how Russian puts 'blow, strike' and 'holes' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'There we go, all wrapped up.' After wrapping a henchman in kitchen cling film, Senya quips 'packaged'.
- 'Spring salads from Senya.' Senya puns on the rhyming sound of 'spring' and his nickname 'from Senya'.
- 'You were whipped and defeated.' Short-form past passive participles mimic cheesy translated action-movie one-liners.
