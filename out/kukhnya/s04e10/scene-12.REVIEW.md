## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Barricade the doors, let's stick together!' The reflexive first-person plural verb with 'together' means 'let's stick together'.
- 'Vika, what did you hit me for?' Asking 'for what' with the accusative case asks what offense caused a punishment.
- 'Oh, Max, forgive me.' Vika lowers the frying pan when she hears Max speaking normally.
- 'Vika, I am not a zombie at all!' Max protests his humanity with 'not at all'.
- 'I am simply tired of your lying.' Even in the middle of a zombie apocalypse, Vika brings up Max's habit of lying to her.
- 'Why are you looking at me like that?' When the cooks notice Senya's bitten hand, he backs toward the chopping block.
- 'It is only just a finger.' The two-word limiter means 'merely' or 'only just'.
- 'I will chop it off quickly right now.' The prefix 'ot-' on the verb for 'to chop' means to sever or chop completely off.
- 'Right now I am definitely going to chop.' Senya stalls by switching to the imperfective future while raising the cleaver.
- 'Yes, I am almost chopping it already.' Repeating the present-tense verb shows his hesitation.
- 'Senya, you will not be able to do that.' The perfective future of 'can' means 'will manage to'.
- 'Lower the big knife, Senya.' The singular command tells him to put the cleaver down.
