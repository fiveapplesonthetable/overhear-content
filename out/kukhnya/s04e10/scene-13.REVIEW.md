## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'All right, guys, let's do it differently.' The adverb 'differently/otherwise' proposes a less painful plan.
- 'Just tie me up tightly.' The imperative with prefix 's-' means 'bind/tie together'.
- 'What if I do not turn into one after all?' At the start of a question, the word for 'suddenly' means 'what if?'.
- 'Or scientists will invent a vaccine soon.' The perfective future verb means 'will think up' or 'invent'.
- 'And you will always have time to shoot me.' The verb 'you will be in time' pairs with the infinitive.
- 'Hold him tighter, guys!' The comparative adverb 'tighter/stronger' modifies the plural command 'hold'.
- 'The zombies are already breaking down the doors!' The plural present-tense verb describes the attackers breaking through.
- 'Everyone get into the van quickly!' Motion into a vehicle uses the preposition 'into' plus the accusative case.
- 'I will drive you to the safe zone.' The prefixed transport verb means 'I will drive/deliver you there'.
- 'Does everyone have eight hundred rubles each?' Even during the end of the world, businessman Timur uses the distributive preposition 'po' to charge a fare per person.
- 'Timur, are you bargaining even now?' The reflexive verb means to haggle or bargain over prices.
- 'Gasoline is very expensive right now, brother.' Timur justifies his fare with a simple present-tense statement.
