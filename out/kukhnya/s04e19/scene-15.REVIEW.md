## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello, respected Dmitry Vladimirovich.' When the chairs turn around after the vote, Elena greets the host politely.
- 'Lena, I simply did not know!' Recognizing Viktor Petrovich's talented friend, Nagiyev repeats a negated masculine past verb.
- 'I did not know that this was your dish.' In Russian indirect statements, the subordinate clause keeps the present-tense perspective.
- 'But after all, that is the point of your contest.' Elena points out that blind judging is the whole purpose of the competition.
- 'It is nothing serious, anything happens in life.' She accepts the result calmly using the reassuring idiom meaning no big deal.
- 'I am truly very sorry, Elena Pavlovna.' Nagiyev pairs 'really' with 'it is a pity, too bad' directly, since spoken Russian skips English filler words like 'the', 'a', and 'is'.
- 'You are a wonderful and very talented chef.' Notice how Russian puts 'fine, wonderful' and 'cook' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'Friends, let's support our contestant!' Turning to the studio audience, Nagiyev calls for applause with a plural future verb.
- 'Definitely come back to us next year.' In everyday Russian, 'definitely' flows straight into 'year' without extra linking words.
- 'Thank you very much to you and all the viewers.' Elena uses a polite everyday Russian courtesy expression that sets the tone of the conversation right away.
- 'I wish you great luck in everything!' Notice how Russian puts 'wish' and 'good luck' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'Goodbye, all the very best to you.' Elena leaves the stage with a gracious traditional Russian farewell.
