## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Vitya, hello, how are you doing?' Elena greets Barinov warmly using his affectionate diminutive name.
- 'Lena, hi, what a pleasant surprise!' Notice how Russian puts 'so, like this' and 'surprise' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'Have you been back in Moscow for long?' In spoken Russian, the words stay in their normal statement order and Chef Barinov's rising voice at the end turns the line into a question.
- 'Yes, I have been here for two months already.' In everyday Russian, 'already' flows straight into 'months' without extra linking words.
- 'And what about your expensive restaurant?' Barinov asks about the prestigious Swiss restaurant where she had gone to work.
- 'Nothing came of it, it did not work out for me.' Russians say 'by me' or 'with us' where English says 'I have' or 'we have', without needing a separate word for 'have'.
- 'The owner and I did not get along in temperament.' Here Elena anchors the thought on 'to converge, click' and finishes on 'owner, proprietor, host', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'Now I cannot get hired at a good job.' In everyday Russian, 'now, nowadays' flows straight into 'work' without extra linking words.
- 'That is why I came to this show.' Elena admits that entering the reality contest was her last resort to find work.
- 'You should be working in the best restaurant!' Barinov insists that a chef of her caliber belongs in a top establishment.
- 'All right, I will handle everything myself.' Elena centers this short reply on 'will manage, cope', letting a few direct words carry the whole meaning.
- 'I was very glad to see you.' In everyday Russian, 'very, really' flows straight into 'to see' without extra linking words.
