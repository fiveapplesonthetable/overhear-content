## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Well hello there, my dear friend Dima.' Notice how Russian puts 'expensive' and 'friend' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'First I added ginger to the dish.' Here Chef Barinov anchors the thought on 'at first, first' and finishes on 'dish', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'It will make you forget the other dishes.' The ginger cleanses the palate so Nagiyev forgets everything he tasted earlier.
- 'I drizzled the ginger with oil for shine.' Chef Barinov pairs 'to water' with 'shine' directly, since spoken Russian skips English filler words like 'the', 'a', and 'is'.
- 'After all, you adore bright things so much.' Notice how Russian puts 'so, like this' and 'thing' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'You love seafood and meat equally.' Since Nagiyev can never choose between surf and turf, Barinov combines both on one plate.
- 'Therefore I prepared beef with shrimp.' In everyday Russian, 'therefore' flows straight into 'shrimp, prawn' without extra linking words.
- 'The meat turned out very juicy, without blood.' He cooks the beef medium-well just the way Nagiyev likes it.
- 'On top I covered the meat nicely with vegetables.' Hiding the meat under vegetables lets Nagiyev pretend he is eating a healthy diet meal.
- 'And then I slightly overcooked those vegetables.' He slightly overcooks the garnish on purpose so Nagiyev will not recognize his perfectionism.
- 'Dima, I know your taste all too well.' An adverb of excess modifies the adverb well before a first-person singular verb.
- 'This is simply an incredibly tasty dish!' Taking a single bite with his back turned, Nagiyev is immediately blown away and hits the button.
