## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hi, Vika, do you need help again?' Denis tests quietly if there is more going on behind the scenes.
- 'I need to move the couch in the office.' Bringing up the office to Denis, Katya lands on "kabinete" ("office", built on "kabinet") to keep the statement grounded.
- 'Oh, I dreamed about that dog again!' Touching on the dog with Katya, the "-sya" ending on "prisnilas" ("to dog") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'Listen, maybe you should go see a doctor?' Katya inquires promptly where this conversation is heading next.
- 'No, everything is fine with me.' Mentioning the order to Katya, Denis lands on "poryadke" ("order", built on "poryadok") to keep the statement grounded.
- 'Let me make you some hot tea.' Pointing to the tea on the spot, Katya starts with "davay" ("come on, let's") to invite Denis right into the action.
- 'No, thank you, let's just go back to sleep.' Denis reacts dryly around Katya, without trying to dress up how he really feels.
- 'The ship requests permission to come ashore.' Touching on the permission before Nastya, Denis centers the thought on "razresheniya" ("permission", from "razresheniye") without wasting a word.
- 'Nothing is going to happen between us tonight.' Holding firm directly with Denis, Nastya pairs the negative word with "ne" ("not"), since Russian naturally doubles its negatives for emphasis.
- 'First you will agree to a second child.' Nastya cuts in pointedly facing Denis, keeping the focus on what actually matters here.
- 'Only then will I allow you everything.' Nastya steps in candidly with Denis and centers the thought on "razreshu" ("I will allow", from "razreshit") without wasting a word.
- 'Nastya, come on, why are you starting like that?' Pushing back candidly at once, Denis puts the question word "zachem" right at the start so Nastya hears the focus immediately.
