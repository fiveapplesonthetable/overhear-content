## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hi, Vika, do you need help again?' In Denis's dream, he once again meets Vika inside the chef's office.
- 'I need to move the couch in the office.' Notice how Russian puts 'office' and 'to office' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'Oh, I dreamed about that dog again!' Waking up beside Katya, Denis quickly blames his gasp on the imaginary dog.
- 'Listen, maybe you should go see a doctor?' In spoken Russian, the words stay in their normal statement order and Katya's rising voice at the end turns the line into a question.
- 'No, everything is fine with me.' Denis centers this short reply on 'order', letting a few direct words carry the whole meaning.
- 'Let me make you some hot tea.' The hortative particle with a first-person singular future verb offers to do something for someone.
- 'No, thank you, let's just go back to sleep.' Denis declines the tea and tries to go back to sleep without dreaming of Vika.
- 'The ship requests permission to come ashore.' In the next apartment, Kostya uses a playful naval metaphor to approach Nastya in bed.
- 'Nothing is going to happen between us tonight.' Nastya shuts down his advances using an impersonal negative future construction.
- 'First you will agree to a second child.' Notice how Russian puts 'at first, first' and 'child' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'Only then will I allow you everything.' Here Nastya anchors the thought on 'then, in that case' and finishes on 'to allow, to permit', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'Nastya, come on, why are you starting like that?' Kostya sighs in frustration as Nastya turns off the bedside lamp.
