## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Viktor Petrovich, I will be a little late tomorrow.' Lyova uses a polite first-person singular perfective future reflexive verb meaning will be delayed.
- 'Lyova, just think about this.' Still fuming about Nagiyev, Barinov barely hears Lyova and vents his own frustration.
- 'Nagiyev forbids me to participate in the contest!' A third-person singular present verb of forbidding takes a dative person and an infinitive.
- 'Why would I even go there at all?' A dative pronoun with an infinitive of motion forms a rhetorical question about purpose.
- 'In my opinion, it is a very worthy contest.' Lyova defends the competition using a parenthetical opinion word and a masculine adjective.
- 'Lyova, oh, I beg you, please!' This idiomatic exclamation expresses strong skepticism, similar to 'oh, please!' in English.
- 'Wait, where are you being delayed tomorrow?' Barinov suddenly connects the dots and asks a direct question in the present tense.
- 'Viktor Petrovich, I decided to test myself.' A masculine past verb of deciding governs a perfective infinitive and the reflexive pronoun.
- 'So you want to test yourself out there?' Barinov repeats Lyova's phrase in disbelief using a second-person singular verb of wanting.
- 'You are still very far from being a head chef.' An impersonal dative phrase with an adverb of distance and the genitive preposition means having a long way to go.
- 'Lyova, do not go, do not embarrass me.' Two negated imperfective imperatives forbid Lyova from entering the contest.
- 'I thought that you would support me.' Russian uses the future tense in the subordinate clause after a past verb of thinking for would.
