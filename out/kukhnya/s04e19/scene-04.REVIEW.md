## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Katya, look at my hands.' Startled awake, Denis uses a singular perfective imperative with an accusative plural phrase.
- 'Tell me, is everything all right with them?' An instrumental plural pronoun accompanies the neuter pronoun everything and a predicate adverb.
- 'Good grief, you have two of them.' Sleepy Katya reassures him using a genitive plural pronoun and the feminine numeral two.
- 'Lie back down and go to sleep.' Two singular imperatives urge Denis to lie back down and go back to sleep.
- 'I just had a strange dream.' Russian expresses dreaming impersonally with a dative experiencer and a reflexive past verb.
- 'You will tell me everything in the morning, good night.' An instrumental time word meaning in the morning precedes a perfective future verb.
- 'Kostya, it is six o'clock already, time to get up.' In the other apartment, Nastya wakes Kostya using a predicate noun meaning it is time.
- 'We need to take Styopa to my parents' place.' A perfective infinitive of vehicular transport takes an animate accusative object and dative plural destination.
- 'Nastya, maybe he can get there by himself?' Half-asleep, Kostya jokes that their infant son can take himself to his grandparents' house.
- 'I will explain everything to him in detail.' He continues the joke with a dative pronoun and a first-person singular perfective future verb.
- 'It is not complicated at all.' A negated neuter predicate adverbial means it is not difficult.
- 'All right, do not be angry, I am getting up already.' Seeing Nastya's glare, Kostya quickly uses a first-person singular present verb meaning getting up.
