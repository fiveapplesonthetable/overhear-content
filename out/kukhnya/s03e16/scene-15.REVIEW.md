## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Taste it, well, did it turn out delicious?' Questioning whether taste it well did it turn out delicious is how Katya tests Max's story. In Russian, she does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'Yes, great, the taste remained the exact same!' Around the moment and dish, Max makes his point loud and clear.
- 'Now all that is left is to test it on someone.' Claiming that all that is left is to test it on someone sounds confident, even if Katya has doubts.
- 'Actually that is a jack, not a king, then I have three of a kind!' Once that is a jack not a king then he has three of a kind, Nagiyev makes sure Katya hears every word. In Russian you say "by me" or "with us" rather than "I have", so the situation sits right with the person.
- 'Dima, I will go to the office for now, okay?' Wondering if she will go to the office for now okay shows Katya caught off guard.
- 'I need to sort through some important papers in there.' Noting that she needs to sort through some important papers in there keeps Katya's side of the story simple and clear. Russian packs the whole idea of what is allowed or needed into one impersonal word at the front.
- 'Go ahead, sweetheart, go sort through the papers in there.' Noting that Katya should go ahead sweetheart go sort through the papers in there keeps Nagiyev's side of the story simple and clear. Starting with "davay" is the everyday Russian way to say "come on, let us do this".
- 'Max, let's test the sauce on Nagiyev!' Declaring that they should test the sauce on Nagiyev brings Katya's temper right to the surface.
- 'Katya, have you lost your mind completely?' Here checking whether Katya lost Katya's mind completely shows Max looking for the real truth. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
- 'He has already drunk so much whiskey tonight!' Insisting that he has already drunk so much whiskey tonight shows Katya is not backing down. Russian puts the key word right up front so her emotion lands on the first beat.
- 'He will never in his life suspect your sauce!' Once he will never in his life suspect Max's sauce, Katya makes sure Max hears every word.
- 'Eva, please take this dish over to Dmitry Vladimirovich!' Insisting that Katya should take this dish over to Dmitry Vladimirovich shows Max is not backing down. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
