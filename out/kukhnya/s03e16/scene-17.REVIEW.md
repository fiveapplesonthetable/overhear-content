## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'There is more whiskey in Nagiyev right now than in the bar, and nothing happened!' Touching on the whisky right here, Max links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
- 'Alright, then make four more portions for the guests.' Katya chimes in sharply without any doubt, grounding the moment in plain reality.
- 'So, what are your plans, maybe we should celebrate our sauce?' Turning to the plans beside Max, Katya chooses "plany" ("plans", from the root "plan") so Max hears the exact meaning.
- 'And will Vika not mind that?' Pressing on plainly before Max, Katya keeps normal statement word order here, turning the line into a question for Max purely through rising pitch.
- 'No, she left far away and for a long time, she has a new life.' Max opens up boldly without any doubt, leaving no doubt about where he stands.
- 'In my new life I want to have a drink and cook salmon under Sauce Lavrov.' Mentioning the life around Katya, Max lands on "prigotovit" ("to cook, to prepare") as the key everyday word here.
- 'Great, then maybe we should go to my place?' Pressing on evenly beside Max, Katya leans on "otlichno" ("great, excellent"), which carries the whole predicate in Russian without needing "it is".
- 'Nagiyev has already ordered his seventh glass of whiskey!' Max responds dryly across the room, bringing things back to earth after the buildup.
- 'Do you think it is hard to drink a liter of apple juice?' Confirming how difficult things are at once, Kostya lands on "yablochnogo" ("apple", built on "yablochnyy") to keep the statement grounded.
- 'That is just his clever tactic tonight.' Stressing how clever things are to Max, Kostya uses the little word "prosto" ("simply, just") to make the point sound effortless.
- 'He pretends to be drunk, and at the end takes all the winnings!' Kostya counters firmly in this moment, before Max can change the subject.
- 'It looks like someone at the table just got sick!' Bringing up the table beside Kostya, Max centers the thought on "kazhetsya" ("it seems, I think", from "kazatsya") without wasting a word.
