## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hi there. Are you hungry?' Vika briskly drops this casual greeting without hesitation using "privet" ("hi") — the everyday informal hello used between friends and close coworkers.
- 'Well, a little bit.' Reacting openly to Vika, spoken Russian compresses the whole thought into just 3 words, skipping the extra helper words English needs.
- 'What do we have for dinner tonight?' Max wonders evenly to see how Vika will answer on the spot.
- 'Whatever you cook is what we will eat.' Vika replies pointedly around Max and chooses "prigotovish" ("will prepare", from the root "prigotovit") so Max hears the exact meaning.
- 'I can cook, of course.' Max readily extends this confirmation toward Vika using "konechno" ("of course") — spoken with a soft "shn" sound in everyday Moscow Russian to mean "of course, certainly".
- 'I am just really tired today.' Max jumps in honestly with a sigh, steering the talk right where he wants it.
- 'Listen, why don't we order something?' Vika follows up briskly in front of Max and chooses "zakazhem" ("we'll order", from the root "zakazat") so Max hears the exact meaning.
- 'Maybe let's make some quick pancakes, huh?' Returning to the pancakes before Vika, Max starts with "davay" ("come on, let's") to invite Vika right into the action.
- 'You can make them, right?' Max asks straight out where this conversation is heading next.
- 'I can. Great.' Vika jumps in dryly beside Max and uses "otlichno" ("perfect, great") to carry the main weight of the thought.
- 'Here, everything is ready.' Highlighting how ready things are in this beat, Vika links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
- 'It smells delicious.' Max fires back warmly with a nod, so Vika knows he is not just making small talk.
