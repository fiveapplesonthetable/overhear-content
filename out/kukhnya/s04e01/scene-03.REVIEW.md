## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Let's send him to his grandmother's.' Since they should send him to his grandmother, Kostya does not waste words on excuses. Starting with "davay" is the everyday Russian way to say "come on, let us do this".
- 'For a couple of days.' Over the apartment and the shift, Kostya answers Max without a second thought.
- 'Hi there.' Around the apartment and moment, Max sets a calm, everyday tone with Kostya.
- 'Can you show a little respect for my hard work?' Asking whether Kostya shows a little respect for her hard work cuts straight through the tension between Vika and Kostya. Russian packs the whole idea of what is allowed or needed into one impersonal word at the front.
- 'Vika, come on, don't be angry.' Since Kostya should come on do not be angry, Max does not waste words on excuses.
- 'Come here to me.' Coming here to him is how Max lays out the situation for Kostya.
- 'We need to take a medical check-up in two days.' By needing to take a medical check up in two days, Vika keeps her side of the story grounded. There is no "you" needed in the Russian here: one short word states the rule for everyone in the room.
- 'We have to be patient.' Vika mentions that they have to be patient so Kostya sees what is at stake.
- 'Two whole days?' Over the doctor and the work, Max presses Kostya for a straight answer. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'Do not forget.' Noting that do not forget keeps Vika's side of the story simple and clear.
- 'Set a reminder on your phone.' Here setting a reminder on Kostya's phone sets up the next turn in their exchange.
- 'Something has to be done.' Admitting that something has to be done shows Max being honest for a change. Russian packs the whole idea of what is allowed or needed into one impersonal word at the front.
