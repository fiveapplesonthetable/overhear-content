## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Action, let's begin!' Chef Barinov centers this short reply on 'started', letting a few direct words carry the whole meaning.
- 'My career climb was long and difficult.' Notice how Russian puts 'climb' and 'difficult, hard' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'I cooked for the presidents of Russia and France.' The past-tense verb ending marks the grammatical gender and number of the subject directly.
- 'Vitya, what is going on here, pal?' In everyday Russian, 'is happening' flows straight into 'kid, little one' without extra linking words.
- 'I do not meddle in your kitchen.' Dmitry addresses the listener with the informal singular pronoun, showing a close or familiar relationship.
- 'Listen to me very carefully now.' Notice how Russian puts 'listen' and 'now, nowadays' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'Tomorrow a very important guest is coming to us.' Here Nagiyev anchors the thought on 'tomorrow' and finishes on 'guest', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'Haven't all the important guests visited us already?' Russians say 'by me' or 'with us' where English says 'I have' or 'we have', without needing a separate word for 'have'.
- 'No, not all of them wanted this.' Putting the short word for 'not' right in front of the action word is all Russian needs to make a sentence negative, with no extra helper words like 'do' or 'did'.
- 'He wants to make me a general of the army.' Notice how Russian puts 'wants' and 'army' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'And Emperor of France.' Here Nagiyev anchors the thought on 'france' and finishes on 'France', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'Do you understand?' In spoken Russian, the words stay in their normal statement order and Nagiyev's rising voice at the end turns the line into a question.
