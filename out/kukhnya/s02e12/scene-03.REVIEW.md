## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Viktor Petrovich, you have a visitor.' Adding that Elena has a visitor fills in the missing piece for Elena. Notice how he sticks to the formal "you", keeping a professional line between them.
- 'She says she is an acquaintance of yours, Elena Sokolova.' Vika tells Barinov that she says she is an acquaintance of Barinov's Elena Sokolova without dressing it up.
- 'I do not want to see her, I cannot.' Since she does not want to see her she cannot, Elena does not waste words on excuses.
- 'Tell her I am not here, tell her I died.' Mentioning that Elena should tell her he is not here tell her he died moves the talk in a new direction.
- 'Wait, why.' Between the truth and the visit, Vika gives Barinov a quick, grounded response.
- 'Did you not tell me yourself that it was allowed?' By asking whether Barinov not tell her themselves that it was allowed, Elena presses Barinov for a straight answer. Russian packs the whole idea of what is allowed or needed into one impersonal word at the front.
- 'Well, a little bit is allowed.' Explaining that a little bit is allowed lays the cards on the table for Elena. There is no "you" needed in the Russian here: one short word states the rule for everyone in the room.
- 'I will not hide the truth from you.' Vika mentions that she will not hide the truth from Barinov so Barinov sees what is at stake.
- 'At the very...' Around the truth and work, Elena sets a calm, everyday tone with Barinov.
- 'And so what?' Around the hospital and visit, Barinov wants to know where Elena really stands. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'We sailed without a captain for half a year.' With the plan and visit in play, Vika keeps this short reply brisk and direct.
- 'We simply must bring in a new head chef.' Realizing that they simply must bring in a new head changes how Elena handles the situation. Russian expresses having something as "by me is", keeping the phrase short and grounded.
- 'So allow me to introduce our new head chef to you.' Explaining that Elena should allow him to introduce their new head to Elena lays the cards on the table for Elena. Notice how he sticks to the formal "you", keeping a professional line between them.
