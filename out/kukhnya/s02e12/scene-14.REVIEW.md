## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'We are glad to see you.' Adding that they are glad to see Kostya fills in the missing piece for Kostya. Even in the middle of the drama, she keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
- 'Good evening.' With the room and plan in play, Kostya keeps this short reply brisk and direct.
- 'Right this way. Give it a try.' Around the room and situation, Vika sets a calm, everyday tone with Kostya.
- 'Vika, I need to talk to you.' Realizing that he needs to talk to Vika changes how Kostya handles the situation. There is no "you" needed in the Russian here: one short word states the rule for everyone in the room.
- 'Get out of the dining room.' Explaining that Kostya should get out of the dining room lays the cards on the table for Kostya.
- 'I wanted to warn you.' Because he wanted to warn Vika, Kostya treats the matter as settled.
- 'There is this guy here.' Explaining that there is this guy here lays the cards on the table for Kostya.
- 'He is lying.' Because he is lying, Kostya treats the matter as settled.
- 'Are you talking about yourself?' By checking whether Kostya talking about themselves, Vika presses Kostya for a straight answer.
- 'And what business is it of yours?' Asking whether what business is it puts Vika right on the defensive. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
- 'Well, I am worried about you.' Saying that she is worried about Kostya is how Vika sets the tone with Kostya.
- 'And who are you to me?' Here wondering who Vika to him is shows Kostya looking for the real truth.
- 'None of your business.' Around the shift and plan, Vika sets a calm, everyday tone with Kostya.
