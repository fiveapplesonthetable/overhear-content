## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Our dinner service is starting.' Russians say 'by me' or 'with us' where English says 'I have' or 'we have', without needing a separate word for 'have'.
- 'Quick, head to the storage room.' Timur brings out the contrast between 'quickly' and 'on' in a few short words.
- 'May I step out to the restroom?' Spoken Russian keeps this brisk, leaning on 'it is possible' and finishing on 'will step'. Timur answers right away.
- 'I nearly sliced my fingers off.' Notice how Russian expresses 'I' and 'cut off' with crisp inflection rather than helper words.
- 'Where is the first-aid kit?' Lyova anchors the line on 'where', while 'first-aid kit' completes the thought.
- 'Do you need any help?' Timur raises the pitch on 'is needed' to turn the phrase into a direct spoken question. Lyova answers right away.
- 'All right, I will take the dish out and do it.' Here the Russian phrasing highlights 'like this' before landing on 'will make'.
- 'At table seven.' The grammar packs 'behind' and 'table' into a tight, idiomatic kitchen exchange.
- 'Hello, Lyova, Timur is calling for you urgently.' Notice how Russian expresses 'hello' and 'calls' with crisp inflection rather than helper words. Timur answers right away.
- 'Table eight is ready to go.' Timur brings out the contrast between 'eighth' and 'on' in a few short words.
- 'I have a banquet on my hands.' Russians say 'by me' or 'with us' where English says 'I have' or 'we have', without needing a separate word for 'have'.
- 'And they are in no hurry to speed up.' Putting the short word for 'not' right in front of the action word is all Russian needs to make a sentence negative, with no extra helper words like 'do' or 'did'.
- 'Where is everybody?' The meaning hinges on 'and' working together with 'all' in spoken Russian.
