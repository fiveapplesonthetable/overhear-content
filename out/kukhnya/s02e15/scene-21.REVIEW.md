- 'I never learn anything in life, huh?' Sasha brings the reply at once: Russian happily doubles the negative here — pairing "never" or "nothing" with "ne" ("not") — which is the only natural way to say it.
- 'What else could I have told you?' Max quizzes wryly if there is more going on behind the scenes.
- 'You could have told me the exact truth.' Nastya underscores one practical expression for Max: "skazat" means "tell", and it carries the main weight of what Nastya is saying.
- 'Because I was afraid that you would not like me.' Sasha shapes this statement in earnest: the "-sya" ending on "ponravitsya" ("to be liked") turns the action inward or makes it happen between people naturally.
- 'And I really want you to like me.' Max jumps in pointedly hearing Nastya, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'I am on my way to the airport.' Nastya leans on one memorable expression on cue: "aeroport" means "the airport", and it carries the main weight of what Nastya is saying.

- 'And it is wonderful when you do not want to go back.' Nastya underscores one familiar detail with Max: "khochetsya" means "want, feel like" (from "khotetsya"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'When you actually like the changes.' Sasha lays out that point briskly now: spoken Russian trims this down to just 4 words, dropping the extra helper words that English has to spell out.
