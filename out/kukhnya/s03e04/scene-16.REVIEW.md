## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Excuse me, Rodion Sergeyevich, I didn't mean to do that.' Mentioning that he did not mean to do moves the talk in a new direction.
- 'It's nothing. Even my horoscope says that today is an unlucky day.' Saying that it is nothing is how Rodion sets the tone with Barinov. Russian expresses having something as "by me is", keeping the phrase short and grounded.
- 'Do you actually believe in that nonsense?' By asking whether Barinov actually believe in that nonsense, Max presses Barinov for a straight answer. Even in the middle of the drama, he keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
- 'Now I always have a lucky day!' Rodion insists that he always has a lucky day, leaving Barinov no room to argue.
- 'Chef, we turned everything upside down — there is no elephant.' Max mentions that they turned everything upside down there is no elephant so Barinov sees what is at stake. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'Search, Senya, search!' Here telling Max to search Senya search lets Barinov's real feelings break through. Russian puts the key word right up front so his emotion lands on the first beat.
- 'Find me the elephant!' Here finding him the elephant lets Barinov's real feelings break through.
- 'Max, you and I still haven't talked.' Barinov explains that Max and he still has not talked, keeping his voice calm and direct. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
- 'Yes, Vika, forgive me, I got swamped.' Realizing that he got swamped changes how Max handles the situation.
- 'Vika, let's do it later, okay? Otherwise Nagiyev gets impatient.' Asking whether they should do it later okay puts Barinov right on the defensive.
- 'It looks cool.' Even though it looks cool, Max keeps a steady face in front of Barinov.
- 'And it tastes even better!' Saying that it tastes even better is how Max takes charge of the moment. Russian puts the key word right up front so his emotion lands on the first beat.
