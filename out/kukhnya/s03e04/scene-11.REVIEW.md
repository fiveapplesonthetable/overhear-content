## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Calm down! Get out of here.' Here getting out of here lets Max's real feelings break through. Russian puts the key word right up front so his emotion lands on the first beat.
- 'The examination results showed that the level is also low.' Fedya reminds Max that the examination results showed that the level is also low before the moment passes.
- 'So you mean to say...' Because Fedya means to say, Vika treats the matter as settled.
- 'You need to have a baby in the next two years, otherwise it will be difficult later.' Explaining that Max needs to have a baby in the next two years otherwise it will be difficult later lays the cards on the table for Max. Russian packs the whole idea of what is allowed or needed into one impersonal word at the front.
- 'Guys, Dmitry Vladimirovich asked for aspic out there.' Max mentions that Dmitry Vladimirovich asked for aspic out there so Fedya sees what is at stake.
- 'Who will prepare it?' By wondering who prepare it will, Max presses Fedya for a straight answer.
- 'How? We are clumsy, after all! We don't know how to cook.' When Fedya asks if they are clumsy after all, Max has to answer fast. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'Especially since it is not from the menu.' Fedya explains that especially since it is not from the menu, keeping his voice calm and direct.
- 'Guys, come on, forgive me, I was wrong.' Coming on forgive him he was wrong is how Max lays out the situation for Fedya.
- 'All right, we will help.' Noting that all right they will help keeps Fedya's side of the story simple and clear.
- 'Give me the receipt. What, no receipt?' Over the work and the dish, Fedya presses Max for a straight answer. Starting with "davay" is the everyday Russian way to say "come on, let us do this".
- 'Well sorry, no receipt, no dish.' With the doctor and shift in play, Fedya keeps this short reply brisk and direct.
