## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I found your little elephant here.' Mentioning that she found Barinov's little elephant here moves the talk in a new direction.
- 'Where? It was in the restaurant, right? I knew it.' Wondering if it was in the restaurant right shows Barinov caught off guard. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'Thank you so much. You can't imagine how dear this thing is to me.' Because Katya cannot imagine how dear this thing is to him, Barinov treats the matter as settled.
- 'Dad, try not to lose what is dear to you anymore.' Explaining that dad try not to lose what is dear to Barinov anymore lays the cards on the table for Barinov. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
- 'Sometimes we try long and hard to find the key to unlock a door.' With the moment and key in play, Max keeps this short reply brisk and direct.
- 'But in the end we realize that the right key was never on the keyring.' Claiming that in the end they realize that the right key was never on the keyring sounds confident, even if Barinov has doubts.
- 'Something that we will never know about.' Pointing out that something that they will never know gives Barinov a sobering reality check. Russian stacks "never" or "nothing" together with "not", doubling the refusal for extra weight.
- 'Although we understand that it is the door to the future.' Max explains that although they understand that it is the door to the future, keeping his voice calm and direct. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'To the future in which we would like to find ourselves.' Realizing that to the future in which they would like to find themselves changes how Max handles the situation.
- 'If you open the wrong door, someone else will open yours.' Noting that if Barinov opens the wrong door someone else will open keeps Max's side of the story simple and clear. Notice how he sticks to the formal "you", keeping a professional line between them.
- 'Viktoria, it seems to me...' Even though it seems to him, Barinov keeps a steady face in front of Katya.
- 'It seems that I love you.' Admitting that it seems that he loves Katya shows Barinov being honest for a change.
