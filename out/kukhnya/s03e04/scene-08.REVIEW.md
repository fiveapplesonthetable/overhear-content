## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Vika, I wanted to talk with you.' Max pushes back pointedly in the exchange and brings in "pogovorit" ("to talk") to make the meaning unmistakable.
- 'I have no time, I need to leave urgently.' Vika chimes in frankly in this moment, leaving no doubt about where she stands.
- 'I just wanted to say that yesterday it seemed to me...' Max jumps in quietly around Vika and lands on "pokazalos" ("seemed", built on "pokazatsya") to keep the statement grounded.
- 'That things between you and me are good again, or did it just seem that way to me?' Looking over smoothly around Vika, Max reaches for "u nas" ("by us, at our place") to talk about what they share.
- 'I don't know.' Vika speaks boldly without holding back, refusing to let the moment slip past.
- 'And what will happen next?' Max cuts in decisively to Vika and lands on "dalshe" ("next, go on") as the key everyday word here.
- 'I need to think.' Jumping in smoothly before Max, Vika leans on "nado" ("it is necessary, one needs to"), which carries the whole predicate in Russian without needing "it is".
- 'Let me come over, we will talk about everything.' Max opens up honestly during the talk, without trying to dress up how he really feels.
- 'Where are you heading in the middle of the workday?' Showing how work things are beside Vika, Max leans on "rabochego" ("work", a form of "rabochiy") to give the remark its edge.
- 'None of your business. Curious? To my lover.' Turning frankly before Max, Vika keeps normal statement word order here, turning the line into a question for Max purely through rising pitch.
- 'Ah, got it all, no question.' Max pushes back at once with a smile, before Vika can change the subject.
- 'See how the guy sang here? Everything from scratch.' Touching on the guy at once, Max puts the emphasis on "patsan" ("guy, lad") in the middle of the talk.
