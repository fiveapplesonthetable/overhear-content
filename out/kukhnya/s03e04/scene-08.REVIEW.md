## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Vika, I wanted to talk with you.' Max speaks up dryly with a nod, testing Vika's reaction in the middle of the exchange.
- 'I have no time, I need to leave urgently.' Vika follows up frankly watching Max, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'I just wanted to say that yesterday it seemed to me...' Max features one direct term for Vika: "pokazalos" means "seemed" (from "pokazatsya"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'That things between you and me are good again, or did it just seem that way to me?' Max offers this line right away: the phrase "u nas" literally means "by us" or "at our place", the standard Russian way to talk about what a group or couple shares.
- 'I don't know.' Vika jumps in boldly toward Max, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'And what will happen next?' Max emphasizes one telling word in earnest: "dalshe" means "next, go on", and it carries the main weight of what Max is saying.
- 'I need to think.' Vika lays out that reply without pause: the single Russian word "nado" ("it is necessary, one needs to") sets up the whole statement without needing a separate word for "it is".
- 'Let me come over, we will talk about everything.' Max chimes in honestly with a smile, grounding the moment in plain reality.
- 'Where are you heading in the middle of the workday?' Max grounds this telling word with Vika: "rabochego" means "work" (from "rabochiy"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'None of your business. Curious? To my lover.' Vika phrases this statement plainly here: Russian keeps the exact word order of a normal statement here, turning it into a question for Max purely through rising pitch at the end.
- 'Ah, got it all, no question.' Max answers at once to be clear, shifting the whole mood of the room in a single beat.
- 'See how the guy sang here? Everything from scratch.' Max highlights this clear expression on cue: "patsan" means "guy, lad", and it carries the main weight of what Max is saying.
