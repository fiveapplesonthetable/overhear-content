- 'Do you want war? You will get a torpedo to the hull.' Fedya prompts straight out if there is more going on behind the scenes.
- 'Senya, come on, don't.' Barinov fires back sharply right here and uses "nado" ("it is necessary") to carry the main weight of the thought.
- 'Hello, how are things?' Leaning in promptly during the talk, Barinov links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
- 'Nothing special.' Barinov fires back wryly with a smile, steering the talk right where he wants it.
- 'When do you fly in? Tomorrow?' Barinov pushes back quietly during the talk and brings in "priletayesh" ("arrive by plane", from "priletat") as the key Russian word here.
- 'Nagiyev gave me a surprise here.' Mentioning a surprise in this beat, Barinov reaches for the Russian word "syurpriz" ("surprise") right at the heart of the line.
- 'Well, when you arrive, I will tell you.' Barinov speaks calmly in their talk, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'Lena, I will call you back.' Barinov weighs in calmly around Fedya and uses "perezvonyu" ("will call back", from "perezvonit") to make the point stick.
- 'Knock it off right now!' Pressing on plainly in this beat, spoken Russian compresses the whole thought into just 2 words, skipping the extra helper words English needs.
- 'Everyone get to work!' Barinov cuts in candidly toward Fedya, before Fedya can change the subject.
