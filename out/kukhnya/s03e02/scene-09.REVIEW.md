## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'The taste is good, really natural somehow!' Nagiyev replies pointedly facing Barinov, refusing to let the moment slip past.
- 'Well done! A creative person, guys.' Nagiyev picks one concrete expression for Senya: "kreativnyy" means "creative", and it carries the main weight of what Nagiyev is saying.
- 'Clever girl! Let's go talk.' Nagiyev puts this update beside Barinov: spoken Russian trims this down to just 3 words, dropping the extra helper words that English has to spell out.
- 'These rich people are so strange, huh? He eats it and rejoices.' Senya inquires deadpan before Nagiyev can change the subject.
- 'Have you completely lost all fear?' Barinov points to this key choice with Nagiyev: "poteryali" means "lost" (from "poteryat"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'And Katya cooked it. They started it first.' Senya chimes in firmly without holding back, watching Nagiyev's face to see how the news lands.
- 'And I will talk with you later.' Barinov continues quietly with a shrug, saying out loud what both of them already know.
- 'What will happen when Dima finds out what he ate?' Barinov grounds one key term in earnest: "uznayet" means "will find out" (from "uznat"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'God forbid any of you lets it slip!' Barinov makes that answer briskly now: the "-sya" ending on "progovoritsya" ("lets it slip") turns the action inward or makes it happen between people naturally.
- 'Go work! I said, go work.' Barinov stands firm promptly with a smile, bringing things back to earth after the buildup.
- 'I am going, I am going. Why are you laughing? Nothing.' Senya focuses on one vivid piece in reply: "rzhyosh" means "are laughing" (from "rzhat"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'That is a head start for you while I am resting.' Senya colors this direct choice right here: "otdykhayu" means "am resting" (from "otdykhat"), giving the line its exact edge.
