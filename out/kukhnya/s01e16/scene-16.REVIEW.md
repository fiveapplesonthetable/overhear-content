## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Chef, here's another one.' 'Eshchyo odin' means 'one more', the usual Russian 'another'. 'Tut', 'here', is the casual partner of 'zdes'.
- 'Good Lord, where am I supposed to put you all?' 'Devat' means 'to put away' or 'to get rid of', and 'kuda vas devat' is a set groan when there are too many of something.
- 'Let him sit in the bathroom for now.' 'Posidit' is 'sidit', 'to sit', with 'po-', which makes it 'sit for a while'. 'Poka' here means 'for now', not 'bye'.
- 'Then he can go out through the main entrance.' 'Vykhod', 'exit', and 'vkhod', 'entrance', are built on the same root: 'vy-' is 'out', 'v-' is 'in'. 'Vyydet', 'he'll go out', comes from 'vyyti'.
- 'Think you are so clever? They are guarding the entrance.' Russian says 'na vkhode', literally 'on the entrance', where English says 'at'. 'Samy umny', 'the cleverest', is a sarcastic way to say 'smart guy'.
- 'Let him sit in the dining room, like Ainura.' 'Pust syadet' means 'let him sit down', the moment of sitting, and 'pust posidit' means 'let him sit a while'. 'Kak', 'how', also means 'like'.
- 'Go over to Kostya at the bar and get him some clothes.' 'Skhodi' means 'go and come back', a quick errand. 'Odezhda', 'clothes', is singular in Russian, so it is literally 'take clothing'.
- 'Viktor Petrovich, congratulations.' 'Pozdravlyayu vas' is literally 'I congratulate you', and the '-yu' ending says 'I'. It's also how you say 'happy birthday': 'pozdravlyayu!'.
- 'You're in the clear.' 'U vas vsyo chisto' is literally 'at you everything clean'. 'Chisto', 'clean', ends in '-o' to match 'vsyo', 'everything'.
- 'Well, I told you that right away.' 'Zhe' adds 'didn't I?' to 'ya vam skazal', 'I told you'. 'Vam', 'to you', is the polite 'vy' in its 'to' form.
- 'Forgive me, but I can't just take your word for it.' 'Ne imeyu prava' means 'I have no right', a formal phrase from officials. 'Verit na slovo', literally 'to believe on the word', is 'to take someone's word'.
- 'You have so few employees.' After 'malo', 'few', the word changes ending: 'sotrudniki' becomes 'sotrudnikov'. 'Ved' means 'after all', and it makes the remark sound like a little trap.
- 'We're professionals.' Russian needs no 'are': 'my professionaly', 'we professionals'. The word is borrowed, so it sounds almost like the English.
