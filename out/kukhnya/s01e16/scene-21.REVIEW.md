## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Chef, can I help?' In speech Russians shorten it to 'Chef, maybe help?', leaving out the 'I'. 'Maybe' and 'can' are the same word, which is why it can start a sentence like this.
- 'I don't know.' It's two words with no 'I' at all, since the ending on 'know' already says it. A flat, tired answer.
- 'He fined me.' The Russian is 'me he fined', with the object first, to put the weight on Barinov himself. 'Fine' is built from the same root as the noun for a fine, with an 'o' on the front.
- 'He wants to deport Louis.' The ending 'irovat' turns many foreign words into Russian action words, which is how 'deport' arrives. The name stays as it is.
- 'What should we do?' Literally 'what to do?', and it's one of the best-known questions in Russian, since a famous nineteenth-century Russian novel is named after it. The little 'zhe' adds a note of desperation.
- 'I don't think so.' Russian says 'I think, no', where English says 'I think not'. It's a handy way to answer a question you don't like.
- 'Maybe with another...' 'With' makes the next word take an 'im' ending. He trails off, which is also what the dots in the subtitle suggest.
- 'We could negotiate.' The second word means 'it will work out', and it has no subject, no 'we' and no 'I'. Together they say 'to come to terms, it will succeed'.
- 'There.' This little pointing word, 'vot', can mean 'here you are' or 'that's it', and here Barinov seems to have thought of something.
- 'Though...' It's a hinge word, the kind you say when you are turning an idea around. Russians often let it hang.
- 'If you save his life...' Barinov uses the casual 'you' with his staff. 'Will save' is an action seen as complete, so the future is built into the ending.
- 'Maybe he will forgive us.' 'Maybe' is the same word as at the start of the scene. The little 'i' in the middle can mean 'even', so it's 'he might even forgive us'.
- 'All right, Chef. I will try.' 'I will try' treats the action as done, meaning 'I'll give it a go'. Ainura calls her boss by his job title, as everyone in the kitchen does.
