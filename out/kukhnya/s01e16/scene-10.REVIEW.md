## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Half go through the back door.' The back door is 'chyorny khod', literally 'the black way', the staff and delivery entrance. 'Polovina', 'half', takes a singular verb, 'idyot', 'goes'.
- 'Half go through the dining room.' 'Zal' means 'hall', and in a restaurant it is the dining room, where the guests sit. 'Cherez', 'through', is followed by the place unchanged.
- 'Chef, the back door is blocked!' Russian needs no 'is' here: 'khod zakryt' is literally 'door closed'. 'Zakryt' is the word on a shop sign that says 'closed'.
- 'What kind of idiots are you?' 'Chto za' means 'what kind of', and here the 'vy' is plural, for the whole group. The original line uses 'invalidy', 'cripples', an insult Barinov uses a lot.
- 'You can't even run away!' 'Dazhe', 'even', goes right in front of what fails, here 'sbezhat', 'to run away'. 'Mozhete', 'you can', is the plural 'vy' form.
- 'Quickly, to my office.' Russian leaves out 'go' and gives only the direction. 'Ko mne' means 'to me', and 'k' becomes 'ko' before 'mne' so it is easier to say.
- 'Good afternoon, Viktor Petrovich.' In Russian, the respectful way to address someone is first name plus father's name, and this one is made from 'Pyotr', so it means 'son of Pyotr'.
- 'I am a major of the migration service.' In Russian, the 'of' shows in the endings: 'migratsionnaya sluzhba' becomes 'migratsionnoy sluzhby'.
- 'Can we go to your office?' 'Proyti' means 'to go through' or 'to step into', and it's the polite word for moving to another room: 'proydite, pozhaluysta', 'come in, please'.
- 'Unfortunately, I cannot.' A polite no in two parts: 'k sozhaleniyu', unfortunately, then 'ya ne mogu'. Word for word the first part is 'to regret', and it softens almost any refusal.
- 'I need to cook for the guests.' Russian says 'mne nuzhno', literally 'to me it is needed'. 'Dlya', 'for', changes 'gosti', 'guests', to 'gostey'.
- 'Alright, I will wait for you here.' 'Podozhdu' means 'I'll wait', from 'zhdat', 'to wait'. The 'po-' makes it a wait for a while, and the major is in no hurry.
