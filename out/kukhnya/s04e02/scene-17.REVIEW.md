## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Fine, since you and I are no longer friends.' Notice how Russian puts 'time, occasion' and 'friend' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'I am not going down to the bottom alone.' Putting the short word for 'not' right in front of the action word is all Russian needs to make a sentence negative, with no extra helper words like 'do' or 'did'.
- 'I have a copy of it.' Russians say 'by me' or 'with us' where English says 'I have' or 'we have', without needing a separate word for 'have'.
- 'You are way too lazy to have a copy.' Russians say 'by me' or 'with us' where English says 'I have' or 'we have', without needing a separate word for 'have'.
- 'Go on, beat it from here, you grand schemer.' Starting with the everyday word for 'let us' is how Russian speakers warmly invite someone to do something together.
- 'Wait, what is burning?' Lyova centers this short reply on 'burns', letting a few direct words carry the whole meaning.
- 'Go get a cloth.' In everyday Russian, 'go, walk' flows straight into 'rag, cloth; pushover, wimp' without extra linking words.
- 'And here I was afraid that chaos would start without me.' Chef Barinov pairs 'was afraid' with 'to begin, to start, to break out' directly, since spoken Russian skips English filler words like 'the', 'a', and 'is'.
- 'Well, now my mind is at ease.' Notice how Russian puts 'now, nowadays' and 'calm, quiet' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'I will pack my things and get out of here.' Here Chef Barinov anchors the thought on 'to collect, to gather' and finishes on 'from here', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'Sorry, I will pay for everything out of my first paycheck.' Lyova uses a polite everyday Russian courtesy expression that sets the tone of the conversation right away.
- 'What first paycheck? You are fired.' Lyova addresses the listener with the informal singular pronoun, showing a close or familiar relationship.
