## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Forgive me for what happened yesterday.' Barinov openly sends this personal apology at the start of the beat using "prosti" ("forgive me") — the close, informal way to say "forgive me" or "I'm sorry" to someone dear.
- 'And forgive me for everything else too.' Barinov steps in sharply in this beat and centers the thought on "ostalnoye" ("remaining, other", from "ostalnoy") without wasting a word.
- 'Oh my goodness, what a speech. Do you like that speech, huh?' Nagiyev tests wryly how Lyova expects that to work in practice.
- 'He looks kind of strange today. Sober.' Lyova speaks up plainly at once and chooses "trezvyy" ("sober") so Nagiyev gets the point right away.
- 'I am serious. Take me back, Dima.' Opening up dryly to Lyova, Barinov leans on "seryozno" ("seriously"), which carries the whole predicate in Russian without needing "it is".
- 'That is the problem. For five years I have been Dima to you.' Lyova speaks evenly hearing Barinov, making his position completely clear.
- 'When in fact I am Dmitry Vladimirovich.' Holding firm openly beside Lyova, Nagiyev links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
- 'After all, I was your boss.' Nagiyev fires back decisively beside Lyova, watching Lyova's face to see how the news lands.
- 'And what did you think, pal?' Nagiyev inquires straight out how Lyova expects that to work in practice.
- 'That I am your buddy, right? Your pal.' Bringing up the pal in front of Lyova, Nagiyev chooses "druzhban" ("pal") so Lyova gets the point right away.
- 'That is why you took advantage of me.' Nagiyev opens up lightly without a pause, grounding the moment in plain reality.
- 'Go on, march home.' Nagiyev opens up warmly facing Lyova, keeping the focus on what actually matters here.
