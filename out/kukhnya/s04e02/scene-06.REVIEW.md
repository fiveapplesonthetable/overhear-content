## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I will find work for all the jokers here.' Lyova steps in quietly hearing Barinov, to clear the air between them once and for all.
- 'Lavrov, get on vegetable prep.' Addressing the preparation before Barinov, Lyova reaches for the Russian word "zagotovki" ("preparation", built from "zagotovka") right at the center of the line.
- 'That is an order.' Turning to an order before Barinov, Lyova links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
- 'Good morning!' Barinov plainly gives this morning greeting without hesitation using "dobroye utro" ("good morning") — literally "kind morning", with the neuter ending matching "utro" ("morning").
- 'Why are you frozen there, seals?' Talking about the frozen on the spot, Barinov lands on "zastyli" ("frozen") as the key everyday word here.
- 'Chef, what about yesterday though?' Lyova asks evenly if there is more going on behind the scenes.
- 'And you believed it. Well, I acted foolishly.' Barinov chimes in at once with Lyova, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'Dima lost his temper. It happens to everyone.' Barinov speaks up vividly in front of Lyova and brings in "pogoryachilsya" ("to lost", from "pogoryachitsya") as the key Russian word here.
- 'He is not foolish enough to fire me.' Mentioning the fool in front of Nagiyev, Barinov uses "durak" ("fool, idiot") to carry the main weight of the thought.
- 'Quite a fool indeed.' Nagiyev proceeds quietly without a pause, without trying to dress up how he really feels.
- 'Why are we standing around? Get to work.' Nagiyev proceeds evenly to Barinov and chooses "rabotayem" ("we work, let's work", from the root "rabotat") so Barinov hears the exact meaning.
- 'In a few days there will be a new chef. And you leave.' Nagiyev reacts earnestly in this moment, grounding the moment in plain reality.
