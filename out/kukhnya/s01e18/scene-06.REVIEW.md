## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Here, look, I'll show you a clever way.' "Vot" points at something: here. "Smotri", look, is how you tell one friend to watch.
- 'What other trick do you want to show me?' "Khochesh", you want, comes from "khotet": "khochu" for I, "khochesh" for you.
- 'Guests often know nothing about alcohol.' "Razbiratsya v" means to know your way around a subject, literally to sort things out in it. "Alkogol" picks up an 'e' after "v".
- 'So what have you come up with now?' "Uzhe" means already. "Pridumal" is "dumat", to think, with "pri-" added: to think something up.
- 'We sold cheap cognac for a thousand roubles!' A price goes after "za", and thousand turns into "tysyachu". Roubles become "rubley" after it.
- 'Are you out of your mind? That's a real scam!' "S uma soshyol", literally 'went off the mind'. "Obman" is a cheat, a con.
- 'But at least we made easy money fast.' "Lyogkie dengi": "lyogkiy" means both light and easy. "Zarabotali", we earned, from "rabota", work.
- 'If they catch us, they'll put us away straight off!' "Posadyat" is literally 'they'll seat', slang for sending to prison. There's no word for 'they': "poymayut" alone means 'they catch'.
- 'Don't be scared, nobody will ever find out the truth.' "Ne boysya", don't be scared, is said to a friend; to a stranger it's "ne boytes". "Uznaet", will find out, is built on "znat", to know.
- 'That's illegal, Max!' "Nezakonno" is "ne" plus "zakonno", lawful, from "zakon", law. "Zhe" adds 'come on, surely you see that'.
- 'You're a smart guy, you'll protect us.' Max is buttering him up: "umnyy paren", smart guy. "Zashchitish" is a promise about the future, you'll protect.
- 'I'm not going to defend you!' "Ne budu" plus a plain verb means 'I won't'. "Tebya", you, is the person being defended.
- 'Calm down, let's just hide the money!' "Uspokoysya" ends in "-sya": calm yourself. "Davay spryachem", let's hide, is "davay" plus the 'we' form.
