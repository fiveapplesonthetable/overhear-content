## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'What are these small numbers in the report?' "Chto eto za" asks 'what sort of thing is this'. "V otchyote", in the report: "otchyot" picks up an 'e' after "v".
- 'Why do we have so little money?' After "malo", little, money turns into "deneg". "Dengi" to "deneg" is irregular, so learn the pair together.
- 'Dmitry Vladimirovich, we have few guests right now.' First name plus patronymic is how Vika addresses the boss. "Malo gostey": after "malo", guest, "gost", becomes "gostey".
- 'A new Italian restaurant opened nearby.' "Otkrylsya" ends in "-sya", so nobody opens it; the restaurant simply opened. "Ryadom" means right next door, nearby.
- 'And what am I supposed to do now?' Literally 'and to me what now to do'. "Mne" plus a bare verb, "delat", is the Russian way to ask what you should do.
- 'I need to feed my big family!' "Mne nuzhno", I need, then the verb "kormit", to feed. "Semya", family, is feminine, so as the thing being fed it becomes "svoyu bolshuyu semyu".
- 'Dima, there just aren't any people right now.' Barinov, an old friend, calls him by the short form of Dmitry. "Net lyudey": after "net", people, "lyudi", becomes "lyudey".
- 'We can't drag them in here by force.' "Sila" is force; "siloy" means 'by force', the "-oy" ending saying how it's done. "Tashchit" is to drag, a heavy, physical verb.
- 'You always have the exact same story!' "Odna i ta zhe", literally 'one and that same'. It's feminine to match "istoriya", story.
- 'We will definitely come up with a good solution.' "Obyazatelno" promises 'without fail'. "Pridumaem" is future: we'll think something up. "Reshenie", a solution, comes from "reshit", to solve.
- 'I'm not going anywhere today.' Russian doubles the negative: "nikuda ne poedu", literally 'nowhere not I'll go'. "Poedu" means going by car, the drive home he's skipping.
- 'I'm staying at the restaurant with my colleagues!' "Ostayus", I'm staying. "S", with, becomes "so" before "svoimi", and "kollegami" carries the plural "-ami" ending that goes with "s".
