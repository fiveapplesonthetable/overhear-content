## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Senya, are you all right?' "U tebya vsyo khorosho?", literally 'at you all good?', is the everyday way to ask if someone is okay.
- 'No, I feel really bad.' How you feel goes to 'me': "mne ochen plokho", 'to me very bad'.
- 'I told you not to eat that fish.' "Zhe" adds 'didn't I'. In "ne yesh etu rybu", don't eat that fish, "ryba" becomes "rybu" as the thing eaten.
- 'Did you take activated charcoal?' Russians drink their medicine: "pit", to drink, covers tablets too. "Ugol" is coal, and charcoal.
- 'Yes, I've already taken two packs.' "Vypil" means drank down, all of it. It's "dve", not "dva", because "pachka", pack, is feminine.
- 'But it didn't help me at all.' Help goes 'to me': "mne ne pomoglo". "Sovsem ne" is not at all.
- 'When I served in the navy, I accidentally ate a jellyfish.' You serve 'on' the fleet: "na flote". "Nechayanno" means by accident.
- 'Then the boatswain poured me something strong.' "Botsman", boatswain, is an old sailors' borrowing. "Nalil mne krepkogo" leaves out the drink itself: 'poured me some strong'.
- 'Do you hear what I'm saying?' "Slyshish", you hear, is about catching sounds. On its own, "slyshish" is also a rough 'hey, listen'.
- 'You need to drink something strong.' "Tebe nado", 'to you it's needed'. Tagging "-nibud" onto "chego" makes 'anything at all'.
- 'All right, I'll go and look for some at the bar.' "Poydu poishchu", 'I'll go, I'll look', with no 'and': very spoken Russian. "V bare", at the bar.
- 'If I survive, I'll stop stealing food!' "Vorovat", to steal, comes from "vor", thief. "Produkty" means groceries, not products.
