## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Are you all right?' Russian forms this yes-or-no question without a question word, relying on rising pitch on 'good'.
- 'No, I am very bad.' Russian omits the present-tense verb 'to be' here, linking 'very much' directly with 'badly', combining 'very much' with 'badly'.
- 'I told you.' The verb for 'said' is in the masculine past tense, marked by the '-l' ending.
- 'Do not eat that fish.' The negative particle sits directly before the verb for 'eat' to negate the action.
- 'I thought she was normal.' Senya chooses the imperfective verb meaning 'thought' for the ongoing action, followed by 'normal'.
- 'Did you drink charcoal?' There is no question word here — in Russian, rising intonation on 'was drinking' is enough to make it a question.
- 'Yeah, I ate two packs.' The phrasing opens with 'yeah' before bringing in 'two' and 'pack' to make the point clear.
- 'No shit.' Fedya delivers a punchy one-word reaction built around the Russian word meaning 'horseradish'.
- 'My stomach hurts.' Senya uses the classic Russian possession phrasing — literally 'at me' rather than a verb meaning 'to have', combining 'hurts' with 'belly'.
- 'You need to go home.' Russian builds this impersonally with the dative ('to me' or 'to you') and 'home'.
- 'I cannot work today.' The negative particle sits directly before the verb for 'can' to negate the action. Then Fedya responds.
- 'Go home and rest.' Fedya leads with the direct command form meaning 'go', pairing it with 'home' and 'rest'.
