## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Well, have we arrived?' "Priekhali" is arriving by car or train; on foot it would be "prishli".
- 'Let's go and see how things are.' "Poshli", let's go, looks like a past form but works as an invitation. "Kak u nas dela", 'how at us the affairs'.
- 'Guys, these past few days I went too far.' "Peregibat palku" is 'to bend the stick too far': to overdo it.
- 'Dima, we understand.' The short name, rather than "Dmitry", shows old friends talking. "Vsyo ponimaem", 'all we understand'.
- 'It's your restaurant, it hurts.' Russians say the soul aches: "dusha bolit". "Tvoy", your, is the friendly form.
- 'At home I sat and thought it over.' "Posidel", sat a while, and "podumal", had a think: "po-" makes each one a short spell.
- 'Kristina and I talked it over.' Russian says "my s Kristinoy", 'we with Kristina'. "Posovetovalis" comes from "sovet", advice.
- 'The restaurant should be run by someone who knows.' "Upravlyat", to manage, needs the 'by' form: "restoranom". "Znayushchiy", knowing, is built from "znat".
- 'Yes, quite right.' "Nu da" is a grudging yes. "Sovershenno" here means completely, not perfectly.
- 'That person is right here among us.' "Sredi nas", among us. "Etot chelovek", this person.
- 'Dima, I'm always ready to help.' "Gotov", ready, is a short form; a woman would say "gotova". Barinov clearly thinks the job is his.
- 'This is my wife, Kristina.' Just "eto moya zhena": 'this my wife', with no 'is'.
