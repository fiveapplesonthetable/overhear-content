## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Our rent?' The word for rent is glued together from 'apartment' and 'pay'. Kostya repeats it as a question.
- 'What do you mean?' Russian says 'in the sense?', with a small word for 'in'. It's a short phrase you can use anywhere.
- 'Exactly what I said.' The same phrase again, now 'in the direct sense', or 'literally'. 'Direct' also means 'straight'.
- 'I'll explain everything to you now.' 'Explain' is built from 'clear'. 'To you everything' has the 'to you' form first.
- 'I don't understand anything.' 'Nothing' and 'not' appear together, as Russian requires. 'I understand' has a clear 'u' ending.
- 'Let's suppose one simple situation.' 'Suppose' is the 'we' form, used as an invitation. 'One' and 'simple' both take the 'u' ending.
- 'Timur brings us cognac.' 'Brings' is the word for bringing by vehicle. 'Us' takes the 'to us' form.
- 'Five liters of cognac every week.' After 'five', 'liters' takes an 'ov' ending. 'Every week' needs no word for 'during'.
- 'Does he bring it for free?' 'For free' is built from 'without' and 'pay'. It's the same 'pay' word as in 'rent'.
- 'Yes, absolutely free.' 'Absolutely' is a borrowed word, and it sits right before 'free'.
- 'And what do we do with it?' 'With it' picks up an 'n', so 'nim'. 'We do' ends in 'em'.
- 'We swap the bottles and pay for the apartment.' 'We swap' ends in 'em'. 'For' brings the apartment into the 'u' form.
