## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Our rent?' "Kvartplata" is glued together from "kvartira", flat, and "plata", payment.
- 'What do you mean?' "V smysle?", literally 'in the sense?', is the everyday way to ask someone to explain.
- 'Just what I said.' Max answers "v pryamom smysle", 'in the direct sense': literally. "Pryamoy" also means straight.
- 'I'll explain it all to you now.' "Obyasnyu", I'll explain, is about something to come. "Tebe", to you, is the friendly "ty" form.
- 'I don't understand a thing.' "Nichego ne ponimayu", literally 'nothing not I understand'. "Ponimayu" ends in 'u' for 'I'.
- 'Picture a simple situation.' "Predstav sebe", literally 'present to yourself': imagine. Situation, "situatsiya", becomes "situatsiyu" as the thing imagined.
- 'Timur brings us cognac.' "Privozit" means he brings it by car, regularly. "Nam", to us, is the 'to us' form of "my".
- 'Five litres of cognac every week.' After five, litres become "litrov", and cognac becomes "konyaka". "Kazhduyu nedelyu", every week, needs no 'on' or 'during'.
- 'He brings it for free?' A plain statement turns into a question just by the voice going up. "Ego" here means it, the cognac.
- 'Yes, completely free.' "Absolyutno" is easy to recognise: absolutely. "Besplatno" is "bez", without, plus "plata", payment.
- 'And what do we do with it?' "S nim", with it: after a preposition, "ego" gains an 'n' and becomes "nim".
- 'We switch the bottles and pay for the flat.' "Menyaem", we swap. "Platim za kvartiru": after "za", for, "kvartira" ends in 'u'.
