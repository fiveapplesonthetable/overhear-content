## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hang on, why are you so worked up?' "Kipyatishsya" comes from "kipyatit", to boil: you are boiling. "Chego" here is a casual "why".
- 'A grown man, and you're acting like a little girl.' "Zdorovyy dyadka" is literally "a healthy uncle", a big grown man. "Vesti sebya", to behave, is literally "to lead yourself".
- 'I told you: fire them and you lose me.' Two future verbs side by side make an "if... then": "uvolish", you fire, "poteryaesh", you lose.
- 'Let's just sit down and have a drink.' "Davay" plus a "we" form makes "let's": "davay posidim", let's sit a while.
- 'I've got nothing more to say to you.' "Nechego" means "nothing" in the sense of "there is nothing to". "Mne" is "to me": "to me there is nothing to say".
- 'I promise you, it's not as bad as all that.' "Uveryayu" means "I assure". "Ne tak strashno" is literally "not so scary".
- 'Vitya, you can quit any time you like, right?' "Vit" is a short, friendly call-form of Vitya, Viktor. "V lyuboy moment" means "at any moment".
- 'But what will you do when the bills have to be paid?' You pay "po schetam", along the bills. "Pridyotsya" means "you'll have to", with no "you" needed.
- 'Where does your eldest daughter study? In Paris?' "U tebya" is "you have". "Starshaya" means older or eldest. "Uchitsya" is "studies".
- 'How much do you pay? Probably twenty thousand a year.' Putting the number after the noun, "tysyach dvadtsat", makes it a rough guess. "V god" is "a year".
- 'Dima, I'll find a job.' "Rabotu" comes first for weight: work, that I'll find. "Naydu" is the future of "nayti", to find.
- 'But where will you find an idiot who'll put up with your drinking?' "Terpet" means to put up with. "Pyanstvo", heavy drinking, comes from "pyanyy", drunk.
- 'They'll kick you out after the first binge.' "Vygonyat", they'll throw out, needs no "they". "Pyanka", a binge, is the one-off cousin of "pyanstvo".
- 'Fine, do what you want.' Barinov gives in. "Delayte" ends in "-te": he means Nagiyev and his new management together, not just one man.
- 'Of course. Come on, let's drink.' "Davay vypyem" is the classic "let's have a drink". "Konechno" here sounds smug: Nagiyev has won.
