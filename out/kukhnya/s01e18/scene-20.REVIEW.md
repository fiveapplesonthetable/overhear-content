## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Guys, we'll have to part with you.' "Nam pridyotsya", 'to us it will come': we'll have to. "Rasstatsya s vami", to part with you.
- 'Where are you all off to so early?' "Kuda" is where to; where at would be "gde". "Sobralis", set off, is literally 'gathered yourselves'.
- 'We got fired.' "Uvolili", they fired, names nobody: someone fired "nas", us.
- 'What do you mean, fired? Me too?' "Kak uvolili?", 'how fired?', is how Russians question bad news.
- 'Yes, we're all leaving.' "Vse", everyone, is different from "vsyo", everything. "Ukhodim", we're leaving: "u-" again means away.
- 'And what did the chef say?' "A" opens a new question: and what about. "Shef" came from French.
- 'The chef did nothing.' "Nichego ne sdelal", 'nothing not did': the double 'not' is normal Russian.
- 'Now we're out of work.' "Bez", without, turns "rabota" into "raboty".
- 'What do we do now?' "Chto nam delat?", 'what to us to do?': the "nam" carries the 'we'.
- 'I don't know, let's go home.' "Poydyom", let's go. "Domoy" is homeward; at home is "doma".
- 'Here's your pay-off.' "Raschyot", the final pay, comes from "schitat", to count: everything is settled up.
- 'Only twenty thousand?' "Dvadtsat" is "dva", two, plus a worn-down 'ten': two tens.
- 'The rest will come at the end of the month.' "Ostalnoe", the rest, from "ostatsya", to remain. "V kontse mesyatsa", at the end of the month.
