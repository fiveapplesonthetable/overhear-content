## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I wonder how a bomb disposal expert feels.' Touching on the sapper right here, Max lands on "chuvstvuyet" ("feels", built on "chuvstvovat") to keep the statement grounded.
- 'Probably the exact same way we do.' Barinov follows up decisively before Max and lands on "navernoye" ("probably") as the key everyday word here.
- 'When we make the decision to tell the bitter truth or stay silent.' Max responds bluntly in this moment, testing Barinov's reaction in the middle of the exchange.
- 'We make our choice.' Barinov follows up readily in front of Max and centers the thought on "vybirayem" ("choose", from "vybirat") without wasting a word.
- 'When are you people finally going to leave, it is impossible to sleep.' Max proceeds briskly beside Barinov, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'We work until the very last customer.' Barinov answers wryly across the room, so Max knows he is not just making small talk.
- 'Wait, what are you still doing here?' Max replies plainly during the talk and leans on "delayesh" ("are you doing", a form of "delat") to give the remark its edge.
- 'I still have one more order.' Answering boldly in this beat, spoken Russian compresses the whole thought into just 4 words, skipping the extra helper words English needs.
- 'Have they completely lost their minds?' Max inquires plainly where this conversation is heading next.
- 'It is two o'clock in the morning.' Emphasizing the hours before Max, Barinov centers the thought on "chasa" ("hours", from "chas") without wasting a word.
- 'Tell them that the kitchen is closed.' Turning to the kitchen beside Barinov, Max chooses "kukhnya" ("kitchen") so Barinov gets the point right away.
- 'Wait, is this dessert for them?' Barinov probes plainly while looking straight at Max here.
- 'Why did you not say so right away?' Max replies briskly right here and reaches for the Russian word "srazu" ("at once") right at the heart of the line.
