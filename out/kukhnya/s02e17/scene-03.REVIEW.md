## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'It is none of your business where I am calling.' Admitting that it is none of Kostya's business where he is calling shows Max being honest for a change.
- 'This turned out really badly.' Saying that this turned out really badly is how Kostya sets the tone with Max.
- 'Or rather, I do not even know how this happened.' Once rather he does not even know how this happened, Max can finally tell Kostya what he means. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'Oh, hey there, Kostyan.' Over the phone and the morning, Kostya answers Max without a second thought.
- 'I am calling Max right now.' Being calling Max right now is how Max lays out the situation for Kostya.
- 'Wait, do not call anywhere.' Since do not call anywhere, Kostya does not waste words on excuses.
- 'I do not want to lose him, do you understand?' Asking whether he does not want to lose him do Kostya understands puts Kostya right on the defensive. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'Me, go to sleep?' With the conversation and morning hanging in the air, Kostya puts Max on the spot.
- 'And about football.' Around the phone and point, Max sets a calm, everyday tone with Kostya.
- 'Just be quiet, I know it myself!' Once just be quiet he knows it himself, Kostya makes sure Max hears every word. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
- 'Who did not see that I wanted to sit down and stand up?' By wondering who not see that he wanted to sit down and stand up, Max presses Kostya for a straight answer.
- 'And so what?' With the conversation and work hanging in the air, Kostya puts Max on the spot. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'Because that is what I wanted to do!' Saying that because that is what he wanted to do is how Max takes charge of the moment. Russian puts the key word right up front so his emotion lands on the first beat.
