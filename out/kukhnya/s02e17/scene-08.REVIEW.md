## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'We are out of milk.' Admitting that they are out of milk shows Barinov being honest for a change.
- 'Louis, some girl out there wants to see you.' Saying that some girl out there wants to see Barinov is how Vika sets the tone with Barinov. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
- 'What is it, Louis?' Here asking what it Louis is shows Louis looking for the real truth.
- 'I do not understand what she wants from me.' Questioning whether he does not understand what she wants from him is how Barinov tests Louis's story. There is no "you" needed in the Russian here: one short word states the rule for everyone in the room.
- 'Ah, well, apparently she liked it.' Vika tells Barinov that apparently she liked it without dressing it up.
- 'Louis simply walked her home.' Since Louis simply walked her home, Louis does not waste words on excuses.
- 'So nothing happened at all, right?' Over the moment and the friend, Barinov presses Louis for a straight answer. Russian stacks "never" or "nothing" together with "not", doubling the refusal for extra weight.
- 'Hand the three grand back over.' Vika explains that Barinov should hand the three grand back, keeping her voice calm and direct.
- 'What three grand?' Over the conversation and the guest, Louis presses Barinov for a straight answer.
- 'Do not touch that!' Barinov insists that do not touch, leaving Louis no room to argue. Russian puts the key word right up front so his emotion lands on the first beat.
- 'Your friend, who is she?' Vika asks whether Barinov's friend who is she, refusing to let the point slide. In Russian, she does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'Meet Tamara Ivanovna.' Realizing that Barinov should meet Tamara Ivanovna changes how Louis handles the situation.
- 'And you must be Viktor Petrovich, right?' Wondering if Louis must be Viktor Petrovich right shows Barinov caught off guard. Even in the middle of the drama, he keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
