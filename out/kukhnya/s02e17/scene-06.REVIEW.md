## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Why did you not warn me you were coming?' Elena wonders frankly whether Kostya really thought this through.
- 'Why, is it strictly necessary to give a warning?' Kostya speaks warmly right here and leans on "preduprezhdat" ("to warn") to give the whole sentence its punch.
- 'Well, no.' Elena quietly chooses this direct affirmation for Kostya using "da" ("yes") — a crisp "da" ("yes") that confirms the point without extra words.
- 'A brand-new aquarium.' Kostya counters promptly toward Elena, bringing things back to earth after the buildup.
- 'So why did you stop by?' Elena counters boldly in the exchange and brings in "zashla" ("dropped in", from "zayti") as the key Russian word here.
- 'A party. And who else if not you.' Kostya responds quietly with a smile, watching Elena's face to see how the news lands.
- 'Alright then, let her come over here.' Elena quietly voices this word of agreement at the start of the beat using "ladno" ("alright") — built from the old Russian root for harmony and order, used every day for "okay, fine".
- 'No, she asked for us to drive over to her place.' Kostya pushes back firmly at once and chooses "podyekhali" ("to drive up", from the root "podyekhat") so Elena hears the exact meaning.
- 'She has no free time at all.' Addressing the time in this beat, Elena leans on "sovershenno" ("quite, completely") to give the whole sentence its punch.
- 'Come on, Vitya, please.' Kostya responds quietly on the spot, to clear the air between them once and for all.
- 'And everything will be right there.' Pushing back firmly at once, spoken Russian compresses the whole thought into just 3 words, skipping the extra helper words English needs.
- 'And then for me, well...' Kostya speaks up briskly with a sigh, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'There you go, just like that.' Elena follows up earnestly with a shrug, without trying to dress up how she really feels.
