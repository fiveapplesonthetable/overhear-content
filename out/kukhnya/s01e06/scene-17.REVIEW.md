## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Chef, why is this health inspector so angry?' Russian says 'such angry', with the word for 'such' in front. The word for 'angry' is one you can also use for a dog or a boss.
- 'I get it, this strict man is on the wagon!' The slang for 'on the wagon' is literally 'in a knot', as if he had tied himself up. 'I understood' is how Russians say 'I get it'.
- 'Do you think he quit drinking completely?' The word for 'quit' literally means 'threw', so he threw drinking away. 'Completely' sits right in front of it.
- 'Yes, that is why he is so nervous.' 'That is why' is built from 'by this', and it joins two ideas neatly. 'Nervous' is the same word you use in English.
- 'And how are we to calm him now?' 'To us now him to calm' has no 'should', and 'him' sits in the middle. The command 'calm down' comes from the same word.
- 'Sasha, come here to me quickly.' 'Come up' and 'to me' both show direction, so Barinov says it twice for force. The word for 'here' is the kind you use for movement.
- 'I am listening to you carefully, Chef.' It's the waiter's way of saying 'I'm all ears'. 'Carefully' is built from 'attention'.
- 'Do you see that bald man at the table?' 'That' and 'bald' both end in the same way, as they describe one man. 'At the table' uses 'behind', since you sit behind a table in Russian.
- 'Yes, he sits with a very gloomy expression.' 'With a gloomy look' uses 'with' and a changed ending. 'Look' is the word that also means 'view' or 'sort'.
- 'Offer him the very best expensive wine.' The word for 'offer' is a command. 'Him' takes the 'to' form, and 'very best' is literally 'most best'.
- 'And what if he refuses alcohol?' The word for 'refuses' ends in 'sya' and takes 'from' with the thing refused. 'Alcohol' is borrowed, so you know it.
- 'Say that this is a gift on the house!' The Russian is 'a gift from the establishment', and a restaurant is one kind of 'establishment'. You could use the same phrase in any shop.
- 'Fine, Chef, I understood everything.' The word for 'understood' ends in 'la' because Sasha is a woman. 'Everything' follows it.
