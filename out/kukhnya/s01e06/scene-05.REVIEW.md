## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello, guys! How are your things?' 'Guys' is the plural of the word for a young man. 'How are your things' is the usual Russian 'how are you', and 'things' is plural.
- 'Senya, are you nearly done? I need the foam!' 'Are you soon' has no verb, just 'you soon'. 'I need foam' puts the need first: 'to me needed foam'.
- 'I will just shave my right arm, and that's it.' 'arm' also means 'hand', and Russians do not separate the two. 'And that's it' is a neat little ending that is literally 'and all'.
- 'And what are you doing? Shaving your arms?' Max asks twice, once with 'what' and once with a bare guess. The second question has only two words, so the voice makes it a question.
- 'Today is the fifteenth, a big sanitary inspection!' 'fifteenth' agrees with 'date', which is neuter. 'Sanitary' is borrowed and looks like English.
- 'Hair must not fall into the food!' Russian uses a plural word for hair, so it's closer to 'the hairs'. 'Must' also takes a plural ending.
- 'Come on, this is complete nonsense!' The first words mean 'come on, no way'. The word at the end is the plural of 'silly thing', so it's 'silly things'.
- 'This is a strict rule of the sanitary inspection.' 'Strict' and 'rule' agree. 'Of the inspection' is shown by changing the last letters of the word, not by a separate word for 'of'.
- 'If they find one hair, they fire you immediately!' 'They find' has no person named, so it sounds like 'someone'. A single hair is a different form from the plural 'hairs'.
- 'Why didn't you warn me earlier?' 'Warn' is built on 'before'. 'Earlier' is a handy word.
- 'I don't even have a razor with me.' 'At me there is no' is the way Russian says 'I don't have', and the missing thing changes its ending. 'With me' is literally 'with myself'.
- 'All right, runt, take my razor.' Fedya is being generous, which should be suspicious. 'Take' is a command, and 'my' agrees with 'razor'.
- 'For arms I don't mind the razor at all.' 'To me not sorry' is how Russian says 'I don't mind lending'. 'At all' sits in front of 'not'.
- 'Thank you, Fedya, I will quickly shave everything.' 'shave' has a little 'po' at the front, which makes it a one-off job. 'Everything' is a short word, and it's coming from Max with no suspicion.
