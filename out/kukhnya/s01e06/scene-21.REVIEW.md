## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Viktor, a terrible scandal happened in the dining room.' Vika calls Barinov by his first name and says "ty", because she's close to him. "Uzhasnyy", terrible, is from "uzhas", horror.
- 'Calm down, Vika, we will show the video to the inspection.' "Uspokoysya", calm down, ends in -sya, 'calm yourself'. Video never changes its ending, and "proverke" means 'to the inspectors'.
- 'We will surely successfully pass the inspection.' "Proydyom", we'll get through, is what Russians say for passing an exam or a check. "Navernyaka", surely, is more confident than "navernoe", probably.
- 'But this vagrant caused a real nightmare!' "Brodyaga", tramp, is from "brodit", to wander. "Ustroil", arranged, is the verb for setting things up, which makes the chaos sound deliberate.
- 'He shouted and scared off all the guests!' "Raspugal", scared off, has ras-, in all directions, so the guests scattered. "Vsekh gostey", all the guests, changes both words because they're the ones scared.
- 'And then he beat up the writer Pelevin!' "Izbil", beat up, takes "bit", to hit, and adds iz- for 'thoroughly'. "Pisatelya Pelevina" has -ya and -a because he's the one hit.
- 'Is this really complete nonsense to you?' "Neuzheli", really, carries disbelief, like 'can it be that...'. "Dlya tebya", for you, turns "ty" into "tebya" after "dlya".
- 'Of course, this is not nonsense at all.' Barinov repeats her word, "eto ne erunda", and adds "sovsem", at all. Echoing someone's word is a good way to argue in Russian.
- 'This is a very big important event!' "Bolshoe" and "vazhnoe" both end in -oe to agree with "sobytie", event, a neuter word. Two adjectives with no 'and' is how Barinov piles on the drama.
- 'And what benefit is there in this for the restaurant?' "Polza", benefit, gives you "polezno", 'it's good for you'. "Restoranu", to the restaurant, has -u for who gets the benefit.
- 'Now we are not just a good restaurant.' "Ne prosto", not just, sets up something bigger to follow. Here it's the next line.
- 'Here they punched a famous person in the face!' "Dali po morde", 'gave on the snout', is rude slang for punching someone. "Morda" is an animal's muzzle, so it's not a word for polite company.
- 'You will always find a reason for joy!' "Naydyosh", you'll find, is a future form that also works as 'you always manage to find'. "Povod", reason, is a pretext or excuse, not the logical kind of reason.
