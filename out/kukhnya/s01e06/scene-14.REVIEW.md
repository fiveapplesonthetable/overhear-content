## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Wait, let's just pay money.' "Podozhdi", wait, is the "ty" command, the one you'll hear most. "Zaplatim", we'll pay, is za- plus "platit", so a single payment and done.
- 'Bargaining is completely inappropriate here, young man.' "Torg", bargaining, is the word you'd hear at a market. "Neumesten", out of place, is "ne" plus umesten, fitting.
- 'But this is a very expensive restaurant!' "Zhe" adds an 'after all', as if Kostya is stating the obvious. "Dorogoy", expensive, also means 'dear', the word that opens a letter.
- 'I know this fine restaurant perfectly well.' Rodion uses "prekrasno" and "prekrasnyy" back to back, 'perfectly' and 'fine', and he clearly enjoys it. "Znayu", I know, is the plain 'I know' for facts and places.
- 'The administrator will throw you out into the street immediately.' "Vygonit", will throw out, is vy-, out, plus "gnat", to chase. "Na ulitsu", onto the street, has -u because that's where he'll land.
- 'In a decent suit nobody will recognize me.' Russians say "v kostyume", 'in a suit', just as in English. "Uznaet", will recognize, is "znat", to know, with u- in front.
- 'And what if you accidentally ruin someone else's clothes?' A "esli", 'and if', is how 'what if' begins. "Sluchayno", by accident, is from "sluchay", a chance event.
- 'I promise to behave extremely neatly.' "Vesti sebya", to behave, is literally 'to lead oneself'. "Krayne", extremely, is from "kray", edge.
- 'Where exactly are our missing truffles hidden?' "Spryatany", hidden, ends in -y because the truffles are plural. "Propavshie", missing, belongs with "propast", to vanish.
- 'You will receive the truffles right after lunch.' "Poluchite", you'll receive, is a future form with "vy". After "posle", after, "obed" becomes "obeda".
- 'The Chef will get very angry if he finds out the truth.' "Razozlitsya", will get angry, is from "zloy", angry, with raz- and -sya around it. "Uznaet pravdu", finds out the truth, uses "uznaet" again, now as 'find out' rather than 'recognize'.
- 'Then wish me bon appétit, Konstantin.' "Priyatnogo appetita", 'of pleasant appetite', is the Russian 'enjoy your meal', with the -ogo and -a coming from "pozhelat". Konstantin is Kostya's full name, which Rodion uses to sound lofty.
