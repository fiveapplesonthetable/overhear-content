## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Of juicy marbled beef.' Max underscores one vivid detail with Barinov: "mramornoy" means "marbled, marble" (from "mramornyy"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'Nobody can resist a rack of young lamb.' Barinov sends that reply plainly here: Russian happily doubles the negative here — pairing "never" or "nothing" with "ne" ("not") — which is the only natural way to say it.
- 'They will not offer you a well-done steak.' Max speaks lightly without holding back, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'Can someone really refuse this miracle?' Barinov anchors one key piece at once: "otkazatsya" means "to refuse, decline", and it carries the main weight of what Barinov is saying.
- 'Tell me, do you have anything vegetarian?' Max aims the comeback to Barinov: using "u vas" (literally "by you") is how Russian politely asks or talks about what someone has on their side.
- 'Are you a vegetarian?' Barinov questions deadpan if there is more going on behind the scenes.
- 'Me too.' Max favors one sharp choice on cue: "tozhe" means "too", and it carries the main weight of what Max is saying.
- 'There are people like that.' Barinov lays out the comeback without fuss: spoken Russian trims this down to just 2 words, dropping the extra helper words that English has to spell out.
- 'And where is the meat, grandma?' Max probes calmly while looking straight at Barinov here.
- 'You will eat meat at home.' Barinov leans on one familiar choice toward Max: "myaso" means "meat", and it carries the main weight of what Barinov is saying.
- 'Here we have a healthy, delicious breakfast.' Max hands over this point with candor: the phrase "u nas" literally means "by us" or "at our place", the standard Russian way to talk about what a group or couple shares.
- 'Just like in the finest homes of Paris.' Barinov jumps in openly before Max, testing Max's reaction in the middle of the exchange.
- 'Enjoy your meal.' Max colors this key touch for Barinov: "priyatnogo" means "pleasant" (from "priyatnyy"), giving the line its exact edge.
