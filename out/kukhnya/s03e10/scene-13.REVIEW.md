## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Chef, well then, can we congratulate you?' Senya raises the pitch on 'to congratulate' to turn the phrase into a direct spoken question.
- 'The two of you are simply total idiots!' In Russian, the idea of 'you' flows naturally into 'both' without extra filler words.
- 'Chef, I do not understand what you are talking about.' Fedya chooses the respectful plural 'you' alongside 'you' to keep a polite distance.
- 'I do not even know a word like that.' Fedya anchors the line on 'I', while 'know' completes the thought.
- 'But we only wanted to do what was best!' An emphatic spoken line centered on the verb meaning 'wanted'.
- 'Things were not going very well for you.' Notice how Russian expresses 'at' and 'to work out' with crisp inflection rather than helper words. Chef Barinov answers right away.
- 'Shut your mouth, first I am going to fire you!' An emphatic spoken line centered on the verb meaning 'close'.
- 'Then I will tie you up and hang you by your feet!' The meaning hinges on 'then' working together with 'legs' in spoken Russian.
- 'All day long I will be tearing something off you!' An emphatic spoken line centered on the verb meaning 'will be'.
- 'Hello, yes, Dima, are you in the restaurant already?' Chef Barinov brings out the contrast between 'hello' and 'yes' in a few short words.
- 'Dinner with Vika, of course I remember.' Here the Russian phrasing highlights 'dinner' before landing on 'remember'.
- 'I will come over right now, yes, we will come over.' Chef Barinov brings out the contrast between 'now' and 'will come over' in a few short words.
