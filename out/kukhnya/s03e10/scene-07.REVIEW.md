## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Lyova, pull yourself together, we are going all in now.' Underscoring the ending in the exchange, Max uses "kontsa" ("ending", from "konets") to make the point stick.
- 'I am inviting her out on a date tonight.' Pointing to a date beside Lyova, Max centers the remark on "svidaniye" ("date") without any extra fuss.
- 'And you are the one who goes to the meeting.' Max pushes back briskly on the spot, without trying to dress up how he really feels.
- 'From there you act on your own, chin up!' Highlighting the nose to Lyova, Max centers the thought on "deystvuyesh" ("to act", from "deystvovat") without wasting a word.
- 'Alright, Max, we have a deal.' Stepping in readily with Max, the "-sya" ending on "dogovorilis" ("agreed, deal") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'So, where shall we go to drink coffee?' Max asks calmly whether Katya really thought this through.
- 'Maxim, you and I are not going anywhere.' Katya opens up openly at once and leans on "poydyom" ("let's go", a form of "poyti") to give the remark its edge.
- 'What do you mean we are not going anywhere?' Addressing the sense on the spot, Max keeps normal statement word order here, turning the line into a question for Katya purely through rising pitch.
- 'Lyova has simply worn me out.' Katya reacts plainly across the room, without trying to dress up how she really feels.
- 'All day long he keeps walking around me.' Katya pushes back vividly with Max and chooses "tselyy" ("whole, all") so Max gets the point right away.
- 'I try to hint to him, but he does not understand.' Katya replies openly around Max and brings in "ponimayet" ("understands", from "ponimat") as the key Russian word here.
- 'I thought if I invited you in front of him, he would back off.' Katya cuts in quietly with a nod, grounding the moment in plain reality.
