## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Vika, Kristina wants to tell you something.' Dmitry uses the casual everyday 'you' with 'Vika', as close colleagues and friends do.
- 'You are not fired, I was simply joking.' Here Kristina anchors the line on the word meaning 'fired' and builds the thought around 'just' and 'was joking'.
- 'You have a simply terrible sense of humor.' Vika uses the classic Russian possession phrasing — literally 'at you' rather than a verb meaning 'to have', combining 'just' with 'awful' and 'feeling'.
- 'But I am not planning to return here.' the self-directed ending on the verb for 'am planning' turns the action back onto the subject.
- 'Well, no means no.' Russian omits the present-tense verb 'to be' here, linking 'oh' directly with 'no', combining 'oh' with 'no' and 'so'.
- 'Vika, what are you doing?' Nagiyev delivers a punchy one-word reaction built around the Russian word meaning 'are doing'.
- 'Well, you showed off and that is enough.' Notice how naturally this everyday Russian phrase expresses the speaker's point.
- 'I will not return.' Notice the self-directed ending on the verb for 'will come back', which directs the action back onto the subject.
- 'What does it mean, you do not want to?' Nagiyev puts the word for 'then' together with 'do you want' in a crisp, natural spoken turn of phrase.
- 'Can you really abandon all this?' Notice how naturally this everyday Russian phrase expresses the speaker's point.
- 'This is your life.' Nagiyev delivers a punchy one-word reaction built around the Russian word meaning 'life'.
- 'Everything here is made by your efforts.' Russian leaves out 'am', 'is', or 'are' in the present tense, linking 'here' straight to 'effort'.
- 'You bought these plush sofas yourself!' Nagiyev chooses the action verb meaning 'to buy' for the ongoing action, followed by 'plush' and 'sofa'.
