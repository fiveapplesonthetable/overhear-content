## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I am telling you, you need to rinse.' Realizing that he is telling Kostya Kostya needs to rinse changes how Fedya handles the situation.
- 'Your medicine will not help at all.' Adding that Kostya's medicine will not help at all fills in the missing piece for Kostya. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
- 'Right here is real cold ice.' Fedya tells Kostya that right here is real cold ice without dressing it up. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'Here, you can check for yourself.' Since here Kostya can check for themselves, Fedya does not waste words on excuses.
- 'Well, have you checked already?' By checking whether Kostya checked already, Fedya presses Kostya for a straight answer. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
- 'Yes, your rival is certainly serious.' Kostya reminds Fedya that Fedya's rival is certainly serious before the moment passes. In Russian you say "by me" or "with us" rather than "I have", so the situation sits right with the person.
- 'It is good that he does not like Nastya.' Because it is good that he does not like Nastya, Kostya treats the matter as settled.
- 'I will go talk with him calmly.' Explaining that he will go talk with him calmly lays the cards on the table for Fedya.
- 'Max, wait, where are you going?' By asking where Fedya going is, Kostya presses Fedya for a straight answer. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
- 'Dmitry Vladimirovich, are you free right now?' Checking whether Fedya free right now cuts straight through the tension between Max and Fedya.
- 'Let me call you back later.' Because Fedya should let him call Fedya back later, Kostya treats the matter as settled. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
- 'We need to talk seriously.' Here needing to talk seriously sets up the next turn in their exchange. Russian packs the whole idea of what is allowed or needed into one impersonal word at the front.
