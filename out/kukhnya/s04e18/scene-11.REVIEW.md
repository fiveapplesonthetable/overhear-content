## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Where is my knife, has nobody seen it?' Double negation with the negative subject pronoun meaning nobody is standard in Russian questions.
- 'Who is having so much fun in here today?' An impersonal dative question asks who is feeling amused in the kitchen.
- 'I am asking once again, where is my knife?' German repeats his demand using an adverbial phrase meaning one more time.
- 'Isn't this one right here your knife?' A pointing particle and a question word introduce a polite plural possessive pronoun.
- 'Why is my knife so hot?' After grabbing the heated handle, German uses a masculine singular adjective meaning hot.
- 'Excuse me, it was lying near the stove.' A polite plural apology precedes a past-tense verb of position and a genitive preposition.
- 'All right, stop, what are you hiding there?' German spots Fedya near the office and uses a singular imperative meaning stop or stand still.
- 'What is that in your hand right now?' A possessive phrase combines with the prepositional singular of the noun for hand.
- 'It is just fresh green dill.' Having swallowed the pill, Fedya holds up a bunch of dill and names it in the nominative case.
- 'Dill? Why do you need it in here?' A masculine short adjective of need agrees with the pronoun referring to the dill.
- 'Do you want some? It is very healthy.' Fedya offers the herbs to the baffled head chef using a neuter short predicate adverbial.
- 'Let's get out of here to the kitchen faster.' Barinov hustles Fedya away using a plural past verb functioning as a brisk imperative.
