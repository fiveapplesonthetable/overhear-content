## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'We need to introduce a new menu.' Nagiyev uses an impersonal modal word of need with a perfective infinitive and an indeclinable neuter noun.
- 'I have seen it, it works wonderfully.' A masculine past verb of perception pairs with an adverb meaning wonderfully and a present-tense verb.
- 'Dima, let's step aside for a minute.' Barinov interrupts his boss with a first-person plural perfective verb meaning step aside.
- 'What are you holding there in your hands?' Nagiyev eyes the tablet using a prepositional plural noun phrase and a second-person singular verb.
- 'Vitya, are you playing games?' The verb meaning to play takes the preposition meaning in followed by the accusative plural of games.
- 'Speak, I have no secrets from colleagues.' A negated existential phrase requires the genitive plural on the masculine noun meaning secrets.
- 'Dima, we have a big problem.' Barinov sets a serious tone with a first-person plural possessive phrase and a feminine noun.
- 'You have warmed a snake on your chest.' This classic Russian idiom describes welcoming a treacherous person who will later betray you.
- 'My ex-wife is far away right now.' Nagiyev jokes that the only snake he knows is his ex-wife Kristina.
- 'I am talking about your German.' The colloquial preposition meaning about governs the animate accusative singular of the possessive modifier and name.
- 'Take a look at this yourself.' An emphatic pronoun reinforces the singular perfective imperative meaning look.
- 'Well go on, show me the video.' Nagiyev agrees to watch using a perfective imperative and a dative personal pronoun.
