## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I won a lucky ticket.' Pointing to a lucky ticket right here, Denis centers the remark on "schastlivyy" ("lucky") without any extra fuss.
- 'When do new relationships begin?' Mentioning the relations to Katya, the "-sya" ending on "zarozhdayutsya" ("to new") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'Then a bright flame flares up.' Denis weighs in plainly with a shrug, making his position completely clear.
- 'Over time a flame can go out.' Focusing on the time right here, Denis centers the thought on "vremenem" ("time", from "vremya") without wasting a word.
- 'But just one spark is enough.' Touching on the enough in front of Katya, Denis links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
- 'Are you running away again today?' Denis prompts frankly how Katya expects that to work in practice.
- 'No, I am not running away.' Katya responds naturally with Denis and puts the weight on "ubegayu" ("run away", from "ubegat") as the exchange unfolds.
- 'Everything is just happening so fast.' Cutting in warmly in the exchange, Katya uses the little word "prosto" ("simply, just") to make the point sound effortless.
- 'Do you feel bad with me right now?' Denis asks evenly what Katya is actually getting at here.
- 'I feel very good with you.' Katya chimes in firmly in the exchange and chooses "khorosho" ("good, fine") so Denis gets the point right away.
- 'Katya, I beg you, stay.' Opening up candidly during the talk, the "-sya" ending on "ostansya" ("to remain, to be left") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'All right, I will stay a little longer.' Katya openly gives this word of agreement with Denis using "ladno" ("alright") — built from the old Russian root for harmony and order, used every day for "okay, fine".
