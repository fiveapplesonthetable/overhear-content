## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I won a lucky ticket.' Denis uses a masculine past perfective verb with an inanimate accusative adjective-noun phrase.
- 'When do new relationships begin?' A reflexive third-person plural present verb agrees with the plural subject noun meaning relationships.
- 'Then a bright flame flares up.' The neuter singular noun for flame takes a neuter adjective and a third-person singular verb.
- 'Over time a flame can go out.' An instrumental time phrase leads into a modal verb followed by a perfective infinitive.
- 'But just one spark is enough.' An impersonal adverbial of sufficiency governs the feminine genitive singular phrase meaning one spark.
- 'Are you running away again today?' In the morning, Denis asks a direct question using an imperfective second-person singular present verb.
- 'No, I am not running away.' Katya denies leaving by negating the first-person singular form of the same motion verb.
- 'Everything is just happening so fast.' A neuter singular pronoun serves as the subject of a third-person singular verb meaning happens.
- 'Do you feel bad with me right now?' An impersonal dative construction with an instrumental pronoun asks how she feels in his company.
- 'I feel very good with you.' She reassures him using the parallel dative pronoun and a positive predicate adverb.
- 'Katya, I beg you, stay.' A first-person verb of pleading with an accusative pronoun leads into a singular perfective imperative.
- 'All right, I will stay a little longer.' A prefixed perfective future verb expresses staying for a short while after an adverb of degree.
