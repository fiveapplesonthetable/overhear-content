## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'You are overcooking the vegetables again, Maxim Leonidovich.' Vika chimes in frankly across the room, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'That will not be al dente anymore.' Vika counters smoothly around Max and brings in "dente" ("dente") to make the meaning unmistakable.
- 'May I show you?' Speaking frankly in front of Vika, Max leans on "mozhno" ("it is possible, one may"), which carries the whole predicate in Russian without needing "it is".
- 'Right after you drain the boiling water.' Vika opens up candidly with a sigh, making her position completely clear.
- 'You have to plunge the vegetables into ice water.' Emphasizing the vegetables right here, Max chooses "ovoshchi" ("vegetables", from the root "ovoshch") so Vika hears the exact meaning.
- 'That is what is called al dente.' Opening up firmly to Max, the "-sya" ending on "nazyvayetsya" ("is called") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'Which translated from Italian means...' Max answers decisively facing Vika, grounding the moment in plain reality.
- 'Do you taste the difference? Not bad, right?' Vika cuts in firmly on the spot and leans on "chuvstvuyete" ("feel", a form of "chuvstvovat") to give the remark its edge.
- 'I never would have thought of that.' Opening up plainly in this beat, Max pairs the negative word with "ne" ("not"), since Russian naturally doubles its negatives for emphasis.
- 'You know what.' Vika replies steadily hearing Max, keeping the focus on what actually matters here.
- 'No offense, Rodion Sergeyevich.' Underscoring the hard feelings with Vika, Max leans on "obid" ("hard feelings", a form of "obida") to give the remark its edge.
- 'None taken at all.' Bringing up the offense to Max, Vika puts the question word "kakiye" right at the start so Max hears the focus immediately.
- 'I spotted something in your refrigerator earlier.' Max responds firmly around Vika, watching Vika's face to see how the news lands.
