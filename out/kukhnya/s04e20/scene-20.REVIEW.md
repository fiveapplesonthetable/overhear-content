## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Since childhood they teach us to believe in miracles.' The verb meaning to believe takes the directional preposition with the accusative plural of miracles.
- 'Parents read us kind fairy tales in the evening.' A plural subject takes a third-person plural present verb, dative pronoun, and accusative plural object.
- 'We believe that a miracle will definitely happen.' A neuter singular subject pairs with a third-person singular perfective future reflexive verb.
- 'Sometimes a miracle happens completely unexpectedly.' An adverb of manner meaning unexpectedly modifies the present-tense verb happens.
- 'When it seems that the fairy tale has come to an end.' The idiom meaning approached the end uses a feminine past motion verb and dative singular.
- 'Your pregnancy is progressing completely normally right now.' At the clinic, the doctor reassures Vika as Max walks in and takes her hand.
- 'You have no reasons for worry at all.' A negated existential phrase requires the genitive plural on the noun for reasons.
- 'But if a miracle still did not happen...' The voiceover turns to Viktor Petrovich after his defeat in the show finale.
- 'That means you need to write your own fairy tale yourself!' The emphatic dative pronoun agrees with the implied dative subject of the modal predicate.
- 'Chef, tell us, what is this place?' Standing before a spacious new space, the cooks ask Viktor Petrovich where he has brought them.
- 'Friends, I did not call you here for nothing.' Viktor Petrovich smiles proudly at his loyal team gathered around him.
- 'Guys, this will be our new restaurant!' He announces that they will build their own new restaurant together.
