## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Good evening, my dear friends!' Nagiyev opens the evening broadcast with a warm vocative plural greeting.
- 'I am Dmitry Nagiyev, this is the show 'Head Chef'.' Russian omits the present-tense linking verb in both clauses of the host's introduction.
- 'Only one will become the head chef of our restaurant.' In everyday Russian, 'to become' flows straight into 'restaurant' without extra linking words.
- 'Our celebrity jury will choose the winner.' Nagiyev pairs 'winner' with 'jury' directly, since spoken Russian skips English filler words like 'the', 'a', and 'is'.
- 'And your humble servant, the best host.' Nagiyev grandly refers to himself with the traditional polite phrase meaning your humble servant.
- 'Stop, we have a problem with the sound!' The director halts the recording when a loud ringtone interrupts the broadcast.
- 'Guys, let's reshoot everything from the beginning right now.' An inclusive hortative particle introduces a first-person plural verb of re-filming.
- 'Did they not teach you how to behave during filming?' In spoken Russian, the words stay in their normal statement order and Nagiyev's rising voice at the end turns the line into a question.
- 'You must always turn off your phone!' Notice how Russian puts 'need to, must' and 'phone' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'Excuse me, I just forgot to turn it off.' Barinov apologizes politely in the plural and uses a masculine past verb meaning forgot.
- 'And did you not forget your head at home?' In spoken Russian, the words stay in their normal statement order and Nagiyev's rising voice at the end turns the line into a question.
- 'All right, Dima, I already apologized, after all.' Remembering that he needs to win the contest, Barinov holds his tongue and apologizes.
