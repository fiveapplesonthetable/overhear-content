## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello, dear friends, this is the finale of the show!' Nagiyev chimes in quietly with Barinov, to clear the air between them once and for all.
- 'Our third judge did not come today.' Underscoring the judge in front of Barinov, Nagiyev puts the emphasis on "tretiy" ("the third") in the middle of the talk.
- 'German Land will judge the final round!' Mentioning the land in front of German, Nagiyev lands on "raund" ("land") as the key everyday word here.
- 'The former head chef of our restaurant Claude Monet.' Nagiyev speaks up bluntly with a nod, bringing things back to earth after the buildup.
- 'Stop, cut, let's take a break for a minute!' Touching on a break in this beat, Barinov chooses "pereryv" ("break, recess") so Nagiyev gets the point right away.
- 'What on earth are you doing here right now?' Pressing on boldly in front of German, Barinov drops in "voobshche" ("in general, altogether") to sum up the whole situation at once.
- 'I am very glad to see you again.' German proceeds earnestly across the room, watching Barinov's face to see how the news lands.
- 'What, have you gotten tired of being free already?' Touching on the freedom before German, Barinov chooses "svobode" ("freedom, liberty", from the root "svoboda") so German hears the exact meaning.
- 'That guy's bruises healed a long time ago.' Bringing up the bruise beside Barinov, German centers the thought on "sinyaki" ("bruise", from "sinyak") without wasting a word.
- 'And did you forget about our video recording?' Barinov inquires firmly where this conversation is heading next.
- 'Do you really not know at all yet?' German fires back decisively on the spot and uses "yeshchyo" ("more, still") to carry the main weight of the thought.
- 'I bought that recording from your cooks.' Emphasizing the cooks around Barinov, German centers the thought on "povarov" ("cooks", from "povar") without wasting a word.
