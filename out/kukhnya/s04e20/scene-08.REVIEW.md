## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Vika, I really need your help.' Denis pairs 'very, really' with 'help' directly, since spoken Russian skips English filler words like 'the', 'a', and 'is'.
- 'I already told you, solve your problems yourself.' Notice how Russian puts 'told' and 'problems' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'Katya does not want to see me at all.' Here Denis anchors the thought on 'at all' and finishes on 'to see', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'She does not even answer phone calls.' In everyday Russian, 'call; bell' flows straight into 'is responsible, answers' without extra linking words.
- 'Maybe you will talk with her yourself?' He suggests that Katya might listen if Vika speaks to her woman to woman.
- 'Yes, she will certainly believe me right away!' Vika points out sarcastically that a jealous girlfriend will hardly believe her supposed rival.
- 'Tell her that nothing happened.' Just as Senya walks past, Denis blurts out the sensitive details of the rumor.
- 'Excuse me, I did not hear anything here.' Eyes wide with gossip, Senya backs out of the room with a polite plural apology.
- 'Wait, does everyone know about this already?' Realizing that her own reputation in the restaurant is now at stake, Vika changes her mind.
- 'All right, come along with me quickly.' Notice how Russian puts 'let's go' and 'quickly' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'The camera in the office records everything around the clock.' The adverb meaning twenty-four hours a day modifies the third-person singular verb records.
- 'You will be able to show Katya that recording.' In everyday Russian, 'will be able' flows straight into 'the recording' without extra linking words.
