## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Katya, I beg you, hear me out.' The prefixed perfective imperative means to listen to someone all the way through.
- 'This is a recording from our Vika's office.' Denis holds up the tablet and identifies the video using a genitive prepositional phrase.
- 'Look, nothing happened between us.' The preposition meaning between governs the instrumental plural pronoun meaning us.
- 'I am not going to watch anything at all!' The reflexive present verb meaning to intend or be about to takes an imperfective infinitive.
- 'I will not leave until you watch it.' In Russian, the conjunction meaning until requires the negative particle before a perfective future verb.
- 'Denis, are you completely sure about that?' A short masculine predicate adjective asks whether Denis is certain of his stand.
- 'Yes, just look at this screen.' Denis stands his ground and presses play on the tablet screen.
- 'You see, I just walked over to the window.' On the recording, Katya watches Denis back away from Vika toward the open window.
- 'Do not come near me, I will jump!' The video plays Denis's panicked shout right before he tumbles out the window alone.
- 'Denis, had you lost your mind back then?' Watching the absurd leap on screen, Katya cannot help asking if he went crazy.
- 'I told you, I had a bad dream!' Denis reminds her that he jumped because he thought he was still inside his nightmare.
- 'All right, let me pass quietly right now.' Realizing she accused Denis and Vika falsely, an embarrassed Katya slips past him.
