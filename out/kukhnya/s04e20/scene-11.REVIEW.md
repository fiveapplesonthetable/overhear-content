## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello, Dad, is it convenient for you to talk right now?' In spoken Russian, the words stay in their normal statement order and Katya's rising voice at the end turns the line into a question.
- 'All right, Dad, I was wrong.' Putting the short word for 'not' right in front of the action word is all Russian needs to make a sentence negative, with no extra helper words like 'do' or 'did'.
- 'I admit it, he did not cheat on me.' Notice how Russian puts 'to admit, to recognise' and 'cheated' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'There is nothing between him and Vika.' Katya centers this short reply on 'Vika', letting a few direct words carry the whole meaning.
- 'I told you that right from the start.' Barinov reminds his daughter that he defended his young cook earlier.
- 'Denis really is a good guy.' Russians say 'by me' or 'with us' where English says 'I have' or 'we have', without needing a separate word for 'have'.
- 'Just spare me your male solidarity.' Starting with the everyday word for 'let us' is how Russian speakers warmly invite someone to do something together.
- 'All right, sweetheart, what do you think yourself?' Barinov softens his tone and addresses Katya affectionately as little daughter.
- 'What do you think, should I forgive him?' In everyday Russian, 'how / as' flows straight into 'to forgive; excuse me' without extra linking words.
- 'You know, I am a poor adviser for you right now.' Chef Barinov pairs 'know' with 'right' directly, since spoken Russian skips English filler words like 'the', 'a', and 'is'.
- 'My own life is a complete mess.' Russians say 'by me' or 'with us' where English says 'I have' or 'we have', without needing a separate word for 'have'.
- 'All right, I will not distract you, bye.' Katya says goodbye using a negated compound future verb and informal farewell.
