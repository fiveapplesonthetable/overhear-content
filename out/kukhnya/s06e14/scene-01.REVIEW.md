## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I wonder, are they Japanese or Chinese?' Talking about the man at once, Kostya uses "yapontsy" ("Japanese man", from "yaponets") to make the point stick.
- 'Can you really not tell them apart at all?' Turning boldly beside Kostya, Nastya adds "sovsem" ("completely, at all") so the statement leaves zero room for doubt.
- 'Of course, I tell them apart very well.' Kostya cuts in warmly facing Nastya, without trying to dress up how he really feels.
- 'It turns out these guests arrived from Korea.' Highlighting the visitors in front of Katya, Kostya puts the weight on "okazyvayetsya" ("to turn out to be", from "okazyvatsya") as the exchange unfolds.
- 'I need to calmly figure myself out.' Looking over briskly to Kostya, Katya leans on "nado" ("it is necessary, one needs to"), which carries the whole predicate in Russian without needing "it is".
- 'And what happened yesterday on the roof?' Nastya inquires plainly why things suddenly took this turn between them.
- 'Did something happen between you and Denis?' Reacting openly right here, Nastya uses "u tebya" (literally "by you") where English says "you have" or "your".
- 'Did you agree to marry Nikita?' Looking over naturally around Katya, the "-sya" ending on "soglasilas" ("to agree") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'I do not know, I am completely confused.' Katya chimes in calmly in this moment, without trying to dress up how she really feels.
- 'Hello, girls, what are you whispering about?' Focusing on the girl during the talk, Kostya brings in "devochki" ("girl's", from "devochka") as the key Russian word here.
- 'Hello, Kostya, we are just chatting.' Opening up promptly beside Kostya, Katya uses the little word "prosto" ("simply, just") to make the point sound effortless.
- 'Nastya, your new blouse suits you very well.' Katya jumps in quietly without holding back, keeping the focus on what actually matters here.
