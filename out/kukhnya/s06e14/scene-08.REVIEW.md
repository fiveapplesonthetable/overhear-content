## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello, you arrived right in time for lunch!' Between the moment and the meat, Senya delivers a sharp reaction. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
- 'Lyova, what is that you have on your head?' Wondering what that Barinov has on Barinov's head is cuts straight through the tension between Senya and Barinov. In Russian you say "by me" or "with us" rather than "I have", so the situation sits right with the person.
- 'This is a sign of respect for her culture.' Admitting that this is a sign of respect for her culture shows Lyova being honest for a change.
- 'Yeah right, as if I believed you.' With the conversation and respect in play, Senya keeps this short reply brisk and direct. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
- 'I cooked a delicious lunch for you today.' Over the conversation and the respect, Barinov answers Lyova without a second thought. Even in the middle of the drama, he keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
- 'And you wanted to bury this meat!' Because Lyova wanted to bury this meat, Barinov does not bother softening the blow for Lyova.
- 'Guys, what is going on with you here?' Wondering what going on with Barinov here is is how Lyova puts Barinov on the spot. Russian expresses having something as "by me is", keeping the phrase short and grounded.
- 'Nothing special, we are just watching.' Since nothing special they are just watching, Senya does not waste words on excuses.
- 'The chef cooked strange meat for our lunch.' Around the point and respect, Senya sets a calm, everyday tone with Barinov.
- 'Tell me, how are you even eating this?' With the conversation and respect hanging in the air, Senya puts Barinov on the spot. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
- 'In my opinion, this is very good fresh meat.' Because in his opinion this is very good fresh meat, Lyova treats the matter as settled. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'Eat faster before everything gets cold.' Barinov mentions that Lyova should eat faster before everything gets cold so Lyova sees what is at stake.
