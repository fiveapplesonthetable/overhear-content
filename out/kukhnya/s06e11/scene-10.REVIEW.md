## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Katya, meet Andrey Nikolaevich Dyagilev, my father.' Mentioning that Fedya should meet Andrey Nikolaevich Dyagilev his father moves the talk in a new direction.
- 'I am very pleased too, I have heard a lot about you.' Admitting that she is very pleased too she has heard a lot about Fedya shows Katya being honest for a change. Notice how she sticks to the formal "you", keeping a professional line between them.
- 'And you, as I understand it, work here as a chef?' Fedya asks whether Katya as he understands it work here as, refusing to let the point slide. Even in the middle of the drama, he keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
- 'Yes, Katya is in charge of molecular cuisine at our place.' Nikita mentions that Katya is in charge of molecular cuisine at their place so Fedya sees what is at stake.
- 'In her field she is simply a great professional.' Realizing that in her field she is simply a great professional changes how Nikita handles the situation.
- 'It would be wrong to step away from my workplace for long.' Noting that it would be wrong to step away from her workplace for long keeps Katya's side of the story simple and clear.
- 'So excuse me, please, I have to go work.' By having to go work, Katya keeps her side of the story grounded.
- 'He called me his girlfriend, that stresses me out.' Admitting that he called her his girlfriend that stresses her out shows Katya being honest for a change. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'Why are you throwing to the side, catch it right with your mouth!' Because why are Katya throwing to the side catch it right with Katya's mouth, Fedya does not bother softening the blow for Katya. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
- 'And can you catch two cherries at once like that?' Checking whether Fedya catches two cherries at once like is how Katya puts Fedya on the spot.
- 'Nobody in our kitchen has ever managed that yet.' Adding that nobody in their kitchen has ever managed that yet fills in the missing piece for Katya. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'Exactly, let us make a bet, I will catch them easily!' Declaring that exactly let them make a bet she will catch them easily brings Katya's temper right to the surface. Starting with "davay" is the everyday Russian way to say "come on, let us do this".
