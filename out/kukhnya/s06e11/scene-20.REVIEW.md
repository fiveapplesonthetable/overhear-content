## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Andrey, please meet my beloved daughter Katya.' Focusing on the daughter right here, Eleonora puts the weight on "lyubimaya" ("favourite, beloved", from "lyubimyy") as the exchange unfolds.
- 'Hello, Andrey Nikolaevich, I have heard very much about you.' Katya cuts in at once beside Eleonora, so Eleonora knows she is not just making small talk.
- 'Probably you have also heard a lot about me today.' Katya pushes back briskly around Eleonora and reaches for the Russian word "navernoye" ("probably") right at the heart of the line.
- 'Katya works as a chef right here in our restaurant.' Focusing on her restaurant with Katya, Eleonora reaches for "u nas" ("by us, at our place") to talk about what they share.
- 'In the kitchen she is in charge of molecular cuisine.' Eleonora speaks up right away toward Katya, testing Katya's reaction in the middle of the exchange.
- 'And she has a very big future ahead of her.' Eleonora reacts evenly at once and leans on "budushcheye" ("future, next", a form of "budushchiy") to give the remark its edge.
- 'Mom, do not exaggerate, I am just an ordinary chef.' Returning to the mum during the talk, Katya uses the little word "prosto" ("simply, just") to make the point sound effortless.
- 'Forgive me, Katya, I behaved very foolishly.' Nikita follows up promptly with a nod, shifting the mood of the conversation in a single beat.
- 'And understand me too, I panicked a bit today.' Nikita speaks openly before Eleonora and centers the thought on "zapanikoval" ("panicked", from "zapanikovat") without wasting a word.
- 'After all, you are the first girl Nikita has introduced me to.' Eleonora chimes in pointedly without holding back, so Nikita knows she is not just making small talk.
- 'I could not even imagine that everything was so serious.' Nikita speaks lightly with a nod, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'Forgive me once again and all the best to you!' Nikita replies dryly at once and brings in "dobrogo" ("good", from "dobryy") as the key Russian word here.
