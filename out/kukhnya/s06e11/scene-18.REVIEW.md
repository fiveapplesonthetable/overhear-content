## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Wait, have you not signed any papers yet?' Notice how Russian expresses 'wait' and 'to sign' with crisp inflection rather than helper words.
- 'You cannot do business with my brother.' Here the Russian phrasing highlights 'with' before landing on 'business'.
- 'It is a lost cause, all his plans end in failure.' In Russian, the idea of 'this' flows naturally into 'in failure' without extra filler words.
- 'Actually, your brother has a chain of oyster farms in America.' the guest anchors the line on 'actually', while 'of farms' completes the thought. Chef Barinov answers right away.
- 'Oh really, and then I have big vineyards!' Chef Barinov anchors the line on 'yes', while 'vineyards' completes the thought.
- 'I am speaking seriously, we have been cooperating for a year and a half already.' The meaning hinges on 'I' working together with 'we cooperate' in spoken Russian.
- 'Valya, why did you tell me nothing about this?' The '-l' ending on the verb meaning 'told' places the action in the masculine past tense. Mikhail answers right away.
- 'I tried this morning, but you did not listen to me.' The negative particle sits right before the verb meaning 'tried' to negate the statement.
- 'Good evening, excuse me, your door was unlocked.' the guest brings out the contrast between 'good' and 'evening' in a few short words.
- 'Oh, you drove me from the airport this morning!' Here the Russian phrasing highlights 'about' before landing on 'gave a ride'. the guest answers right away.
- 'Yes, this morning you forgot your suitcase in my car.' the guest anchors the line on 'yes', while 'forgot' completes the thought.
- 'Thank you, it is nice to know that such honest people remain!' The grammar packs 'thank you' and 'people' into a tight, idiomatic kitchen exchange.
