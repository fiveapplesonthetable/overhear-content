## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Come on, throw them quickly on the count of three, four!' Returning to the counting at once, Fedya starts with "davay" ("come on, let's") to invite Katya right into the action.
- 'Hooray, look everyone, we won fair and square today!' Katya speaks evenly hearing Fedya, leaving no doubt about where she stands.
- 'Katya, that was simply incredibly cool!' Fedya counters decisively in the exchange and reaches for the Russian word "neveroyatno" ("incredibly") right at the heart of the line.
- 'Hello, Katya, may I have literally two words with you?' Touching on the words during the talk, the guest leans on "mozhno" ("it is possible, one may"), which carries the whole predicate in Russian without needing "it is".
- 'Understand me, I have nothing against you.' The guest counters at once without a pause, leaving no doubt about where he stands.
- 'Surely a guy will be found who will be proud of you.' Talking about the boyfriend in front of Fedya, the guest puts the emphasis on "navernyaka" ("surely, definitely") in the middle of the talk.
- 'That his girlfriend knows how to catch two cherries with her mouth.' Emphasizing the girlfriend at once, the guest reaches for the Russian word "devushka" ("girlfriend") right at the heart of the line.
- 'But that guy will definitely not be my son.' The guest replies candidly in their talk, leaving no doubt about where he stands.
- 'It seems to me that Nikita should decide that himself.' Katya pushes back openly on the spot and uses "dolzhen" ("has to, must") to carry the main weight of the thought.
- 'No, you must break up with Nikita immediately.' The guest cuts in candidly with a smile, so Katya knows he is not just making small talk.
- 'And in your place I would keep quiet about our conversation.' The guest fires back lightly with a sigh, to clear the air between them once and for all.
- 'Otherwise I will very quickly ruin your whole life.' Pointing to the life before Katya, the guest brings in "isporchu" ("I'll spoil", from "isportit") as the key Russian word here.
- 'Tell me straight, was that a real threat just now?' Bringing up a real threat during the talk, Katya keeps normal statement word order here, turning the line into a question for the guest purely through rising pitch.
