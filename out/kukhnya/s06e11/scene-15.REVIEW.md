- 'Since she is staging such foolishness for your sake.' Senya favors one pointed choice in reply: "ustraivayet" means "to organize" (from "ustraivat"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'Nikita, tell me quickly, where is your father right now?' Senya wonders quietly what Sveta is actually getting at here.
- 'It seems to me he went to his restaurant recently.' Denis features one classic touch for Senya: "restoran" means "restaurant", and it carries the main weight of what Denis is saying.
- 'Denis, hi, does your face not hurt anymore?' Sveta sends that answer right away: Russian says "u tebya" (literally "at you" or "by you") where English uses "you have" or "your", keeping the phrasing close and informal.

- 'He sure is bothering me, he calls me constantly.' Sveta sharpens this direct expression on cue: "nadoyedayet" means "bothers, annoys" (from "nadoyedat"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'I am afraid to go home, what if he waits in ambush.' Sveta steps in briskly without any doubt, to clear the air between them once and for all.

- 'And thinks that I am hitting on you again!' Denis uses one colorful word in reply: "podumayet" means "to think" (from "podumat"), giving the line its exact edge.
