## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'So this is what this system looks like.' "Tak vot" is a pair of little words for "so here is". Inside "vyglyadit", "looks", you'll recognise "glyadet'", to look.
- 'I thought it was just a cash register.' "Ya dumal" is "I thought", and Russian leaves out "it was": "I thought, this just a till". "Kassa" is the cash register, and the till in any shop.
- 'Make two espressos, please.' "Sdelay" is the friendly "make". "Espresso" doesn't change after "dva", two, because borrowed words ending in -o never change. "Pozhaluysta" does the work of "please".
- 'What are you doing here?' Nastya says "tut", the casual "here", which is shorter than "zdes'". She's using the friendly "you" with Max.
- 'And I am now the new bartender.' A cheeky "a ya", "and I", begins it. "Teper'" is "now", in the sense of "from now on", and "novyy" is "new".
- 'Oh, stop making things up.' "Da ladno tebe" is "oh, come on", as Vika said to Max. "Vydumyvat'", "to make up", has "dumat'", to think, inside it: you think something out of nothing.
- 'I saw you in the kitchen.' The -a on "videla" shows Nastya is a woman. And Russian always says "na kukhne", "on the kitchen", where English says "in".
- 'You are a cook after all.' "Ty zhe povar" has no "are", just "you cook". The small word "zhe" brings in "after all".
- 'I have very many talents.' "U menya", "at me", is the "I have" pattern. After "mnogo", many, "talant" takes "-ov": "mnogo talantov".
- 'And at heart I am a bartender.' "V dushe" is "in the soul", from "dusha". Russians use it like English "deep down".
- 'Did Kostya already fix the machine?' "Pochinil" is "fixed". "Apparat" is any machine or gadget, and here it means the coffee machine.
- 'Of course, everything works wonderfully.' It's spelled with a "ch" but said "koneshno". And "vsyo" means "everything".
- 'Then make coffee faster.' Russians boil coffee, so the command is "vari", boil, from the verb "varit'". "Bystreye" is "faster".
- 'Right away. Everything will be ready.' "Siyu sekundu" is an old-fashioned "this very second", what a waiter says. "Gotovo" ends in -o to agree with "vsyo", everything.
