## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Vika says that you started a fight.' "Started" here is the word "arranged", the same family as the word for "get a job". "Fight" has an -u as the object.
- 'We were simply fooling around in the backyard.' "Fooled around" comes from the word for "fool". "Backyard" is "back" and "yard", with endings after "in".
- 'Show me the video of this fight of yours.' "Show" is the polite command. "This" and "of yours" both change to the "of" shape, so the whole phrase has the same ending.
- 'Is that really a fight? You did not even fight back!' "Razve" at the start means "really?" and carries doubt. "Fight" has "-sya" at the end, a little "self".
- 'So there is no reason to fire the cook yet.' "Not for what" is how Russian says "no reason". "Yet" is the word that also means "bye".
- 'Dmitry, thank you for the fair decision.' "Thank you" plus "for" is the frame from the first scene of the series. "Fair" and "decision" are both easy to spot.
- 'Listen to me everyone: forget about this video!' The chef addresses the whole room. "About this" uses "ob" because the next word starts with a vowel.
- 'Vika still has to work here as art director.' "To Vika still to work" is the Russian for "Vika still has to work". "As art director" has the "-om" ending for "as".
- 'Whoever dares laugh will immediately get out!' "Dares" is one word, and "laugh" has "-sya" at the end. "Will go out" finishes the threat.
- 'Every coin has two sides.' Russian says "medal", not "coin", for the two sides of a story. "Two" is feminine to match "side".
- 'One day everything around can change drastically.' "One day" is a single word, "odnazhdy". "Drastically" is built on the word for "sharp".
- 'A former enemy unexpectedly becomes your friend.' "Former" is built on "was". "Unexpectedly" has "expect" in it. And "becomes" is followed by "-im", the "as" ending.
- 'And a strict tyrant becomes a little bit kinder.' "Kinder" ends in "-eye", the same "more" ending you've heard on "faster". "A little" is one word.
- 'Life goes on, and tomorrow is a new day!' "Goes on" ends in "-sya", as if life continues itself. "Tomorrow" is a word you've heard before.
