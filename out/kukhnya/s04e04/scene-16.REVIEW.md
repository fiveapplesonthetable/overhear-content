## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Friends, I have gathered all of you here.' Having gathered all of Lyova here moves the conversation forward without extra drama.
- 'Let's talk. Come with me.' Proposing that they talk is how Max lays out the situation for German. Starting with "davay" is the everyday Russian way to say "come on, let us do this".
- 'And here is what I thought.' German explains that here is what he thought, keeping his voice calm and direct.
- 'All of you should work like Denis.' Realizing that all of Lyova should work like Denis changes how German handles the situation.
- 'Peeling potatoes by the sack?' Over the plan and the truth, Lyova presses German for a straight answer. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'No, more efficiently.' Between the room and the moment, German gives Lyova a quick, grounded response.
- 'If everyone works like Denis,.' Admitting that if everyone works like Denis shows German being honest for a change.
- 'The coefficient of useful efficiency will rise.' Saying that the coefficient of useful efficiency will rise is how German sets the tone with Lyova.
- 'We will not need such a large number of cooks.' Once they will not need such a large number of cooks, German can finally tell Lyova what he means.
- 'You understand, right? You are fired.' Wondering if Lyova understands right shows German caught off guard.
- 'What are you doing?' By wondering what German doing is, Lyova presses German for a straight answer. Even in the middle of the drama, he keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
- 'What is it? Is there something you do not like?' Asking what it is cuts straight through the tension between German and Lyova. Notice how he sticks to the formal "you", keeping a professional line between them.
