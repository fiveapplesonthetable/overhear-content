## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Excuse me, please, what is this?' German casually delivers this formal apology at the start of the beat using "prostite" ("forgive me") — a warmer, more earnest polite apology that literally asks someone to forgive you.
- 'Astonishing. Did you make this yourself? Out of a carrot?' German challenges wryly how Denis expects that to work in practice.
- 'Yes. Thank you.' Denis naturally drops this word of thanks while facing German using "spasibo" ("thank you") — the everyday word for "thank you", which grew out of the old blessing "spasi Bog" ("God save you").
- 'You peeled all the carrots and even made a flute. Well done.' Underscoring the carrot in this beat, German uses "morkovku" ("carrot", from "morkovka") to make the point stick.
- 'It will not be much trouble for you to peel a sack of potatoes.' German replies warmly toward Denis, so Denis knows he is not just making small talk.
- 'Better make it two for you. Two sacks. Will you manage by evening?' Mentioning the evening in the exchange, German lands on "vechera" ("evening", built on "vecher") to keep the statement grounded.
- 'Chef, I won't finish in time by evening.' Pointing to the evening in the exchange, Denis chooses "vechera" ("evening", from the root "vecher") so German hears the exact meaning.
- 'You will finish in time. You are very talented.' German answers lightly without holding back, making his position completely clear.
- 'And if you don't finish in time, out you go.' German continues candidly around Lyova and chooses "uspeyete" ("to make it", from the root "uspet") so Lyova hears the exact meaning.
- 'German Mikhailovich, why do we need so many potatoes?' Discussing the potatoes before German, Lyova leans on "nuzhno" ("it is needed, one has to"), which carries the whole predicate in Russian without needing "it is".
- 'For educational purposes. I will not tolerate slacking.' German chimes in steadily across the room, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'I do not like this either.' Lyova speaks up dryly in this beat and lands on "nravitsya" ("is pleasing") as the key everyday word here.
