## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'You cannot treat people like that.' Claiming that German cannot treat people like sounds confident, even if German has doubts. There is no "you" needed in the Russian here: one short word states the rule for everyone in the room.
- 'Fine, I am not holding you back.' Pointing out that fine he is not holding Lyova back gives Lyova a sobering reality check. Even in the middle of the drama, he keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
- 'You may follow right behind them.' German explains that Lyova may follow right behind them, keeping his voice calm and direct.
- 'And I will leave, make no mistake.' Realizing that he will leave make no mistake changes how Lyova handles the situation.
- 'I do not intend to tolerate this.' Noting that he does not intend to tolerate this keeps Lyova's side of the story simple and clear. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'Perhaps there are others who wish to join them?' German asks whether perhaps there are others who wish to join them, refusing to let the point slide.
- 'In that case, colleagues, back to work.' Between the room and the truth, German gives Lyova a quick, grounded response.
- 'And what should we do with the potatoes?' By wondering what they do with the potatoes should, Lyova presses German for a straight answer. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'I don't care, you can even throw them out.' Once he does not care Lyova can even throw them out, German can finally tell Lyova what he means.
- 'Viktoriya Sergeyevna, German Mikhailovich wants to speak with you.' Adding that Viktoriya Sergeyevna German Mikhailovich wants to speak with German fills in the missing piece for German. Even in the middle of the drama, he keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
- 'Alright, thank you. And where is he?' Over the situation and the shift, Vika presses German for a straight answer. In Russian, she does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'He said he will be here in a couple of minutes and asked you to wait.' Since he said he will be here in a couple of minutes and asked German to wait, Lyova does not waste words on excuses.
