## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Well, what does it say?' Turning warmly with Vika, Max puts the question word "chto" right at the start so Vika hears the focus immediately.
- 'Only one line again.' Vika weighs in steadily with Max, steering the talk right where she wants it.
- 'Phew, thank goodness.' Emphasizing the glory in front of Vika, Max uses "slava" ("glory") to carry the main weight of the thought.
- 'Oops, sorry, out of habit.' Max directly delivers this informal apology as they meet using "izvini" ("sorry") — the casual singular apology you use with someone you know well.
- 'Nothing terrible. Judging by the tests, you are healthy.' The doctor reacts warmly with a nod, so Max knows he is not just making small talk.
- 'Over the next two days I advise you to work really hard.' The doctor answers openly during the talk and brings in "blizhayshiye" ("next, coming", from "blizhayshiy") as the key Russian word here.
- 'Excuse me, and how often should we try?' Weighing in quietly around the doctor, Vika puts the question word "kak" right at the start so the doctor hears the focus immediately.
- 'Well, that depends on you.' The doctor responds dryly hearing Vika, before Vika can change the subject.
- 'Some people can manage seven times.' The doctor weighs in plainly without holding back, shifting the mood of the conversation in a single beat.
- 'Well, seven it is then.' Opening up openly to the doctor, Vika links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
- 'Seven times?' Max quizzes candidly before the doctor can change the subject.
- 'I mean over the course of two days.' Focusing on the view in this beat, the doctor brings in "imeyetsya" ("is available", from "imetsya") as the key Russian word here.
