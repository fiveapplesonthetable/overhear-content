## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Lena, well how did you like today's relaxation?' The idiom 'how is it to you' asks for someone's impression.
- 'Excellent, I had already forgotten how relaxing it is!' Feminine past-tense verb marks Elena as the speaker.
- 'I am very glad that you liked everything.' Notice how Russian puts 'very, really' and 'pleased, was liked' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'But that is far from the whole surprise.' Negated determiner 'all/the whole' builds suspense for the second part of his plan.
- 'I booked a table for us for seven o'clock.' In everyday Russian, 'ordered' flows straight into 'hours' without extra linking words.
- 'Vitya, tell me honestly, what is going on today?' Elena is intrigued by Barinov's sudden romantic gestures.
- 'Lena, believe me, I really need to talk with you.' Notice how Russian puts 'believe, trust' and 'to talk' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'And it is more convenient to talk in a calm evening setting.' Here Chef Barinov anchors the thought on 'more comfortable' and finishes on 'setting', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'Well all right, but after all we have work right now.' Elena hesitates to leave her restaurant during the dinner rush.
- 'Do not worry, Lyova will supervise everything here himself.' Chef Barinov pairs 'worry' with 'to keep an eye on, to check, to supervise' directly, since spoken Russian skips English filler words like 'the', 'a', and 'is'.
- 'Vitya, are you definitely sure about that?' In spoken Russian, the words stay in their normal statement order and Elena's rising voice at the end turns the line into a question.
- 'Yes, Elena Pavlovna, I will supervise everything!' Lyova loyally backs up Barinov's scheme so they can leave.
