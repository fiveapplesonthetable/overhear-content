- 'Volodya, pal, what brings you here?' In spoken Russian, the words stay in their normal statement order and Chef Barinov's rising voice at the end turns the line into a question.

- 'And people told me that you were fired from here.' Impersonal third-person plural past expresses rumor or hearsay.

- 'All of Moscow is chasing after me right now!' In everyday Russian, 'right now' flows straight into 'to right' without extra linking words.

- 'And where are you working as head chef now?' Notice how Russian puts 'where' and 'chef' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'Right over here, I am in charge at the neighboring restaurant.' Here Chef Barinov anchors the thought on 'neighboring' and finishes on 'run', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'It is a very beautiful and fashionable place.' Neuter singular adjectives agree with the noun for place.
