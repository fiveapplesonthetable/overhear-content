## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Lyova, my guests will come for lunch today.' Notice how Russian puts 'today' and 'guests' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'They will definitely ask for the head chef at their table.' Here Chef Barinov anchors the thought on 'definitely' and finishes on 'chef', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'Make sure that I find out about this first.' In everyday Russian, 'make' flows straight into 'first' without extra linking words.
- 'Vitya, where do such generous gifts suddenly come from?' Elena admires the spa voucher with a question adverb of origin.
- 'You did so much good for me recently.' Notice how Russian puts 'so many' and 'took, made' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'Can I also repay you with kindness?' In spoken Russian, the words stay in their normal statement order and Chef Barinov's rising voice at the end turns the line into a question.
- 'Wait, is this session scheduled for today already?' Elena notices the time printed on the spa appointment card.
- 'And does it start in just half an hour?' In spoken Russian, the words stay in their normal statement order and Elena's rising voice at the end turns the line into a question.
- 'Yes, so why are you still sitting here?' Barinov hurries her out the door before his friend arrives.
- 'Come on, go quickly, and I will cover here.' Starting with the everyday word for 'let us' is how Russian speakers warmly invite someone to do something together.
- 'After all, you will only be away during lunch.' Putting the short word for 'not' right in front of the action word is all Russian needs to make a sentence negative, with no extra helper words like 'do' or 'did'.
- 'Well all right, thank you, it is just very unexpected.' Elena leaves happily for the spa, leaving the kitchen to Barinov.
