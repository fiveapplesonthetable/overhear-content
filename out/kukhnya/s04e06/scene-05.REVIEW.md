## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Lyova, my guests will come for lunch today.' Preposition 'for' with the accusative noun 'lunch' marks the mealtime.
- 'They will definitely ask for the head chef at their table.' Preposition 'to/toward' governs the dative singular of 'table'.
- 'Make sure that I find out about this first.' Instrumental adjective 'first' marks being the first person to learn the news.
- 'Vitya, where do such generous gifts suddenly come from?' Elena admires the spa voucher with a question adverb of origin.
- 'You did so much good for me recently.' Neuter genitive adjective 'good' follows the quantifier 'so much'.
- 'Can I also repay you with kindness?' The verb for repaying takes a dative recipient and instrumental noun 'with good'.
- 'Wait, is this session scheduled for today already?' Elena notices the time printed on the spa appointment card.
- 'And does it start in just half an hour?' Preposition 'in/after' governs the accusative of the compound noun for half an hour.
- 'Yes, so why are you still sitting here?' Barinov hurries her out the door before his friend arrives.
- 'Come on, go quickly, and I will cover here.' Prefixed perfective future verb means backing someone up or covering for them.
- 'After all, you will only be away during lunch.' Impersonal negated future 'will not be' takes the genitive pronoun 'of you'.
- 'Well all right, thank you, it is just very unexpected.' Elena leaves happily for the spa, leaving the kitchen to Barinov.
