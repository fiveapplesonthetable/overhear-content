## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Yes, Volodya, hi, I am already waiting for you!' Present-tense verb 'waiting' governs the accusative pronoun.
- 'Vitya, we will not be able to drop by for lunch.' Impersonal reflexive future verb expresses whether a plan will work out.
- 'Our plane is in the evening, and there is still much to do.' Genitive plural 'of matters' follows the quantity adverb.
- 'How can you let me down like that, folks?' Barinov pretends to be disappointed that they are missing lunch.
- 'You have just upset me very much right now.' Perfective past plural verb describes causing disappointment.
- 'Please forgive me, my old pal.' Singular perfective imperative asks for pardon between close friends.
- 'I tried so hard to surprise you deliciously today.' Barinov lays the guilt on thick, causing his friend to reconsider.
- 'All right, then we will stop by your place for dinner!' To Barinov's horror, Volodya switches the visit to dinnertime.
- 'We will drop in right before our evening flight.' Preposition 'before' governs the instrumental singular of 'flight'.
- 'Wait, you want to come in the evening for dinner?' Barinov realizes he wasted his spa gift on the wrong shift.
- 'Yes, in the evening we will definitely be at your place!' Instrumental singular 'in the evening' marks the time of day.
- 'No problem, Volodya, I will wait for you in the evening.' Forced to agree, Barinov now needs a new plan for tonight.
