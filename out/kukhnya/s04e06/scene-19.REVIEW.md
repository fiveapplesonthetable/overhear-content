## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Vitya, I do not understand you at all today.' Elena confronts Barinov calmly after catching his act.
- 'For what sake did you stage this whole performance?' Preposition 'for the sake of' governs the genitive of 'what'.
- 'Just to flatter your own pride?' Purpose conjunction 'in order to' takes an infinitive.
- 'And what was I supposed to do with him?' Masculine short adjective 'obliged' with past 'was' means 'should have'.
- 'You should have simply told him the truth.' Feminine accusative 'truth' is the direct object of 'tell'.
- 'Say that I am just a regular cook here now?' Barinov admits how painful it is for a fifty-year-old master chef.
- 'Yes, at least tell that simple truth.' Particle phrase 'at least' urges honesty over pretension.
- 'Fine, I will go right now and tell him everything!' Stung, Barinov marches out to Volodya's table to confess.
- 'Vitya, I see things are going well for you!' The idiom 'affairs go well' praises Barinov's apparent success.
- 'Volodya, that is just what I wanted to tell you about.' Demonstrative prepositional phrase focuses his friend's attention.
- 'In fact I am not the head chef here at all...' Barinov begins his confession with 'in actual fact'.
- 'Chef, excuse me, you accidentally dropped your handkerchief in the kitchen.' Seeing his courage, Elena steps in and addresses him as 'Chef'.
- 'Thank you very much, Elena Pavlovna.' Barinov thanks her with quiet gratitude for saving his dignity.
