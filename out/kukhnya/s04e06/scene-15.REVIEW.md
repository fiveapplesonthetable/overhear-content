## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Somehow I do not recognize you at all today, Vitya.' Putting the short word for 'not' right in front of the action word is all Russian needs to make a sentence negative, with no extra helper words like 'do' or 'did'.
- 'First a gift, and now an evening restaurant.' Temporal adverbs 'at first' and 'now' structure the contrast.
- 'Lena, excuse me, I need to step away one more time.' Russian expresses feelings and needs by saying 'to me it is needed' or 'to me it seems', putting the person in the 'to me' form.
- 'Vitya, if your stomach hurts, let's leave.' Concerned for his health, Elena offers to go home.
- 'No, I will return in literally a couple of minutes!' Keep Elena seated at Claude Monet is essential to his plan.
- 'We waited so long, and there will be no dessert today?' In spoken Russian, the words stay in their normal statement order and Elena's rising voice at the end turns the line into a question.
- 'Excuse us, let's prepare something else for you.' Vika steps in smoothly to apologize for Denis's burned pastry.
- 'It will be very fast and on the house.' The idiom 'at our expense' means complimentary or on the house.
- 'Good, if it is really fast, then let's do it.' Barinov agrees so he can finish his ruse next door.
- 'We will serve you our signature blueberry sorbet.' Relational adjective 'blueberry' modifies the masculine loanword 'sorbet'.
- 'Denis, make a quick blueberry sorbet for the guests!' Back in the kitchen, Vika hands Denis his next challenge.
- 'Louis always prepares it right in front of the guests.' Here Vika anchors the thought on 'always' and finishes on 'guests', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
