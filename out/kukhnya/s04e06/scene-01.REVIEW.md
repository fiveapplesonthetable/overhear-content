## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'A woman always had to maintain order in the house.' Highlighting a woman beside Vika, Max uses "zhenshchina" ("woman") to carry the main weight of the thought.
- 'And a man had to guard this house.' Addressing a man during the talk, Max lands on "muzhchina" ("man") as the key everyday word here.
- 'Since then almost nothing has changed.' Max counters bluntly with a shrug, keeping the focus on what actually matters here.
- 'Max, who is that arriving so late?' Vika continues vividly at once and lands on "prishyol" ("came, have come", built on "priyti") to keep the statement grounded.
- 'That is a courier from the store.' Bringing up the store at once, Max leans on "magazina" ("store", a form of "magazin") to give the remark its edge.
- 'A comfortable grip and a very good camera!' Denis speaks calmly with a smile, testing Max's reaction in the middle of the exchange.
- 'Max, tell me, what do you need this rifle for?' Pointing to the gun with Max, Vika chooses "ruzhyo" ("a gun, rifle") so Max gets the point right away.
- 'What are you, still a tiny child?' Underscoring how short things are right here, Vika adds "sovsem" ("completely, at all") so the statement leaves zero room for doubt.
- 'Do not call me a little child.' Max steps in frankly without any doubt, shifting the mood of the conversation in a single beat.
- 'I am an adult and I decide everything myself.' Max weighs in promptly in front of Vika and reaches for the Russian word "vzroslyy" ("grown-up, adult") right at the heart of the line.
- 'Vika, could you chip in a little?' Pushing back readily to Vika, Max keeps normal statement word order here, turning the line into a question for Vika purely through rising pitch.
- 'Otherwise I do not have quite enough money.' Max follows up pointedly without holding back, steering the talk right where he wants it.
