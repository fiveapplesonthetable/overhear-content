## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Max, tell me, what happened again?' Vika quizzes bluntly whether Max really thought this through.
- 'Listen, Vika, just do not get too angry.' Max speaks naturally with Vika and uses "slushay" ("listen", from "slushat") to make the point stick.
- 'Do you remember how we made up last night?' Weighing in naturally with Vika, the "-sya" ending on "pomirilis" ("reconciled") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'You and I drank tasty wine back then.' Max counters steadily watching Vika, leaving no doubt about where he stands.
- 'Well then, I took it from the bar.' Bringing up the bar right here, Max centers the remark on "bara" ("of the bar") without any extra fuss.
- 'And it suddenly turned out to be very expensive.' Max jumps in vividly beside Vika and lands on "dorogim" ("expensive, dear", built on "dorogoy") to keep the statement grounded.
- 'And just how much does this bottle of wine cost?' Vika checks evenly before Max can change the subject.
- 'It costs a whole five thousand dollars.' Underscoring the dollars in front of Vika, Max leans on "dollarov" ("dollars", a form of "dollar") to give the remark its edge.
- 'Vika, do not worry, I will solve everything.' Leaning in briskly to Vika, the "-sya" ending on "volnuysya" ("worry") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'I know well how this usually ends.' Vika jumps in boldly watching Max, making her position completely clear.
- 'I have to clean up your problems myself afterwards!' Discussing the problems with Max, Vika centers the remark on "prikhoditsya" ("to have to") without any extra fuss.
- 'Stop lecturing me, I will fix everything myself!' Max weighs in earnestly on the spot and reaches for the Russian word "otchityvat" ("to fix") right at the heart of the line.
