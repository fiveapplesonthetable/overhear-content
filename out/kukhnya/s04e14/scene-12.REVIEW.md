## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'German Mikhailovich, and what should we do right now?' Senya asks for permission to leave using a dative subject and infinitive.
- 'The restaurant is closed, maybe we will go home?' The short passive participle explains why they want to head home.
- 'Clean up your workstations and go.' At first German tells them to leave, then notices their festive suits.
- 'Although wait, just one moment, stop!' He changes his mind using two plural imperatives.
- 'Tonight I will need two cooks.' The numeral two governs the genitive singular form of cook.
- 'You will stay here in the kitchen until ten.' A second-person plural future verb sets a late evening shift.
- 'But why do I in particular have to stay?' The protest only makes German extend the punishment.
- 'Until eleven then, are there any more questions?' German raises the hour using the preposition until plus genitive.
- 'And I also need two waiters.' The plural short adjective for needed agrees with the two waiters.
- 'Ekaterina, I already said everyone is free to go.' German notices Katya still working at her prep table.
- 'I need to calmly finish one task.' Katya replies with the modal predicate and a perfective infinitive.
- 'All right, see for yourself, just do not take long.' German leaves her in the kitchen and heads to his office.
