## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'German Mikhailovich, and what should we do right now?' Senya wonders calmly while looking straight at German here.
- 'The restaurant is closed, maybe we will go home?' Bringing up the restaurant in front of German, Senya centers the remark on "restoran" ("restaurant") without any extra fuss.
- 'Clean up your workstations and go.' Discussing the places during the talk, German chooses "rabochiye" ("workers", from the root "rabochiy") so Senya hears the exact meaning.
- 'Although wait, just one moment, stop!' German counters dryly without any doubt, without trying to dress up how he really feels.
- 'Tonight I will need two cooks.' Mentioning the cook around Senya, German leans on "ponadobyatsya" ("to be needed", a form of "ponadobitsya") to give the remark its edge.
- 'You will stay here in the kitchen until ten.' Mentioning the kitchen during the talk, the "-sya" ending on "ostanetes" ("to remain, to be left") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'But why do I in particular have to stay?' Senya prompts calmly how German expects that to work in practice.
- 'Until eleven then, are there any more questions?' Touching on the questions during the talk, German chooses "odinnadtsati" ("to then") so Senya gets the point right away.
- 'And I also need two waiters.' Bringing up the waiter at once, German brings in "ofitsianta" ("waiter", from "ofitsiant") as the key Russian word here.
- 'Ekaterina, I already said everyone is free to go.' German fires back dryly with a sigh, so Katya knows he is not just making small talk.
- 'I need to calmly finish one task.' Mentioning the matter before German, Katya chooses "zakonchit" ("to finish") so German gets the point right away.
- 'All right, see for yourself, just do not take long.' German responds decisively in the exchange and lands on "khorosho" ("good, fine") as the key everyday word here.
