- 'They bloomed in order to lift your mood.' A purpose clause with an infinitive explains why the flowers bloomed.
- 'Thank you, of course, for the kind words.' Vika responds politely while keeping her emotional guard up.
- 'Believe me, I have nothing to do with it at all.' German uses a formal plural imperative and an idiomatic prepositional phrase.
- 'These lovely flowers chose you themselves.' The plural emphatic pronoun reinforces the personified subject.
- 'I really appreciate your kind attention.' The first-person singular present verb governs a neuter accusative object.
- 'But I will not take this bouquet.' Vika places the direct object first for contrast before the negated perfective future verb.
- 'Excuse me, it is already time for me to go.' The predicative noun for time to go governs a dative pronoun and an infinitive.
- 'Ainura, take these, please, this is for you now.' Left holding the rejected bouquet, German hands it to Ainura with a dative pronoun.
- 'Oh, thank you very much, German Mikhailovich!' Ainura blushes and accepts the flowers with delight.
- 'What do you need here, go to work!' Embarrassed that the cooks saw him get rejected, German barks at them to get busy.
