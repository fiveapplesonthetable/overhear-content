## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Friends, all of you know our wonderful occasion.' Barinov keeps his wording artfully vague so both groups of guests nod along.
- 'Let's all drink to it together right now!' Starting with the everyday word for 'let us' is how Russian speakers warmly invite someone to do something together.
- 'That strange mustachioed host is speaking again.' A charity guest whispers near Senya using a third-person singular verb.
- 'Wait, what did you just say about him?' Overhearing the insult, Senya steps up to defend his beloved chef.
- 'You came to a man's birthday party!' Notice how Russian puts 'came' and 'birthday, birth' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'You are drinking here at his personal expense!' Here Senya anchors the thought on 'are drinking' and finishes on 'score', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'Whose party did I come to?' The wealthy donor stares at Senya in disbelief.
- 'I gave huge money for the protection of leopards!' the guest pairs 'to give' with 'leopards' directly, since spoken Russian skips English filler words like 'the', 'a', and 'is'.
- 'Who are you to insult me here?' Spoken Russian keeps this brief and expressive, built right around 'to insult' without extra helper words.
- 'I am the best friend of our birthday boy, that is who!' Here Senya anchors the thought on 'best' and finishes on 'friend', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'Guys, it seems it is time for us to leave here quickly!' Seeing the angry charity crowd, Barinov signals an immediate retreat.
- 'Wait, where did you all run off to?' The donors watch in bewilderment as Barinov's group bolts for the exit.
