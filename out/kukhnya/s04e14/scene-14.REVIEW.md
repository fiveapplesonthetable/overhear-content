## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Excuse me, please, may I have a word for a minute?' A well-dressed guest politely stops Senya near the buffet table.
- 'Yes, of course, I am listening to you closely.' Senya answers smoothly using a first-person singular present verb.
- 'Do you happen to know where to leave money here?' the guest pairs 'know' with 'money' directly, since spoken Russian skips English filler words like 'the', 'a', and 'is'.
- 'In principle, you can hand the envelope to me.' Notice how Russian puts 'principle' and 'the envelope' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'I will definitely pass it on to the right person.' Here Senya anchors the thought on 'definitely' and finishes on 'need to, must', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'Forgive me for my indiscretion.' the guest uses a polite everyday Russian courtesy expression that sets the tone of the conversation right away.
- 'And how much did you put in yourself today?' Curious about standard contributions, the guest asks Senya in the plural past.
- 'I am here for the first time, I put in only a hundred.' The guest modestly mentions a hundred, leaving the unit unspoken.
- 'And I put in a whole five hundred today!' Thinking of his five-hundred-ruble gift share, Senya boasts proudly.
- 'Whoa, you are a very generous person!' In everyday Russian, 'very, really' flows straight into 'man' without extra linking words.
- 'And most importantly, you are a very noble guest.' The parenthetical word for main thing introduces a second compliment.
- 'After all, five hundred thousand is a serious sum!' The guest reveals he meant thousands, leaving Senya wide-eyed.
