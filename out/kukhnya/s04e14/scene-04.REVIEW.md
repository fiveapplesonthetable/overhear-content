## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Senya, what are you drinking over there right now?' Fedya continues earnestly around Senya and lands on "pyosh" ("drink") as the key everyday word here.
- 'I am drinking warm chamomile here for my throat.' Noting how warm he is right here, Senya leans on "tyopluyu" ("warm", a form of "tyoplyy") to give the remark its edge.
- 'Guys, someone stole the blue cheese!' Katya counters at once toward Senya, steering the talk right where she wants it.
- 'Just this morning I saw it in the refrigerator.' Mentioning the fridge around Senya, Katya chooses "kholodilnike" ("fridge", from the root "kholodilnik") so Senya hears the exact meaning.
- 'What scoundrels, they have completely lost their conscience!' Bringing up the scoundrels in front of Katya, Senya adds "sovsem" ("completely, at all") so the statement leaves zero room for doubt.
- 'Do you know what a shortage we have?' Katya checks earnestly while looking straight at Senya here.
- 'Give me some chamomile, I strained my throat.' Underscoring the chamomile to Senya, Katya leans on "romashki" ("chamomile") to give the whole sentence its punch.
- 'Sorry, Katya, there is almost none left in the cup.' Mentioning the cup in the exchange, Senya lands on "chashke" ("cup", built on "chashka") to keep the statement grounded.
- 'You had better pay attention to our newcomer.' Senya replies dryly with a smile, to clear the air between them once and for all.
- 'Before his arrival there was no such shortage.' Focusing on the shortage in the exchange, Senya brings in "nedostachi" ("shortage") to make the meaning unmistakable.
- 'As soon as he appeared, groceries immediately started disappearing!' Focusing on the groceries with Katya, Senya chooses "produkty" ("groceries", from the root "produkt") so Katya hears the exact meaning.
- 'Thank you, Senya, I will keep an eye on him.' Katya steps in calmly facing Senya, without trying to dress up how she really feels.
