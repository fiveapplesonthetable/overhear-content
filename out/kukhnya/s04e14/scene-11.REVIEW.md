## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Simply amazing people have gathered here today!' On stage, the charity host praises the crowd with a plural reflexive past verb.
- 'We are glad to welcome you at the charity auction.' The short plural adjective for glad governs an imperfective infinitive.
- 'Lyova, that rat closed Monet on purpose!' Barinov vents to Lyova about German's dirty trick.
- 'In forty minutes my guests will arrive here.' An accusative time phrase with through/in sets the urgent countdown.
- 'Where am I supposed to put all my friends now?' An infinitive question with a dative subject expresses a dilemma.
- 'And what if we celebrate the holiday right here?' Barinov's eyes light up as he surveys the festive dining room.
- 'What do you mean here, there is someone else's event here!' Lyova protests using a neuter nominative noun phrase.
- 'Look, all the people are having fun, drinking, and eating.' Three third-person plural present verbs describe the lively party atmosphere.
- 'One can drink to leopards, or one can drink to me!' In Russian, drinking a toast uses the preposition for plus the accusative case.
- 'Lyova, quickly warn the kitchen and the bar.' Barinov issues a singular perfective imperative to his loyal sous-chef.
- 'After you, the lottery tickets are in the storeroom.' Barinov lures the unsuspecting charity organizer into a side closet.
- 'Hey, open the door, this is not funny at all!' Locked inside, the host pounds on the door using a plural imperative.
