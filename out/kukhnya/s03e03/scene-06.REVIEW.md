## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'And what is this person doing here?' Elena prompts sharply how Barinov expects that to work in practice.
- 'She works here. As a cook.' Discussing a cook on the spot, Barinov reaches for the Russian word "povarom" ("as a cook", built from "povar") right at the center of the line.
- 'A female cook in your kitchen?' Checking on a female cook during the talk, Elena uses "u tebya" (literally "by you") where English says "you have" or "your".
- 'Well, Nagiyev forced her on me, she is a molecular cuisine specialist.' Barinov pushes back frankly without a pause, before Elena can change the subject.
- 'And why did you actually drop by?' Barinov chimes in briskly in this beat and chooses "zashla" ("dropped in", from the root "zayti") so Elena hears the exact meaning.
- 'I need a car for a couple of hours.' Turning to a car before Barinov, Elena puts the emphasis on "mashina" ("a car") in the middle of the talk.
- 'Will you lend me yours?' Elena checks calmly if there is more going on behind the scenes.
- 'For heaven's sake, be careful.' Barinov proceeds earnestly at once and uses "akkuratneye" ("more carefully", from "akkuratno") to make the point stick.
- 'Who are you worrying about right now?' Speaking briskly to Barinov, Elena keeps normal statement word order here, turning the line into a question for Barinov purely through rising pitch.
- 'About the car or about me?' Elena asks skeptically whether Barinov really thought this through.
- 'Both of you are dear to me in different ways.' Insisting on how dear Elena is at once, Barinov uses "dorogiye" ("dear", from "dorogoy") to make the point stick.
- 'Thank you, Vitya.' Elena lightly drops this word of thanks while facing Barinov using "spasibo" ("thank you") — the everyday word for "thank you", which grew out of the old blessing "spasi Bog" ("God save you").
