## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Cutting it wrong? Well, show me how.' Replying naturally on the spot, Katya puts the question word "kak" right at the start so Fedya hears the focus immediately.
- 'Do you think she likes that we are cutting her?' Fedya asks sharply why things suddenly took this turn between them.
- 'I don't know about the flounder, but I find it very pleasant.' Pointing to the flounder beside Fedya, Katya leans on "kambaly" ("flounder", a form of "kambala") to give the remark its edge.
- 'And I can teach you how to cut meat properly.' Fedya counters boldly in their talk, refusing to let the moment slip past.
- 'Any fool can cut meat.' Fedya steps in right away around Katya, leaving no doubt about where he stands.
- 'He cannot do anything at all except fish.' Turning to the fish right here, Fedya brings in "voobshche" ("at all") to make the meaning unmistakable.
- 'Yekaterina, come into my office.' Reacting boldly with Fedya, spoken Russian compresses the whole thought into just 4 words, skipping the extra helper words English needs.
- 'What kind of explosion did you stage in my kitchen?' Barinov checks deadpan how Katya expects that to work in practice.
- 'What explosion? I am simply blending into the new team.' Pointing to the staff in the exchange, Katya lands on "kollektiv" ("staff, collective") as the key everyday word here.
- 'And can you not lecture me? I haven't been fifteen for a long time.' Looking over warmly with Barinov, Katya leans on "mozhno" ("it is possible, one may"), which carries the whole predicate in Russian without needing "it is".
- 'In my kitchen you will always be fifteen.' Barinov cuts in steadily with a smile, watching Katya's face to see how the news lands.
- 'I forbid you! Where are you going?' Barinov proceeds sharply around Katya and lands on "zapreshchayu" ("forbid", built on "zapreshchat") to keep the statement grounded.
