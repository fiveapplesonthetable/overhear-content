## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'What happened, Vitya?' Turning from the wedding to the kitchen, Mikhail asks Barinov directly. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'The prefect did not eat his fill.' Barinov explains that the prefect did not eat his fill, keeping his voice calm and direct.
- 'Misha, forgive me for not saying anything to Eleonora back then.' Over the work and the menu, Barinov answers Kostya without a second thought. Russian stacks "never" or "nothing" together with "not", doubling the refusal for extra weight.
- 'Understand, for her it is important.' Noting that Kostya should understand for her it is important keeps Barinov's side of the story simple and clear. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
- 'No problem at all, Vitya.' Around the work and menu, Mikhail sets a calm, everyday tone with Barinov.
- 'To the new menu and to the star!' With the shift and menu at stake, Barinov reacts without holding back. Russian puts the key word right up front so his emotion lands on the first beat.
- 'Katya is already drinking her third glass.' Saying that Katya is already drinking her third glass is how Kostya sets the tone with Barinov.
- 'In my opinion, it is time to go.' Once in his opinion it is time to go, Kostya can finally tell Barinov what he means.
- 'Go ahead, good luck.' Claiming that Barinov should go ahead good luck sounds confident, even if Barinov has doubts. Starting with "davay" is the everyday Russian way to say "come on, let us do this".
- 'Well, do you think it is time to talk to Katya already?' By asking whether Barinov thinks it is time to talk to Katya already, Kostya presses Barinov for a straight answer. Russian packs the whole idea of what is allowed or needed into one impersonal word at the front.
- 'Yes, and where is the wine?' Wondering where the wine is cuts straight through the tension between Mikhail and Barinov.
- 'No question, I will pour right now.' Mentioning that question he will pour right now moves the talk in a new direction.
