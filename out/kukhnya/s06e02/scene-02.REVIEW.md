## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'What is the occasion?' Bringing up the reason on the spot, Elena links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
- 'I have very important news.' Barinov jumps in right away with a smile, before Elena can change the subject.
- 'They offered me a new job.' Mentioning a new job during the talk, Barinov reaches for the Russian word "predlozhili" ("offered", built from "predlozhit") right at the center of the line.
- 'I did not tell you, I wanted to make a surprise.' Elena counters decisively on the spot, bringing things back to earth after the buildup.
- 'In two days I have a meeting with readers.' Mentioning the readers around Barinov, Elena lets Barinov see exactly where things stand.
- 'Sorry, a new job?' Elena warmly extends this personal apology as they meet using "prosti" ("forgive me") — the close, informal way to say "forgive me" or "I'm sorry" to someone dear.
- 'Yes, but I decided to turn it down.' Pressing on candidly in front of Elena, the "-sya" ending on "otkazatsya" ("to refuse, decline") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'I am going to fight for the star.' Barinov counters calmly beside Elena, so Elena knows he is not just making small talk.
- 'Right now I am going to introduce a new auteur menu.' Describing how auteur he is during the talk, Barinov reaches for the Russian word "avtorskoye" ("auteur, signature", built from "avtorskiy") right at the center of the line.
- 'That is wonderful, you will be the first chef in Russia.' Elena steps in dryly hearing Barinov, leaving no doubt about where she stands.
- 'And you wrote a culinary book.' Barinov chimes in openly without a pause, testing Elena's reaction in the middle of the exchange.
- 'I am very tired today.' Barinov steps in boldly on the spot and brings in "segodnya" ("today") to make the meaning unmistakable.
