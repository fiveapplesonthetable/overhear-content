## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'What is the occasion?' The grammar packs 'what' and 'reason' into a tight, idiomatic kitchen exchange.
- 'I have very important news.' Notice how Russian expresses 'at' and 'news' with crisp inflection rather than helper words.
- 'They offered me a new job.' Here the Russian phrasing highlights 'to me' before landing on 'work'.
- 'I did not tell you, I wanted to make a surprise.' The '-a' ending on the past-tense verb for 'wanted' marks a female speaker or subject.
- 'In two days I have a meeting with readers.' Russians say 'by me' or 'with us' where English says 'I have' or 'we have', without needing a separate word for 'have'.
- 'Sorry, a new job?' Elena brings out the contrast between 'forgive' and 'new' in a few short words.
- 'Yes, but I decided to turn it down.' Notice how Russian expresses 'yes' and 'to refuse' with crisp inflection rather than helper words.
- 'I am going to fight for the star.' Spoken Russian keeps this brisk, leaning on 'I' and finishing on 'star'.
- 'Right now I am going to introduce a new auteur menu.' Chef Barinov anchors the line on 'now', while 'menu' completes the thought.
- 'That is wonderful, you will be the first chef in Russia.' The grammar packs 'this' and 'of Russia' into a tight, idiomatic kitchen exchange.
- 'And you wrote a culinary book.' Notice how Russian expresses 'and' and 'book' with crisp inflection rather than helper words.
