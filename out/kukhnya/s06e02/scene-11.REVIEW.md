## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'There she is, pouring wine.' Pointing out that there she is pouring wine gives Barinov a sobering reality check.
- 'And mine is flirting with Louis.' Mikhail explains that his is flirting with Louis, keeping his voice calm and direct.
- 'Let's drink to our girls, they are the very best.' Here drinking to their girls they are the very best together sets up the next turn in their exchange.
- 'You should not have been rude to the prefect.' Noting that Barinov should not have been rude to the prefect keeps Mikhail's side of the story simple and clear. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
- 'We have democracy, one citizen has the right to be rude to another.' Even though they have democracy one citizen has the right to be rude to another, Barinov keeps a steady face in front of Eleonora. In Russian you say "by me" or "with us" rather than "I have", so the situation sits right with the person.
- 'Is it forbidden to be rude to a prefect?' Asking whether it forbidden to be rude to a prefect is how Barinov puts Eleonora on the spot.
- 'I do not understand, what is this celebration?' When Eleonora asks if she does not understand what is this celebration, Barinov has to answer fast. In Russian, she does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'We have a new menu, Eleonora Andreyevna.' Once they have a new menu Eleonora Andreyevna, Barinov can finally tell Eleonora what he means. In Russian you say "by me" or "with us" rather than "I have", so the situation sits right with the person.
- 'I am aware of that.' Mentioning that she is aware moves the talk in a new direction.
- 'And I am also aware that you kicked out the prefect.' Being also aware that Barinov kicked out the prefect is how Eleonora lays out the situation for Barinov. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
- 'Vitya, this is my restaurant!' Saying that this is her restaurant is how Eleonora takes charge of the moment. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'Bring back the old menu tomorrow, understood?' Wondering if bring back the old menu tomorrow understood shows Eleonora caught off guard.
