## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I do not know, I did not understand anything.' Around the conversation and menu, Senya sets a calm, everyday tone with Barinov.
- 'Chef, why are the dishes so tiny?' Wondering why the dishes so tiny are cuts straight through the tension between Senya and Barinov. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'Masterpieces are not served in buckets.' Realizing that masterpieces are not served in buckets changes how Barinov handles the situation.
- 'Viktor Petrovich, have you already calculated the prices?' Here asking whether Barinov already calculated the prices shows Nikita looking for the real truth.
- 'We calculated them, from three to five thousand per dish.' Between the conversation and the menu, Barinov gives Nikita a quick, grounded response.
- 'Five thousand for a dish?' With the dish and conversation hanging in the air, Nikita puts Barinov on the spot. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'Maybe we should keep the old menu for now?' Questioning whether maybe they should keep the old menu for now is how Nikita tests Barinov's story.
- 'There is no old menu, there is only the new menu.' Once there is no old menu there is only the new menu, Barinov can finally tell Nikita what he means.
- 'Timur, bring the new menu here.' Telling Nikita to bring the new menu here is how Barinov lays out the situation for Nikita. Starting with "davay" is the everyday Russian way to say "come on, let us do this".
- 'It even still smells of ink.' With the work and menu in play, Senya keeps this short reply brisk and direct.
- 'You will thank me yet when we get the star.' Since Nikita will thank him yet when they get the star, Barinov does not waste words on excuses. Even in the middle of the drama, he keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
- 'Study the recipes!' Once Nikita should study the recipes, Barinov makes sure Nikita hears every word. Russian puts the key word right up front so his emotion lands on the first beat.
