## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Chef, I added a little sage, try it.' Highlighting a sage on the spot, Senya leans on "shalfey" ("sage") to give the whole sentence its punch.
- 'You added sage?' Pressing on candidly right here, Barinov keeps normal statement word order here, turning the line into a question for Senya purely through rising pitch.
- 'This is a dish by Viktor Barinov!' Barinov fires back promptly with a smile, watching Nastya's face to see how the news lands.
- 'Chef, can we prepare ribs from the old menu?' Emphasizing the ribs in the exchange, Nastya reaches for the Russian word "rebryshki" ("ribs", built from "ryobryshko") right at the center of the line.
- 'The prefect just arrived out there and demands ribs.' Dwelling on the ribs during the talk, Nastya uses the little word "prosto" ("simply, just") to make the point sound effortless.
- 'He said that he did not understand half the words on the menu.' Nastya replies lightly in this moment, watching Barinov's face to see how the news lands.
- 'Do I have an inspection from city hall?' Focusing on the test right here, Barinov puts the emphasis on "proverka" ("a test") in the middle of the talk.
- 'Nikolai Maryanovich, my respects!' Checking on the respect on the spot, spoken Russian compresses the whole thought into just 4 words, skipping the extra helper words English needs.
- 'Hello, Vitya, are you going to feed me?' Senya challenges evenly about what Barinov just brought up.
- 'I am glad to offer you dishes from my auteur menu.' Explaining how glad he is at once, Barinov puts the weight on "avtorskogo" ("author's, signature", from "avtorskiy") as the exchange unfolds.
- 'Do not worry, everything will be delicious and fast.' Opening up firmly before Senya, the "-sya" ending on "volnuytes" ("worry") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'Choose a dish.' Barinov speaks lightly with a shrug, before Senya can change the subject.
