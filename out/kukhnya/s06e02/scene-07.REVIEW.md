- 'You added sage?' Barinov shares the remark plainly here: Russian keeps the exact word order of a normal statement here, turning it into a question for Senya purely through rising pitch at the end.
- 'This is a dish by Viktor Barinov!' Barinov fires back promptly beside Nastya, steering the talk right where he wants it.
- 'Chef, can we prepare ribs from the old menu?' See how Nastya reminds Senya plainly here — the Russian word "rebryshki" ("ribs", built from "ryobryshko") does the heavy lifting.
- 'The prefect just arrived out there and demands ribs.' Nastya states the comment facing Barinov: the little word "prosto" ("simply, just") works like a shrug in the sentence, making a big claim sound effortless.

- 'Nikolai Maryanovich, my respects!' Barinov puts this point around Senya: spoken Russian trims this down to just 4 words, dropping the extra helper words that English has to spell out.

- 'I am glad to offer you dishes from my auteur menu.' Barinov underscores this clear touch on cue: "avtorskogo" means "author's, signature" (from "avtorskiy"), giving the line its exact edge.

- 'Choose a dish.' Barinov proceeds lightly toward Senya, bringing things back to earth after the buildup.
