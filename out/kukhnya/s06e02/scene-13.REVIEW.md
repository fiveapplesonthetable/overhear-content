## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'They suddenly moved our filming to tomorrow.' Russians say 'by me' or 'with us' where English says 'I have' or 'we have', without needing a separate word for 'have'.
- 'And we need to answer urgently.' Notice how Russian expresses 'and' and 'urgently' with crisp inflection rather than helper words.
- 'They want to film tomorrow, what should we do?' Spoken Russian keeps this brisk, leaning on 'want' and finishing on 'to do'.
- 'After all, Eleonora forbade everything.' Chef Barinov anchors the line on 'Eleonora', while 'forbade' completes the thought.
- 'Agree to it, tomorrow she will be gone all day.' The negative particle sits right before the verb meaning 'agree' to negate the statement.
- 'She will even thank you afterward.' Notice how Russian expresses 'she' and 'will tell' with crisp inflection rather than helper words. Chef Barinov answers right away.
- 'Alright, come tomorrow.' The meaning hinges on 'well' working together with 'tomorrow' in spoken Russian.
- 'The crew will be at our place at four o'clock.' The meaning hinges on 'group' working together with 'hours' in spoken Russian.
- 'It is no star, of course, but it is already something.' Spoken Russian keeps this brisk, leaning on 'this' and finishing on 'something'.
- 'Let's go celebrate, I will catch up right now.' Notice how Russian expresses 'let's go' and 'will catch up with' with crisp inflection rather than helper words.
- 'Thank you for the help, friend.' Here the Russian phrasing highlights 'thank you' before landing on 'friend'.
- 'As we say in Russia, one good turn deserves another.' Spoken Russian keeps this brisk, leaning on 'how' and finishing on 'good'.
