## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Are the three of us going to drink coffee?' Talking about the coffee before Nikita, Denis keeps normal statement word order here, turning the line into a question for Nikita purely through rising pitch.
- 'Why do you need me, you two need to be alone together.' Denis counters warmly with a sigh, refusing to let the moment slip past.
- 'But she is your girlfriend.' Bringing up the girlfriend with Denis, Nikita uses "devushka" ("girlfriend") to carry the main weight of the thought.
- 'You told me yourself yesterday that Sveta was your girlfriend.' Discussing the girlfriend hearing Nikita, Denis puts the real issue right on the table for Nikita.
- 'No, wait, it was you who said she was your girlfriend.' Nikita responds sharply with a smile, shifting the mood of the conversation in a single beat.
- 'No, I was not talking about her, I meant Katya.' Mentioning the view in front of Nikita, Denis leans on "govoril" ("said, were saying", a form of "govorit") to give the remark its edge.
- 'No, I was the one who said Katya was my girlfriend.' Touching on his girlfriend to Katya, Nikita leans on "devushka" ("girlfriend") to give the whole sentence its punch.
- 'Katya, tell him that you and I lived together.' Denis proceeds at once hearing Katya, bringing things back to earth after the buildup.
- 'Katya, were you really with him?' Bringing up the honestly to Katya, Nikita puts the emphasis on "pravda" ("honestly") in the middle of the talk.
- 'Do you not see, she does not want to talk to you.' Denis chimes in earnestly around Nikita and leans on "razgovarivat" ("to talk, to speak") to give the whole sentence its punch.
- 'Let my girlfriend decide for herself what to do.' Nikita answers firmly with a shrug, before Denis can change the subject.
- 'Quiet, stop it both of you!' Katya continues candidly before Nikita and uses "perestante" ("stop it", from "perestat") to make the point stick.
