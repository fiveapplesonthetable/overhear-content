## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'You let such a wonderful woman slip away, Max!' Notice how Russian expresses 'you' and 'Max' with crisp inflection rather than helper words.
- 'But nothing at all happened between her and Nagiyev.' The meaning hinges on 'but' working together with 'was' in spoken Russian.
- 'Wait, Chef, how do you know that?' Max chooses the respectful plural 'you' alongside 'know' to keep a polite distance. Chef Barinov answers right away.
- 'Believe me, I know for sure!' Chef Barinov brings out the contrast between 'believe' and 'to me' in a few short words.
- 'And remember, Dima, the kitchen is real magic!' In Russian, the idea of 'and' flows naturally into 'magic' without extra filler words.
- 'We soak the rabbit in water and add wine vinegar.' The meaning hinges on 'soak' working together with 'vinegar' in spoken Russian.
- 'This will tenderize the meat and remove the unpleasant smell.' Chef Barinov pairs 'will tenderize / soften' with 'aroma, smell' directly, since spoken Russian skips English filler words like 'the', 'a', and 'is'.
- 'Dima, when you chop onions, always chew gum.' Spoken Russian keeps this brisk, leaning on 'Dima' and finishing on 'chewing gum'.
- 'Then you definitely will not cry from the onions.' The negative particle sits right before the verb meaning 'to cry' to negate the statement. Kostya answers right away.
- 'Nastya, they just gave me a bonus, fifty thousand!' The meaning hinges on 'Nastya' working together with 'thousand' in spoken Russian.
- 'And fifty thousand for you too, Vika rewarded us before leaving!' The '-a' ending on the past-tense verb for 'rewarded' marks a female speaker or subject.
- 'Now that is really very cool!' Max anchors the line on 'here', while 'cool' completes the thought.
