## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Max, I think it is time for you to go home now.' Reacting promptly at once, the "-sya" ending on "kazhetsya" ("it seems, I think") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'What do you mean time to go, everything was going so well!' Max weighs in warmly without any doubt, grounding the moment in plain reality.
- 'And are things going well between you and Katya too?' Vika opens up firmly to Max and brings in "khorosho" ("good, fine") to make the meaning unmistakable.
- 'Nothing ever happened or will happen between me and Katya!' Turning boldly during the talk, Max pairs the negative word with "ne" ("not"), since Russian naturally doubles its negatives for emphasis.
- 'Max, I am tired and I know how all of this will end.' Vika proceeds honestly facing Max, leaving no doubt about where she stands.
- 'You and I have already been through this before.' Vika replies evenly in the exchange and leans on "prokhodili" ("to go through", a form of "prokhodit") to give the remark its edge.
- 'I was recently offered a good job abroad.' Highlighting a good job to Max, Vika centers the thought on "khoroshuyu" ("good", from "khoroshiy") without wasting a word.
- 'Farewell, Max, I am flying away soon.' Vika cuts in frankly without a pause, testing Max's reaction in the middle of the exchange.
- 'Sometimes it is very hard to express in words what is going on inside.' Pointing out how difficult things are to Vika, Max lands on "slozhno" ("difficult", built on "slozhnyy") to keep the statement grounded.
- 'Some people manage to find a good substitute for those words.' Talking about the word in this beat, the "-sya" ending on "poluchayetsya" ("works out") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'And for others, actions speak louder than any words.' Max weighs in wryly with Vika, testing Vika's reaction in the middle of the exchange.
- 'He stands soaked in the center of the city and does not know what to do.' Touching on the center with Vika, Max puts the weight on "tsentre" ("center", from "tsentr") as the exchange unfolds.
