## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I would have been better off not texting her at all.' Max highlights this telling detail in reply: "voobshche" means "at all", and it carries the main weight of what Max is saying.
- 'She replied to me: thank you, and who is this?' Max states the reply without fuss: the everyday word "spasibo" ("thank you") grew out of an old blessing meaning "save you", and it anchors the whole line here.
- 'That means she simply deleted your number.' Kostya holds firm firmly without holding back, making his position completely clear.
- 'That is it, Kostya, everything is over between us.' Max leans on one key detail in earnest: "koncheno" means "finished", and it carries the main weight of what Max is saying.
- 'Yes, Max, it looks like this really is the end.' Kostya directs the reply around Eva: notice how Russian needs no word for "am", "is", or "are" in the present — placing the words side by side says it all.
- 'Listen, so what did you decide, are you buying the car from me?' Eva asks earnestly whether Max really thought this through.
- 'Listen, Eva, probably not after all.' Max uses this sharp element for Eva: "navernoye" means "probably", and it carries the main weight of what Max is saying.
- 'Its color is too girly for me.' Max brings that answer at once: notice how Russian needs no word for "am", "is", or "are" in the present — placing the words side by side says it all.
- 'And why are you not offering it to us?' Kostya wonders candidly if there is more going on behind the scenes.
- 'Do you think we cannot buy it, how much does it cost?' Kostya chooses this vivid choice in earnest: "dumayesh" means "think" (from "dumat"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'Five hundred and fifty thousand rubles.' Eva lays out the reaction at once: spoken Russian trims this down to just 4 words, dropping the extra helper words that English has to spell out.
- 'We are a tiny bit short, but we will think about it.' Kostya answers warmly without any doubt, letting the remark speak for itself.
