## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'She is a good one you have.' Russians say 'by me' or 'with us' where English says 'I have' or 'we have', without needing a separate word for 'have'.
- 'Listen, Elya, do you think I understand nothing?' Chef Barinov addresses the listener with the informal singular 'you' alongside 'understand'.
- 'You blabber about your ex-husband.' Spoken Russian keeps this brisk, leaning on 'about' and finishing on 'chat'.
- 'Do you want to ruin our relationship?' Notice how Russian expresses 'want' and 'relationship' with crisp inflection rather than helper words. Eleonora answers right away.
- 'Calm down, I will leave everything to a professional.' Here the Russian phrasing highlights 'calm down' before landing on 'professional'.
- 'Especially since you have already started.' Eleonora brings out the contrast between 'that' and 'more' in a few short words.
- 'After all, you told her nothing about me.' The negative particle sits right before the verb meaning 'told' to negate the statement. Chef Barinov answers right away.
- 'You are simply jealous of me.' Chef Barinov brings out the contrast between 'yes' and 'you' in a few short words.
- 'All men run away from you.' In Russian, the idea of 'from' flows naturally into 'run away' without extra filler words.
- 'He ran so far that you will not reach him.' The '-l' ending on the verb meaning 'ran away' places the action in the masculine past tense. Kostya answers right away.
- 'Maybe she was joking?' Kostya raises the pitch on 'joked' to turn the phrase into a direct spoken question.
- 'Well whose child is it, if not yours?' Kostya brings out the contrast between 'come on' and 'whose' in a few short words.
