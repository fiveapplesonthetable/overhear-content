## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'You see, I cannot move in with you.' 'Ponimaesh', literally 'you understand', is a softener, like 'you see'. 'K tebe' is 'to you', and with a verb of moving it means 'to your place'.
- 'Why do you not want to live with me?' 'So mnoy' is 'with me': 's' becomes 'so' in front of 'mnoy', which is easier to say. 'Zhit', to live, follows 'khochesh' in its basic form.
- 'Kostya and I pay for the flat together.' Russian says 'my s Kostey', 'we with Kostya', for 'Kostya and I'. After 'za', pay for, 'kvartira' becomes 'kvartiru'.
- 'That is just a silly excuse.' 'Otgovorka' comes from 'otgovorit', to talk your way out of something. It's feminine, so 'silly' is 'glupaya'.
- 'He cannot afford it on his own.' 'Ne potyanet' is literally 'won't pull', everyday Russian for 'can't manage' or 'can't afford'. 'Odin', alone, comes first for weight.
- 'Max, if you do not want to live with me, just say so.' 'Tak i skazhi' is literally 'so and say', a set way to say 'then just say it'. 'Esli' is 'if'.
- 'I do want to. It is Kostya who does not want to.' The 'da' here isn't 'yes'; it pushes back, like 'but I do'. 'Eto Kostya ne khochet' points the finger: 'it's Kostya who doesn't'.
- 'Then talk to him today.' 'Pogovori' is the order form of 'pogovorit', to have a talk. 'S nim' is 'with him', and 'on' turns into 'nim' after 's'.
- 'I understand him. But I will talk to him.' 'Ego' is 'him' as the thing understood. 'Pogovoryu' is a single word for 'I will have a talk': the 'po' form has no present, so it points ahead.
- 'Do you promise?' No word order change, no helper word: in Russian a statement becomes a question just by rising at the end.
- 'I promise.' 'Obeshchayu' is one word, and its ending already means 'I', so nobody says the 'ya'.
