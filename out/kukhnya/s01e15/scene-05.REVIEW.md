## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Where are you going?' 'Ty kuda' is 'you where to', with no verb at all. In Russian the movement is understood.
- 'I have to go.' Just two words, 'mne pora', 'to me time'. It's the standard way to say you must leave.
- 'Stay with me for one more minute.' 'Ostansya' ends in 'sya', 'stay yourself'. 'Na odnu minutu' uses 'na' for a stretch of time, and 'odna minuta' changes to 'odnu minutu' after it.
- 'I cannot, I am rushing very much.' Russian turns up the volume with 'ochen silno', literally 'very strongly', where English would just say 'in a real hurry'. 'Speshu' is the 'I' form of 'speshit', to hurry.
- 'Do you again have many important things?' 'Del' is 'of affairs', so 'many important affairs' ends in plural 'of' forms. 'U tebya' is 'at you', which she uses for 'do you have'.
- 'I need to stop by home, take a shower, change.' Russians 'accept' a shower, 'prinyat dush', where English says 'take'. 'Pereodetsya' is 'dress again', that is, change clothes.
- 'Listen, are you not tired of this?' Literally, 'to you has it not got boring'. It's how Russians ask 'aren't you fed up with this?'.
- 'And what exactly am I fed up with?' Max turns her question round. 'Nadoelo' is the same verb she used, and again the person who's fed up is 'mne', to me.
- 'Sleeping here, but taking a shower far from here.' Vika uses no 'you' at all: 'spat' and 'prinimat' stay in their basic form, like 'to sleep here, to shower there'. 'Otsyuda' is 'from here'.
- 'Yes, it takes a lot of my time.' 'Zanimaet' means 'occupies', so the thing takes up your time. 'Moego vremeni' is 'of my time', with an 'ego' ending.
- 'Then why do you do this every day?' 'Zachem' is 'what for', asking about the purpose. 'Kazhdyy den', every day, needs no little word in front, unlike English 'on'.
- 'Because all my things lie there.' Things 'lie', 'lezhat', in a place, as in the flat. Max gives a plain reason, which is the joke.
- 'Maybe you should move in with me?' 'Mozhet byt' is literally 'can be', so 'maybe'. 'K' becomes 'ko' before 'mne', to make it easier to say. 'Pereedesh' is the same 'move in' verb as in the first scene.
