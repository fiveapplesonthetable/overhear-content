## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Well? Did you talk to Kostya?' The person you talk with goes into the 'with' form in Russian, so 'Kostya' becomes 'Kostey'. Vika sounds brisk.
- 'Yes, he begs me not to move.' 'Umolyaet' is stronger than 'asks', closer to 'pleads'. After 'ne' the verb is 'pereezzhat', the imperfective, which is the usual choice when you ask someone not to do something.
- 'Why does he strongly not want this?' After 'ne khochet', 'this' changes to 'etogo'. Russian puts the object after a 'not want' in that shape.
- 'He has a problem with money now.' 'At him' stands for 'he has'. Money is only ever plural in Russian.
- 'But you still decided and moved out.' 'Reshilsya' has a 'sya', so it's 'made up your own mind'. 'Syekhal' is 'rode off', Russian for 'moved out'.
- 'Yes so fast that I forgot the wallet.' 'Tak bystro, chto' is 'so fast that', the pattern for results. 'Bumazhnik' is a wallet, built on 'bumaga', paper.
- 'And where do you live?' 'Gde ty zhivyosh' is 'where you live', with no helper word. 'I' at the start means 'and'.
- 'Nowhere for now.' You know 'poka' as 'bye', but its first meaning is 'for now', 'for the time being'. 'Nigde' is 'nowhere', and it needs no 'ne' when it stands alone.
- 'So better nowhere than with me?' 'To est', literally 'that is', opens the line like 'so you mean'. 'Chem' is 'than', the link between the two choices.
- 'Well alright, let me come to you.' 'Davay priedu' is 'let's say I'll come', the usual offer. 'K tebe' is 'to your place'.
- 'No, you do not need to do me a favor.' 'Delat odolzhenie' is 'to do a favour', and Russians often use it the way English uses 'don't do me any favours'. 'Mne ne nado' is 'to me not needed'.
- 'So you will not come?' 'To est' again, summing up. 'Ne priedesh' is 'you won't come', and the 'pri' at the front is the part that means arriving.
- 'You understand, it is important that we want.' 'Ty poymi' is the order 'understand!', with 'ty' added for weight, like 'you have to understand'. After 'vazhno, chtoby' the verb goes into the past form, 'khoteli'.
