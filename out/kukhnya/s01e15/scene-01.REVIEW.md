## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Nastya moved in with us a week ago.' Russian puts the time first and 'back' after it, so 'a week back' is 'a week ago'. The word for 'moved in' is built from 'across' and 'ride', since moving house is a ride across town.
- 'Yes, now we live here as three.' One word does the work of 'the three of us', and it sounds like 'v-troyom'. Russian is fond of such handy counting words.
- 'Our life barely changed from that.' 'Almost not' is how Russians say 'barely', so the 'not' is part of it. The word for 'changed' ends in 'las', because 'life' is feminine and the thing changes itself.
- 'We still play together just the same.' 'Just the same' is three small words, 'vsyo tak zhe', literally 'all so same'. 'Play' is the word Russians use for video games as well as for football.
- 'Kostya, hurry up, shoot at them!' Max calls him 'Kostyan', a chummy form that you'd only use with a mate. After the little word 'at', 'them' gets an extra 'n' in front, so it's not 'ikh' but 'nikh'.
- 'I am choosing my new weapon.' The word for 'weapon' is neuter, so 'new' ends in 'oe', and 'my own' matches it. Kostya is calm, as if he were shopping.
- 'They surrounded me from three sides here!' Russian says 'me surrounded', and nobody is named. The enemies in the game stay in the background, as 'they'.
- 'Hold on, I am already running to help!' 'Derzhis'' ends in 'sya', so it's 'hold yourself'. 'Na pomoshch' is 'onto help', which is how Russians say 'to the rescue'.
- 'Great work, this is our victory!' The word for 'victory' is feminine, so 'our' ends in 'a'. It's one of those words that Russians put on every wall in May.
- 'We are a great team in this game.' 'V etoy igre' is 'in this game', and 'igra' changes its last letter to 'e'. There's no 'are' anywhere.
- 'The main thing is that Nastya does not disturb us.' 'Glavnoe, chtoby' is a handy opener, 'the main thing is that'. After 'chtoby' the action takes its past form, even though it means the present.
- 'She is cooking a tasty dinner in the kitchen.' 'Na kukhne' is 'on the kitchen', where English says 'in'. 'Uzhin' is the evening meal, and it's masculine, which is why 'tasty' ends in 'y'.
