## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Good morning, Viktor.' 'Utro' is a neuter word, so 'dobroe', 'good', ends in 'oe'. Lyova's mother calls him by his first name, as an equal.
- 'Good morning.' It's the same greeting in two words. The mother says it to the room.
- 'Viktor prepared such an omelet.' 'Takoy omlet' is 'such an omelette', and 'takoy' is masculine because 'omlet' is. The English word is borrowed from French and arrives in Russian.
- 'Is this a kind of apology?' 'Tipa' is slang for 'sort of', and it's the kind of word teenagers use. 'Izvineniya' is 'apologies', always plural.
- 'This is a kind of omelet.' Barinov copies 'tipa' back, to avoid the subject. It's a neat way to say nothing.
- 'Come on, eat.' 'Esh' is the order 'eat!', for a friend. 'Davay' on the end makes it a nudge.
- 'I will be late for work.' 'Opozdayu' is 'I will be late'. 'Na rabotu' is 'for work'.
- 'Yes, eat faster.' 'Kushayte' is a cosy word for 'eat', and it ends in 'te' because it's polite and plural. 'Bystree' is 'faster'.
- 'Alright, I will eat this.' The word for 'I will eat up' starts with 's', which means 'finish'. 'Ladno' is the shrug.
- 'This is very delicious.' Without a verb, 'eto ochen vkusno'. It's high praise from a chef.
- 'It is time for us to go.' 'Nam pora' is 'to us time'. The mother says it to herself and the men.
- 'Thank you for the breakfast.' 'Spasibo za' is 'thanks for', and what follows takes the 'for' form. 'Zavtrak' is the same word as 'breakfast'.
