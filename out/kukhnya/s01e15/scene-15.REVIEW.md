## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Viktor Petrovich, are you not sleeping?' Lyova gently checks if the chef is awake, turning a simple statement into a question by raising his tone on "sleeping."
- 'I heard everything, Lyova.' Barinov shatters the illusion of sleep with a quick, decisive action word for "heard."
- 'Did you hear my words?' Lyova panics slightly and double-checks if his entire heartfelt speech was caught, raising his tone at the end to make it a question.
- 'I almost had a stroke.' The chef dramatically exaggerates his reaction to the apology, using a past-completed action word for "was enough" to mean a stroke nearly took him.
- 'Your speech was very fiery.' Barinov pokes fun at the impassioned apology, using the familiar "you" form to describe the speech as flaming or passionate.
- 'I spoke from a pure heart.' Defending his sincerity, Lyova uses a past action word to confirm that his words came from a clean place.
- 'This is already the second heart attack today.' Keeping up the melodrama, Barinov connects "already" directly to "second heart attack" without bothering with "is."
- 'So, you are not angry at me?' Seeking final confirmation that they are good, Lyova asks a direct question about being mad.
- 'No, I am not angry at you.' Barinov reassures him, using an action word with a special ending that reflects the feeling back onto himself.
- 'Will you stay to live with us?' Eager for things to go back to normal, Lyova asks if the chef will remain using the "at us" structure instead of an ownership word.
- 'Yes, I will stay here.' The chef agrees to stick around, utilizing a self-directed action word to say he will remain.
- 'I am very glad to hear this.' Thrilled by the news, Lyova easily blends the words for "very much," "glad," and "to hear."
- 'And now let me sleep.' Having settled the drama, Barinov orders his friend to let him rest, giving a straightforward command for peace.
