## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Maksim, I carefully read your résumé.' Elena uses the full "Maksim", as an interviewer would. "Prochla", read, ends in -la because she is a woman, and "vashe" is the polite "your".
- 'I hope you liked my experience.' Russian turns it round: "vam ponravilsya moy opyt", to you was pleasing my experience. "Ponravilsya" ends in -sya with no -a, because "opyt" is masculine.
- 'You are a very capable and promising cook.' "Sposobnyy", capable, grows out of "sposob", a way of doing something. "Perspektivnyy" sounds like "perspective", and means a cook with a future.
- 'Thank you, I truly want to learn a lot.' "Deystvitel'no", truly, has "deystviye", action, inside it: true in deed. "Mnogomu nauchit'sya" is "to learn to much", the way Russian says "learn a lot".
- 'I will gladly take you into the kitchen.' "S radost'yu" is "with joy", which is how Russian says "gladly". "Voz'mu", I will take, is the future of "vzyat'".
- 'At the Claude Monet restaurant they do not appreciate me.' "Ne tsenyat" is "they don't value", with nobody named. The verb has "tsena", price, at its root.
- 'I do not understand why they act like that.' "Postupat'" is to act or behave, usually about how people treat someone. "Tak" is "like that".
- 'There they only give me vegetables to peel.' "Mne dayut", to me they give, has no named subject. "Chistit'", to peel, comes from "chistyy", clean, so peeling is cleaning.
- 'With us you will cook complex dishes.' "U nas", at us, is "at our place". "Budete gotovit'" builds the future from "budete", you will be, plus "to cook".
- 'This is exactly what I dreamed about!' The Russian runs "eto imenno to, o chyom ya mechtal", this is exactly that about which I dreamed. After "o", "chto" turns into "chyom".
- 'Here are my contacts just in case.' "Na vsyakiy sluchay" is "for any occasion", and it is the fixed phrase for "just in case". "Kontakty" is borrowed.
- 'When should I expect your call?' "Mne ozhidat'", to me to expect, makes the "should I" question with no verb for "should". "Zvonka" is "zvonok", a call, in the "of" form after "ozhidat'".
- 'I will call you in a couple of days.' "Para" is a pair, so "paru dney" is a couple of days. "Cherez", across, marks time from now: "cherez paru dney", in a couple of days.
