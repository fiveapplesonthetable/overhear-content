- 'Please get acquainted.' Katya grounds one concrete word right here: "oznakomtes" means "familiarize yourself" (from "oznakomitsya"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'Denis, this is my mom.' Katya offers the comeback at once: notice how Russian needs no word for "am", "is", or "are" in the present — placing the words side by side says it all.
- 'Mom, this is my boyfriend Denis.' Katya replies sharply facing Denis, keeping the focus on what actually matters here.
- 'Very nice to meet you.' Eleonora picks one familiar touch toward Katya: "priyatno" means "pleasant", and it carries the main weight of what Eleonora is saying.
- 'Do not be afraid, Mom does not bite.' Katya shares this update with candor: the "-sya" ending on "kusayetsya" ("bites") turns the action inward or makes it happen between people naturally.
- 'Yes, I already know that.' Denis counters decisively in their talk, to clear the air between them once and for all.
- 'Denis, and what do you do for a living?' Eleonora stresses one pointed word in reply: "zanimayetes" means "are busy with" (from "zanimatsya"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'I work right here for you.' Denis shapes this statement calmly now: using "u vas" (literally "by you") is how Russian politely asks or talks about what someone has on their side.
- 'I work as a cook in the kitchen.' Denis chimes in boldly with a nod, watching Eleonora's face to see how the news lands.
- 'Very good, and closer to Katya.' Eleonora colors one clear expression with Katya: "khorosho" means "good, fine", and it carries the main weight of what Eleonora is saying.
- 'You are under my roof now.' Eleonora makes that remark without pause: notice how Russian needs no word for "am", "is", or "are" in the present — placing the words side by side says it all.
- 'So we will see each other often.' Eleonora weighs in promptly without a pause, testing Katya's reaction in the middle of the exchange.
- 'Excuse me, I need to go.' Denis emphasizes one clear choice toward Eleonora: "izvinite" means "sorry, excuse me" (from "izvinit"), giving the line its exact edge.
