## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Please get acquainted.' Katya warmly drops this courtesy word while facing Eleonora using "pozhaluysta" ("please") — the all-purpose Russian politeness word that covers both "please" and "you're welcome".
- 'Denis, this is my mom.' Touching on the mum at once, Katya links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
- 'Mom, this is my boyfriend Denis.' Katya counters sharply during the talk, so Denis knows she is not just making small talk.
- 'Very nice to meet you.' Eleonora follows up earnestly with Katya and leans on "priyatno" ("pleasant") to give the whole sentence its punch.
- 'Do not be afraid, Mom does not bite.' Discussing the mum to Eleonora, the "-sya" ending on "kusayetsya" ("bites") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'Yes, I already know that.' Denis jumps in decisively on the spot, to clear the air between them once and for all.
- 'Denis, and what do you do for a living?' Eleonora replies dryly in the exchange and chooses "zanimayetes" ("are busy with", from the root "zanimatsya") so Denis hears the exact meaning.
- 'I work right here for you.' Denis opens up earnestly with a nod, refusing to let the moment slip past.
- 'I work as a cook in the kitchen.' Denis responds boldly hearing Eleonora, keeping the focus on what actually matters here.
- 'Very good, and closer to Katya.' Eleonora pushes back candidly in this beat and leans on "khorosho" ("good, fine") to give the whole sentence its punch.
- 'You are under my roof now.' Returning to her roof on the spot, Eleonora links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
- 'So we will see each other often.' Eleonora counters promptly facing Denis, refusing to let the moment slip past.
- 'Excuse me, I need to go.' Denis simply chooses this polite apology at this moment using "izvinite" ("excuse me") — the respectful form of "excuse me" or "sorry" used when stepping in or apologizing.
