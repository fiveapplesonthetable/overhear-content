- 'Oh sure, that is a very valid reason.' Since sure that is a very valid reason, Barinov does not waste words on excuses. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'Lena, I do not want to depend on anyone.' Mentioning that someone she does not want to depend on anyone moves the talk in a new direction.
- 'Especially not on a woman.' Between the kitchen and the restaurant, Elena gives Barinov a quick, grounded response.
- 'Women have always moved in with me.' Because women have always moved in with him, Barinov treats the matter as settled.
- 'I told you I fixed it.' Explaining that she told Barinov she fixed it lays the cards on the table for Barinov.
- 'Hold on, I will get the door.' Elena explains that Barinov should hold on she will get the door, keeping her voice calm and direct.
- 'Chef, is this a convenient time for you to talk?' Checking whether this a convenient time for Elena to talk is how Barinov puts Elena on the spot. Even in the middle of the drama, he keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
- 'He is not going to forgive you anyway.' Explaining that he is not going to forgive Barinov anyway lays the cards on the table for Barinov. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
- 'Oh, remember that time you had a huge fight with Vika?' When Elena asks if remember that time Barinov had a huge fight with Vika, Barinov has to answer fast.
- 'Nah, that is not an option.' Realizing that someone that is not an option changes how Barinov handles the situation.
- 'Good morning to you.' Between the shift and the menu, Vika gives Barinov a quick, grounded response.
- 'Here is your menu.' Even though here is Barinov's menu, Elena keeps a steady face in front of Barinov.
- 'No way, is that really you?' Questioning whether way is that really Elena is how Barinov tests Elena's story. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
