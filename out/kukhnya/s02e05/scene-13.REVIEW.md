## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello, good afternoon.' Over the kitchen and the moment, Max answers Nastya without a second thought.
- 'He cannot come to the phone right now.' Noting that he cannot come to the phone right now keeps Vika's side of the story simple and clear.
- 'Hello, I am a friend of his.' Realizing that she is a friend changes how Nastya handles the situation.
- 'Oh, it is good that you called.' Admitting that it is good that Nastya called shows Max being honest for a change.
- 'I have bad news for you.' Claiming that she has bad news for Max sounds confident, even if Max has doubts. Russian expresses having something as "by me is", keeping the phrase short and grounded.
- 'We lost him.' Once they lost him, Nastya can finally tell Max what she means.
- 'He walked out into the hallway and disappeared somewhere.' Adding that he walked out into the hallway and disappeared somewhere fills in the missing piece for Nastya.
- 'Now we cannot find him anywhere.' Here refusing to find him anywhere sets up the next turn in their exchange.
- 'Wait a second, so he is alive?' Asking whether a second so he is alive puts Max right on the defensive. In Russian, she does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'Listen, was that you playing a prank?' Asking whether that Nastya playing a prank cuts straight through the tension between Max and Nastya.
- 'Well, where are you?' Wondering where Max is is how Vika puts Max on the spot. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
- 'It looks like I ate something bad.' Because it looks like she ate something bad, Nastya treats the matter as settled.
- 'So here I am lying down and resting.' Explaining that here he is lying down and resting lays the cards on the table for Nastya.
