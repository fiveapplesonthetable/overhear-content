## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Oh, what a tragedy.' Between the kitchen and the conversation, Vika gives Sasha a quick, grounded response.
- 'Is it really that hard for you?' By checking whether it really that hard for Vika, Sasha presses Vika for a straight answer. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
- 'You know that the guest is always right.' Even though that the guest is always right, Vika keeps a steady face in front of Sasha.
- 'In my kitchen, I am always right.' Admitting that in her kitchen she is always right shows Sasha being honest for a change.
- 'This conversation is over.' Saying that this conversation is is how Vika sets the tone with Sasha.
- 'I wake up next to Sasha.' Saying that she wakes up next to Sasha is how Sasha sets the tone with Vika.
- 'I nearly lost my mind.' Adding that she nearly lost her mind fills in the missing piece for Sasha.
- 'Listen, clowns are way scarier though.' Sasha tells Vika that clowns are way scarier though without dressing it up.
- 'And besides.' Around the shift and truth, Vika sets a calm, everyday tone with Sasha.
- 'Thank goodness Sasha flew off to Cyprus.' Thanking goodness Sasha flew off to Cyprus is how Sasha lays out the situation for Vika.
- 'For a couple of days I do not have to think about it.' Vika reminds Sasha that for a couple of days she does not have to think about it before the moment passes. Russian packs the whole idea of what is allowed or needed into one impersonal word at the front.
- 'And after that, make your choice.' With the moment and kitchen in play, Sasha keeps this short reply brisk and direct.
- 'Sooner or later you will have to anyway.' Explaining that sooner or later Sasha will have to anyway lays the cards on the table for Sasha.
