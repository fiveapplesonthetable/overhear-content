## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Before you is our new hotel.' Bringing up the hotel to Barinov, Denis reaches for the Russian word "novyy" ("new") right at the heart of the line.
- 'Our restaurant is located right here.' Returning to his restaurant with Barinov, the "-sya" ending on "nakhoditsya" ("to be located") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'We now work in this hotel.' Denis jumps in candidly facing Barinov, so Barinov knows he is not just making small talk.
- 'How does a hotel restaurant differ?' Pointing to a hotel restaurant in the exchange, Denis brings in "otlichayetsya" ("differs", from "otlichatsya") as the key Russian word here.
- 'In a hotel there are always breakfasts.' Focusing on the breakfasts with Barinov, Denis centers the thought on "zavtraki" ("breakfasts", from "zavtrak") without wasting a word.
- 'The cooks have to get up early.' Denis fires back boldly across the room, before Barinov can change the subject.
- 'We get up early every morning.' Discussing the morning in this beat, Barinov puts the weight on "kazhdoye" ("every, each", from "kazhdyy") as the exchange unfolds.
- 'Restaurant guests are not just casual visitors.' Pointing to the visitors in this beat, Denis links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
- 'They are also hotel residents.' Denis steps in plainly without holding back, testing Barinov's reaction in the middle of the exchange.
- 'We must feed all the guests.' Turning to the guests during the talk, Denis puts the weight on "dolzhny" ("must, should", from "dolzhen") as the exchange unfolds.
- 'Someone does not want to get out of bed.' Touching on the bed at once, Denis leans on "krovati" ("bed", a form of "krovat") to give the remark its edge.
- 'They are too lazy to come down to the restaurant!' Barinov counters at once on the spot, refusing to let the moment slip past.
