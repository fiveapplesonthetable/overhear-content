## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'You cannot imagine what trouble I am in.' Barinov fires back openly facing Mikhail, testing Mikhail's reaction in the middle of the exchange.
- 'Alright, thank you very much, everyone.' Mikhail smoothly directs this word of thanks without hesitation using "spasibo" ("thank you") — the everyday word for "thank you", which grew out of the old blessing "spasi Bog" ("God save you").
- 'What is happening over there right now?' Jumping in firmly before Vika, Mikhail puts the question word "chto" right at the start so Vika hears the focus immediately.
- 'We have a small problem in the room.' Vika pushes back pointedly with a smile, testing Mikhail's reaction in the middle of the exchange.
- 'The fire alarm went off up there.' Emphasizing the fire alarm around Mikhail, Vika reaches for the Russian word "signalizatsiya" ("alarm") right at the heart of the line.
- 'Our head chef went in there.' Holding firm earnestly during the talk, spoken Russian compresses the whole thought into just 4 words, skipping the extra helper words English needs.
- 'What did he do in there again?' Mikhail challenges frankly while looking straight at Vika here.
- 'Have you completely lost your mind?' Addressing the mind in front of Barinov, Mikhail centers the thought on "soshyol" ("gone", from "soyti") without wasting a word.
- 'I was simply doing your job for you.' Returning to the job on the spot, Barinov uses the little word "prosto" ("simply, just") to make the point sound effortless.
- 'It is very cold here in the room.' Barinov cuts in boldly without a pause, bringing things back to earth after the buildup.
- 'What is going on here at all?' Mikhail continues warmly beside Barinov and centers the remark on "proiskhodit" ("is going on") without any extra fuss.
- 'Have you never heard about the holiday?' Dwelling on the festival in front of Mikhail, Barinov pairs the negative word with "ne" ("not"), since Russian naturally doubles its negatives for emphasis.
