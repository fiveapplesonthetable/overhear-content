## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'A new order for our room.' Turning to a new order in the exchange, Senya reaches for the Russian word "novyy" ("new") right at the heart of the line.
- 'I am carrying this myself today!' Senya weighs in calmly with a sigh, watching Barinov's face to see how the news lands.
- 'What huge tips they give in there!' Talking about the tips in front of Barinov, Senya uses "ogromnyye" ("huge", from "ogromnyy") to make the point stick.
- 'I have never had tips like these before.' Looking over dryly at once, Senya pairs the negative word with "ne" ("not"), since Russian naturally doubles its negatives for emphasis.
- 'You could at least share with your friends.' Barinov steps in openly without a pause, before Senya can change the subject.
- 'Immediately take this off the table.' Emphasizing the table to Barinov, Mikhail brings in "nemedlenno" ("immediately") to make the meaning unmistakable.
- 'I do not like it when the meat is poorly cooked.' Focusing on the meat on the spot, Mikhail uses "prozhareno" ("cooked through", from "prozharit") to make the point stick.
- 'Right now I will redo everything quickly.' Barinov jumps in earnestly with a shrug, so Mikhail knows he is not just making small talk.
- 'No need anymore. Bring some coffee.' Addressing the coffee in front of Barinov, Mikhail uses "prinesite" ("bring", from "prinesti") to make the point stick.
- 'He is just joking like that today.' Pushing back calmly around Senya, Mikhail uses the little word "prosto" ("simply, just") to make the point sound effortless.
- 'This guest got really lucky, right?' Senya quizzes openly why things suddenly took this turn between them.
- 'The president himself would envy service like this.' Bringing up the service beside Mikhail, Senya centers the thought on "obsluzhivaniyu" ("service", from "obsluzhivaniye") without wasting a word.
