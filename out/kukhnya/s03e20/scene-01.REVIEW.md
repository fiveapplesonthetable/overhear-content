## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Vika, please marry me!' Max pushes back boldly in this beat and puts the weight on "vykhodi" ("go out, marry", from "vykhodit") as the exchange unfolds.
- 'Max, why on earth did you climb onto a horse?' Pointing to a horse around Max, Vika drops in "voobshche" ("in general, altogether") to sum up the whole situation at once.
- 'Vika, from the very first day of our meeting!' Max speaks bluntly hearing Vika, making his position completely clear.
- 'Max, you need not continue, my answer is no!' Focusing on her answer around Max, Vika uses "prodolzhat" ("to continue") to carry the main weight of the thought.
- 'Why no? Vika, wait, please!' Pressing on plainly with Vika, Max puts the question word "pochemu" right at the start so Vika hears the focus immediately.
- 'Wait a second, how on earth did I end up here?' Max prompts plainly to see how Vika will answer on the spot.
- 'You just fell hard off a horse.' Touching on a horse in this beat, Vika reaches for the Russian word "loshadi" ("from the horse", built from "loshad") right at the center of the line.
- 'Does your head hurt badly right now?' Talking about the head before Max, Vika uses "u tebya" (literally "by you") where English says "you have" or "your".
- 'Vika, forgive me for running away from you in the morning.' Max speaks openly with a shrug, without trying to dress up how he really feels.
- 'I was just late for a job interview this morning.' Underscoring a job interview during the talk, Max lands on "sobesedovaniye" ("interview") as the key everyday word here.
- 'Where did you run away to? To what job interview?' Stepping in earnestly on the spot, Vika puts the question word "kuda" right at the start so Max hears the focus immediately.
- 'When I ran out of the apartment, you smashed a vase.' Max follows up frankly without a pause, leaving no doubt about where he stands.
- 'I remember how we met at the club.' Focusing on the club at once, Max chooses "poznakomilis" ("met", from the root "poznakomitsya") so Vika hears the exact meaning.
- 'I spent the night at your place and ran off to apply for a job.' Addressing a job right here, Max uses "u tebya" (literally "by you") where English says "you have" or "your".
