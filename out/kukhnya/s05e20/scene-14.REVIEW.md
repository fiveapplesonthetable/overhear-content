## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'By the way, technically you won the bet.' Turning to the bet to Max, Barinov opens with "kstati" ("by the way") as if the thought just crossed his mind.
- 'Congratulations to you, but there is also bad news.' Barinov weighs in briskly across the room, shifting the mood of the conversation in a single beat.
- 'As of today you are fired from our kitchen.' Barinov follows up decisively on the spot and leans on "segodnyashnego" ("of today's", a form of "segodnyashniy") to give the remark its edge.
- 'What do you mean fired, why, that is unfair!' Max reacts sharply at once and uses "nespravedlivo" ("unfairly") to carry the main weight of the thought.
- 'Two great chefs cannot coexist in one kitchen.' Barinov weighs in openly in this moment, making his position completely clear.
- 'So tomorrow you start as head chef at Restaurant Monet!' Touching on the head beside Max, Barinov uses "shef-povarom" ("head chef", from "shef-povar") to make the point stick.
- 'In a year every restaurant in Moscow will be chasing after him.' Touching on the restaurant beside Max, Barinov chooses "restorany" ("restaurant", from the root "restoran") so Max hears the exact meaning.
- 'Because right now he is me in my youth.' Barinov jumps in openly without any doubt, before Nagiyev can change the subject.
- 'Think how much you will save if you sign him now.' Barinov counters naturally right here and brings in "sekonomish" ("to save", from "sekonomit") as the key Russian word here.
- 'So, Maxim, now you are the new head chef of Monet.' Bringing up the new head on the spot, Nagiyev links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
- 'Really? I am the new head chef of Restaurant Monet?' Max checks eagerly about what Barinov just brought up.
- 'Make sure you do not let me down there, Oguzok!' Barinov continues earnestly with Max and uses "podvedi" ("to let down", from "podvesti") to make the point stick.
- 'Thank you so much, Chef, may I hug you?' Pointing to the thank to Barinov, Max leans on "mozhno" ("it is possible, one may"), which carries the whole predicate in Russian without needing "it is".
