## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Oh, Nikita, what are you doing here?' Around the fish and work, Lyova wants to know where Barinov really stands.
- 'I stained my shirt, so I stopped in to wash it.' Nikita tells Barinov that he stained his shirt so he stopped in to wash it without dressing it up.
- 'And why did you come down here, Lyova?' Wondering why Barinov comes down here is how Nikita puts Barinov on the spot. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
- 'I also came to wash my uniform.' Mentioning that he also came to wash his uniform moves the talk in a new direction.
- 'Maybe you can come back later, after me?' Asking whether maybe Barinov can come back later after him puts Barinov right on the defensive. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
- 'No, I cannot walk around the dining room with a stain.' By refusing to walk around the dining room with a stain, Nikita keeps his side of the story grounded.
- 'And you can, the guests do not see you.' Explaining that Barinov can the guests do not see Barinov lays the cards on the table for Barinov.
- 'I am not allowed to either under sanitary rules.' Here being not allowed to either under sanitary rules sets up the next turn in their exchange. There is no "you" needed in the Russian here: one short word states the rule for everyone in the room.
- 'Let's each prepare half of the fish.' Since they should each prepare half of the fish, Barinov does not waste words on excuses. Starting with "davay" is the everyday Russian way to say "come on, let us do this".
- 'It is a shame to waste a whole fish on your humiliation.' Explaining that it is a shame to waste a whole fish on Lyova's humiliation lays the cards on the table for Lyova.
- 'Well, and which one of us is the lovebird now?' Wondering if which one of them is the lovebird now shows Nikita caught off guard. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'Agreed, one half will be plenty for me.' Realizing that agreed one half will be plenty for him changes how Barinov handles the situation.
