## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Look at what our Lyova is writing there!' Fedya follows up sharply with Lyova and puts the weight on "pishet" ("is writing", from "pisat") as the exchange unfolds.
- 'Lyova is completely winning me over with his messages.' Addressing the letters to Lyova, Gulya uses the little word "prosto" ("simply, just") to make the point sound effortless.
- 'Just a little bit more, and I will melt completely.' Gulya proceeds plainly on the spot, leaving no doubt about where she stands.
- 'It turns out he is a very versatile man.' Pointing out how versatile he is before Fedya, Gulya leans on "raznostoronniy" ("versatile, well-rounded") to give the whole sentence its punch.
- 'Did you know that he has a blue belt in karate?' Opening up candidly in the exchange, Fedya puts the question word "chto" right at the start so Gulya hears the focus immediately.
- 'And he also studied at a music school!' Fedya responds at once around Gulya, steering the talk right where he wants it.
- 'He played in the violin class there.' Turning to the violin at once, Fedya lands on "skripki" ("violin", built on "skripka") to keep the statement grounded.
- 'A violinist and karate fighter, I would go see that movie!' Mentioning a violinist before Gulya, Fedya brings in "skripach" ("violinist") to make the meaning unmistakable.
- 'That is why you have such strong, handsome hands.' Gulya speaks up plainly hearing Fedya, watching Fedya's face to see how the news lands.
- 'Thank you, Gulya, I am very pleased to hear that.' Pointing to the thank with Gulya, Lyova lands on "spasibo" ("thank you") as the key everyday word here.
- 'Go on, Katya, spoil your nice guy a little.' Pointing to the boyfriend in this beat, Fedya starts with "davay" ("come on, let's") to invite Lyova right into the action.
- 'Let's meet up this evening after the shift.' Gulya proceeds pointedly without holding back, without trying to dress up how she really feels.
