## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Quiet everyone, I need complete silence!' "Mne nuzhna" is "to me needed", and "nuzhna" ends in -a to match "tishina", "silence". For a table it would be "nuzhen", for milk "nuzhno". Barinov shouts for quiet, which is a nice paradox.
- 'Chef, we have already turned off all noisy equipment.' Only the front changes between off and on: "vyklyuchili", switched off, and "vklyuchili", switched on. "Shumnye", noisy, comes from "shum", noise. Lyova reports it in short plain words.
- 'Be quiet, I demand absolute calm in the kitchen.' "Molchat" is the plain dictionary form, "to be silent", used as an order, and it sounds like a drill sergeant. "Trebuyu" is "I demand". He is not asking.
- 'All the cooks fell silent and are standing in place.' "Zamolchali" is "molchat", "be silent", with za- in front, which marks the moment it starts: "went quiet". "Na meste" is "on the place", from "mesto".
- 'Do not breathe, I want to hear every quiet sound.' "Ne dyshat" is another order in the dictionary form, with "ne" in front. "Kazhdy" is "every", and "zvuk" is "sound", easy to remember. The kitchen holds its breath.
- 'Are you sure our little guest is here?' "Gost" is a guest; "gostya" is a female guest. "Vy uvereny", "you are sure", is the polite form Lyova uses with the chef. He is sceptical, but careful about it.
- 'She definitely hid somewhere in this dining room.' "Gde-to", "somewhere", is "gde", "where", with "-to" tacked on, which turns the question into "some place". "Spryatalas" is "hid herself", with the woman's -la.
- 'I hear a strange faint rustling near the table.' "Lyogky", "faint" here, also means "light" and "easy". "Shorokh" is a rustle. "Vozle stola" is "near the table": after "vozle", "stol" becomes "stola".
- 'Who is standing there near the big refrigerator right now?' "Kholodilnik", "refrigerator", is built on "kholod", "cold". "Okolo" is another "near", and it also changes "kholodilnik" to "kholodilnika". Barinov has seen a shadow.
- 'There is no one there, only large boxes of groceries.' "Nikogo net" is "nobody isn't", a double negative, and it's correct Russian. "Korobki produktov" is "boxes of groceries", and "produktov" is the "of" form.
- 'Look carefully, the girl is sitting right under the table!' "Vnimatelno", "carefully", grows out of "vnimanie", "attention". "Pod stolom", "under the table", ends in -om, while "vozle stola" ended in -a. Barinov has sharp eyes when it matters.
- 'What joy, we finally found our missing one!' "Propazha", "the lost thing", comes from "propast", the same "propal" Max used when the chocolate vanished. "Kakoe schastie" is "what happiness", and the whole kitchen breathes out.
