## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Alisa, sit down at this table quickly.' "Sadis" is "sit down", meaning take a seat. Barinov used "sidi", "sit", for being seated. Russian also sits "behind" a table, so it's "za stol". Vika's tone is brisk.
- 'Aunt Vika, will we play here?' Children call any aunt "tyotya", and then the name. "Budem igrat" is "we will play", the "will be" trick again. It's a hopeful question from a girl who wants company.
- 'No, I need to work a lot right now.' "Mne nuzhno" is the "to me it's needed" pattern, and then "mnogo rabotat", "a lot to work". Vika puts it plainly. She has no time to play.
- 'And what am I to do here alone?' The girl says "odnoy" for "alone", a feminine form of the "odin" Louis used. It changes to suit a girl. And "chto mne delat" is the "what to me to do" from the knife scene.
- 'Sit quietly and play calmly.' "Posidi" starts with "po-", so it means "sit for a while", just as before. "Tikhonechko" is "quietly" with an extra tender ending, the way you talk to a child. It's gentle, but it's a command.
- 'Will important restaurant guests arrive soon?' "Skoro" is "soon", and "pridut" is "will come". It's the same word as the arriving banquet order, in the plural. Alisa asks as if she runs the place.
- 'Yes, a big banquet will begin soon.' "Nachnyotsya" is "will begin", with a -sya that means "begins by itself". "Bolshoy banket" is "a big banquet", almost like the English. Vika is giving a straight answer.
- 'I promise to behave very well.' "Obeshchayu" is "I promise". And "vesti sebya" is "to lead oneself", the Russian way to say "behave". It's the same word as "lead through" in the earlier scene about spending time.
- 'You definitely will not let me down today?' "Tochno" means "exactly" or "for sure". "Ne podvedyosh" is "you won't let me down", from "podvesti", another relative of "vesti", "to lead", like "vesti sebya" a moment ago.
- 'I will be calmness itself, honest word!' "Samo" is "itself", and it matches the neuter word for calm. "Chestnoe slovo" is "honest word", a promise on honour. Alisa uses every big phrase she knows.
- 'You are a real clever girl, dear niece.' "Umnitsa" is a clever girl, and it's also what you say to a child who has done well. "Dorogaya" means both "dear" and "expensive", just as in old English "dear" could mean costly.
- 'I will simply draw pictures quietly.' "Prosto" is "simply", as before. "Risovat" is "to draw", and "kartinki" are little pictures. Alisa plays the good girl well, and she sounds sincere.
