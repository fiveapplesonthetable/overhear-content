## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Chef, I have almost finished this cake.' "Zakonchil", "finished", ends in the man's -l, and "pochti" is "almost". It's a modest, tired sentence from a pastry chef who has been working all day.
- 'Show it to me, Louis.' "Pokazhi" is the same short command the chef used about Alisa's face. "Pokazhi mne ego" is "show me it", with no "please". Barinov wants to look first.
- 'I think the cake can be served soon.' "Mozhno", "it's possible" or "allowed", is the opposite of "nelzya" from the chocolate talk. "Podavat" is "to serve" a dish. Louis says it with caution.
- 'Wait, what on earth is this?' The Russian is "eto eshchyo chto takoe", "this still what such", and "eshchyo" adds the "on earth". "Podozhdi" is "wait", said to one person. Barinov has seen it, and it can't be good.
- 'Oh, this is an iron clamp!' "Zheleznaya", "iron", grows from "zhelezo", the metal, and ends in -aya to match "strubtsina", "clamp". A tool in a banquet cake is a disaster.
- 'Where did it come from in the cream?' "Otkuda" is the third "where", "from where", after "gde" and "kuda". "Vzyalas" literally says "took itself", the Russian way to ask how something turned up.
- 'This is a celebration cake for the guests!' "Prazdnichny", "festive", grows from "prazdnik", "holiday". "Dlya gostey" is "for the guests". Barinov is outraged, because a tool in the cake would ruin the whole evening.
- 'Probably the little girl was playing here.' "Navernoe" is "probably". "Igrala", "was playing", has the girl's -la. Louis names the culprit without saying her name, but he means Alisa.
- 'Did Alisa touch your cake?' "Trogala" is "touched", with a girl's -la. There is no question word, only the voice rising at the end. Barinov's anger is already building.
- 'Chef, calm down, no need to shout.' "Spokoyno", "calmly", works alone as "calm down". "Ne nuzhno krichat" is "not needed to shout", the "nuzhno" pattern again. Louis tries to soothe.
- 'I will quickly fix everything right now.' "Ispravlyu", "I will fix", grows from "pravy", "right", so it means "make right". "Vsyo" is "everything". Louis knows how to rescue a cake.
- 'Make it pretty so the guests notice nothing.' "Sdelay krasivo" is "make beautifully". "Chtoby" is "so that". And "nichego ne zametili" is "nothing not noticed", another double negative.
- 'I promise, the cake will be perfect.' "Obeshchayu" is "I promise". "Idealnym" is the word "ideal" with a Russian ending, the one that comes after "budet", "will be". Louis keeps his word.
