## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Vika, pour a little water, please.' "Naley" is "pour", as a bare command. "Nemnogo vody" is "a little of water", with an ending that means "some of". And "pozhaluysta" is both "please" and "you're welcome". It's a very useful word.
- 'Kostya will pour for you, and I have no time.' "Tebe nalyot" is "to you will pour". "Mne nekogda" is "to me no-time", one little word where English needs three. Vika is busy, and she's quick about it.
- 'Wow, I did not know about your children!' "Ogo" is the sound of surprise, like "wow". Max says "ya ne znal" with the man's -l on the end. Then it's "about your children", which has its own plural ending.
- 'This is not my daughter, but my niece.' "A" after "ne" means "but rather". Max would say "moy" for a masculine thing, but a daughter is feminine, so it's "moya". The russian for niece is "plemyannitsa". Vika sets him straight.
- 'She looks very much like you externally.' Max says "pokhozha na tebya" again, the same words Alisa used in the office. "Vneshne" means "on the outside". This is a kind of compliment, and a nosy one.
- 'Her name is Alice, and she is a good girl.' Russian says "her they-call Alisa", with no "name" in it at all. "Khoroshaya devochka" is "good little girl". Vika, who is proud of her, says it lightly.
- 'And why is she talking about our chef?' "Pochemu" is "why", and "pro" is "about". "Nashego shefa" is "our chef", with an ending that shows he's the one being talked about. Max can't see why a child cares about the chef.
- 'Because our chef is her own dad!' "Potomu chto" is "because", seen in the dining room scene. "Rodnoy" means "own" in the sense of family, from the term for kin. So it's a real father, "dad by blood". Vika drops it in.
- 'Can it be that the chef is your close relative?' "Neuzheli" is "can it be?" and carries real disbelief. "Rodstvennik" is a relative, from the same word for kin as "rodnoy". Max is stunned, and it shows in the one word.
- 'That is completely none of your business, Max!' "Delo" is the "matter" or "business" from the earlier question "what's the matter". Here it is "not your matter".
- 'Now I understand many strange things.' "Teper" is "now", and "ponimayu" is "I understand". "Mnogie strannye veshchi" is "many strange things". The chef's moods finally make some sense to him.
- 'Go to the kitchen and work quietly!' "Idi na kukhnyu" is "go to the kitchen", and the ending changes to show motion toward it. Earlier it was "na kukhne" for being there. "Molcha" is "without a word". Vika has had enough.
