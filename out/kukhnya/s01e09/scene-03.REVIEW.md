## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Chef, you need to go to the kitchen urgently.' Lyova says "vam", the polite "to you", and "nuzhno", "it's needed". Together it's "to you it's needed urgently to the kitchen". He doesn't even say "go". The direction is enough.
- 'Lyova, don't you see I am busy?' Barinov says "ty" to him, as a boss does. "Zanyat" is "busy", and there's no word for "am". In Russian you simply say "I busy", and that's the whole sentence.
- 'Guests in the dining room are waiting for special dishes.' Russian says "wait dishes", with no "for" at all. The dining room is "zal", which really means "hall". Lyova lists the problem as calmly as he can.
- 'I am playing with my daughter right now.' "Seychas" is "right now". "Doch", "daughter", becomes "docheryu" after "s", "with", just as "ty" became "toboy" in the last scene. He thinks this settles it.
- 'But the guests ordered the chef's dishes.' "Blyuda ot shefa" is literally "dishes from the chef", and that's how Russian menus mark the chef's own specials. "Zakazali" comes from "zakaz", "order", the word from the banquet scene.
- 'You are a wonderful cook and will manage yourself.' Barinov flatters him. "Spravishsya" is the same "manage" verb Louis used. It's a compliment and a brush-off in one, and "sam", "yourself", closes the door.
- 'They want specifically your signature meat.' Lyova says "vashe", the polite "your". "Firmenny", "signature", comes from the Russian for company, so it means the house speciality. "Imenno", "specifically", you've heard before.
- 'Cook our usual tasty meat yourself.' "Prigotov" is a bare command, "prepare", with no "you". It's the same verb as in the first scene. And he says "our" meat, as if the dish belonged to the whole kitchen.
- 'And what if the guests do not like the taste?' Russian turns this round: "if the taste will not please to the guests". The guests are the ones it's pleasing to. Lyova worries aloud, and "esli" is the "if".
- 'Add a little salt and fresh pepper.' Barinov's idea of expert advice is almost an insult. "Nemnogo" is "a little", literally "not much". "Soli" and "pertsa" are "of salt" and "of pepper", the "some of" ending.
- 'Chef, real chaos is starting in the kitchen.' "Khaos" you can read at once. Russian says "on the kitchen", "na kukhne", not "in", and "nastoyashchy" is "real", as in "a real friend".
- 'Close the door and bring order there.' "Zakroy" is a bare command, "close", and "navedi poryadok" is the fixed phrase for "sort things out", literally "bring order". Barinov wants the door shut more than he wants the order.
- 'Alright, chef, I will do everything myself.' "Khorosho" means "good", and here it's a tired "all right then". "Vsyo sdelayu" is "everything I'll do". Lyova gives in. He is loyal, and the kitchen is his now.
