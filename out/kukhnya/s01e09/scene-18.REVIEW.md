## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Louis, you are handling Alisa great.' The "cope with" word is the one Louis used in the first scene, when he said he couldn't manage alone. Now it's a compliment. Barinov flatters, and this time he means it.
- 'Chef, she was simply drawing on my face!' Louis protests, and we can imagine the marker lines. "On my face" has an ending that means "on", and the girl's "was drawing" has her -la on the end. He sounds more hurt than angry.
- 'Fine, let her stay here with you.' The "let" word is the same one Louis used when he said "let him wash dishes". Barinov is easy about it, as long as someone else is in charge. He leaves the child with the nearest adult.
- 'Just remember the main and strict rule.' The term for "main" is also "the main thing". "Strict" comes from the same family as the strict chef himself. He is serious now, because the rule is serious, and everybody knows it.
- 'Under no circumstances can she have whiskey!' Russian says "not under any circumstances", with the double negative. And the "not allowed" word is the one from the chocolate rule. The Russian for whiskey never changes its ending, since it's borrowed.
- 'Chef, I remember your strict words perfectly.' Louis says "your" the polite way, as he does with the chef. The Russian for "perfectly" is the same as "wonderfully". He is a calm man again, after the mess with the clamp.
- 'I prepared a magnificent cake for the important banquet.' The "prepared" word is the one from the first scene, now in the past with the man's -l. Louis is proud of his work, and rightly. He drops "banquet" in again as if it were a rank.
- 'The guests paid huge money for it!' "Money" is always plural in Russian, so it is "huge monies" there. Barinov states how much is at stake. And "paid" has the guests' plural ending. Everyone in the room has an opinion on the price.
- 'They gave a whole hundred thousand rubles!' "A whole" is the same as in "a whole half-hour". The hundred is a short, plain word, and the thousand follows. Louis gets to name the price, and it sounds enormous.
- 'They know that you are a famous pastry chef.' The Russian for "famous" is built from the term for "known". Barinov hands out a compliment, which is rare. Louis can enjoy it, and probably wants to frame it.
- 'French desserts always cost very much.' The Russian for "dearly" comes from the Russian for "dear", so "dear" and "expensive" are the same idea. Louis is proud to be French, and proud of the price. He is a happy man at last.
- 'The main thing is that all guests are satisfied.' The "so that" word returns from the baking lesson. "Satisfied" is the Russian for "pleased". Barinov names the point of the whole day, and he sounds like a chef again.
- 'Chef, our banquet will go at the highest level!' It's an idiom: "at the highest level" for "top-notch". The term for "go" is really "pass", the same one as in "how did it go". Louis finishes on a flourish.
