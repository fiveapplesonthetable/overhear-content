## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Viktor, good evening. Where is Alisa?' "Dobry vecher" is "kind evening", the sibling of "dobry den". And "gde Alisa" has no "is", as usual. Tanya is polite, and already suspicious.
- 'And she and I decided to stay.' Russian says "my s ney", "we with her", for "she and I". "Reshili ostatsya" is "decided to stay". Barinov invents a plan on the spot, and he sounds very sure, which is how you can tell.
- 'She will stay with me one more day.' "Pobudet" is "budet", "will be", with po- in front for "a while". "Denyok" is a tiny, friendly version of "den", "day", to make the request sound harmless.
- 'You do not mind, Tanya?' "Ty zhe ne vozrazhaesh" is "you after all don't object": the little "zhe" makes it half a question and half a plea. Barinov tries a friendly tone, and it's wobbling.
- 'Well, if she decided so herself.' Feminine endings run through this one: "sama", herself, and "reshila", decided, both end in -a because the girl decided. Tanya gives him a little room, but there's a warning in the "nu" at the start.
- 'Let us go, I will quickly say goodbye to her.' "Poydyom", "let's go", is the word Alisa used when she wanted to play. "Poproshchayus" is "I will say goodbye", ending in -s, the short -sya. She is not wasting time.
- 'What for? I will pass on everything myself!' "Zachem" is not quite "why": it means "what for", "to what purpose". "Peredam" is "I will pass on", literally "give across". Barinov is panicking, and the speed gives him away.
- 'She is having fun there in the kitchen.' "Razvlekaetsya" is "amuses herself", with -sya on the end. "Na kukhne" is "in the kitchen": Russian says "on the kitchen", and "kukhnya" becomes "kukhne".
- 'She asked me to say goodbye to you.' "Poka" here is "bye", the same word that means "while". "Prosila", "asked", ends in the woman's -la, because the daughter asked. Barinov is inventing messages from his daughter.
- 'Wait, what happened to her?' "Tak" at the start works like a snap: "right, so". "Chto s ney sluchilos" is "what with her happened", and "sluchilos" ends in -o because "chto" is the subject. She is not stupid.
- 'Everything is fine, she is just playing.' "Normalno" is the everyday "fine", the Russian "normal" with -o. "Igraet" is "is playing". A calm voice is the hardest thing to fake.
- 'And you still have not learned how to lie.' Literally, "to lie you so and not learned"; "tak i ne" means "never did, in the end". "Vrat" comes first because lying is the point. Tanya sounds fond and weary at the same time.
- 'Tanya, wait, I will explain everything!' "Podozhdi" is the same "wait" Barinov used on Louis. "Obyasnyu" is "I will explain". The scene ends mid-chase, which is a good place to end it.
