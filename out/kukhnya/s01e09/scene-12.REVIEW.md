## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Chef, the girl is not in the bathroom either.' Russian says "of the girl also no", and "no" here means "there is not". There's no "is" to drop, because the language never had one. Fedya reports it flatly.
- 'Where can she possibly be right now?' The words for "can be" also mean "maybe", so Barinov is half asking where, half thinking aloud. And the little filler that means "at all" returns from the knife scene.
- 'She definitely did not leave the restaurant building.' Fedya is sure, and he says it in the girl's past, with her -la ending. The term for the building is "zdanie", and "from the building" is "out of the building". He keeps it factual.
- 'Did you check recordings from all interior cameras?' Barinov says "you" the friendly way, which is how a chef talks to a cook. "Checked" ends in the man's -l. It's a question made only of a rising voice, as so many Russian questions are.
- 'The interior cameras were ordered to be turned off this morning.' The Russian says "it was ordered to switch off" and names nobody. That's how people dodge blame. And "this morning" is simply "in the morning", with the "in" inside the ending.
- 'What foolish person thought of doing that?' In Russian, "thought of" is really "guessed", so he's asking who guessed that this was a good idea. Barinov is indignant. He hasn't yet worked out that the answer is himself.
- 'You gave such a strange order yourself, Chef.' Russian has two words where English has "order". One is what you place in a restaurant. The other is a command. Fedya uses the command word, and "yourself" is the polite plural form.
- 'Now it definitely cannot get any worse.' It's the classic line that tempts fate. Russian says "worse already exactly to be cannot". The term for worse is a short, sharp one. Barinov says it with complete confidence.
- 'Her mother is calling you on the phone, Chef.' In Russian the phone rings to you: "to you rings her mother". Fedya sounds careful here, because he knows this is bad news. The ringing phone reappears from the pastry bench.
- 'Hello, Tanya, everything is fine with our daughter.' Barinov uses the phone word that only exists for phones. And "at us with the daughter" is the Russian way to say "we and our daughter". He lies without a pause.
- 'We will turn the whole kitchen upside down and find her!' Fedya says "we", pulling in the whole brigade. The Russian for "whole" has a feminine form here, since kitchen is feminine. It is the same word that makes "everything".
- 'Get out of here with your foolish advice!' The first words are a rude "get lost", literally "went out". And "with your own" uses what Russian calls "own" that you heard in the dining room. Barinov's temper wins again.
