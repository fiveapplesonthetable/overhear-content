## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Chef, the girl is not in the bathroom either.' "Devochki net" is "there's no girl": with "net" for "there isn't", "devochka" changes to "devochki". "Tozhe" is "also", and with a no it means "either".
- 'Where can she possibly be right now?' The words for "can be" also mean "maybe", so Barinov is half asking where, half thinking aloud. And the little filler that means "at all" returns from the knife scene.
- 'She definitely did not leave the restaurant building.' "Ne vykhodila" has the woman's -la ending. "Iz zdaniya restorana" is "out of the building of the restaurant": after "iz", "zdanie" becomes "zdaniya", and "restorana" is the "of".
- 'Did you check recordings from all interior cameras?' Barinov says "you" the friendly way, which is how a chef talks to a cook. "Checked" ends in the man's -l. It's a question made only of a rising voice, as so many Russian questions are.
- 'The interior cameras were ordered to be turned off this morning.' The Russian says "it was ordered to switch off" and names nobody. And "utrom", "this morning", is "utro", "morning", with an -om ending that alone means "in the".
- 'What foolish person thought of doing that?' In Russian, "thought of" is really "guessed", so he's asking who guessed that this was a good idea. Barinov is indignant. He hasn't yet worked out that the answer is himself.
- 'You gave such a strange order yourself, Chef.' English has one "order", Russian has two: "zakaz" is what you place in a restaurant, "prikaz" is a command. Fedya uses "prikaz". And "vy sami", "you yourself", is polite.
- 'Now it definitely cannot get any worse.' It's the classic line that tempts fate. Russian says "worse already exactly to be cannot". "Khuzhe", "worse", is worth learning together with "luchshe", "better".
- 'Her mother is calling you on the phone, Chef.' In Russian the phone rings to you: "vam zvonit", "to you rings". "Zvonit" is the same word as in "telefon zvonit", the phone that rang at the pastry bench.
- 'Hello, Tanya, everything is fine with our daughter.' "Allo" again, the hello you only say on the phone. "U nas s dochkoy" is "at us with the daughter", the Russian way to say "we and our daughter". He lies without a pause.
- 'We will turn the whole kitchen upside down and find her!' Fedya says "we", pulling in the whole kitchen. "Vsyu", "the whole", is shaped to fit "kukhnyu", a feminine word; it's a relative of "vsyo", "everything".
- 'Get out of here with your foolish advice!' "Poshyol von" is a rude "get out", literally "went out". "So svoimi", "with your own", is the same "svoy" Barinov used in "so svoim papoy".
