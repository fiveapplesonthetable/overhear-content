## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Louis, are you still working in the kitchen?' "Vsyo eshchyo", "still", is made from "vsyo", "everything", and "eshchyo", "yet". Max says "ty", so they are friends by now. "Rabotaesh" is "you work".
- 'Yes, I am making delicate chocolate cream.' "Nezhny" means "tender" or "delicate", and Russians use it for cream and for people. "Shokoladny" is "chocolate" turned into a describing word, "chocolatey".
- 'It seems to you that he is indifferent to everything.' "Tebe kazhetsya", "to you it seems", works like "tebe nravitsya". "Ravnodushen", "indifferent", is built from "ravny", "equal", and "dusha", "soul".
- 'Then you realize you know little about him.' "O nyom" is "about him": "on", "he", turns into "nyom", with an n at the front, as it often does after a small word. "Malo" is "little".
- 'Louis, do not take offense at my silly words.' "Ne obizhaysya" ends in -sya, "don't offend yourself". "Na moi glupye slova" is "at my silly words". He's calling his own words foolish, and that is brave.
- 'I was completely wrong about you.' "Ya byl" is "I was", the past of "to be", which Russian keeps even though it drops "am". "Ne prav" is "not right". "Naschyot tebya" is "regarding you".
- 'Nothing terrible, Max, I understand.' "Nichego strashnogo" again, the third time in this episode: Russians say it all the time. "Ponimayu" is "I understand". Louis forgives him quickly and warmly.
- 'Cooking such desserts is really difficult.' Russian says "gotovit takie deserty, eto realno slozhno", with "eto", "this", as a hinge. "Realno", "really", is a slangy word built from "real".
- 'Many think that these are simple sweets.' "Sladosti", "sweets", grows from "sladky", "sweet". "Mnogie" is "many people". Louis has heard this opinion too many times.
- 'But confectionery art requires great patience.' "Trebuet", "requires", is "trebuyut" from the last scene, now for one thing. "Konditerskoe" is from "konditer", pastry chef. And "terpenie" becomes "terpeniya" after "trebuet".
- 'Now I respect your work very much.' "Trud" is work as toil, and "trudny", "hard", comes from it. "Uvazhayu" is "I respect". It is the heart of the scene.
- 'Thank you, Max, try this fresh dessert.' "Poprobuy" is "try", and it's also "taste". "Svezhy", "fresh", is the word the croissants had in the first scene. Louis offers the dessert as a reward.
- 'How many discoveries still lie ahead for us.' After "kak mnogo", how many, the noun takes the plural "of" form, so "otkrytie" becomes "otkrytiy". In "nam predstoit", to us lies ahead, the discoveries are the subject and "us" sits in the "to" form, like "mne nekogda" earlier.
- 'And this is truly wonderful for us!' "Po-nastoyashchemu" is "truly", literally "in a real way". "Zdorovo", "great", is casual, and it comes from "zdorovy", "healthy". The episode ends on an easy note.
