## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Louis, are you still working in the kitchen?' "Still" is made from two words, "everything" and "yet". Max uses the friendly "you", so they are friends by now. It's a friendly question, asked late at night.
- 'Yes, I am making delicate chocolate cream.' The term for "delicate" means "tender", and Russians use it for cream and for people. Louis is calm, because he is doing what he loves best. The Russian for chocolate is an adjective here.
- 'It seems to you that he is indifferent to everything.' Russian says "to you it seems", with the same pattern as "is it pleasing to you". The term for "indifferent" is built from "equal" and "soul". Max is speaking about someone, and it sounds like himself.
- 'Then you realize you know little about him.' "About him" turns the "him" into "nyom", with an "n" at the front, as Russian often does after a small word. This is a thought said aloud, and it leads to the apology.
- 'Louis, do not take offense at my silly words.' "Don't be offended" ends with the -sya, "offend yourself". Max asks the way you do when you mean it. He's calling his own words foolish, and that is brave.
- 'I was completely wrong about you.' "I was" ends in the man's -l, the past of "to be", which Russian has even though it drops "am". "Not right" means wrong. Max does not make excuses, and that's why it works.
- 'Nothing terrible, Max, I understand.' The "no big deal" phrase turns up again. Russians say it all the time, and this is the third time we've heard it. Louis forgives him quickly and warmly.
- 'Cooking such desserts is really difficult.' Russian says "to cook such desserts, this is really hard", with a little "this" as a hinge. In Russian, "really" is built from "real". Max says the thing he now believes.
- 'Many think that these are simple sweets.' The term for sweets grows from the Russian for sweet. Louis has heard this opinion too many times, and he says it without bitterness.
- 'But confectionery art requires great patience.' In Russian, "requires" is the sibling of the "demand" from the last scene. Louis is happy to be taken seriously, and he says it with a straight face. It's an honest defence of his craft.
- 'Now I respect your work very much.' The Russian for work here means toil, and the term for "hard" comes from it. That is why a hard job feels like labour. Max says it clearly, and it is the heart of the scene.
- 'Thank you, Max, try this fresh dessert.' "Try" is also "taste" in Russian. And "fresh" is the word the croissants had in the first scene. Louis offers the dessert as a reward, and Max takes it.
- 'How many discoveries still lie ahead for us.' "To us" is one word, and "lies ahead" is another, so the sentence is spare. It is a gentle, open line, and it points forward. Max says it half to himself.
- 'And this is truly wonderful for us!' "Truly" is literally "in a real way". The Russian for "great" here, a casual one, comes from the term for health. The scene ends the episode on an easy note.
