## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Forgive me, I did not notice you. You broke my nose!' Eleonora drops the remark without pause: choosing the formal "prostite" ("forgive me") is how you apologize to a boss, a guest, or someone you address with respect.
- 'It hurts me too, I did not notice you.' Elena follows up promptly with a smile, refusing to let the moment slip past.
- 'You should watch where you are going!' Eleonora favors one telling word in earnest: "smotret" means "to watch", and it carries the main weight of what Eleonora is saying.
- 'What happened? How interesting this turned out!' Barinov gives this statement plainly here: the "-sya" ending on "poluchilos" ("worked out") turns the action inward or makes it happen between people naturally.
- 'The ex-wife nearly killed the future one.' Barinov opens up frankly as things stand, so Elena knows he is not just making small talk.
- 'Future? I am not in that status yet.' Elena picks this clear word at once: "budushchuyu" means "future" (from "budushchiy"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'Careful! Lena, are you alright?' Barinov lays out that thought without pause: notice how Russian needs no word for "am", "is", or "are" in the present — placing the words side by side says it all.
- 'I am fine. I bumped into her myself. Until tonight!' Elena responds honestly in this moment, saying out loud what both of them already know.
- 'Everything is fine, girls.' Barinov stresses one memorable piece on cue: "devochki" means "girl's" (from "devochka"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'You always ruin everything!' Eleonora makes that remark without pause: spoken Russian trims this down to just 4 words, dropping the extra helper words that English has to spell out.
- 'Today is Lena's birthday.' Barinov chimes in wryly to be clear, bringing things back to earth after the buildup.
- 'It is morning, and you are not celebrating yet?' Eleonora focuses on this telling element toward Elena: "otmechayesh" means "celebrate" (from "otmechat"), giving the line its exact edge.
