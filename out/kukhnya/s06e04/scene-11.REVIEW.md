## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hands off, is everything in order?' Questioning whether hands off is everything in order is how Barinov tests Eleonora's story. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'Watch, my mom is about to notice us as if by chance.' Here watching her is about to notice them as if by chance sets up the next turn in their exchange.
- 'Olivier, meet my mom Eleonora.' With the dish and sauce in play, Eleonora keeps this short reply brisk and direct.
- 'Very pleased to meet you.' Over the dish and the sauce, the guest answers Barinov without a second thought.
- 'And I am very pleased too, join us.' Saying that she is very pleased too join them is how Eleonora sets the tone with Barinov.
- 'By the way, thank you for moving me to the luxury suite.' Around the dish and sauce, the guest sets a calm, everyday tone with Barinov.
- 'The suite is simply magnificent.' The guest mentions that the suite is simply magnificent so Barinov sees what is at stake.
- 'Do you treat all guests like this?' Asking whether Barinov treats all guests like this is how the guest puts Barinov on the spot. Even in the middle of the drama, he keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
- 'All our guests receive only the very best.' With the dish and table in play, Eleonora keeps this short reply brisk and direct.
- 'Good afternoon, I am the head chef of this restaurant.' Barinov explains that he is the head of this restaurant, keeping his voice calm and direct.
- 'Here is your fillet of wild pigeon in sauce.' Realizing that here is Eleonora's fillet of wild pigeon in sauce changes how Barinov handles the situation. Notice how he sticks to the formal "you", keeping a professional line between them.
- 'Thank you very much, Viktor.' Around the dish and conversation, the guest sets a calm, everyday tone with Barinov.
