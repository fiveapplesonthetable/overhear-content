## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hands off, is everything in order?' Focusing on the order during the talk, Barinov keeps normal statement word order here, turning the line into a question for Eleonora purely through rising pitch.
- 'Watch, my mom is about to notice us as if by chance.' Eleonora continues bluntly toward Barinov, shifting the mood of the conversation in a single beat.
- 'Olivier, meet my mom Eleonora.' Underscoring the mum right here, Barinov reaches for the Russian word "poznakomsya" ("to get acquainted", built from "poznakomitsya") right at the center of the line.
- 'Very pleased to meet you.' Reacting calmly beside Barinov, the "-sya" ending on "poznakomitsya" ("to meet") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'And I am very pleased too, join us.' Eleonora speaks candidly hearing the guest, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'By the way, thank you for moving me to the luxury suite.' Turning to the thank with Eleonora, the guest reaches for the Russian word "spasibo" ("thank you") right at the heart of the line.
- 'The suite is simply magnificent.' Highlighting how magnificent things are around Eleonora, the guest uses the little word "prosto" ("simply, just") to make the point sound effortless.
- 'Do you treat all guests like this?' The guest tests straight out before Eleonora can change the subject.
- 'All our guests receive only the very best.' Discussing the visitors at once, Eleonora lands on "luchsheye" ("best", built on "luchshiy") to keep the statement grounded.
- 'Good afternoon, I am the head chef of this restaurant.' Mentioning the head beside Eleonora, Barinov links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
- 'Here is your fillet of wild pigeon in sauce.' Barinov follows up sharply hearing the guest, grounding the moment in plain reality.
- 'Thank you very much, Viktor.' The guest plainly speaks this word of thanks toward Barinov using "spasibo" ("thank you") — the everyday word for "thank you", which grew out of the old blessing "spasi Bog" ("God save you").
