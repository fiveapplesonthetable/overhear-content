## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Lyova, it is good that you stood up.' Barinov proceeds decisively to Lyova and chooses "khorosho" ("good, fine") so Lyova gets the point right away.
- 'I must make an important announcement.' Mentioning the statement at once, Barinov centers the remark on "zayavleniye" ("statement") without any extra fuss.
- 'I am no longer working as head chef.' Barinov jumps in calmly on the spot, making his position completely clear.
- 'I am not worried about the restaurant.' Talking about the restaurant with Gulya, Barinov uses "restoran" ("restaurant") to carry the main weight of the thought.
- 'Lyova is a great professional and friend.' Checking on a great professional around Lyova, Barinov links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
- 'He is my very best friend.' Barinov steps in lightly during the talk, so Gulya knows he is not just making small talk.
- 'The time has come for you to become head chef.' Gulya answers pointedly in the exchange and reaches for the Russian word "pora" ("it's time") right at the heart of the line.
- 'Chef, forgive me, I cannot.' Lyova jumps in candidly to Barinov and chooses "prostite" ("excuse me", from the root "prostit") so Barinov hears the exact meaning.
- 'I am flying to Bishkek right now.' Lyova follows up decisively without a pause, leaving no doubt about where he stands.
- 'I am flying away, perhaps for five years.' Lyova answers dryly in this beat and brings in "uletayu" ("am flying away", from "uletat") as the key Russian word here.
- 'Excuse me for this decision.' Lyova plainly shares this polite apology beside Gulya using "izvinite" ("excuse me") — the respectful form of "excuse me" or "sorry" used when stepping in or apologizing.
- 'Viktor Petrovich, this is true love.' Gulya cuts in steadily on the spot, refusing to let the moment slip past.
