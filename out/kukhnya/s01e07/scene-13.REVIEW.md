## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Max, we urgently need to clarify something.' 'Something or other' is 'what' with a 'koye' in front. 'Clarify' is based on 'clear'.
- 'Guys, I really do not have time for you now.' 'Guys' is a friendly word for a group. 'Not up to you' is the idiom for 'I haven't time or mood for you'.
- 'What did you tell Nastya about me?' 'told' has a prefix that means 'a lot, and not nice'. 'To Nastya' puts an 'e' on the name.
- 'I was just joking a little, do not be mad.' 'Joked' comes from 'a joke'. 'Don't be mad' ends in 'sya'.
- 'Joking? You called me a real psycho!' 'Called' takes a 'with' form, so 'real psycho' ends in 'om'. 'Joking?' is just a repeat of the word with the voice up.
- 'I wanted to protect you from mistakes.' 'Protect' comes from 'guard'. 'From mistakes' is 'from' with a plural.
- 'First you promised to help, and then bailed!' 'Threw' is the slang for 'let down'. 'Promised' has a prefix that makes it a one-off.
- 'I did not want to hurt you, Kostya.' 'Hurt' comes from 'an offence'. 'Did not want' is the past he-form.
- 'Because of your stupid words Nastya left.' 'Because of' is the blame form, as opposed to a thank-you one. 'Left' ends in 'a' since Nastya is a woman.
- 'Forgive me, I will fix everything today.' 'Forgive' is the informal command. 'Fix' comes from 'right', so it's 'make right'.
- 'Never poke your nose into my business again!' 'Climb' is 'to poke'. 'My business' is 'my affairs', and Russians say it in the plural.
- 'Kostya, wait, let us discuss everything calmly.' 'Wait' is the casual command. 'Calmly' comes from 'calm'.
- 'Some best friend you are, leave!' The first words make a sarcastic phrase: 'also to-me best friend is-called'. It's what you say when someone has just let you down.
