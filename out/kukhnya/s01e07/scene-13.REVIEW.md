## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Max, we urgently need to clarify something.' "Nam nuzhno", 'to us it's needed', turns 'we need' around, so the need happens to you. "Koye-chto", 'something', is chto with koye- in front, for a particular thing the speaker has in mind.
- 'Guys, I really do not have time for you now.' "Mne ne do vas", literally 'to me not up to you', means 'I've no time or patience for you'. "Rebyata", 'guys', is a friendly word for a group.
- 'What did you tell Nastya about me?' "Nagovoril" adds na- to govorit', and suggests saying a lot, usually nasty things about someone. "Naste" is Nastya in her 'to' form.
- 'I was just joking a little, do not be mad.' "Poshutil", 'joked', comes from shutka, a joke, with po- for 'a little'. Ne zlis', 'don't be angry', ends in -s', short for -sya.
- 'Joking? You called me a real psycho!' "Nazvat'", to call someone something, puts the label in the 'with' form: nastoyashchim man'yakom. "Man'yak" is the same word as 'maniac'.
- 'I wanted to protect you from mistakes.' "Uberech'", to protect, is related to beregat', to guard or look after. After ot, 'from', oshibki becomes oshibok, the 'of' plural.
- 'First you promised to help, and then bailed!' "Kinul" is literally 'threw', and in slang it means 'ditched' or 'let down'. "Snachala"... a potom is 'first... and then'.
- 'I did not want to hurt you, Kostya.' "Obidet'", to hurt someone's feelings, is related to obida, an offence. "Khotel" ends in -l because Max is a man.
- 'Because of your stupid words Nastya left.' "Iz-za", 'because of', blames something, and the cause gets 'of' endings: tvoikh glupykh slov. "Ushla", 'left', has the -la of a woman's past.
- 'Forgive me, I will fix everything today.' "Prosti", 'forgive me', fits a friend; to the chef, the cooks said prostite. "Ispravlyu", 'I will fix', comes from pravyy, right, so it means 'make right'.
- 'Never poke your nose into my business again!' "Lezt'" means to climb or crawl, and ne lez' v is 'don't poke into'. Dela, 'affairs', is plural, the way Russians talk about someone's business.
- 'Kostya, wait, let us discuss everything calmly.' "Podozhdi", 'wait', is for one friend; to the chef it would be podozhdite. "Davay obsudim" is 'let's discuss', with davay doing the work of 'let's'.
- 'Some best friend you are, leave!' "Tozhe mne"... nazyvaetsya is a sarcastic set phrase, roughly 'and that calls itself a best friend'. "Ukhodi" is the blunt command 'go away'.
