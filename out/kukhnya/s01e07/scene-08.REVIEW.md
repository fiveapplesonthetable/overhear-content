## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Nastya, do you know where to rent a flat?' The word a tenant uses for 'rent' is the one that also means 'take off' or 'take down', so it's 'where to take a flat'. There is no 'should': 'where to rent' is just two words.
- 'I was looking for a place myself this morning.' There's no 'I' at all, only 'myself was looking', and the 'a' tells you it's a woman. 'A place' is the Russian way to put housing, from 'to live'.
- 'And did you manage to find a room?' 'To you it succeeded' is how Russian says 'did you manage'. It's a smooth chunk to know.
- 'Kostik helped me, I am moving to his place.' The pet form of a name adds a soft 'ik', and it signals warmth. 'Moving' starts with a piece meaning 'across', as if you'd ride across town to a new home.
- 'Wait, Kostya invited you to live together?' 'Wait' is a casual 'hold on'. 'Invited' is literally 'called', so he 'called you to live together'.
- 'Yes, we are dating, after all.' The small word 'ved' means 'after all' and adds a feeling of 'it's obvious'. 'Dating' ends in 'sya' and literally means 'meeting each other'.
- 'To be honest, I do not envy you at all.' Russians envy 'to' someone, so 'you' appears in the 'to' form. 'Not at all' puts the 'at all' word in front of the 'not'.
- 'Why? Kostya is such a sweet guy.' 'Sweet' is the word that Russians use for a lovable person. 'Such' goes in front, and 'guy' is the casual word for a young man.
- 'At night he snores terribly and loudly!' 'Terribly' comes from 'fear'. 'At night' is the 'yu' form again.
- 'Oh, I cannot stand snoring at all!' 'I can stand' means 'I can bear'.
- 'Think well before agreeing.' 'Before' is a two-word phrase. 'Agreeing' ends in 'sya'.
- 'You are right, I will look for another option.' 'Right' has a short form that is used for 'you are right'. 'I will look for' has a prefix that means 'for a while'.
