## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Nastya, do you know where to rent a flat?' A tenant rents with snyat', which also means to take down or take off. "Gde snyat'" is just 'where to rent'; Russian needs no 'should' here.
- 'I was looking for a place myself this morning.' Nastya drops 'I' and starts with sama, the feminine 'myself', and iskala ends in -la for a woman. "Zhilyo", a place to live, came up in Max's chat with Kostya.
- 'And did you manage to find a room?' Tebe udalos', 'to you it succeeded', is Russian for 'you managed'. It works for any success: mne udalos', I managed.
- 'Kostik helped me, I am moving to his place.' "Pomog", 'helped', is a man's past tense with no -l, and it takes mne, 'to me'. K nemu, 'to him', means to his place.
- 'Wait, Kostya invited you to live together?' "Pogodi" is a casual 'hang on'. "Pozval" is literally 'called', so Kostya 'called you to live together'.
- 'Yes, after all he and I are dating.' My s nim, 'we with him', means 'he and I'. "Ved'" adds 'after all', as if it were obvious.
- 'To be honest, I do not envy you at all.' "Zavidovat'", to envy, is aimed at a person with tebe, 'to you': you envy 'to' someone. "Sovsem ne" means 'not at all'.
- 'Why? Kostya is such a sweet guy.' "Milyy" is 'sweet' or 'nice' for a person, and takoy milyy is 'such a sweet'. "Paren'" is the casual word for a young man.
- 'At night he snores terribly and loudly!' "Strashno", 'terribly', shares a root with strakh, fear. "Khrapit", 'snores', comes from the same root as khrap, snoring.
- 'Oh, I cannot stand snoring at all!' "Vynosit'" is literally to carry out, but ne vynoshu means 'I can't stand'. "Khrap" is the noun for snoring.
- 'Think well before agreeing.' "Prezhde chem", 'before', is followed by the 'to' form: prezhde chem soglashat'sya, before agreeing.
- 'You are right, I will look for another option.' "Ty prav" is said to a man; to a woman it would be ty prava. Variant is a false friend: it means 'option'.
