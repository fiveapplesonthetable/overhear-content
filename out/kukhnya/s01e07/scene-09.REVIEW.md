## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Cooks, quickly come over here, everyone!' 'Cooks' is the plural that ends in 'a'. 'All' is placed near the end for force.
- 'What did you cook, chef?' 'Prepared' is in the plural because Max is polite. 'What' is the first word.
- 'Everyone taste the new sauce. Quickly!' The 'we' form of the word is used as an order, so it's 'let us all taste'. 'Sauce' is borrowed.
- 'Lyova, you taste very first.' 'The very first' is 'most first'. 'You taste' ends in 'esh'.
- 'Chef, this is incredibly delicious!' 'Incredibly' is built from 'not' and 'credible'. 'Delicious' comes from 'taste'.
- 'And now it is your turn, Oguzok.' 'Turn' is also the word for a queue. 'Oguzok' is the chef's name for Max.
- 'Haven't you tasted anything like this in Voronezh?' 'Anything like this' is the compact word 'such' in the 'of' form. 'In Voronezh' ends in 'e'.
- 'Amazing sauce, Chef, pure delight!' 'Amazing' comes from 'shake'. 'Delight' is a strong, old word.
- 'I have never tasted anything better.' 'Never' and 'nothing' and 'not' all show up. 'Better' follows 'nothing'.
- 'Learn while I am kind today!' 'While' is the word that also means 'bye'. 'Learn' ends in 'sya'.
- 'I genuinely envy our guest.' 'Genuinely' comes from 'sincere'. 'Envy' is followed by 'guest' in the 'to' form.
- 'Stop chatting, arrange a beautiful presentation.' 'Enough' is followed by 'chat'. 'Arrange' is a polite plural command.
- 'We will make everything perfectly, Chef!' 'We will do' ends in 'em'. 'Perfectly' is a plain word.
