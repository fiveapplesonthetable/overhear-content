## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Have you completely lost your mind?' S uma soshyol is literally 'came down off the mind'. "Ty chto?" at the front is a blunt 'what's wrong with you?'.
- 'Why are you stealing our vinegar?' "Vorovat'", to steal, shares its root with vor, a thief. Zachem asks 'what for', not just 'why'.
- 'Its expiration date will end soon!' "Srok" is a time limit, so here it's the use-by date. U nego, 'at him', refers to the vinegar, because uksus is masculine.
- 'And at home my children want to eat.' "Doma" means 'at home'; heading home would be domoy. "Yest'", 'to eat', is spelled just like yest' in u menya yest', 'I have'.
- 'Of course, the children at home really ask for vinegar!' Fedya throws Senya's words back at him. "Konechno", 'of course', is spelled with ch but said with sh: koneshno.
- 'I just took a little for salad.' "Vzyal", 'took', is the past of vzyat'. After dlya, 'for', salat picks up an -a: dlya salata.
- 'The chef will see and tear off your hands!' "Otorvyot" joins oto-, 'off', to rvat', 'to tear'. 'I'll tear your hands off' is a stock threat, not a literal one.
- 'Fedya, don't tell the chef anything, please!' "Shef" becomes shefu, 'to the chef', for the person you tell. "Nichego", 'nothing', comes with ne: 'don't tell nothing'.
- 'The chef is just leaving for the market now.' "Kak raz" means 'just' or 'right at that moment'. "Ukhodit" begins with u-, which means going away.
- 'Great, that means we have a little time.' "Znachit", 'that means', is again doing the work of 'so'. "Vremya", time, has an odd 'of' form, vremeni, used here after nemnogo, 'a little'.
- 'The chef will return, and everything must be ready!' "Vsyo", 'everything', is neuter, so the words that follow are too: dolzhno and gotovo, both ending in -o.
- 'Let's quickly start cutting the meat together.' "Davay" plus a 'we' verb makes 'let's': davay nachnyom, 'let's start'. "Rezat'", to cut, you'll hear in every kitchen scene.
- 'Put the bottle back in its place and take a knife.' "Stav'", 'put', is for setting something upright, like a bottle. "Butylka" becomes butylku because Senya has to move it, and beri means 'take'.
- 'That is all, I am working and not stealing anymore!' On its own, vsyo, 'everything', means 'that's it, done'. "Bol'she ne", 'more not', gives you 'not anymore'.
