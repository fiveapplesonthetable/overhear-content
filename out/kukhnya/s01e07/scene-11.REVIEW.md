## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Well, let's try your famous signature sauce.' "Nu-ka" is a nudge, like 'come on then'. "Firmennyy", 'signature', comes from firma, a company, so it means the house's own.
- 'This sauce is true high culinary art.' "Iskusstvo", art, is neuter, so nastoyashcheye, vysokoye and kulinarnoye all end in -oye. The dash in writing is where English would put 'is'.
- 'Oh, the fresh bread fell on the floor.' "Upal" ends in plain -l because khleb, bread, is masculine. Na pol is 'onto the floor'; lying on the floor would be na polu.
- 'Right now we will quickly bring you new bread.' "Prinesyom" joins pri-, arrival, to nesti, to carry. Tebe, 'to you', is the ty form; Barinov and the owner are old friends.
- 'No, I am in a big hurry for an important meeting.' "Speshu", 'I hurry', is the everyday way to say you're in a rush. "Vstrecha", a meeting, comes from vstrechat', to meet, and after na it becomes vstrechu.
- 'But you must taste this rare masterpiece!' "Rasprobovat'" adds ras- to probovat', so it means tasting until you really get the flavour. "Ty obyazan", 'you are obliged', is much stronger than plain 'must'.
- 'I will return at exactly seven o'clock tonight.' V sem' chasov is 'at seven hours', the clock pattern again. "Vechera", 'of the evening', marks p.m.
- 'We will prepare the very best sauce for you.' "Samyy luchshiy" is 'the very best', with samyy for emphasis. "Dlya", 'for', puts ty into the form tebya: dlya tebya.
- 'Agreed, Viktor, do not let me down tonight.' "Dogovorilis'" is 'we've agreed', the standard way to close a deal. Ne podvedite, 'don't let me down', ends in -te because it's aimed at Viktor and his whole kitchen.
- 'The Chef never deceives his favorite guests.' "Svoikh", 'his own', points back to the subject, the chef. "Lyubimyy", favourite, is related to lyubit', to love.
- 'Until tonight then, I wish you great work.' Do vechera, 'until the evening', is a friendly 'see you tonight'. After zhelayu, 'I wish', the wish takes 'of' endings: otlichnoy raboty.
- 'We await you in the kitchen with great joy.' "Zhdyom tebya", 'we wait for you', is a warm 'we're expecting you'. S bol'shoy radost'yu, 'with great joy', is s radost'yu from earlier with 'great' added.
