## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'All right, Dima, let's return to my old conditions.' Reflexive future verb governs the preposition 'to' plus dative plural.
- 'Remember, I kick you three times!' Present-tense verb 'beat/strike' takes the instrumental noun 'with a kick'.
- 'What a mean person you are after all, Vitya!' Nagiyev winces at having to honor his ridiculous promise.
- 'And a true gentleman always keeps his word.' Barinov invokes the code of a gentleman to insist on his kick.
- 'Wait, just not here and not right now!' Emphatic particles plead for a delay out of sight of the staff.
- 'Work first, please introduce yourself to our guests.' Reflexive imperative 'introduce yourself' takes the dative plural.
- 'Dear friends, I ask for a minute of your attention!' Out in the dining room, Nagiyev puts on his showman smile.
- 'Allow me to introduce our head chef Viktor Barinov!' Animate accusative endings mark the title and full name.
- 'Hello, Viktor, we are very glad to see you!' Gavril Ilyich beams upon seeing the real Chef Barinov.
- 'Thank you, Dima, you are dismissed for now.' Short masculine adjective 'free' dismisses the owner like an assistant.
- 'It is a debt of honor, there is nothing you can do about it!' Literally 'a card debt', which in Russian tradition is sacred.
- 'And remember, you still owe me two more times today!' The idiom 'from you two more' reminds Nagiyev of the remaining kicks.
