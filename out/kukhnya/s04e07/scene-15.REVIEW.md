## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Fedya, can you distract them for a moment?' In spoken Russian, the words stay in their normal statement order and Senya's rising voice at the end turns the line into a question.
- 'And meanwhile I will unnoticeably pour some sugar in there!' Senya pairs 'for now / bye / while' with 'sugar' directly, since spoken Russian skips English filler words like 'the', 'a', and 'is'.
- 'Just how am I supposed to distract them for you?' Notice how Russian puts 'how / as' and 'distract' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'Fine, give it here, I will distract them right now!' Fedya steps up to block German's line of sight.
- 'Enjoy your meal, taste our new hot dish!' Out in the dining room, the partridge course is served.
- 'The guest said that this is a completely different matter!' Idiom 'another matter' expresses enthusiastic approval.
- 'In every bite the hand of a master is immediately visible!' Short feminine adjective 'visible' agrees with 'hand'.
- 'I would say that I worked with both hands!' Here Chef Barinov anchors the thought on 'said' and finishes on 'hands', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'Senya, what are you doing near the soup right now?' Returning to the pass, Barinov spots Senya with the sugar bowl.
- 'And I thought, what if you need sugar?' Senya hides the spoon behind his back after neutralizing the vinegar.
- 'What do I need sugar for in hot onion soup?' Barinov has no idea that Senya just saved the soup from German's vinegar.
- 'Forgive me, Chef, I am already going back to my station!' Meanwhile, Denis's busy evening in the dining room begins.
