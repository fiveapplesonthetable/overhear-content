## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Enjoy your meal, respected Gavriil Ilyich!' Here Nagiyev anchors the thought on 'pleasant' and finishes on 'ilyich', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'I thank you, I will taste it with pleasure right now.' Formal first-person verb 'I thank' elevates the register.
- 'Tell me, did Barinov definitely cook this dish?' One spoonful is enough for the connoisseur to spot the difference.
- 'You know, we would rather celebrate in another place.' Notice how Russian puts 'know' and 'place' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'I beg you, this is just a small misunderstanding!' Nagiyev scrambles to keep the lucrative banquet from walking out.
- 'Right now we will fix everything very quickly!' In everyday Russian, 'right now' flows straight into 'will fix' without extra linking words.
- 'They will serve you a dish personally from Viktor Barinov.' Impersonal third-person plural future represents the restaurant service.
- 'I will personally oversee this strictly myself!' Notice how Russian puts 'personally' and 'to see to it, to keep an eye on' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'Well then, Mister Know-It-All, chop the tomatoes and egg!' Back in the kitchen, Barinov puts German on basic prep duty.
- 'Judging by everything, I am the one who won our bet!' In everyday Russian, 'judging' flows straight into 'won' without extra linking words.
- 'So put on an apron quickly and get to work!' Singular imperatives assert Barinov's total victory.
- 'Vitya, go to the kitchen quickly to save the banquet!' Before cooking, however, Barinov insists on collecting his first kick from Nagiyev.
