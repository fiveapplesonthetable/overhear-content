## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Enjoy your meal, respected Gavriil Ilyich!' Genitive phrase wishes the guest a pleasant appetite.
- 'I thank you, I will taste it with pleasure right now.' Formal first-person verb 'I thank' elevates the register.
- 'Tell me, did Barinov definitely cook this dish?' One spoonful is enough for the connoisseur to spot the difference.
- 'You know, we would rather celebrate in another place.' Prepositional phrase 'in another place' threatens losing the client.
- 'I beg you, this is just a small misunderstanding!' Nagiyev scrambles to keep the lucrative banquet from walking out.
- 'Right now we will fix everything very quickly!' First-person plural perfective future promises immediate action.
- 'They will serve you a dish personally from Viktor Barinov.' Impersonal third-person plural future represents the restaurant service.
- 'I will personally oversee this strictly myself!' The verb 'to oversee' governs the preposition 'behind/over' plus instrumental.
- 'Well then, Mister Know-It-All, chop the tomatoes and egg!' Back in the kitchen, Barinov puts German on basic prep duty.
- 'Judging by everything, I am the one who won our bet!' Gerund phrase 'judging by' introduces the obvious conclusion.
- 'So put on an apron quickly and get to work!' Singular imperatives assert Barinov's total victory.
- 'Vitya, go to the kitchen quickly to save the banquet!' Before cooking, however, Barinov insists on collecting his first kick from Nagiyev.
