## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Well how is it, Denis, did you accept all the groceries?' Senya checks whether the newcomer finished unloading.
- 'Listen, I made a huge mistake out there today.' Denis confesses his blunder to the veteran chef.
- 'I carried part of the groceries to our storeroom.' Denis pairs 'to carry to, take to' with 'storage' directly, since spoken Russian skips English filler words like 'the', 'a', and 'is'.
- 'And when I returned, the dog had eaten all the pigeons!' Notice how Russian puts 'when' and 'pigeons' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'Everyone knows that this dog is very voracious.' Descriptive feminine adjective means greedy or gluttonous.
- 'Maybe you will call our Timur right now?' In spoken Russian, the words stay in their normal statement order and Denis's rising voice at the end turns the line into a question.
- 'Let him quickly bring us more pigeons!' Denis pairs 'quickly' with 'pigeons' directly, since spoken Russian skips English filler words like 'the', 'a', and 'is'.
- 'Hello, Timur, we have run out of pigeons here.' Russians say 'by me' or 'with us' where English says 'I have' or 'we have', without needing a separate word for 'have'.
- 'What pigeons? I do not have any more!' Timur is already driving through busy Moscow traffic.
- 'If you need them so badly, go catch some on the street!' Timur makes a joke that Denis takes completely seriously.
- 'Senya, please cover for me for one hour!' Denis uses a polite everyday Russian courtesy expression that sets the tone of the conversation right away.
- 'Hello, Fedya, our client has gone out on the hunt!' Senya gleefully alerts Fedya that the rookie is chasing city pigeons.
