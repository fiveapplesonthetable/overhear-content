## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'And here is our signature onion soup!' Barinov offers the thought facing German: notice how Russian needs no word for "am", "is", or "are" in the present — placing the words side by side says it all.
- 'Simply fantastic, what an amazing and tender taste!' The guest chimes in firmly without any doubt, shifting the whole mood of the room in a single beat.
- 'Allow me to interrupt your lovely conversation!' German reaches for one concrete term toward Barinov: "razreshite" means "allow" (from "razreshit"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'Respected guest, they are brazenly deceiving you here!' German stresses this direct expression in reply: "uvazhayemyy" means "respected, esteemed", and it carries the main weight of what German is saying.
- 'And how did you like this onion soup?' German challenges calmly before Barinov can change the subject.
- 'The soup turned out very tasty, and what is the matter?' The guest anchors one concrete touch in reply: "poluchilsya" means "to turn out" (from "poluchitsya"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'But after all it is completely impossible to eat this soup!' German proceeds openly toward Barinov, leaving no doubt about where he stands.
- 'Right now it is as sour as pure vinegar!' German cuts in evenly before the guest, saying out loud what both of them already know.
- 'Allow me, taste it yourself from my bowl.' The guest points to this sharp term for Barinov: "poprobuyte" means "try, taste" (from "poprobovat"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'Well why did you go silent, it is a magnificent soup after all!' Barinov features one sharp term right here: "shikarnyy" means "splendid, luxurious", and it carries the main weight of what Barinov is saying.
- 'Forgive me, I do not understand anything at all right now.' German pushes back wryly without a pause, shifting the whole mood of the room in a single beat.
- 'Oh no, this simply cannot be!' German spotlights one classic choice right here: "prosto" means "just", and it carries the main weight of what German is saying.
