## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Nagiyev himself asked me to save today's banquet!' Barinov brags to the brigade before spotting German behind him.
- 'Because your new chef cannot handle it.' Putting the short word for 'not' right in front of the action word is all Russian needs to make a sentence negative, with no extra helper words like 'do' or 'did'.
- 'Interesting, why was I not invited to this conversation?' German steps forward with icy calm.
- 'Let's go to the office, quickly, we will settle everything there!' Barinov marches into his old office.
- 'Dima, explain to him who is in charge here today!' Chef Barinov pairs 'explain' with 'main' directly, since spoken Russian skips English filler words like 'the', 'a', and 'is'.
- 'Vitya, we agreed that you would simply come.' Notice how Russian puts 'agree' and 'will come' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'You will only show your face to the important guests.' Nagiyev only wanted Barinov as a figurehead for the client.
- 'We had no agreement about any cooking!' Russians say 'by me' or 'with us' where English says 'I have' or 'we have', without needing a separate word for 'have'.
- 'What is that you are saying to me right now, Dima?' Barinov is insulted at being treated like a waiter.
- 'Calm down, I am begging you very much!' Nagiyev knows how explosive Barinov's temper can be.
- 'Today our German Mikhailovich is cooking in the kitchen.' Present tense states the plan for the evening tasting.
- 'And you simply carry the dishes out nicely to the hall!' Barinov refuses to be reduced to a prop and challenges German to a bet.
