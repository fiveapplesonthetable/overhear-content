## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Dmitry Vladimirovich, an important guest has come to see us.' Turning to an important guest at once, Vika puts the emphasis on "vazhnyy" ("important") in the middle of the talk.
- 'Good morning, but our restaurant is still closed.' Nagiyev pushes back sharply during the talk, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'Allow me to introduce myself to you first, gentlemen.' Focusing on the gentlemen before Nagiyev, the guest leans on "predstavitsya" ("to introduce oneself") to give the whole sentence its punch.
- 'I have come on behalf of the famous film director.' Bringing up the director in the exchange, the guest chooses "kinorezhissyora" ("director") so Nagiyev gets the point right away.
- 'For the sake of filming I am ready to clear any dates!' Nagiyev continues steadily without a pause, so the guest knows he is not just making small talk.
- 'Thank you, but I am here on another matter.' Discussing the thank before Nagiyev, the guest chooses "spasibo" ("thank you") so Nagiyev gets the point right away.
- 'We want to hold a banquet in your restaurant.' Touching on the restaurant around Nagiyev, the guest chooses "restorane" ("restaurant", from the root "restoran") so Nagiyev hears the exact meaning.
- 'We had lunch at your place recently, we liked everything.' The guest cuts in wryly hearing Nagiyev, refusing to let the moment slip past.
- 'Everyone was delighted with Viktor Barinov!' Pointing to the delight in the exchange, the guest leans on "vostorge" ("delight", a form of "vostorg") to give the remark its edge.
- 'We eagerly look forward to meeting him again.' Discussing the meeting right here, the guest uses "neterpeniyem" ("to forward", from "neterpenit") to make the point stick.
- 'I will drop by this evening to taste the banquet menu.' The guest responds quietly during the talk, before Nagiyev can change the subject.
- 'You need not doubt it, Barinov will definitely be here!' Nagiyev steps in directly at once and centers the remark on "somnevatsya" ("to doubt") without any extra fuss.
