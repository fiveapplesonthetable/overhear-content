## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'One wise man once spoke important words.' Indefinite temporal adverb 'once/at some point' sets up the proverb.
- 'You can sit for a long time on the quiet bank of a river.' Short locative ending on 'bank' follows the preposition 'on'.
- 'And wait for your enemy to finally lose.' Conjunction 'when' introduces the awaited outcome.
- 'But sometimes you are sitting by the wrong river altogether.' Negated demonstrative 'not that' means 'the wrong one'.
- 'Vitya, I remember that I still owe you a debt.' Diminutive masculine noun 'little debt' softens the awkward moment.
- 'You are supposed to kick me two more times.' Nagiyev braces himself to pay off the rest of their bet.
- 'Well come on, kick me quickly, what are you waiting for?' Singular imperative 'beat/kick' invites Barinov to finish it.
- 'All right, Dima, just go home in peace.' Seeing his friend already defeated by the lost banquet, Barinov waves him off.
- 'What, are you not angry with me anymore?' Reflexive verb 'to be angry' takes 'on' plus the accusative pronoun.
- 'I do not want to kick you anymore, pal.' Barinov chooses friendship over petty revenge.
- 'Suddenly you realize that you do not want victory over your enemy.' Preposition 'above/over' governs the instrumental singular.
- 'After all, old friendship is much more important than any grudge.' Comparative adjective governs the genitive singular.
