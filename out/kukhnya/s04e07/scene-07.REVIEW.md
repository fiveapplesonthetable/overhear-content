## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hi everyone, my dear honest company!' Barinov greets his former brigade with a warm traditional phrase.
- 'I see you are all still holding a grudge against me?' In spoken Russian, the words stay in their normal statement order and Chef Barinov's rising voice at the end turns the line into a question.
- 'Forgive me for my bad behavior.' Chef Barinov uses a polite everyday Russian courtesy expression that sets the tone of the conversation right away.
- 'After all, I did not say all those things out of malice.' Idiom 'not out of evil' means without bad intentions.
- 'And life has already punished me for that.' Feminine past verb agrees with the feminine noun 'life'.
- 'Yes, I know that I am no angel.' Literally 'not a gift', a classic Russian idiom for a difficult person.
- 'But you are my closest people.' Notice how Russian puts 'the most' and 'people' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'I am too old to look for new friends.' Here Chef Barinov anchors the thought on 'to look for' and finishes on 'old', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'And besides, I do not need new friends.' Plural short adjective 'needed' agrees with 'new friends'.
- 'I also wanted to pay back my old debts to you.' Chef Barinov pairs 'still, yet, more' with 'debt; duty' directly, since spoken Russian skips English filler words like 'the', 'a', and 'is'.
- 'Chef, that is what you should have started with!' Senya grins as the tension in the kitchen melts away.
- 'We are very glad to see you home, Chef!' The brigade warmly welcomes their old master back.
