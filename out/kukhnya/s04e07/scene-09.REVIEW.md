## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'What, have you decided to make a clown out of me?' Notice how Russian puts 'decided' and 'clown' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'The guests came here to eat my food specifically!' Emphatic particle 'namely/specifically' highlights 'my food'.
- 'They will realize in a second that I was not the one who cooked it.' In everyday Russian, 'second' flows straight into 'cooked' without extra linking words.
- 'Well then, German, are you ready to make a bet?' Plural short adjective 'ready' matches polite address.
- 'German, I beg you, do not make a bet with him!' Nagiyev knows from bitter experience never to bet against Barinov.
- 'No, why not, I am doing this consciously.' Manner adverb 'consciously' shows German's cool confidence.
- 'I know all your old culinary secrets.' German believes he has mastered Barinov's recipes.
- 'Or are you afraid of losing this bet to me?' German needles Barinov by insulting his favorite football club.
- 'Fine, I bet you right now!' Present-tense 'we bet' is the standard Russian formula for sealing a wager.
- 'If the guests realize that I did not cook it...' Negation particle placed right before 'I' stresses who did the cooking.
- 'Today you will carry out all my orders!' Compound future tense expresses continuous obedience all evening.
- 'Deal, and if not, then you are the one who obeys!' Impersonal verb 'it suits/fits' means 'it is a deal'.
