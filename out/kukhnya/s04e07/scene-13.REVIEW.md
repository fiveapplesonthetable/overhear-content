## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'The signature onion soup for the guest is completely ready.' Masculine short adjective 'ready' agrees with 'soup'.
- 'Arseny, you have been my ears and eyes.' Here German anchors the thought on 'to be; there is' and finishes on 'eyes', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'Now the time has come to become my right hand.' In everyday Russian, 'now, nowadays' flows straight into 'hand' without extra linking words.
- 'I need one small favor from you.' Neuter short adjective 'needed' agrees with the neuter noun 'matter/favor'.
- 'Pour this sour vinegar into the onion soup.' Notice how Russian puts 'to pour' and 'soup' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'German Mikhailovich, I cannot do that!' Even prankster Senya balks at sabotaging Chef Barinov's dish.
- 'After all, that is not decent at all, right?' Manner adverb expresses a moral standard of behavior.
- 'Wait, Arseny, Barinov will leave here tomorrow already.' German switches to a cold, practical threat.
- 'And I will remain here as your head chef!' Notice how Russian puts 'will stay' and 'chef' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'And all of you still have to work with me.' Here German anchors the thought on 'still, yet, more' and finishes on 'to work', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'And for how long depends only on me!' In everyday Russian, 'how / as' flows straight into 'depends' without extra linking words.
- 'Fine, give me your vinegar here, I will pour it.' Caught between two bosses, Senya reluctantly takes the bottle of vinegar.
