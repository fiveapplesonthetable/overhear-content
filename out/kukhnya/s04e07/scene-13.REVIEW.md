## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'The signature onion soup for the guest is completely ready.' Masculine short adjective 'ready' agrees with 'soup'.
- 'Arseny, you have been my ears and eyes.' Instrumental plural nouns follow the past tense of 'to be'.
- 'Now the time has come to become my right hand.' Infinitive 'to become' governs the instrumental singular phrase.
- 'I need one small favor from you.' Neuter short adjective 'needed' agrees with the neuter noun 'matter/favor'.
- 'Pour this sour vinegar into the onion soup.' Polite plural imperative orders Senya to ruin the soup.
- 'German Mikhailovich, I cannot do that!' Even prankster Senya balks at sabotaging Chef Barinov's dish.
- 'After all, that is not decent at all, right?' Manner adverb expresses a moral standard of behavior.
- 'Wait, Arseny, Barinov will leave here tomorrow already.' German switches to a cold, practical threat.
- 'And I will remain here as your head chef!' Reflexive perfective future verb takes the instrumental role.
- 'And all of you still have to work with me.' Dative pronoun 'to you' with an infinitive expresses unavoidable necessity.
- 'And for how long depends only on me!' The verb 'depends' governs the preposition 'from/on' plus genitive.
- 'Fine, give me your vinegar here, I will pour it.' Caught between two bosses, Senya reluctantly takes the bottle of vinegar.
