## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Here are fresh chestnuts and pigeons for the chef.' Presentational particle introduces the gourmet ingredients.
- 'Do guests really eat pigeons in our restaurant?' Animate masculine plural takes the genitive-identical accusative 'pigeons'.
- 'Of course they eat them, after all it is expensive French game!' Collective feminine noun 'game' denotes wild birds used in cuisine.
- 'These chestnuts surprise me much more.' Comparative adverb 'much more' modifies the present-tense verb.
- 'Why people eat them, I do not know.' Purpose question word 'what for' introduces the fronted subordinate clause.
- 'All right, fresh herbs, chestnuts, chicken, and pigeons.' Denis checks off each item on the delivery list.
- 'And here are tasty little bones for the kind dog.' Diminutive plural noun expresses affection toward the stray dog.
- 'Eat calmly while I carry the crates inside.' Conjunction 'while' links the imperative with the perfective future verb.
- 'Shoo from here, bad dog, shoo!' Returning to the courtyard, Denis uses the special Russian interjection for chasing animals away.
- 'No, just do not touch those expensive pigeons!' Animate plural accusative endings mark the ruined delicacy.
- 'Please give me back at least one pigeon!' The stray dog finishes the last bird and trots away happily.
- 'Denis, where are my fresh groceries from the storeroom?' Denis realizes he is in deep trouble with Chef German.
