## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Here are fresh chestnuts and pigeons for the chef.' Timur emphasizes one telling word for Denis: "svezhiye" means "fresh" (from "svezhiy"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'Do guests really eat pigeons in our restaurant?' Denis voices that thought without fuss: starting the question with "razve" ("really?, is it true that...?") shows Denis is pushing back against what he just heard.
- 'Of course they eat them, after all it is expensive French game!' Timur opens up wryly across the room, to clear the air between them once and for all.
- 'These chestnuts surprise me much more.' Timur picks this sharp choice in earnest: "kashtany" means "pigeons", and it carries the main weight of what Timur is saying.
- 'Why people eat them, I do not know.' Timur counters candidly on the spot, watching Denis's face to see how the news lands.
- 'All right, fresh herbs, chestnuts, chicken, and pigeons.' Denis speaks plainly toward Timur, steering the talk right where he wants it.
- 'And here are tasty little bones for the kind dog.' Denis stresses one classic touch in reply: "kostochki" means "kind", and it carries the main weight of what Denis is saying.
- 'Eat calmly while I carry the crates inside.' Denis proceeds firmly watching Timur, without trying to dress up how he really feels.
- 'Shoo from here, bad dog, shoo!' Denis holds firm firmly without holding back, refusing to let the moment slip past.
- 'No, just do not touch those expensive pigeons!' Denis grounds one telling touch in earnest: "dorogikh" means "expensive" (from "dorogoy"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'Please give me back at least one pigeon!' Denis sends that point beside German: dropping in "pozhaluysta" ("please") keeps the request polite on the surface even in a brisk exchange.
- 'Denis, where are my fresh groceries from the storeroom?' German prompts evenly as Timur tries to explain the situation.
