## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Here are fresh chestnuts and pigeons for the chef.' Insisting on how fresh things are to Denis, Timur chooses "svezhiye" ("fresh", from the root "svezhiy") so Denis hears the exact meaning.
- 'Do guests really eat pigeons in our restaurant?' Touching on his restaurant right here, Denis starts the question with "razve" ("really?") to push back against what he just heard.
- 'Of course they eat them, after all it is expensive French game!' Timur reacts wryly with Denis, so Denis knows he is not just making small talk.
- 'These chestnuts surprise me much more.' Pointing to the pigeons in the exchange, Timur leans on "kashtany" ("pigeons") to give the whole sentence its punch.
- 'Why people eat them, I do not know.' Timur counters candidly in their talk, before Denis can change the subject.
- 'All right, fresh herbs, chestnuts, chicken, and pigeons.' Denis jumps in plainly beside Timur, to clear the air between them once and for all.
- 'And here are tasty little bones for the kind dog.' Mentioning the kind in this beat, Denis lands on "kostochki" ("kind") as the key everyday word here.
- 'Eat calmly while I carry the crates inside.' Pointing to the bins to Timur, Denis leans on "yashchiki" ("bins, crates", a form of "yashchik") to give the remark its edge.
- 'Shoo from here, bad dog, shoo!' Denis chimes in firmly across the room, so German knows he is not just making small talk.
- 'No, just do not touch those expensive pigeons!' Discussing the restaurant in front of German, Denis lands on "dorogikh" ("expensive", built on "dorogoy") to keep the statement grounded.
- 'Please give me back at least one pigeon!' Highlighting the pigeon in the exchange, Denis reaches for the Russian word "golubya" ("pigeon") right at the heart of the line.
- 'Denis, where are my fresh groceries from the storeroom?' German prompts evenly whether Denis really thought this through.
