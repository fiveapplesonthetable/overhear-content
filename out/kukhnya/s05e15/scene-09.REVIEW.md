## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'You know, Viktor, I have nothing right now.' The guest fires back honestly on the spot, to clear the air between them once and for all.
- 'I have neither a beloved wife nor loyal friends.' Talking about a beloved wife beside Barinov, the guest leans on "lyubimoy" ("favourite, beloved", a form of "lyubimyy") to give the remark its edge.
- 'But on the other hand I have one big dream.' Following up smoothly right here, the guest pivots with "zato" ("on the bright side, to make up for it") to flip a drawback into a plus.
- 'Very interesting, and what kind of dream do you have?' Barinov checks plainly where this conversation is heading next.
- 'Three hundred thousand rubles—that is my bright dream!' Stressing how light things are around Barinov, the guest chooses "svetlaya" ("light, bright", from the root "svetlyy") so Barinov hears the exact meaning.
- 'What a precise and rather expensive dream you have!' Barinov proceeds quietly without any doubt, without trying to dress up how he really feels.
- 'Tomorrow I am leaving for the north to paint new pictures.' The guest proceeds wryly facing Barinov, making his position completely clear.
- 'And for the beginning I really need money.' Addressing the time to Eleonora, the guest brings in "pervoye" ("first", from "pervyy") as the key Russian word here.
- 'Victoria, I wanted to say thank you very much.' Eleonora replies evenly during the talk, shifting the mood of the conversation in a single beat.
- 'For playing along with me so well today.' Eleonora speaks up calmly watching the guest, bringing things back to earth after the buildup.
- 'It felt as if I had really spoken to you about that!' Focusing on the feeling in the exchange, Eleonora uses "oshchushcheniye" ("feeling, sensation") to carry the main weight of the thought.
- 'No, there is no need to introduce an edible menu, it is nonsense.' Highlighting how edible things are to the guest, Eleonora leans on "nado" ("it is necessary, one needs to"), which carries the whole predicate in Russian without needing "it is".
