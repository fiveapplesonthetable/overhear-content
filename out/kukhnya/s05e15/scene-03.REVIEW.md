## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Well, not bad, it is quite lovely you have it here.' Russians say 'by me' or 'with us' where English says 'I have' or 'we have', without needing a separate word for 'have'.
- 'Thank you, please take a seat, well, how are you?' Eleonora brings out the contrast between 'thank you' and 'ask for' in a few short words.
- 'Half a year ago we opened a restaurant in Milan.' The grammar packs 'we' and 'Milan' into a tight, idiomatic kitchen exchange.
- 'And right now I have just flown in from London.' Notice how Russian expresses 'and' and 'to arrive' with crisp inflection rather than helper words.
- 'There we recently tried such an interesting thing.' Here the Russian phrasing highlights 'there' before landing on 'to try'.
- 'It is called an edible menu, I want to introduce it at my place.' The meaning hinges on 'is called' working together with 'introduce' in spoken Russian.
- 'Big deal, that interesting thing is already a hundred years old!' An emphatic spoken line centered on the verb meaning 'big deal'. the guest answers right away.
- 'I know, but nobody in Moscow has it.' The grammar packs 'I' and 'no' into a tight, idiomatic kitchen exchange.
- 'Actually we already have it in our restaurant!' An emphatic spoken line centered on the verb meaning 'there is'.
- 'Come on, really? Then show it to me!' Spoken Russian keeps this brisk, leaning on 'yes' and finishing on 'his'. Eleonora answers right away.
- 'Victoria, last week I spoke about the menu.' Eleonora anchors the line on 'proper name', while 'menu' completes the thought.
- 'About our new edible menu, do you remember?' Eleonora brings out the contrast between 'about' and 'our' in a few short words.
