## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Chef, hello.' Over the truth and the plan, Barinov answers Fedya without a second thought.
- 'Come on, out with it.' Because Barinov should come on out with it, Vika treats the matter as settled. Starting with "davay" is the everyday Russian way to say "come on, let us do this".
- 'Hello, Chef.' Over the plan and the work, Fedya answers Barinov without a second thought.
- 'How are you doing?' Here wondering how Fedya doing is shows Barinov looking for the real truth.
- 'And how are things with you?' By asking how things with Barinov are, Vika presses Barinov for a straight answer. Russian expresses having something as "by me is", keeping the phrase short and grounded.
- 'There are problems in my...' Pointing out that there are problems gives Barinov a sobering reality check.
- 'In your kitchen.' Around the plan and truth, Barinov sets a calm, everyday tone with Fedya.
- 'Fedya and Senya are arguing all the time.' Realizing that Fedya and Senya are arguing all the time changes how Vika handles the situation.
- 'And all the work has ground to a halt.' Noting that all the work has ground to a halt keeps Fedya's side of the story simple and clear.
- 'Viktor Petrovich, what should I do?' Asking what he does should cuts straight through the tension between Barinov and Fedya.
- 'Lock the two of them in the walk-in fridge.' Claiming that Barinov should lock the two of them in the walk in fridge sounds confident, even if Barinov has doubts.
- 'Then use your brain, Lyova.' By using Barinov's brain, Fedya keeps his side of the story grounded.
- 'Make them give in to each other.' Adding that Fedya should make them give in to each other fills in the missing piece for Fedya.
