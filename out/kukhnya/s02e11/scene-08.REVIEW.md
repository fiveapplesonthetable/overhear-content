## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Tell Senya.' Telling Senya is how Barinov lays out the situation for Fedya.
- 'He will bring them out anyway.' Because he will bring them out anyway, Senya treats the matter as settled.
- 'And tell Fedya.' Even though Barinov should tell Fedya, Fedya keeps a steady face in front of Barinov.
- 'Otherwise he will be running late.' Barinov mentions that otherwise he will be running late so Fedya sees what is at stake.
- 'And you tell him.' Claiming that Barinov tells him sounds confident, even if Barinov has doubts. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
- 'Get out into the dining room.' Here getting out into the dining room sets up the next turn in their exchange.
- 'That is an excellent idea.' Barinov explains that that is an excellent idea, keeping his voice calm and direct. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'That is a very bad idea.' Realizing that that is a very bad idea changes how Senya handles the situation. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'Girls, the walk-in fridge.' With the point and idea in play, Fedya keeps this short reply brisk and direct.
- 'Oh, well then let us go with your plan.' Barinov mentions that they should go with Fedya's plan so Fedya sees what is at stake. Starting with "davay" is the everyday Russian way to say "come on, let us do this".
- 'Oh right, you do not have one.' Admitting that right Barinov does not have one shows Senya being honest for a change.
- 'Take it easy, Chef.' Once Barinov should take it easy, Fedya can finally tell Barinov what he means.
- 'Have you lost your minds?' Here asking whether Fedya lost Fedya's minds shows Barinov looking for the real truth. Even in the middle of the drama, he keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
