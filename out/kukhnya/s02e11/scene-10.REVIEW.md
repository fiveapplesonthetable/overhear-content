## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Did I understand that correctly?' Max follows up smoothly on the spot and brings in "pravilno" ("right") to make the meaning unmistakable.
- 'Will I be able to come visit?' Opening up candidly to Nastya, Kostya keeps normal statement word order here, turning the line into a question for Nastya purely through rising pitch.
- 'All right, fine.' Nastya calmly delivers this word of agreement in one quick breath using "ladno" ("alright") — built from the old Russian root for harmony and order, used every day for "okay, fine".
- 'Come on, call my phone for me, will you?' Max fires back readily during the talk and reaches for the Russian word "naberesh" ("to gather, rack up", built from "nabrat") right at the center of the line.
- 'Nastya, I lost my phone in the walk-in fridge.' Bringing up the fridge in the exchange, Kostya leans on "kholodilnike" ("fridge", a form of "kholodilnik") to give the remark its edge.
- 'What were you doing in the walk-in fridge?' Nastya presses wryly if there is more going on behind the scenes.
- 'Nastya, go make some juice.' Focusing on the juice in this beat, spoken Russian compresses the whole thought into just 3 words, skipping the extra helper words English needs.
- 'Well, that is it.' Reacting promptly around Max, Kostya links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
- 'Your Max probably came up with all of this, right?' Nastya questions sharply about what Max just brought up.
- 'Of course it was Max.' Kostya evenly uses this confirmation to Max using "konechno" ("of course") — spoken with a soft "shn" sound in everyday Moscow Russian to mean "of course, certainly".
- 'Watch your mouth, you sheep.' Talking about the words with Nastya, Kostya reaches for the Russian word "slovami" ("words", built from "slovo") right at the center of the line.
- 'Be quiet right now, dwarf.' Nastya proceeds honestly with a sigh, to clear the air between them once and for all.
- 'They are probably hugging each other already.' Max speaks naturally at once and reaches for the Russian word "obnimayetsya" ("to hug each other", built from "obnimatsya") right at the center of the line.
