## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'People die from falling coconuts.' Between the point and the bet, Fedya gives Barinov a quick, grounded response.
- 'What kind of total nonsense is this?' Asking whether what kind of total nonsense is this puts Barinov right on the defensive. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'A coconut has already fallen on me.' Since a coconut has already fallen on him, Senya does not waste words on excuses.
- 'Well, I am completely alive.' Even though he is completely alive, Senya keeps a steady face in front of Barinov.
- 'You will not withstand a blow from a coconut.' Saying that Barinov will not withstand a blow from a coconut is how Fedya sets the tone with Barinov. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
- 'I will not withstand such a blow?' Questioning whether he will not withstand such a blow is how Senya tests Barinov's story.
- 'Let us make a bet then.' Adding that they should make a bet then fills in the missing piece for Barinov. Starting with "davay" is the everyday Russian way to say "come on, let us do this".
- 'So will I get this star?' Checking whether he gets this star is how Barinov puts Fedya on the spot. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'I hope everything will be great for us.' Mentioning that he hopes everything will be great for them moves the talk in a new direction.
- 'Coconuts never warn you in advance.' Around the plan and bet, Fedya sets a calm, everyday tone with Barinov. Russian stacks "never" or "nothing" together with "not", doubling the refusal for extra weight.
- 'You can throw a coconut at me.' Senya explains that Barinov can throw a coconut at him, keeping his voice calm and direct.
- 'Maybe tomorrow, or maybe right now.' Between the plan and the bet, Fedya gives Barinov a quick, grounded response.
