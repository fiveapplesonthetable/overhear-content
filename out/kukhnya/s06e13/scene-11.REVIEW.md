## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I was told you can organize a game.' The guest opens up briskly around Mikhail, making his position completely clear.
- 'Interesting, and who whispered that to you?' Mikhail quizzes openly to see how the guest will answer on the spot.
- 'Well, that is not so important right now.' Emphasizing how important things are around Mikhail, the guest leans on "vazhno" ("important, mattering", a form of "vazhnyy") to give the remark its edge.
- 'So can you help me or not?' Turning earnestly before Mikhail, the guest keeps normal statement word order here, turning the line into a question for Mikhail purely through rising pitch.
- 'Fine, I will definitely think of something for you.' Mikhail opens up wryly without holding back, grounding the moment in plain reality.
- 'And how can I find you later?' The guest replies plainly before Mikhail and puts the emphasis on "nayti" ("to find") in the middle of the talk.
- 'All the phone numbers are on my business card.' Focusing on the telephone right here, Mikhail leans on "telefony" ("telephone, phone", a form of "telefon") to give the remark its edge.
- 'Look, he has the exact same suit.' Mikhail responds plainly without holding back, before the guest can change the subject.
- 'We gave my suit to the dry cleaners.' Emphasizing the dry cleaner before the guest, Mikhail reaches for the Russian word "khimchistku" ("dry cleaner’s", built from "khimchistka") right at the center of the line.
- 'I have not had such good luck in a long time.' The guest counters openly to Mikhail and uses "khorosho" ("good, fine") to carry the main weight of the thought.
- 'Please, do not worry at all.' The guest directly chooses this courtesy word in one quick breath using "pozhaluysta" ("please") — the all-purpose Russian politeness word that covers both "please" and "you're welcome".
- 'Excuse me, I came in just to ask quickly.' Barinov weighs in vividly beside the guest and leans on "izvinite" ("sorry, excuse me", a form of "izvinit") to give the remark its edge.
