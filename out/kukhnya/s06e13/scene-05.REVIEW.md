## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Senya, take this money for your silence.' Discussing the silence in front of Denis, Barinov reaches for the Russian word "molchaniye" ("silence") right at the heart of the line.
- 'And now everyone get out of here quickly.' Barinov proceeds briskly during the talk, steering the talk right where he wants it.
- 'Tell her that all the fish is fresh.' Confirming how fresh things are right here, Barinov centers the thought on "svezhaya" ("fresh", from "svezhiy") without wasting a word.
- 'Forgive me, things turned out very awkwardly for us.' Holding firm quietly in front of Denis, Barinov reaches for "u nas" ("by us, at our place") to talk about what they share.
- 'You were supposed to be in her place.' Barinov weighs in earnestly toward Denis, watching Denis's face to see how the news lands.
- 'Hello, Katya, let us talk calmly with you.' Denis counters warmly beside Katya and puts the weight on "pogovorim" ("we'll have a talk", from "pogovorit") as the exchange unfolds.
- 'Alright, let us try to discuss everything now.' Holding firm calmly at once, Katya starts with "davay" ("come on, let's") to invite Denis right into the action.
- 'In short, I am leaving here soon.' Denis continues pointedly without a pause, bringing things back to earth after the buildup.
- 'I decided to leave Moscow forever.' Denis fires back dryly in front of Katya and chooses "navsegda" ("forever") so Katya gets the point right away.
- 'I am going back to my native Voronezh.' Leaning in plainly with Katya, the "-sya" ending on "vozvrashchayus" ("to come back") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'My train leaves tomorrow morning already.' Denis counters quietly watching Katya, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'I simply wanted you to know.' Answering plainly to Katya, Denis uses the little word "prosto" ("simply, just") to make the point sound effortless.
