## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Senya, take this money for your silence.' Barinov cuts in sharply as they talk, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'And now everyone get out of here quickly.' Barinov counters briskly with Katya, making his position completely clear.
- 'Tell her that all the fish is fresh.' Barinov focuses on this direct choice toward Denis: "svezhaya" means "fresh" (from "svezhiy"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'Forgive me, things turned out very awkwardly for us.' Barinov frames the remark around Katya: the phrase "u nas" literally means "by us" or "at our place", the standard Russian way to talk about what a group or couple shares.
- 'You were supposed to be in her place.' Barinov counters earnestly to be clear, keeping the focus on what actually matters here.
- 'Hello, Katya, let us talk calmly with you.' Denis anchors this telling piece in reply: "pogovorim" means "we'll have a talk" (from "pogovorit"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'Alright, let us try to discuss everything now.' Katya hands over this statement plainly here: starting with "davay" is the classic everyday Russian way to say "come on, let's do this" between people on close terms.
- 'In short, I am leaving here soon.' Denis responds pointedly as things stand, before Katya can change the subject.
- 'I decided to leave Moscow forever.' Denis leans on one memorable word for Barinov: "navsegda" means "forever", and it carries the main weight of what Denis is saying.
- 'I am going back to my native Voronezh.' Denis makes this point plainly here: the "-sya" ending on "vozvrashchayus" ("to come back") turns the action inward or makes it happen between people naturally.
- 'My train leaves tomorrow morning already.' Denis jumps in quietly hearing Barinov, shifting the whole mood of the room in a single beat.
- 'I simply wanted you to know.' Denis spotlights this clear piece right here: "prosto" means "just", and it carries the main weight of what Denis is saying.
