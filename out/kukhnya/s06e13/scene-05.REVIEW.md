## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Senya, take this money for your silence.' Noting that Denis should take this money for Denis's silence keeps Barinov's side of the story simple and clear.
- 'And now everyone get out of here quickly.' Over the moment and the news, Barinov answers Denis without a second thought.
- 'Tell her that all the fish is fresh.' By telling Denis to tell her that all the fish is fresh, Barinov keeps his side of the story grounded.
- 'Forgive me, things turned out very awkwardly for us.' Since things turned out very awkwardly for them, Barinov does not waste words on excuses. Russian expresses having something as "by me is", keeping the phrase short and grounded.
- 'You were supposed to be in her place.' Mentioning that Denis was supposed to be in her place moves the talk in a new direction. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
- 'Hello, Katya, let us talk calmly with you.' Admitting that they should talk calmly with Barinov shows Denis being honest for a change.
- 'Alright, let us try to discuss everything now.' Because Alright let them try to discuss everything now, Katya treats the matter as settled. Starting with "davay" is the everyday Russian way to say "come on, let us do this".
- 'In short, I am leaving here soon.' Explaining that in short he is leaving here soon lays the cards on the table for Barinov.
- 'I decided to leave Moscow forever.' Denis mentions that he decided to leave Moscow forever so Barinov sees what is at stake.
- 'I am going back to my native Voronezh.' Realizing that he is going back to his native Voronezh changes how Denis handles the situation.
- 'My train leaves tomorrow morning already.' Pointing out that his train leaves tomorrow morning already gives Barinov a sobering reality check.
- 'I simply wanted you to know.' Denis mentions that he simply wanted Barinov to know so Barinov sees what is at stake.
