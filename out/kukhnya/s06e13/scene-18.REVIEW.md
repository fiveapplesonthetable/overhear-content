## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Nikita, I thought very carefully about everything.' The '-a' ending on the past-tense verb for 'thought' marks a female speaker or subject.
- 'I agree with your proposal from yesterday.' Here the Russian phrasing highlights 'I' before landing on 'offer'.
- 'I agree to marry you.' In Russian, the idea of 'I' flows naturally into 'married' without extra filler words.
- 'Katya, are you telling me the truth right now?' Nikita raises the pitch on 'truth' to turn the phrase into a direct spoken question. Katya answers right away.
- 'Yes, this is truly the honest truth.' The grammar packs 'yes' and 'clean' into a tight, idiomatic kitchen exchange.
- 'Listen, I want to share my joy with you.' Nikita chooses the respectful plural 'you' alongside 'joy' to keep a polite distance.
- 'Katya agreed to become my beloved wife.' Nikita brings out the contrast between 'Katya' and 'to agree' in a few short words. Katya answers right away.
- 'Please, stop, people are looking at us.' Spoken Russian keeps this brisk, leaning on 'please' and finishing on 'look'.
- 'Let all the people look right now.' Notice how Russian expresses 'and' and 'look' with crisp inflection rather than helper words.
- 'Let everyone know how happy I am.' Here the Russian phrasing highlights 'let' before landing on 'happy'.
- 'Today I am truly proud and happy.' Nikita brings out the contrast between 'I' and 'tonight' in a few short words.
- 'I promise that I will be the best husband.' Nikita anchors the line on 'I', while 'husband' completes the thought.
