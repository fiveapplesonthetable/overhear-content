## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Right now we will amaze them! Good evening, gentlemen.' Because right now they will amaze them, Barinov does not bother softening the blow for Eleonora.
- 'Good evening, Eleonora Andreyevna.' With the room and meat in play, Barinov keeps this short reply brisk and direct.
- 'Allow me to introduce to you our new cook Artyom.' Since Eleonora should allow him to introduce to Eleonora their new cook Artyom, Barinov does not waste words on excuses. Even in the middle of the drama, he keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
- 'Enjoy your meal!' Declaring that Eleonora should enjoy Eleonora's meal brings Barinov's temper right to the surface.
- 'It is me! My friend's blood pressure went up.' Once it is him, the guest makes sure Barinov hears every word. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'So that means you can drink and eat what is forbidden?' The guest asks whether that means Barinov can drink and eat what is forbidden, refusing to let the point slide. There is no "you" needed in the Russian here: one short word states the rule for everyone in the room.
- 'Calm down! Sit down, I am not drinking for no reason.' Saying that Barinov should sit down she is not drinking for no reason is how Eleonora takes charge of the moment.
- 'There is a reason.' Explaining that there is a reason lays the cards on the table for Barinov.
- 'What reason could you possibly have?' Questioning whether what reason could Barinov possibly have is how the guest tests Barinov's story. In Russian you say "by me" or "with us" rather than "I have", so the situation sits right with the person.
- 'You see, Maxim and Vika are getting divorced.' Claiming that Maxim and Vika are getting divorced sounds confident, even if Barinov has doubts.
- 'Judging by their faces, they are pleased.' Noting that judging by their faces they are pleased keeps Barinov's side of the story simple and clear.
- 'A triumph awaits you!' With the point and meat at stake, Barinov reacts without holding back. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
