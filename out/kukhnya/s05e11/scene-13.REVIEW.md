## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Maxim Ilyich, hold on! What is that clinking in your bag?' Russian expresses 'having' with the preposition 'by' plus the genitive pronoun and 'clinks'.
- 'Oh, I bought lemonade.' In Russian, the idea of 'and' flows naturally into 'bought' without extra filler words.
- 'Will you treat me? Give me the bag, please.' Here the Russian phrasing highlights 'treat' before landing on 'bag'. the guest answers right away.
- 'Do not. If I do not have a drink, I will weep from grief.' the guest anchors the line on 'not', while 'grief' completes the thought.
- 'My Lyubanya asked me not to tell anyone.' The negative particle sits right before the verb meaning 'asked for' to negate the statement.
- 'She has two months left to live. She is dying.' Notice how Russian expresses 'two' and 'she' with crisp inflection rather than helper words.
- 'My checkup is a pretext to come here and see you.' Here the Russian phrasing highlights 'my' before landing on 'to see'.
- 'Are you serious right now?' Vika brings out the contrast between 'you' and 'seriously' in a few short words.
- 'People do not joke about such things!' An emphatic spoken line centered on the verb meaning 'joke'.
- 'Only do not tell Maxim. He loves his grandma very much.' the guest brings out the contrast between 'you' and 'only' in a few short words.
- 'That is why I feel like having a drink.' In Russian, the idea of 'here' flows naturally into 'to drink' without extra filler words.
- 'Stay strong, Maxim Ilyich.' Spoken Russian keeps this brisk, leaning on 'stick' and finishing on 'Ilyich'.
