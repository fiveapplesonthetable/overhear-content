## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I never thought I would say this phrase.' Noting that he never thought he would say this phrase keeps Nagiyev's side of the story simple and clear. Russian stacks "never" or "nothing" together with "not", doubling the refusal for extra weight.
- 'I am very glad to see both of you.' By being very glad to see both of the guest, Nagiyev keeps his side of the story grounded. Notice how he sticks to the formal "you", keeping a professional line between them.
- 'Tell me, what needs to be signed there?' Nagiyev asks whether tell him what needs to be signed there, refusing to let the point slide.
- 'Sasha, bring us some good champagne.' Once the guest should bring them some good champagne, Nagiyev can finally tell the guest what he means.
- 'It seems we have a big celebration today.' Once it seems they have a big celebration today, Nagiyev can finally tell the guest what he means. In Russian you say "by me" or "with us" rather than "I have", so the situation sits right with the person.
- 'Do not hurry, first we need to discuss everything.' Adding that do not hurry first they need to discuss everything fills in the missing piece for the guest.
- 'After all, you are selling me half of the restaurant.' Nagiyev tells the guest that after all the guest is selling him half of the restaurant without dressing it up. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
- 'Yes, but now I want a million.' Noting that she wants a million keeps Kristina's side of the story simple and clear.
- 'If that is a threat, it will cost you dearly.' Mentioning that if that is a threat it will cost Kristina dearly moves the talk in a new direction.
- 'You are both owners of this restaurant.' The guest reminds Kristina that Kristina is both owners of this restaurant before the moment passes. Notice how he sticks to the formal "you", keeping a professional line between them.
- 'And you will manage it together.' Because Kristina will manage it together, the guest treats the matter as settled.
- 'That is very funny and amusing.' Explaining that that is very funny and amusing lays the cards on the table for the guest.
