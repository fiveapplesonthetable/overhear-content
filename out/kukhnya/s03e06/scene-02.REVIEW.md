## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I never thought I would say this phrase.' Pointing to the phrase to Kristina, Nagiyev pairs the negative word with "ne" ("not"), since Russian naturally doubles its negatives for emphasis.
- 'I am very glad to see both of you.' Nagiyev follows up lightly with a shrug, refusing to let the moment slip past.
- 'Tell me, what needs to be signed there?' Nagiyev speaks smoothly at once and leans on "podpisat" ("to sign") to give the whole sentence its punch.
- 'Sasha, bring us some good champagne.' Emphasizing the champagne before Kristina, Nagiyev lands on "shampanskogo" ("of champagne", built on "shampanskoye") to keep the statement grounded.
- 'It seems we have a big celebration today.' Nagiyev speaks up boldly hearing Kristina, shifting the mood of the conversation in a single beat.
- 'Do not hurry, first we need to discuss everything.' Kristina follows up openly to Nagiyev and leans on "snachala" ("first, at first") to give the whole sentence its punch.
- 'After all, you are selling me half of the restaurant.' Nagiyev steps in quietly with Kristina, watching Kristina's face to see how the news lands.
- 'Yes, but now I want a million.' Kristina steps in evenly in this moment, shifting the mood of the conversation in a single beat.
- 'If that is a threat, it will cost you dearly.' Turning to a threat to Kristina, the guest lands on "ugroza" ("threat") as the key everyday word here.
- 'You are both owners of this restaurant.' Pointing to the owners in the exchange, the guest links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
- 'And you will manage it together.' The guest responds firmly with a shrug, without trying to dress up how he really feels.
- 'That is very funny and amusing.' Nagiyev steps in sharply during the talk and reaches for the Russian word "smeshno" ("ridiculous") right at the heart of the line.
