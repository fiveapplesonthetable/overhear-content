## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Tell them that I am very busy.' Admitting that Kristina should tell them that he is very busy shows Barinov being honest for a change.
- 'Vitya, well what is this?' With the truth and shift hanging in the air, Nagiyev puts Barinov on the spot. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'Am I supposed to wait for you there for hours?' With the truth and plan hanging in the air, Nagiyev puts Barinov on the spot.
- 'Enough, do not yell at my chef.' Saying that enough do not yell is how Kristina sets the tone with Barinov.
- 'Vitya, we need to talk urgently.' Needing to talk urgently moves the conversation forward without extra drama. There is no "you" needed in the Russian here: one short word states the rule for everyone in the room.
- 'May I talk with my chef first?' Nagiyev asks whether may he talks with his first, refusing to let the point slide.
- 'My friend has an anniversary here tomorrow.' Nagiyev tells Barinov that his friend has an anniversary here tomorrow without dressing it up.
- 'Therefore we are closing the restaurant.' Since therefore they are closing the restaurant, Nagiyev does not waste words on excuses.
- 'Wait, what do you mean we are closing?' Asking what Barinov means they are closing cuts straight through the tension between Kristina and Barinov.
- 'By the way, I am the one who has an important event tomorrow.' Claiming that she is the one who has an important event tomorrow sounds confident, even if Barinov has doubts. Russian expresses having something as "by me is", keeping the phrase short and grounded.
- 'And I am the one closing our restaurant.' By being the one closing their restaurant, Kristina keeps her side of the story grounded. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'You will have nothing here tomorrow.' Explaining that Barinov will have nothing here tomorrow lays the cards on the table for Barinov.
- 'You will pay me for all your insults.' Kristina mentions that Barinov will pay her for all Barinov's insults so Barinov sees what is at stake. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
