## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I bought this exact mattress for my daughter and her husband.' Because he bought this exact mattress for his daughter and her husband, Kostya treats the matter as settled.
- 'And I also bought them the apartment and the furniture.' Explaining that he also bought them the apartment and the furniture lays the cards on the table for Barinov.
- 'I bought them everything, and it is all mine!' Because he bought them everything and it is all, Kostya does not bother softening the blow for Barinov. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'And we have the sunny side.' Claiming that they have the sunny side sounds confident, even if Barinov has doubts. In Russian you say "by me" or "with us" rather than "I have", so the situation sits right with the person.
- 'There are air conditioners here, but do you know how the meter will spin?' Asking whether there are air conditioners here but do Barinov knows how the meter will spin puts Barinov right on the defensive.
- 'On the bright side, you have a day off today, Vitya.' Nagiyev explains that on the bright side Barinov has a day off today, keeping his voice calm and direct. Russian expresses having something as "by me is", keeping the phrase short and grounded.
- 'And what good is it to me?' Questioning whether what good is it to him is how Barinov tests Kostya's story. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'Eleonora demands a report on the auteur cuisine.' Noting that Eleonora demands a report on the auteur cuisine keeps Barinov's side of the story simple and clear.
- 'If there is no profit, she will shut everything down.' Even though if there is no profit she will shut everything down, Barinov keeps a steady face in front of Kostya.
- 'And I will never see a star.' Admitting that he will never see a star shows Barinov being honest for a change.
- 'Listen, I have something to talk to you about.' Explaining that he has something to talk to Barinov lays the cards on the table for Barinov.
- 'Let's talk face to face.' Mentioning that they should talk face to face moves the talk in a new direction. Starting with "davay" is the everyday Russian way to say "come on, let us do this".
