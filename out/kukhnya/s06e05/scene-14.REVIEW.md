## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Do you think this clever plan will work?' Talking about the plan on the spot, Barinov keeps normal statement word order here, turning the line into a question for Mikhail purely through rising pitch.
- 'Where would he get that kind of big money?' Mikhail tests curiously how Barinov expects that to work in practice.
- 'He will bring that old menu back to you.' Addressing the menu with Nastya, Mikhail brings in "staroye" ("old", from "staryy") as the key Russian word here.
- 'Kostik, I am stepping out for a little while now.' Nastya speaks smoothly on the spot and brings in "nenadolgo" ("for a short time") to make the meaning unmistakable.
- 'I already asked Nikita for time off myself.' Nastya chimes in right away hearing Barinov, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'Where are you heading off to, Nastya?' Barinov replies calmly in the exchange and brings in "sobralas" ("to get ready", from "sobratsya") as the key Russian word here.
- 'Vitya, we need the money tomorrow already.' Talking about the money in front of Barinov, Mikhail centers the remark on "dengi" ("money") without any extra fuss.
- 'Clear, I heard you loud and clear.' Barinov naturally delivers this acknowledgment without hesitation using "yasno" ("clear") — literally "brightly, clearly", used in conversation to mean "got it, I see".
- 'What, do you have no money at all?' Highlighting the money on the spot, Mikhail centers the thought on "deneg" ("money", from "dengi") without wasting a word.
- 'Actually no, I have a million right there.' Focusing on a million in this beat, Barinov leans on "million" ("a million") to give the whole sentence its punch.
- 'Lena and I were saving it for our wedding.' Barinov responds calmly without any doubt, making his position completely clear.
- 'I would definitely help you, my friend.' Focusing on his friend around Barinov, Mikhail centers the remark on "obyazatelno" ("surely, without fail") without any extra fuss.
