## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Max, happy birthday!' "S dnyom rozhdeniya" is the wish itself, literally "with day of birth". "Pozdravlyayu tebya" adds "I congratulate you".
- 'Thank you so much, my loyal friend.' "Vernyy" means loyal or faithful. "Bolshoe spasibo" is literally "big thanks".
- 'Happy birthday from the heart from me too!' "Ot dushi" is literally "from the soul", where English says "from the heart".
- 'Thanks, Nastya, that's really nice.' "Priyatno", pleasant, is the same word Russians use in "ochen priyatno", nice to meet you.
- 'And this present is for you.' "Podarok", gift, comes from "darit", to give as a present. "A vot" draws attention to it.
- 'What are these nice tickets?' "Chto eto za" is how you ask "what kind of... is this?". One "bilet" becomes two "bilety".
- 'These are tickets to a good evening concert.' Tickets in Russian are "onto" an event: "na kontsert".
- 'We'll have a great time together tonight.' "Poveselimsya", we'll have fun, comes from "vesyolyy", cheerful.
- 'Let's go, the three of us: you, me and Nastya.' "Vtroyom" is one word for "the three of us together", from "tri", three.
- 'We'll dance there all night!' "Vsyu noch", all night, needs no "for". "Budem tantsevat" means "we will dance".
- 'Thanks, guys, you're real friends.' "Nastoyashchie" means real or true. "Rebyata" is the usual word for a group of friends.
- 'Come on, it's your big day!' "Da ladno" is "oh, come on", a way to brush off thanks. "Zhe" adds "after all".
- 'After all, today is your birthday.' "Ved" opens the line like "you know", reminding someone of the obvious.
- 'Happy birthday once again, Max!' "Eshchyo raz" is "once more", literally "still one time".
