## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Nastya, clear table five, quickly!' "Stolik" is a little "stol", table, and restaurants number them: "pyatyy stolik", the fifth table. "Uberi" means "clear away".
- 'Have you completely lost all shame at this job?' "Obnagleli" comes from "naglyy", cheeky. "Sovsem" means "completely".
- 'You're here to work, not to cuddle.' "Nado rabotat" is "one has to work". "A ne" means "and not", and "obzhimatsya" is a slangy word for hugging and squeezing.
- 'We were just quietly talking, Viktoria Sergeevna.' Kostya uses her first name and her father's name, "Viktoriya Sergeevna", which is how staff address a boss.
- 'Any relationships are strictly forbidden here!' "U nas", literally "at us", means "here, at our place". "Zapreshcheny", forbidden, is plural to match "otnosheniya", relationships.
- 'We didn't do anything wrong.' "Nichego plokhogo" is literally "nothing of bad", and "ne" still comes before the action.
- 'Quick, go and work in the dining room.' "Zal" is the dining room of a restaurant. "Idite" ends in "-ite" because she's sending both of them.
- 'Vika, why are you so tense?' "Takaya" ends in "-aya" to match Vika; about a man Max would say "takoy".
- 'I have so many difficult things to deal with.' After "mnogo", a lot, the words change shape: "dela" becomes "del", and "slozhnye" becomes "slozhnykh".
- 'Shall we go somewhere cool tonight?' "Klassnyy" is casual praise, like "cool". "Segodnya vecherom" is literally "today in the evening".
- 'Max, I'm far too tired.' "Ustala" ends in "-a" because Vika is a woman; Max would say "ustal". "Slishkom" means "too".
- 'You'll get a really good rest there with me.' "Otdokhnyosh" comes from "otdykhat", to rest, which is also what Russians say for going on holiday. "S" grows to "so" before "mnoy".
- 'We'll see, I need to think about it.' "Posmotrim", we'll see, is the classic way to say neither yes nor no. "O", about, grows to "ob" before the vowel in "etom".
