## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Good afternoon. Glad to see you at our restaurant.' "Dobryy den" is the polite daytime hello. "Rada" ends in "-a" because the hostess is a woman.
- 'Would you like the smoking or the non-smoking room?' "Dlya kuryashchikh" means "for those who smoke", from "kurit", to smoke. "Vam", for you, is polite.
- 'Oh, you have separate sections?' "Razdelenie", division, comes from "delit", to divide. "U vas" means "you have", literally "at you".
- 'Well, well. Two rooms, even.' "Nado zhe" is a set phrase of surprise, here fake surprise. After "dva", two, "zal" picks up an "-a": "dva zala".
- 'And air conditioning.' "Konditsioner" is borrowed, with its stress near the end. A single noun after "i" is enough for a mocking list.
- 'It's a sheikh's palace, really.' "Prosto" is "simply", used here like "just" or "practically". "Sheykha" is "of a sheikh", the genitive.
- 'Such stupid jokes.' "Duratskie", silly, comes from "durak", a fool. "Takie" is "such", matching the plural "shutki", jokes.
- 'A regular smoking room for us.' "Nam", to us, is enough to ask for something: no verb needed. "Prostoy" means simple or ordinary.
- 'This way, please.' "Proshu" is literally "I ask", the usual polite word when you show someone where to go.
- 'Thank you.' "Spasibo" works for a host, a waiter or a friend alike.
