## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Chef, try the new dish.' "Poprobuyte" is the polite "try", ending in "-yte" because Senya says "vy" to his boss.
- 'What? What dish?' "Kakoe eshchyo" is literally "what else", and it sounds grumpy, as if one more dish is the last thing he needs.
- 'But yesterday you gave us a task.' "Zhe" adds "but you did!", so Senya is reminding him. "Zadanie" is a task, the same word as a school assignment.
- 'Well, let's try it.' "Davay poprobuem" is "let's try" said to one person; to the whole team he'd say "davayte".
- 'And what is this?' "Takoe" on the end of "chto" adds suspicion, like "what on earth is this?".
- 'This is tender meat with thin ham.' "Nezhnoe", tender, matches "myaso", meat. After "s", with, "tonkaya vetchina" becomes "tonkoy vetchinoy".
- 'Very tasty, I like this recipe!' "Mne nravitsya" is literally "to me it is pleasing": in Russian the recipe does the pleasing.
- 'Congratulations, your dish is on the menu now.' "Pozdravlyayu", I congratulate, is how Russian says "congratulations". "Menyu" never changes its ending.
- 'Chef, wait, this is completely unfair!' "Nechestno" is "ne", not, glued to "chestno", honestly.
- 'Thank you so much, chef, I'm really glad!' "Rad" means glad when a man says it; a woman would say "rada".
- 'Congratulations on your win, colleague.' "Pozdravlyayu s" is "congratulations on", and "pobeda", victory, turns into "pobedoy" after "s".
- 'Chef, what about our dishes?' "Kak zhe" shows up whenever someone feels forgotten: "a kak zhe my?", and what about us?
- 'I don't need anything else, I've found it.' "Nashyol", found, comes from "nayti", to find. "Bolshe nichego" is "nothing more".
