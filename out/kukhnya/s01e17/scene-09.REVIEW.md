## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Chef, try the seasonal dish.' "Poprobuyte" is the polite "try", ending in "-yte" because Senya says "vy" to his boss. "Sezonnoe" matches "blyudo", dish.
- 'What?' Just "chto", said flat, as if a dish at this hour is the last thing he wants.
- 'Well, yesterday you gave us the task.' "Zhe" adds "but you did!", so Senya is reminding him. "Zadanie" is a task, the same word as a school assignment.
- 'So I get poisoned first thing in the morning?' "Chtoby" is "so that", and the verb after it stays past: "otravilsya", got poisoned. "S samogo utra" is "from the very morning".
- 'Well, let's try something.' "Davayte" is "let's" said to a group; to one person it would be "davay". "Chto-to" is "something".
- 'What is this?' "Eto chto?" puts "this" first, which sounds blunter than "chto eto?".
- 'Veal rolls with ham.' "Iz telyatiny" is "out of veal". After "s", with, "vetchina", ham, becomes "vetchinoy".
- 'Congratulations, cripple, your dish is on the menu.' "Pozdravlyayu", I congratulate, is how Russian says "congratulations". "Menyu" never changes its ending.
- 'Chef, wait, that's not fair!' "Nechestno" is "ne", not, glued to "chestno", honestly. "Podozhdite" is the polite "wait".
- 'You promised not to call the winner a cripple.' After "nazyvat", to call, the name takes the instrumental: "invalid" becomes "invalidom".
- 'Yes, congratulations, colleague.' "Kollega" ends in "-a" but works for a man too, so the chef can say it to Senya.
- 'Chef, and what about us?' "Kak zhe" shows up whenever someone feels forgotten: "a kak zhe my?", and what about us?
- 'No need, I've found it.' "Nashyol", found, comes from "nayti", to find. "Ego", it, points back to the dish, because "blyudo" is not feminine.
- 'Breton lamb chops with asparagus.' Fedya presents his dish anyway, to nobody. "Po-bretonski" is "the Breton way", like "po-russki", the Russian way. "So" is "s", with, before the "sp" of "sparzhey".
