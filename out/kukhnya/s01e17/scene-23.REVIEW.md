## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Show me your passport, Max.' "Pokazhi" is "show" to one person. "Svoy pasport" is "your own passport".
- 'Here's my passport, chef.' "Vot" is what you say as you hand something over: here it is.
- 'And why is your birthday in February?' "V fevrale" means in February, and months aren't capitalised in Russian.
- 'Your birthday isn't today?' Senya checks the obvious: "ne segodnya", not today.
- 'I wanted to do something nice for you.' "Sdelat priyatnoe" is literally "to do the pleasant", a fixed phrase for "do something nice".
- 'You lied to us about the party?' "Sovral", lied, is a blunt, everyday word.
- 'I wanted everyone to make up.' "Khotel, chtoby" is "wanted that", used when you want others to do something.
- 'But you mustn't lie to friends.' "Nelzya", not allowed, is a word you'll see on signs everywhere.
- 'Any lie is bad.' "Lozh", lie, takes "-aya" words like "lyubaya", any, even though it doesn't end in "-a".
- 'I'm very much to blame.' "Vinovat pered vami" is literally "guilty before you", how Russians admit they're in the wrong.
- 'You should have told the truth.' "Dolzhen byl" means "should have", literally "was obliged".
- 'The truth always comes out.' "Vykhodit naruzhu" is literally "goes out to the outside".
- 'Every lie has to be paid for.' "Prikhoditsya platit" is "one has to pay", with no "one" in it. "Obman" means deceit.
