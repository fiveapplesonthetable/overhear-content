## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Oh, you beauty! Hello.' The word krasavitsa, 'beauty', is usually said to a woman; here it is loud praise for Barinov. He says zdravstvuy without the -te because they speak as friends.
- 'Tell me, how do you do it?' The word skazhi, 'tell', is the friendly command; to a stranger you'd say skazhite.
- 'Three days ago I wanted to fire you.' Russian puts nazad, 'ago', after the time: tri dnya nazad.
- 'So you won't fire me?' The little word tak at the start means 'so', the way you check you've understood.
- 'Are you out of your mind? You're a star!' Russian says s uma soshyol, literally 'gone off your mind'.
- 'Look, look.' The word smotri, 'look', is the friendly command from smotret, 'to look'.
- 'Over a million views in two days.' Here za means 'within' a stretch of time: za dva dnya, 'in two days'.
- 'Do you understand what this means?' The phrase eto znachit means 'this means', and you'll hear it everywhere.
- 'I understand. People have nothing better to do.' Russian says delat nechego, literally 'there's nothing to do', and with bolshe it means 'nothing better to do'.
- 'All my friends are asking me to introduce them to you.' Russian says poznakomit s toboy, literally 'acquaint with you'.
- 'All our tables are taken for the weekend.' The word stoliki literally means 'little tables', and it's the usual word in a restaurant.
- 'Everyone wants to see you!' In Russian vse, 'everyone', takes the 'they' ending: vse khotyat, 'everyone want'.
- 'So that means I'm not fired?' The word uvolen, 'fired', works like gotov, 'ready': a woman would say uvolena.
- 'For what? You're a star, you made our restaurant famous.' Russian asks za chto, literally 'for what', when you want to know what someone did to deserve something.
- 'Then maybe a bonus for me?' The word premiya means a prize, and at work a bonus. Russian leaves out 'give': mne premiyu is literally 'to me a bonus'.
- 'Not so fast. Go to the kitchen.' Russian says na kukhnyu, ending in -yu, for going to the kitchen, and na kukhne for being in it.
