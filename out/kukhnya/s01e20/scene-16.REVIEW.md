## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Here. Hang in there, old man.' Russian hands things over with na, a little word like 'here, take it', and derzhis literally means 'hold on'.
- 'Is this for me?' Russian needs no verb here: eto mne, literally 'this to me'.
- 'For you. You need it more.' The word nuzhnee means 'more needed', so tebe nuzhnee is literally 'to you it's more needed'.
- 'Thank you, son.' Older people in Russian call a young man synok, literally 'little son', even a stranger.
- 'Don't mention it.' The reply ne za chto literally means 'not for anything', the usual answer to spasibo.
- 'God bless you.' Russian says dai bog tebe zdorovya, literally 'may God give you health', a thank-you you hear from older people.
