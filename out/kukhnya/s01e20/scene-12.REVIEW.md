## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Miss, when will we sit at a table?' 'Girl' is used to call a waitress. 'We'll sit down' is an action seen as complete.
- 'Wait one minute, please.' 'Wait' is the polite command. 'One minute' takes a 'u' ending.
- 'I've already been waiting twenty minutes!' Russian uses the present, 'I wait already twenty minutes'. After 'twenty', 'minutes' takes the 'of' plural.
- 'Sorry, the restaurant is very full now.' 'Sorry' is the polite word. 'Full' is a masculine short form.
- 'Vika, how are things in the dining room?' 'How are matters' is the usual question. 'In the hall' needs an 'e' ending.
- 'A lot of new guests today.' After 'many', 'new' and 'guests' both take the 'of' plural. 'Today' closes the sentence.
- 'That's good, we had a hard period.' 'At us was' stands for 'we had'. 'Hard' agrees with the masculine 'period'.
- 'Yes, and now we have a lot of work.' 'Much of work' uses the 'of' form for work, 'raboty'. 'Now' is a small word.
- 'We need to thank our team.' Russians 'say thanks to' someone, so 'team' takes the 'to' form. 'Our' carries 'ey' to match.
- 'How can we do this?' 'How' and 'we can' make a plain question. 'This' takes the object form.
- 'Tell everyone there'll be a party on Saturday.' 'On Saturday' is 'in Saturday', with a 'u' ending. 'Tell' is the friendly command.
- 'A party for our team? Excellent!' 'Party' is a word made from 'evening'. 'For our team' takes an 'ey' ending.
- 'Yes, after midnight.' 'After twelve of night' is how Russian says 'after midnight'. Both words take the 'of' form.
- 'Good, I'll tell everyone in the kitchen.' 'I'll tell' treats the action as done. 'To everyone' is 'vsem'.
