## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Miss, tell me, when will we get a table?' In Russian you call out devushka, literally 'girl', to get any young woman's attention, including a waitress.
- 'Just a minute.' The word minutochku is a soft little form of minuta, 'minute', the kind you say to calm someone down.
- 'I've been standing here twenty minutes already!' Russian says ya uzhe stoyu, literally 'I already stand', where English says 'I've been standing'.
- 'Sorry, all the tables are taken right now.' The word zanyaty, 'taken' or 'busy', ends in -y because there are many tables.
- 'We had a hard time, and now look at us.' Russian says von kak, literally 'there's how', to mean 'look how things turned out'.
- 'We should thank our team somehow.' A kollektiv is what Russians call the whole staff at a workplace.
- 'How can we do that?' The word mozhem, 'we can', comes from moch, 'to be able'.
- 'Tell people that on Saturday we work till twelve.' Russian says v subbotu, 'on Saturday', so subbota changes its ending to -u.
- 'And then we have a staff party.' A korporativ is the party a company throws for its own staff.
- 'A staff party? Great!' The word otlichno means 'excellent', and it is also the top mark in a Russian school.
- 'Okay, I'll tell everyone in the kitchen.' Here na kukhne, ending in -e, means 'in the kitchen', because nobody is going anywhere.
