## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Nastya, wait!' The word for 'wait' is the casual command. Kostya calls out her name first.
- 'Bye.' The word 'poka' is the casual goodbye, which Nastya says without looking back.
- 'Oh, are you leaving already?' The word for 'leave' is the ongoing kind, for going away, and 'already' is a small word.
- 'Yeah. It's a bit of a downer here.' The word 'aga' is a casual 'yeah'. The Russian for 'dull' is made by putting 'ne' in front of 'cheerful'.
- 'Don't leave so fast.' The word for a negative command uses the ongoing kind, and 'so fast' is two small words.
- 'I've got a lot to do.' The Russian for 'matters' loses its ending after 'many', and 'at me' stands for 'I have'.
- 'I want to talk to you.' The word for 'with you' is the irregular 'toboy', and the Russian for 'I want' is irregular too.
- 'What do you want to talk about?' The words for 'about what' sound like one. The Russian for 'you want' carries 'sh'.
- 'It's very important.' The word for 'important' ends in 'o', without a subject, and the word for 'very' is placed in front.
- 'Speak faster.' The word for 'faster' is made from 'quickly' with 'ee', and the Russian for 'speak' is the informal order.
- 'Come a bit closer.' The 'po' prefix makes the word for 'closer' into 'a bit closer', and the word for 'come up' has a prefix too.
- 'I'm listening.' The Russian for 'I listen' comes as one word, and it finishes with 'yu'.
- 'I can't keep quiet.' The word for 'I can't' is irregular, and 'keep quiet' is the plain form.
- 'Please hear me out.' The word for 'hear out' has the prefix 'vy', meaning 'to the end', and the word for 'please' is the longer one.
