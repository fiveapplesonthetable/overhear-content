## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Kostya, I wanted to talk something over with you.' The word koe-chto means 'something', one particular thing she has in mind. The -la ending of khotela, 'wanted', marks a woman speaking.
- 'It's a very important conversation.' The word razgovor, 'conversation', comes from govorit, 'to talk'.
- 'Wait.' The word podozhdi is how you'd say it to a friend; to a stranger you'd say podozhdite.
- 'I think my stomach is cramping.' Russian says the stomach 'got twisted', zhivot skrutilo, with nobody doing the twisting. The opening kazhetsya means 'it seems'.
- 'I urgently need the toilet.' Russian leaves out 'to go': mne nuzhno v tualet is literally 'to me it's needed into the toilet'.
- 'Right now?' The word pryamo means 'straight', and pryamo seychas is 'right now'.
- 'Yes, right now. I'll be quick.' Russian says ya bystro, literally 'I quickly', and doesn't need a verb.
- 'Fine, I'll wait.' Her podozhdu, 'I'll wait', is the same word as Kostya's podozhdi, with the ending for 'I'.
