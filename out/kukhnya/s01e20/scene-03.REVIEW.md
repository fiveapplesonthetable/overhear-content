## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Nastya said that we're walking up a staircase.' After po, 'along', lestnitsa takes the ending -e: po lestnitse, just as in her own words.
- 'And if we stop, we'll crash.' The pair esli ... to means 'if ... then'. The word razobyomsya, 'we'll crash', is a future form with the -sya ending.
- 'I didn't understand a thing.' Russian doubles the negative: nichego ne ponyal, literally 'nothing not understood'.
- 'So what didn't you get?' In casual speech Russian says chyo for chto, 'what'.
- 'She's hinting at a wedding.' Russian hints 'on' something: namekat na, so svadba, 'wedding', changes its ending to svadbu.
- 'No way, are you serious?' The phrase da ladno literally means 'yes, fine', but here it says 'come on, really?'.
- 'Pretend you didn't understand anything.' The phrase sdelay vid literally means 'make a look', and it's the Russian for 'pretend'.
- 'But I really didn't understand anything.' Here i tak means 'as it is' or 'already': he doesn't even need to pretend.
- 'Well there, you won't even have to lie.' The phrase ne pridyotsya, 'it won't be necessary', takes tebe, literally 'to you', rather than ty.
- 'What is it? What did she send?' The word chego is a casual chto, 'what'. In prislala, 'sent', the -la ending shows the sender is a woman.
- 'She sent me a photo of a beautiful ring.' After foto, 'photo of', krasivoe koltso, 'beautiful ring', changes both endings: krasivogo koltsa, 'of a beautiful ring'.
- 'She's hinting at an expensive present.' Again namekat na, 'hint at', and podarok, 'present', keeps the same form after na. The word dorogoy means both 'expensive' and 'dear'.
- 'I'd have to work a year for it.' Russian says mne nado, 'to me it is necessary'. The phrase na nego, 'for it', means 'to pay for it'.
