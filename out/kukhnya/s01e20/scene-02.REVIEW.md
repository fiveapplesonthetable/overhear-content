## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Kostya, we're good together, aren't we?' Russian says nam khorosho, 'to us it's good', rather than 'we are good'. The little zhe asks Kostya to agree.
- 'Well, yes, of course it's good.' In konechno, 'of course', the ch is said like sh: 'koneshno'.
- 'It's as if we're walking up a staircase.' The phrase kak budto means 'as if'. After po, 'along', lestnitsa becomes lestnitse.
- 'We're going up, straight towards the sun.' After k, 'towards', solntse becomes solntsu. The l in solntse is written but never said.
- 'We climb higher and higher, step by step.' Put vsyo in front of vyshe, 'higher', and you get 'higher and higher'. Her shag za shagom is literally 'step after step'.
- 'We're not in a hurry to climb, are we?' With ne and zhe together, the question expects the answer 'no'.
- 'I guess not, we're not in a hurry.' The word vrode means 'seemingly', so vrode net is a careful 'I don't think so'.
- 'If we stop, we might fall.' The word ostanovimsya, 'we stop', is a finished-action form, and in the present that points to the future: 'if we come to a stop'.
- 'We could fall down and smash ourselves.' The word vniz means 'downwards', movement towards below; for 'down there' Russian uses vnizu.
- 'On no account must we stop.' The fixed phrase ni v koem sluchae means 'in no case at all', and nelzya, 'not allowed', takes nam, 'for us', in front.
- 'We must always go only forward.' The word dolzhny, 'must', has the plural ending -ny to match my, 'we'; one man alone would say dolzhen.
- 'Yes, I agree with you.' Kostya literally says 'I am agreed with you', ya soglasen. A woman would say soglasna.
