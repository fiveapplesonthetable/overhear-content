## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Kostya, we have to talk.' Russian expresses this impersonally, literally saying 'to me' or 'to you' with 'to talk'. Then Kostya replies.
- 'Can it wait?' Russian forms this yes-or-no question without a question word, relying on rising pitch on 'to wait'.
- 'No, it is important.' Nastya delivers a punchy one-word reaction built around the Russian word meaning 'important'.
- 'Nastya, I have missed you so much.' Notice the reflexive ending on the verb for 'missed', which directs the action back onto the subject.
- 'Please, listen to me.' Nastya leads with the direct command form meaning 'listen' to make the point directly in conversation.
- 'I am listening to you.' Kostya delivers a punchy one-word reaction built around the Russian word meaning 'I'm listening'.
- 'Our relationship is moving fast.' Notice the reflexive ending on the verb for 'develop', which directs the action back onto the subject. Then Kostya responds.
- 'I think this is great.' Kostya chooses the imperfective verb meaning 'I think' for the ongoing action, followed by 'perfect'.
- 'But we need to discuss our future.' Nastya puts the word for 'it is necessary' together with 'to discuss' and 'future' in a crisp, natural spoken turn of phrase.
- 'We have a lot of time.' Kostya uses the classic Russian possession phrasing — literally 'at us' rather than a verb meaning 'to have', combining 'there is' with 'a lot' and 'time'.
- 'No, the time is now.' The phrasing opens with 'time' before bringing in 'to come' and 'now' to make the point clear.
- 'Okay, I am ready to talk.' Kostya expresses this naturally by combining the word for 'good' with 'ready' and 'to talk'.
