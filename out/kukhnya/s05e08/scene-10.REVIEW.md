- 'You and I look alike as two drops of water.' Elena brings out the contrast between 'we' and 'with' in a few short words.
- 'Nobody will notice.' In Russian, the idea of 'nobody' flows naturally into 'notices' without extra filler words.
- 'And my husband only visits me once a month anyway.' The meaning hinges on 'and' working together with 'visits' in spoken Russian.
- 'And he already visited this month.' Elena anchors the line on 'and', while 'was' completes the thought.
- 'No, it is time for me to go.' Spoken Russian keeps this brisk, leaning on 'no' and finishing on 'it's time'.
- 'Then I will have to say that you got me drunk.' The '-a' ending on the past-tense verb for 'got drunk' marks a female speaker or subject.
- 'And your head chef undressed me and harassed me.' Here the Russian phrasing highlights 'and' before landing on 'harassed'.
- 'What kind of nonsense is that?' The meaning hinges on 'what' working together with 'nonsense' in spoken Russian.
- 'And whom will my husband believe?' Elena raises the pitch on 'husband' to turn the phrase into a direct spoken question.
