## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'What is happening here?' The guest probes eagerly to see how Max will answer on the spot.
- 'Excuse us, please.' Max uses one colorful touch in earnest: "izvinite" means "sorry, excuse me" (from "izvinit"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'It is your interpreter's fault.' Max gives the reply at once: notice how Russian needs no word for "am", "is", or "are" in the present — placing the words side by side says it all.
- 'He is using your name for his own purposes.' Max reacts openly around Senya, so Senya knows he is not just making small talk.
- 'I beg your pardon, he will be fired.' The guest anchors this sharp detail in reply: "proshcheniya" means "forgiveness" (from "proshcheniye"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'What did he just say?' Senya lays out that thought calmly now: the "-sya" ending on "seychas" ("now") turns the action inward or makes it happen between people naturally.
- 'He said that if we do not apologize, we will be fired.' The guest answers steadily to be clear, refusing to let the moment slip past.
- 'Excuse us, please!' Senya focuses on one direct expression toward Max: "izvinite" means "sorry, excuse me" (from "izvinit"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'Excuse us, guys, we are leaving already.' Max hands over the thought calmly now: using the polite form "izvinite" ("excuse me, forgive me") keeps a respectful distance as Max steps in.
- 'This will never happen again!' Senya weighs in calmly beside Max, making his position completely clear.
- 'You may return to work.' The guest chooses one key touch on cue: "vozvrashchatsya" means "to return", and it carries the main weight of what the guest is saying.
- 'Let us get out of here to the kitchen quickly.' Max replies warmly watching Senya, saying out loud what both of them already know.
