## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Envy is choking you too, and a very big one at that.' Kostya addresses the listener with the informal singular 'you' alongside 'big'.
- 'Max, Nastya is nowhere to be found, she is not picking up the phone.' Kostya brings out the contrast between 'Max' and 'Nastya' in a few short words.
- 'Did you check the harem?' Katya anchors the line on 'harem', while 'watched' completes the thought.
- 'Oh, thank you, I have not relaxed like this in a year.' The '-a' ending on the past-tense verb for 'relaxed' marks a female speaker or subject.
- 'I live in a golden cage.' Spoken Russian keeps this brisk, leaning on 'live' and finishing on 'cage'.
- 'Both my girlfriends and parties—everything is gone.' Notice how Russian expresses 'and' and 'is gone' with crisp inflection rather than helper words.
- 'Well, that is your choice.' Notice how Russian expresses 'come on' and 'choice' with crisp inflection rather than helper words. Elena answers right away.
- 'What is your name?' The grammar packs 'how' and 'is called' into a tight, idiomatic kitchen exchange.
- 'Katya.' Katya anchors the line on 'Katya', while 'Katya' completes the thought.
- 'And my name is Yulya.' Elena centers this short reply on 'is called', letting a few direct words carry the whole meaning.
- 'Very nice to meet you, Yulya.' Katya brings out the contrast between 'very' and 'pleasant' in a few short words.
- 'Sit with me a little longer.' In Russian, the idea of 'sit for a while' flows naturally into 'a little' without extra filler words.
