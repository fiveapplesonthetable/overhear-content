## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'But this is a secret vote, after all.' Returning to the voting on the spot, Fedya links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
- 'Go on out of here, call the next person in.' Timur opens up wryly during the talk, before Fedya can change the subject.
- 'Good afternoon, I have a question.' Senya promptly extends this daytime greeting at the start of the beat using "dobryy den" ("good afternoon") — literally "good day", the standard Russian greeting from late morning through afternoon.
- 'Can the independent observer not watch?' Addressing the independent observer on the spot, Senya keeps normal statement word order here, turning the line into a question for Timur purely through rising pitch.
- 'No, one cannot do that at all.' Timur speaks up evenly facing Senya, keeping the focus on what actually matters here.
- 'Understood, and what if like this?' Senya plainly sends this acknowledgment for Timur using "ponyatno" ("understood") — a single word meaning "clear, got it", showing the point has landed.
- 'Great, now you have voted fairly.' Holding firm directly in front of Senya, Timur leans on "otlichno" ("great, excellent"), which carries the whole predicate in Russian without needing "it is".
- 'Let us sum up the results of our fair contest.' Timur weighs in decisively with Senya, to clear the air between them once and for all.
- 'Today Arseny won the election!' Underscoring the election on the spot, Timur centers the thought on "vyborakh" ("election", from "vybory") without wasting a word.
- 'How did he suddenly win like that?' Stepping in boldly to Timur, Fedya puts the question word "kak" right at the start so Timur hears the focus immediately.
- 'Does anyone have any objections right now?' Timur questions promptly where this conversation is heading next.
- 'You can take the ballots for salad.' Highlighting the ballots beside Fedya, Timur leans on "byulleteni" ("ballots", a form of "byulleten") to give the remark its edge.
