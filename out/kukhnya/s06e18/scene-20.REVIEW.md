## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Why is everything in life so unfair?' By asking why everything in life so unfair is, Katya presses Denis for a straight answer. In Russian, she does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'A person's life depends on pieces of paper.' With the moment and choice in play, Katya keeps this short reply brisk and direct.
- 'Stop crying, it is too early to give up.' Claiming that Katya should stop crying it is too early to give up sounds confident, even if Katya has doubts.
- 'We will definitely think of something else.' Once they will definitely think of something else, Denis can finally tell Katya what he means.
- 'Katya, Nikita is looking for you over there.' Adding that Nikita is looking for Katya over there fills in the missing piece for Katya. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
- 'Nikita, we need to talk right now.' Needing to talk right now moves the conversation forward without extra drama.
- 'I spoke with my father.' Since he spoke with his father, Nikita does not waste words on excuses.
- 'He will pay for this operation in full.' Mentioning that he will pay for this operation in full moves the talk in a new direction.
- 'And our problems are now shared.' Nikita reminds Denis that their problems are now shared before the moment passes.
- 'I need to do a report urgently.' Adding that he needs to do a report urgently fills in the missing piece for Denis. There is no "you" needed in the Russian here: one short word states the rule for everyone in the room.
- 'Forgive me, I could not say it.' Explaining that she could not say it lays the cards on the table for Denis.
- 'Love also depends on pieces of paper.' Realizing that Katya should love also depends on pieces of paper changes how Denis handles the situation.
