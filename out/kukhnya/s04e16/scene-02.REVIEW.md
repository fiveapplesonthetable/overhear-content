## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'You know, Denis, I like you very much too.' Here liking Denis very much sets up the next turn in their exchange. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
- 'Really? I waited so long for these words!' Questioning whether he waited so long for these words is how Denis tests Katya's story. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'But in our family there is one problem.' Even though in their family there is one problem, Katya keeps a steady face in front of Denis.
- 'My father is against the two of us dating.' Admitting that her father is against the two of them dating shows Katya being honest for a change.
- 'He is a very strict and stern person.' Saying that he is a very strict and stern person is how Katya sets the tone with Denis.
- 'Without his consent nothing is decided in our family.' Once without his consent nothing is decided in their family, Katya can finally tell Denis what she means.
- 'I cannot go against my dad's will.' Pointing out that she cannot go against her dad will gives Denis a sobering reality check.
- 'Why didn't you tell me right away?' Here asking why not Katya tells him right away shows Denis looking for the real truth. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
- 'After all, this is not a difficult problem at all!' Insisting that after all this is not a difficult problem at all shows Denis is not backing down.
- 'I will have a serious talk with him today.' Mentioning that he will have a serious talk with him today moves the talk in a new direction.
- 'Your father will definitely give us his consent!' Declaring that Katya's father will definitely give them his consent brings Denis's temper right to the surface. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
- 'Wait, Denis, it is better not to go to him!' Katya insists that it is better not to go to him, leaving Denis no room to argue.
