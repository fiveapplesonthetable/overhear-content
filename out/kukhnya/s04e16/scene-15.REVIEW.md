## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'After the first number we invite the guests onto the veranda.' In everyday Russian, 'first' flows straight into 'veranda' without extra linking words.
- 'Viktoriya Sergeyevna, they are urgently calling you to the entrance.' German interrupts her with a third-person plural impersonal verb.
- 'They just delivered artificial blood there for the show.' Notice how Russian puts 'there, to that place' and 'blood' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'Oh, they called me, I completely forgot!' Vika hurries down the corridor to sign for the delivery.
- 'Guys, please come over here for one minute!' She calls two stagehands over to the closed vampire coffin.
- 'What is this box doing in the middle of the narrow corridor?' Vika pairs 'box, crate' with 'corridor, hall' directly, since spoken Russian skips English filler words like 'the', 'a', and 'is'.
- 'Carry it down to the quiet basement for now.' Notice how Russian puts 'to carry to, take to' and 'basement, cellar' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'Just carry it carefully, this is a very important prop!' A masculine singular noun means theatrical stage props.
- 'Why did this coffin suddenly become so heavy?' Neither of them suspects that Nagiyev is fast asleep inside.
- 'It is probably made of very solid oak.' Vika assumes the weight comes from heavy oak wood.
- 'Put it downstairs and return to the hall right away.' Notice how Russian puts 'put' and 'hall, large room; courtroom' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'Good, Viktoriya Sergeyevna, we will do everything quickly right now!' The workers prepare to lift the coffin.
