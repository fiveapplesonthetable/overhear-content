## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Look, guys, the costume fits simply perfectly!' In Russian, clothes literally 'sit' on the wearer when they fit well.
- 'Even my new Italian moccasins matched excellently!' Here Nagiyev anchors the thought on 'new' and finishes on 'to come up, to approach', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'And what a funny wooden chest is standing nearby!' Nagiyev points at the coffin prop thinking it is a treasure chest.
- 'Why did you hang a heavy lock on it?' He notices the metal padlock on the lid.
- 'That is needed according to our festive script, Dima.' Notice how Russian puts 'necessary, need to' and 'dima' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'In a she-devil costume, I will open this lock.' Here Vika anchors the thought on 'suit' and finishes on 'to open', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'And I will ceremoniously release Count Dracula to freedom!' In everyday Russian, 'solemnly, ceremoniously' flows straight into 'will; freedom, liberty' without extra linking words.
- 'Wait, who will you release from this coffin?' Nagiyev suddenly realizes who is supposed to be inside the box.
- 'No, guys, I am not climbing into a coffin!' He refuses using the first-person singular future of the verb for climbing.
- 'But the main client himself suggested this number!' Vika invokes the bank chairman who is paying for the banquet.
- 'On the other hand, after all, it is a vampire's home!' German diplomatically reframes the coffin as a cozy vampire residence.
- 'All right, Vika, just do not lose the only key!' Nagiyev relents and warns Vika to guard the padlock key.
