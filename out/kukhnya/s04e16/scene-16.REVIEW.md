## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Kostya, are you already drinking in the middle of the day?' Nastya confronts her husband using the Russian idiom for 'in broad daylight'.
- 'Nastya, well I only tasted a tiny drop!' Notice how Russian puts 'tried' and 'a tiny drop' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'After all, I am simply studying to become a real sommelier.' Kostya defends his wine tasting as homework for his course.
- 'By the way, this dry wine goes excellently with the soup!' He tries to demonstrate his new pairing skills.
- 'Nastya, wait, why did you suddenly start crying?' He stops short when he sees tears in her eyes.
- 'Tell me honestly, did something happen to you?' Russians say 'by me' or 'with us' where English says 'I have' or 'we have', without needing a separate word for 'have'.
- 'When I was little, my dad used to drink.' Here Nastya anchors the thought on 'when' and finishes on 'was drinking', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'At first he also drank only one little drop at a time.' In everyday Russian, 'also, too' flows straight into 'tiny drop, a little bit' without extra linking words.
- 'I am very afraid of a repeat of this sad story.' Nastya pairs 'very, really' with 'stories' directly, since spoken Russian skips English filler words like 'the', 'a', and 'is'.
- 'After all, we have a little son growing up at home.' She reminds him of their young boy.
- 'I want a completely different childhood for him!' Here Nastya anchors the thought on 'want' and finishes on 'childhood', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'Forgive me, darling, I will not do it anymore!' Kostya hugs her and promises to stop drinking during shifts.
