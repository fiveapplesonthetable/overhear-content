## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Allow me to offer you a wonderful wine with your lamb.' Notice how Russian puts 'to allow, to permit' and 'wine' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'Why are you offering me the most expensive wine?' Here the guest anchors the thought on 'why' and finishes on 'wine', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'Because expensive wine is always the very best!' In everyday Russian, 'because, therefore' flows straight into 'the best' without extra linking words.
- 'Expensive wine does not mean the very best at all.' The guest corrects him using the third-person singular verb for meaning.
- 'This heavy wine will completely overpower the flavor of the dish!' Notice how Russian puts 'heavy; hard, difficult' and 'of dish' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'Let tasty wine dominate on your table!' Kostya makes matters worse with a clumsy third-person injunction.
- 'I asked to call a sommelier, not a bartender!' Putting the short word for 'not' right in front of the action word is all Russian needs to make a sentence negative, with no extra helper words like 'do' or 'did'.
- 'Call the manager here, I want to have a serious talk!' the guest pairs 'call' with 'to talk' directly, since spoken Russian skips English filler words like 'the', 'a', and 'is'.
- 'Excuse me, choose any good wine on my tab!' To avoid a complaint, Kostya offers to pay for the bottle himself.
- 'Kostik, let's sign you up for sommelier courses!' Starting with the everyday word for 'let us' is how Russian speakers warmly invite someone to do something together.
- 'That is too expensive, Nastya, I will read on the internet.' Kostya hesitates over the tuition cost.
- 'Good education is an investment in our future!' Nastya pairs 'good' with 'future, next' directly, since spoken Russian skips English filler words like 'the', 'a', and 'is'.
