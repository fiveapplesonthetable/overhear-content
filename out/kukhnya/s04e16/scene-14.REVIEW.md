## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Viktor Petrovich, look, your palace is completely ready!' Making clear how ready things are in front of Barinov, the "-sya" ending on "dvorets" ("palace") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'You have already been running errands for two weeks.' Barinov answers quietly hearing Denis, steering the talk right where he wants it.
- 'I will rest when you allow me to go out with Katya!' Denis jumps in pointedly in front of Barinov and puts the emphasis on "vstrechatsya" ("to meet") in the middle of the talk.
- 'Dad, do not stay silent, give him an impossible task!' Underscoring the dad in front of Denis, Katya uses "nevypolnimoye" ("task", from "nevypolnimyy") to make the point stick.
- 'What other task? My imagination has run out!' Barinov asks quietly how Katya expects that to work in practice.
- 'After all, you were just reading Gogol's fairy tale!' Mentioning the tale around Barinov, Katya reaches for the Russian word "gogolya" ("tale") right at the heart of the line.
- 'Right! Did Nagiyev put on his expensive moccasins for nothing?' Focusing on the moccasins before Katya, Barinov keeps normal statement word order here, turning the line into a question for Katya purely through rising pitch.
- 'Listen to me closely, Denis, there is one last test.' Barinov fires back plainly without any doubt, refusing to let the moment slip past.
- 'Bring me Dmitry Nagiyev's famous slippers today!' Bringing up the slippers to Denis, Barinov chooses "znamenityye" ("famous, celebrated", from the root "znamenityy") so Denis hears the exact meaning.
- 'Excuse me, what slippers do you want to get?' Reacting frankly in this beat, Denis puts the question word "kakiye" right at the start so Barinov hears the focus immediately.
- 'Bring me Nagiyev's red moccasins, then we will talk!' Barinov speaks up sharply without any doubt, so Denis knows he is not just making small talk.
- 'Are you speaking completely seriously right now, Viktor Petrovich?' Pointing out how serious things are during the talk, Denis uses "absolyutno" ("completely") to carry the main weight of the thought.
