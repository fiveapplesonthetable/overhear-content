## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello, please come on through.' The formal greeting is followed by the polite plural command inviting the officer inside.
- 'They stole a copper sauté pan.' The adjective for 'copper' agrees with the masculine inanimate noun in the accusative case.
- 'Nothing else disappeared.' Russian uses double negation here: the negative pronoun 'nothing' requires the negative particle before the neuter past-tense verb.
- 'I cannot find the sturgeon.' The feminine noun for sturgeon meat takes the accusative ending '-u' as the direct object of 'to find'.
- 'It seems to me they stole it along with the pan.' The impersonal phrase 'it seems to me' softens Senya's convenient lie before the instrumental case after 'with'.
- 'You called us in over this nonsense?' The compound preposition meaning 'because of' always requires the genitive case on the following phrase.
- 'Excuse me, this is not nonsense.' German opens with the polite plural apology before flatly contradicting the claim.
- 'For a chef, this is the most important thing.' The neuter superlative phrase acts as a noun meaning 'the main thing' after the preposition 'for' plus the genitive.
- 'And what if someone stole a pistol from you?' In Russian, stealing something 'from' someone is expressed with the preposition for 'by/at' plus the genitive pronoun.
- 'Well then, shall we have some fun?' The particle for 'let's' combines with the first-person plural future verb to suggest an activity together.
- 'No, we must draw on paper.' The impersonal word for 'must' pairs with the imperfective infinitive 'to draw'.
- 'Have you already started having fun without me?' The preposition for 'without' governs the genitive form of 'me' right before the past-tense verb.
