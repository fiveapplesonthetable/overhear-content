## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'German Mikhailovich, here is your coffee.' The demonstrative particle 'here is' introduces the item being served without needing a verb.
- 'Just the way you like it.' Literally 'everything how you love', this is a classic phrase servers use when presenting a favorite order.
- 'Why are you standing there, go on.' In casual speech, the genitive pronoun 'of what' is often used instead of 'why'.
- 'And why does it have to be me right away?' Adding the adverb 'right away' highlights Fedya's protest at being volunteered first.
- 'Let's play rock, paper, scissors for it.' Notice that Russian lists the three items in the order 'stone, scissors, paper'.
- 'We already flipped a coin.' The diminutive noun for 'little coin' takes the feminine accusative ending '-u' before the past-tense verb.
- 'Maybe we should play the cities game too?' In Russian, playing a named game uses the preposition 'in' plus the accusative plural.
- 'Go on, reach into his pocket.' The imperative 'climb/reach' combines with 'to him into the pocket' to show whose pocket is being searched.
- 'It is very funny to you, is it?' Feelings and reactions are expressed impersonally with the dative pronoun 'to you' and an adverb in '-o'.
- 'You had better help me instead.' Putting the comparative word 'better' alongside a command means 'you'd better help me instead'.
- 'Well, what are you doing in here?' Louis whispers an urgent question using the colloquial adverb for 'here'.
- 'I cannot get my hand back out.' The perfective infinitive means to reach or pull something out from a tight spot.
- 'I am afraid that he will wake up right now.' The reflexive future verb means 'will wake up' on one's own.
- 'He will not wake up, he is sleeping soundly.' Russian says someone sleeps 'strongly' or 'firmly' where English says 'soundly'.
