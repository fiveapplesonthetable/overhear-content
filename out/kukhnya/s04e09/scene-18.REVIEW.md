## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'And what just happened out here?' Adding 'only what' before the past-tense verb is how Russian says 'just now'.
- 'Nothing happened, we are breathing fresh air.' In everyday Russian, 'air' flows straight into 'to breathe' without extra linking words.
- 'Chef, we tried very hard.' Senya pairs 'chef' with 'tried' directly, since spoken Russian skips English filler words like 'the', 'a', and 'is'.
- 'Everything is fine, guys.' Barinov reassures his bruised cooks even before Elena reveals her surprise.
- 'I have the sauté pan, here it is.' Elena reveals the pan using the masculine pronoun 'he/it' after 'here is'.
- 'Vitya, do not behave like a child.' In Russian, 'to behave' is literally 'to lead oneself'.
- 'And will German not cry?' Putting the short word for 'not' right in front of the action word is all Russian needs to make a sentence negative, with no extra helper words like 'do' or 'did'.
- 'I took your sauté pan out of the safe.' Notice how Russian puts 'took away' and 'safe' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'And I bought him an exact same one.' Adding the particle 'zhe' after 'such' means 'the very same' or 'identical'.
- 'A toy for each of you.' Elena teases the two grown chefs by calling their copper pans 'toys'.
- 'Wait, why did you fight then?' The reciprocal past verb 'fought each other' is paired with the question word 'for what purpose'.
- 'So that he would not call the police!' After the conjunction 'so that', Russian always uses the past-tense form of the verb regardless of time.
- 'So say thank you to me.' Chef Barinov uses a polite everyday Russian courtesy expression that sets the tone of the conversation right away.
- 'Get ready, we are going to the banya!' True to his word, Barinov keeps his promise and invites everyone — even Louis — to the bathhouse.
