## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Here is this little boy!' Adding the emphatic particle right after 'here is' expresses sudden recognition.
- 'Wait, I will explain everything right now.' The polite plural imperative asks everyone to pause before jumping to conclusions.
- 'Come on in, dear guests.' Putting the adjective 'dear' after the noun 'guests' gives a traditional, ironic Russian welcome.
- 'We tried, it did not work out for us.' Success or failure in Russian is expressed impersonally: literally 'by us it did not turn out'.
- 'A dubious way to stop me.' The adjective 'dubious' modifies 'way/method', followed by the infinitive phrase.
- 'That is it, I am leaving.' In spoken Russian, saying 'I went' in the past tense means 'I'm off' or 'I'm leaving right now'.
- 'Close the walk-in fridge quickly!' In a restaurant kitchen, the walk-in cooler is simply called 'the chamber'.
- 'Let's not shout.' Negative 'let's not' in Russian always uses 'let's not be' plus the imperfective infinitive.
- 'Calm down, everyone, please.' The reflexive command urges the whole room to settle down.
- 'This is Konstantin Anisimov's child.' Both the first name and surname of the father take genitive endings to mark possession.
- 'Do you know Konstantin?' Because a person is animate, the masculine accusative direct object takes the genitive ending '-a'.
- 'Then where is our child?' The adverb 'then' links the realization to the urgent question 'where'.
