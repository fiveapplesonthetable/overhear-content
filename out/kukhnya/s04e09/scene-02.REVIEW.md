## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Lyova, hand me the sauté pan.' The singular imperative form gives a direct kitchen command, followed by the dative pronoun meaning 'to me'.
- 'What kind of nonsense is this?' The colloquial question pattern with 'what for' asks 'what kind of' when expressing surprise or irritation.
- 'I need my copper sauté pan.' Need is expressed with the dative pronoun 'to me' and a short-form adjective that agrees in masculine gender with the pan.
- 'Your copper sauté pan stayed there.' Lyova uses the polite possessive 'your' because he always addresses Chef Barinov respectfully.
- 'It was in the other restaurant.' After the preposition for 'in', both the adjective 'other' and the noun 'restaurant' take prepositional case endings.
- 'I need it right now.' Repeating the masculine short-form predicate highlights that the pan itself is the grammatical subject.
- 'Here, take the compote.' The informal command literally means 'hold' and is the standard Russian way to say 'here, take this'.
- 'Do not give him too much, okay?' Negating an imperfective command tells the listener not to do an action at all or in excess.
- 'Otherwise it will go badly.' The two-word conjunction at the start is the everyday spoken way to warn 'otherwise' or 'or else'.
- 'It will be like last time.' The time phrase uses the accusative case after 'in' to refer back to a specific occasion.
- 'Here are the discs with cartoons.' The noun for animated cartoons is a colloquial shortened plural that takes the instrumental ending after 'with'.
- 'I understood everything, chef.' Putting the object pronoun 'everything' before the past-tense verb gives a brisk, reassuring confirmation.
