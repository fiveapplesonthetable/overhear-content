## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I did not expect that you would manage.' Vika speaks up directly with Max and brings in "spravishsya" ("will manage", from "spravitsya") as the key Russian word here.
- 'You were with a child for half a day.' Vika continues openly with a nod, grounding the moment in plain reality.
- 'And nothing happened.' Vika replies candidly in their talk, so Max knows she is not just making small talk.
- 'Well yes, and what could have happened?' Max continues firmly to Vika and brings in "sluchitsya" ("to happen") to make the meaning unmistakable.
- 'You will not be afraid to stay with our baby.' Vika cuts in lightly with a shrug, before Max can change the subject.
- 'Vika, forgive me, please.' Max calmly uses this personal apology at this moment using "prosti" ("forgive me") — the close, informal way to say "forgive me" or "I'm sorry" to someone dear.
- 'I should have told the truth about Denis.' Talking about the truth in front of Vika, Max centers the thought on "pravdu" ("truth", from "pravda") without wasting a word.
- 'I will not lie to you anymore.' Max proceeds bluntly with a smile, to clear the air between them once and for all.
- 'I will try very hard to tell you the truth.' Max counters decisively beside Vika, shifting the mood of the conversation in a single beat.
- 'Always?' Vika speaks evenly in front of Max and chooses "vsegda" ("always") so Max gets the point right away.
- 'How hard it is for a person to change.' Insisting on how difficult things are to Vika, Max centers the thought on "slozhno" ("difficult", from "slozhnyy") without wasting a word.
- 'Someone is simply afraid to do it.' Max fires back right away toward Vika, without trying to dress up how he really feels.
- 'But one thing I know for sure.' Max replies openly right here and puts the emphasis on "tochno" ("definitely") in the middle of the talk.
- 'For the sake of the person you love, you must change.' Turning to the person during the talk, Max leans on "nuzhno" ("it is needed, one has to"), which carries the whole predicate in Russian without needing "it is".
