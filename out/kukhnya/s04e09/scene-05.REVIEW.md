## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello, Viktoriya Sergeyevna.' Using a person's first name together with their patronymic is the standard respectful form of address at work.
- 'What happened?' Vika centers this short reply on 'happened', letting a few direct words carry the whole meaning.
- 'Viktoriya, we were robbed.' German repeats the dramatic plural past-tense verb for robbing a place clean.
- 'They stole a sauté pan.' Without a subject pronoun, the plural past verb focuses entirely on the stolen object at the end.
- 'And is that all?' Two tiny words with rising intonation express Vika's disbelief that nothing else is missing.
- 'You called me in over a saucepan?' Vika downgrades the fancy French sauté pan to an ordinary kitchen pot to show how absurd the alarm is.
- 'I called you in for a reason.' German uses the masculine singular past tense with the formal pronoun 'you'.
- 'Your Barinov stole the sauté pan.' Putting the object first and the culprit's name at the very end of the sentence delivers the accusation with maximum emphasis.
- 'We must stop him right now.' Notice how Russian puts 'necessary, need to' and 'to stop' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'Otherwise tomorrow he will carry out the stove.' Here German anchors the thought on 'tomorrow' and finishes on 'stove', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'German, it is just a saucepan.' Combining the emphatic particle with the word for 'simply' underscores how trivial the problem is.
- 'I might as well have called the police too.' The conditional particle pairs with the feminine past-tense verb to make a sarcastic hypothetical remark.
