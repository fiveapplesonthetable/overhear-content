## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I could invite him for a cup of coffee.' Vika mentions that she could invite him for a cup of coffee so Denis sees what is at stake. Russian packs the whole idea of what is allowed or needed into one impersonal word at the front.
- 'Although I have no special desire to.' Claiming that although she has no special desire sounds confident, even if Denis has doubts. In Russian you say "by me" or "with us" rather than "I have", so the situation sits right with the person.
- 'German, our evening was simply wonderful.' Pointing out that their evening was simply wonderful gives Denis a sobering reality check.
- 'And coffee at night is very unhealthy.' Vika explains that coffee at night is very unhealthy, keeping her voice calm and direct.
- 'In relationships everything can be like in war.' Realizing that in relationships everything can be like in war changes how Denis handles the situation.
- 'But all the main battles are still ahead.' Noting that all the main battles are still ahead keeps Denis's side of the story simple and clear.
- 'Katya, do you want ice cream right now?' Here asking whether Katya wants ice cream right now shows Denis looking for the real truth. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
- 'Let's do it, I really love chocolate ice cream.' Realizing that they should do it she really loves chocolate ice cream changes how Katya handles the situation. Starting with "davay" is the everyday Russian way to say "come on, let us do this".
- 'Hello, give us two chocolate ones, please.' Giving them two chocolate ones please moves the conversation forward without extra drama.
- 'Will you give me the ice cream or eat both?' Checking whether Denis gives her the ice cream or eat both is how Katya puts Denis on the spot. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
- 'It seems I won a lucky ticket!' Denis insists that it seems he won a lucky ticket, leaving Katya no room to argue. Russian puts the key word right up front so his emotion lands on the first beat.
- 'Then collect your prize!' Declaring that Denis should collect Denis's prize brings Katya's temper right to the surface.
