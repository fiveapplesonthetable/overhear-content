## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'My dad is an irresponsible eccentric.' Singing in a sad minor key from Katya's perspective, Denis pairs a long compound adjective with a noun meaning eccentric.
- 'And I have been waiting for my prince for a long time.' A present-tense verb with an adverb meaning for a long time expresses waiting that began long ago and continues.
- 'Probably his horse fell ill.' He jokes about why her fairy-tale prince on a white horse has not arrived yet.
- 'And the restaurant owner is no better than the horse.' Here Denis anchors the thought on 'owner' and finishes on 'horse; knight', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'He parties himself, yet scolds me.' A contrastive conjunction contrasts the boss's behavior with his unfair scolding.
- 'But you should perceive everything like this!' Denis switches the piano accompaniment to a lively, cheerful major key.
- 'My dad is still full of energy!' Notice how Russian puts 'still' and 'ofpower' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'And soon I will have a brother!' Russians say 'by me' or 'with us' where English says 'I have' or 'we have', without needing a separate word for 'have'.
- 'There will be no prince, there will be a king instead!' Putting the short word for 'not' right in front of the action word is all Russian needs to make a sentence negative, with no extra helper words like 'do' or 'did'.
- 'The owner shouted for no reason today.' Denis pairs 'owner' with 'to shout' directly, since spoken Russian skips English filler words like 'the', 'a', and 'is'.
- 'On the bright side, I am young and beautiful!' Notice how Russian puts 'young' and 'beautiful' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'Denis, this song is simply wonderful!' Katya laughs warmly, touched by his musical wit.
