## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'My dad is an irresponsible eccentric.' Singing in a sad minor key from Katya's perspective, Denis pairs a long compound adjective with a noun meaning eccentric.
- 'And I have been waiting for my prince for a long time.' A present-tense verb with an adverb meaning for a long time expresses waiting that began long ago and continues.
- 'Probably his horse fell ill.' He jokes about why her fairy-tale prince on a white horse has not arrived yet.
- 'And the restaurant owner is no better than the horse.' A comparative adjective meaning better takes the genitive singular of horse for the object of comparison.
- 'He parties himself, yet scolds me.' A contrastive conjunction contrasts the boss's behavior with his unfair scolding.
- 'But you should perceive everything like this!' Denis switches the piano accompaniment to a lively, cheerful major key.
- 'My dad is still full of energy!' He reframes her father's youthfulness using a short adjective meaning full and the genitive plural of strength.
- 'And soon I will have a brother!' A future existential verb turns the news of a sibling into a celebration.
- 'There will be no prince, there will be a king instead!' Notice the case shift: the negated future takes the genitive of prince, while the affirmative future takes the nominative of king.
- 'The owner shouted for no reason today.' An idiomatic negative prepositional phrase means for no reason at all.
- 'On the bright side, I am young and beautiful!' A compensating conjunction highlights a positive truth using two short feminine predicate adjectives.
- 'Denis, this song is simply wonderful!' Katya laughs warmly, touched by his musical wit.
