## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Right here is where you will live now.' Chef Barinov shows off the apartment using the compound future tense.
- 'The metro is around the corner, the restaurant is close.' Chef Barinov pairs 'metro' with 'closely' directly, since spoken Russian skips English filler words like 'the', 'a', and 'is'.
- 'I knew that you are a noble man.' Eva flatters his generosity with a descriptive masculine adjective meaning noble.
- 'I hope this will remain between us?' In spoken Russian, the words stay in their normal statement order and Chef Barinov's rising voice at the end turns the line into a question.
- 'Oh, I suddenly felt sick all of a sudden.' In everyday Russian, 'suddenly, what if' flows straight into 'badly' without extra linking words.
- 'Won't you drive me to work?' A prefixed verb of vehicular motion means to drive someone all the way to a destination.
- 'Denis, it is time to go, I am waiting outside!' Notice how Russian puts 'it's time' and 'street; outside' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'Well, are you feeling a bit better already?' Here Chef Barinov anchors the thought on 'already' and finishes on 'easier, better', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'Viktor Petrovich, buy me a car!' Emboldened by the apartment, Eva asks for a car with a direct request.
- 'Do you begrudge money for our child?' In spoken Russian, the words stay in their normal statement order and Eva's rising voice at the end turns the line into a question.
- 'I never begrudged money for my children.' Putting the short word for 'not' right in front of the action word is all Russian needs to make a sentence negative, with no extra helper words like 'do' or 'did'.
- 'Give birth to the child, then I will buy it.' Here Chef Barinov anchors the thought on 'to give' and finishes on 'will buy', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
