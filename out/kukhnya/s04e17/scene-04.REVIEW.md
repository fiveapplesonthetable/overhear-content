## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Well, what do you see, doctor?' Chef Barinov questions the physician using a polite plural present verb.
- 'Yes, there is definitely a child here.' The doctor confirms the diagnosis with an existential verb and an adverb of certainty.
- 'Do you believe me now?' In Russian, the verb meaning to believe takes a dative personal pronoun for the person trusted.
- 'And is that absolutely certain, doctor?' Barinov double-checks the bad news with two adverbs of certainty.
- 'Yes, the term is already two weeks.' The feminine numeral for two agrees in gender with the noun for week and requires the genitive singular.
- 'I will wait for you in the corridor.' Stunned by the result, Barinov excuses himself using a perfective future verb and prepositional location.
- 'I told you he would believe it.' Once alone, Eva gloats with an emphatic discourse particle and a perfective future verb.
- 'And what will you do next?' Her accomplice asks about her next step using the compound future tense and an adverb meaning further.
- 'He will get scared and give money.' A reflexive perfective future verb predicts the frightened reaction of a wealthy man.
- 'And I will not object.' She pairs a negated future auxiliary verb with an imperfective infinitive meaning to object.
- 'Here, take this, it is your fee.' Eva hands over some cash using an informal imperative verb and a masculine noun meaning professional fee.
- 'Listen, and is this really everything?' The doctor complains about the small payout using a summarizing pronoun as the predicate.
