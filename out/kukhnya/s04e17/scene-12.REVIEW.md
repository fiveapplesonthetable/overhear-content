## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I did not understand, what child of yours?' Katya tests skeptically why things suddenly took this turn between them.
- 'Katya, this is not your business at all.' Focusing on the matter to Katya, Barinov reaches for the Russian word "delo" ("matter") right at the heart of the line.
- 'You promised that you would change your ways!' Following up boldly right here, the "-sya" ending on "ispravishsya" ("to ways") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'How will people look at you?' Katya challenges point-blank about what Barinov just brought up.
- 'I am a grown man and will figure it out myself.' Barinov steps in frankly beside Katya and reaches for the Russian word "vzroslyy" ("grown-up, adult") right at the heart of the line.
- 'I know very well how you figure things out.' Looking over warmly in the exchange, the "-sya" ending on "razbirayeshsya" ("to understand well") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'You already abandoned two of your children!' Katya weighs in firmly with a sigh, making her position completely clear.
- 'Yes, I did not live with my children.' Discussing his children on the spot, Barinov centers the thought on "detmi" ("children", from "rebyonok") without wasting a word.
- 'But I always did everything for you.' Barinov speaks up boldly with Katya, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'You never lacked for anything.' Barinov weighs in plainly with a nod, shifting the mood of the conversation in a single beat.
- 'After all, you studied in France itself!' Barinov pushes back promptly beside Katya and leans on "uchilas" ("to study", a form of "uchitsya") to give the remark its edge.
- 'I needed a father, not money.' Bringing up a father in this beat, Katya lands on "nuzhen" ("is needed", built on "nuzhnyy") to keep the statement grounded.
