## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Meet my fiancé Denis.' Katya proudly presents Denis to her childhood friend at the table.
- 'He is the head chef of our restaurant.' She explains his white jacket by claiming he runs the entire kitchen.
- 'Good evening, how do you like it here?' Russians say 'by me' or 'with us' where English says 'I have' or 'we have', without needing a separate word for 'have'.
- 'I must go back to the stove.' Before he can say anything foolish about cooking, Denis excuses himself to the stove.
- 'Denis, come over here quickly!' Here Nagiyev anchors the thought on 'go, walk' and finishes on 'here, this way', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'We are going to drink delicious wine right now.' Nagiyev outlines his evening plan using the compound future tense.
- 'And at the grand piano you will play something romantic.' Nagiyev pairs 'to something' with 'romantic' directly, since spoken Russian skips English filler words like 'the', 'a', and 'is'.
- 'But you planned the date for tomorrow.' Denis reminds his boss that their lesson was supposed to be for tomorrow.
- 'Never put off love until tomorrow!' Here Nagiyev anchors the thought on 'never' and finishes on 'tomorrow', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'Friends, I prepared a musical gift for you.' Stopping by Katya's table first, Denis turns his piano duty into a gallant gesture.
- 'Yes, my fiancé plays wonderfully.' Katya beams with pride as Denis begins playing a gentle melody.
- 'A talented person is talented in everything.' Notice how Russian puts 'talented' and 'man' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
