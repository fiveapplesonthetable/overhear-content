## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello! Glad to see you here again.' With the room and plan at stake, Katya reacts without holding back. Notice how she sticks to the formal "you", keeping a professional line between them.
- 'Does your father not know the truth yet?' Here asking whether Katya's father not know the truth yet shows the guest looking for the real truth. Even in the middle of the drama, he keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
- 'Why did you not tell him anything?' Here wondering why Katya not tell him anything shows the guest looking for the real truth.
- 'There simply was not a good moment yet.' Noting that there simply was not a good moment yet keeps Katya's side of the story simple and clear.
- 'Katya, what is this guest saying right now?' Wondering what this guest saying right now is is how Nagiyev puts the guest on the spot. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'He says that he does not like it here.' Admitting that he says that he does not like it here shows Katya being honest for a change.
- 'For him this is a very boring place.' Saying that for him this is a very boring place is how Katya sets the tone with the guest. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'Tell him to stop by later.' Once the guest should tell him to stop by later, Nagiyev can finally tell the guest what he means. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
- 'Please do not tell my father about me.' Adding that do not tell her father fills in the missing piece for the guest.
- 'It is better if I tell him everything myself.' Saying that it is better if she tells him everything herself is how Katya sets the tone with the guest.
- 'Alright, I will wait for your truth.' Since Alright he will wait for Katya's truth, the guest does not waste words on excuses.
- 'Tell your father that I will stop by tomorrow.' Adding that Katya should tell Katya's father that he will stop by tomorrow fills in the missing piece for Katya.
- 'Do not translate that to him, do not!' Declaring that do not translate that to him do not brings Nagiyev's temper right to the surface. There is no "you" needed in the Russian here: one short word states the rule for everyone in the room.
