## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello! Glad to see you here again.' Katya replies sharply hearing the guest, shifting the mood of the conversation in a single beat.
- 'Does your father not know the truth yet?' The guest prompts curiously why things suddenly took this turn between them.
- 'Why did you not tell him anything?' Answering firmly in front of Katya, the guest pairs the negative word with "ne" ("not"), since Russian naturally doubles its negatives for emphasis.
- 'There simply was not a good moment yet.' Touching on a good moment with the guest, Katya uses the little word "prosto" ("simply, just") to make the point sound effortless.
- 'Katya, what is this guest saying right now?' Nagiyev probes skeptically how the guest expects that to work in practice.
- 'He says that he does not like it here.' Katya speaks evenly right here and reaches for the Russian word "nravitsya" ("is pleasing") right at the heart of the line.
- 'For him this is a very boring place.' Underscoring how boring things are in the exchange, Katya links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
- 'Tell him to stop by later.' Nagiyev counters at once hearing Katya, before Katya can change the subject.
- 'Please do not tell my father about me.' Underscoring her father in the exchange, Katya puts the weight on "govorite" ("say, speak", from "govorit") as the exchange unfolds.
- 'It is better if I tell him everything myself.' Katya fires back candidly during the talk and leans on "luchshe" ("better") to give the whole sentence its punch.
- 'Alright, I will wait for your truth.' The guest speaks up candidly watching Katya, without trying to dress up how he really feels.
- 'Tell your father that I will stop by tomorrow.' Touching on the father at once, the guest puts the weight on "peredayte" ("to pass on", from "peredat") as the exchange unfolds.
- 'Do not translate that to him, do not!' Pushing back quietly beside the guest, Nagiyev leans on "nado" ("it is necessary, one needs to"), which carries the whole predicate in Russian without needing "it is".
