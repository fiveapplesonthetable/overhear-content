## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello! Mom, here is our chef Viktor Petrovich.' Touching on the mum in front of Barinov, Max centers the remark on "zdravstvuyte" ("hello") without any extra fuss.
- 'Very nice to meet you, I am Viktor Petrovich, Katya's father.' Barinov jumps in dryly in this moment, testing Max's reaction in the middle of the exchange.
- 'Where are we going to celebrate our wedding?' Emphasizing his wedding in the exchange, Max reaches for the Russian word "prazdnovat" ("to celebrate") right at the heart of the line.
- 'Max, you can end this performance already.' Touching on the performance during the talk, Katya chooses "zakanchivat" ("to finish, to end") so Max gets the point right away.
- 'I told Dad that there was nothing between us.' Katya pushes back lightly around Max, steering the talk right where she wants it.
- 'Chef, I told you this truth right away!' Highlighting the truth in the exchange, Max reaches for the Russian word "pravdu" ("truth", built from "pravda") right at the center of the line.
- 'Thank you very much, here is your money for the act.' Focusing on the thank at once, Max links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
- 'Every person has their own secret fears.' Max weighs in calmly without any doubt, so Katya knows he is not just making small talk.
- 'Some people try never to open that door.' Touching on the door in this beat, Max brings in "starayutsya" ("to try", from "staratsya") as the key Russian word here.
- 'Others try to hide their fears from everyone.' Returning to the fears in front of Katya, the "-sya" ending on "pytayutsya" ("to try, to attempt") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'And some people in life are very lucky.' Max jumps in briskly with Katya, making his position completely clear.
- 'They have no fear in their soul at all.' Turning to the fear with Katya, Max brings in "strakha" ("fear", from "strakh") as the key Russian word here.
- 'Because brave guys are not afraid of anything.' Emphasizing how bold things are with Katya, Max pairs the negative word with "ne" ("not"), since Russian naturally doubles its negatives for emphasis.
