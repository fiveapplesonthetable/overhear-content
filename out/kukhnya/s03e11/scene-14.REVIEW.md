## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Good evening! How do you like our new cuisine?' Pressing on calmly in the exchange, Kostya puts the question word "kak" right at the start so Katya hears the focus immediately.
- 'May we invite the cook Katya here right now?' Kostya presses point-blank where this conversation is heading next.
- 'Good evening! Did you not like something today?' Pointing to the evening in this beat, Katya uses "ponravilos" ("pleased, was liked", from "ponravitsya") to make the point stick.
- 'No, not at all, everything is simply great!' Pressing on readily to Katya, Kostya uses the little word "prosto" ("simply, just") to make the point sound effortless.
- 'How does your dish hold its shape like that?' Kostya questions skeptically whether Katya really thought this through.
- 'What is the secret? I can show you everything!' Focusing on the secret to Kostya, Katya puts the emphasis on "sekret" ("secret") in the middle of the talk.
- 'He said that there is old furniture here.' Highlighting the furniture on the spot, Katya reaches for the Russian word "staraya" ("old", built from "staryy") right at the center of the line.
- 'The guest will now go with me to the kitchen.' Katya continues evenly with a shrug, making her position completely clear.
- 'Nagiyev told Vika that the furniture needs to be changed.' Mentioning the furniture with Katya, Kostya uses "mebel" ("furniture") to carry the main weight of the thought.
- 'Kostya, I am sure that Katya is lying.' Insisting on how sure he is beside Katya, Max chooses "uveren" ("sure, confident", from the root "uverennyy") so Katya hears the exact meaning.
- 'You should have seen her face this afternoon!' Max answers warmly in their talk, testing Kostya's reaction in the middle of the exchange.
- 'She thinks that only she knows how to bluff.' Max pushes back boldly at once and uses "umeyet" ("knows how to", from "umet") to make the point stick.
- 'Never mind, we have already been through this before!' Max follows up warmly in the exchange and lands on "prokhodili" ("to go through", built on "prokhodit") to keep the statement grounded.
