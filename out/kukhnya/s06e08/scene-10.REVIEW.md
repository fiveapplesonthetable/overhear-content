## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'The shashlik is simply great, it melts right in the mouth!' Saying that the shashlik is simply great it melts right in the mouth is how the guest takes charge of the moment.
- 'Thank you very much, I tried very hard today.' Mentioning that very much she tried very hard today moves the talk in a new direction.
- 'Kostya, and why are you not eating anything?' Wondering why Kostya not eating anything is is how the guest puts Kostya on the spot. Russian stacks "never" or "nothing" together with "not", doubling the refusal for extra weight.
- 'Thank you, I simply do not want shashlik.' Because he simply does not want shashlik, Kostya treats the matter as settled.
- 'Kostik, just try it, even I ate a piece!' Once someone just try it even she ate a piece, Nastya makes sure the guest hears every word. Russian puts the key word right up front so her emotion lands on the first beat.
- 'Nastya, what are you saying, you are a vegetarian!' Declaring that what are the guest saying the guest is a vegetarian brings Kostya's temper right to the surface. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
- 'For the sake of the family I am ready for anything today.' Claiming that for the sake of the family she is ready for anything today sounds confident, even if the guest has doubts.
- 'Go on, try at least one piece.' Claiming that the guest should go on try at least one piece sounds confident, even if the guest has doubts. Starting with "davay" is the everyday Russian way to say "come on, let us do this".
- 'In my opinion, this meat is dry and not tasty at all.' Saying that in his opinion this meat is dry and not tasty at all is how Kostya sets the tone with the guest. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'I do not want to eat his shashlik!' With the peace and point at stake, Kostya reacts without holding back.
- 'Kostya, you are behaving like a little child!' Insisting that the guest is behaving like a little child shows Nastya is not backing down. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
- 'Well then, men, shall we have a quiet talk now?' Asking whether men shall they have a quiet talk now puts Kostya right on the defensive. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
