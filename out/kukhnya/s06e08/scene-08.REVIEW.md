## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Our Katya is already a completely grown-up girl.' Mentioning the girl in the exchange, Nagiyev adds "sovsem" ("completely, at all") so the statement leaves zero room for doubt.
- 'I thought for a long time about what to give her today.' Nagiyev speaks openly on the spot, watching Barinov's face to see how the news lands.
- 'And I decided to give her a grown-up gift.' Addressing the gift before Barinov, Nagiyev leans on "vzroslyy" ("grown-up, adult") to give the whole sentence its punch.
- 'Please bring my gift into the studio!' Discussing his gift on the spot, Nagiyev chooses "podarok" ("gift, voucher") so Barinov gets the point right away.
- 'Wow, hold my box for a second.' Raising the hold without a pause, Barinov keeps Nagiyev's attention locked on what matters.
- 'Oh my goodness, thank you very much, this is so expensive!' Touching on the good in front of Barinov, Katya leans on "bolshoye" ("big, very much", a form of "bolshoy") to give the remark its edge.
- 'Nothing is too much for people close to me.' Highlighting how close things are right here, Nagiyev pairs the negative word with "ne" ("not"), since Russian naturally doubles its negatives for emphasis.
- 'After all, I am a young and very observant man.' Nagiyev weighs in steadily with Katya, making his position completely clear.
- 'I saw that your motorcycle is already old.' Turning to the motorcycle during the talk, Nagiyev puts the emphasis on "mototsikl" ("motorcycle") in the middle of the talk.
- 'It immediately reminded me of your dad.' Mentioning the daddy on the spot, Nagiyev chooses "napomnil" ("to remind", from the root "napomnit") so Katya hears the exact meaning.
- 'So I decided to do something nice for you.' Nagiyev speaks honestly in their talk, shifting the mood of the conversation in a single beat.
- 'Why are we standing around, let us congratulate the birthday girl!' Addressing the birthday girl in front of Katya, Nagiyev brings in "imeninnitsu" ("birthday girl", from "imeninnitsa") as the key Russian word here.
