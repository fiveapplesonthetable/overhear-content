## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'My goodness, you look simply wonderful.' Holding firm evenly at once, Barinov uses the little word "prosto" ("simply, just") to make the point sound effortless.
- 'I hardly recognized you at first.' Vika responds earnestly facing Barinov, so Barinov knows she is not just making small talk.
- 'Well, that means.' Barinov speaks up readily with Vika and reaches for the Russian word "znachit" ("then, so") right at the heart of the line.
- 'Viktoriya Sergeyevna.' Pressing on naturally during the talk, spoken Russian compresses the whole thought into just 2 words, skipping the extra helper words English needs.
- 'Welcome to our restaurant.' Barinov speaks up wryly with a sigh, leaving no doubt about where he stands.
- 'Thank you very much.' Vika firmly shares this polite thanks at this moment using "blagodaryu" ("I thank you") — literally "I give thanks", a slightly more formal way to say thank you than "spasibo".
- 'And I see this is not your first time here.' Barinov answers wryly with a shrug, bringing things back to earth after the buildup.
- 'I have been coming to your place for two weeks already.' Vika steps in earnestly around Barinov, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'Is that so, how did I not know about it?' Barinov presses frankly where this conversation is heading next.
- 'That is my personal method of working with actors.' Discussing the actor around Barinov, Vika links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
- 'I wanted to see what you are like in real life.' Vika replies quietly without a pause, before Barinov can change the subject.
- 'Brilliant, simply brilliant.' Vika pushes back openly in this beat and lands on "genialno" ("brilliant") as the key everyday word here.
- 'Yes, well, let us see what is over there.' Jumping in calmly with Vika, Barinov links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
