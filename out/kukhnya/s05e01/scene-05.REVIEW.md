## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Listen, tell me right now.' Jumping in plainly in front of Barinov, Mikhail starts with "davay" ("come on, let's") to invite Barinov right into the action.
- 'Excuse me, what exactly am I ignoring?' Mikhail naturally gives this formal apology for Barinov using "prostite" ("forgive me") — a warmer, more earnest polite apology that literally asks someone to forgive you.
- 'I have been asking for a restroom for two months.' Pointing to the months in front of Mikhail, Barinov leans on "mesyatsa" ("months", a form of "mesyats") to give the remark its edge.
- 'A restroom must be earned first.' Dwelling on the toilet at once, Mikhail leans on "nado" ("it is necessary, one needs to"), which carries the whole predicate in Russian without needing "it is".
- 'We work better than everyone here.' Barinov weighs in bluntly watching Mikhail, without trying to dress up how he really feels.
- 'Yeah? Come on then, let's go.' Mikhail follows up plainly with Barinov and reaches for the Russian word "poshli" ("let's go", built from "poyti") right at the center of the line.
- 'Did they bring the mussels today? No.' Turning to the mussels in the exchange, Mikhail keeps normal statement word order here, turning the line into a question for Barinov purely through rising pitch.
- 'Where is the menu for breakfast?' Mikhail quizzes candidly where this conversation is heading next.
- 'Why is there a meat shortage here?' Bringing up a meat shortage with Barinov, Mikhail leans on "nedostacha" ("shortage") to give the whole sentence its punch.
- 'Not everyone has their health booklets.' Emphasizing the books at once, Mikhail uses "sanitarnyye" ("sanitary", from "sanitarnyy") to make the point stick.
- 'You are simply spying on me.' Barinov weighs in decisively with a nod, refusing to let the moment slip past.
- 'I know everything in my hotel.' Discussing his hotel right here, Mikhail uses "otele" ("in the hotel", from "otel") to make the point stick.
- 'There are cockroaches in your kitchen.' Mentioning the cockroaches in this beat, Mikhail reaches for the Russian word "tarakany" ("cockroaches", built from "tarakan") right at the center of the line.
