## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I did not understand anything myself.' Holding firm openly before Denis, Mikhail pairs the negative word with "ne" ("not"), since Russian naturally doubles its negatives for emphasis.
- 'Katya, please forgive me.' Denis calmly voices this personal apology beside Katya using "prosti" ("forgive me") — the close, informal way to say "forgive me" or "I'm sorry" to someone dear.
- 'Where did all these cockroaches come from?' Emphasizing the cockroaches around Katya, Denis chooses "tarakany" ("cockroaches", from the root "tarakan") so Katya hears the exact meaning.
- 'I meant well, understand.' Denis steps in firmly right here and reaches for the Russian word "luchshe" ("better") right at the heart of the line.
- 'It is not about the cockroaches at all.' Katya jumps in briskly with a shrug, refusing to let the moment slip past.
- 'Love is not just beautiful words.' Pointing out how beautiful things are around Denis, Katya lands on "krasivyye" ("beautiful", built on "krasivyy") to keep the statement grounded.
- 'Love is genuine trust.' Making clear how real things are during the talk, Katya links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
- 'Katya, I honestly trust you.' Denis opens up right away in their talk, shifting the mood of the conversation in a single beat.
- 'And it is no longer about you.' Addressing the matter in front of Denis, Katya leans on "delo" ("matter") to give the whole sentence its punch.
- 'Now I have doubts myself.' Pressing on boldly beside Denis, the "-sya" ending on "somnevayus" ("doubt") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'It is better for us not to date for now.' Katya fires back frankly without a pause, to clear the air between them once and for all.
- 'Forgive me, Denis.' Katya directly voices this personal apology beside Denis using "prosti" ("forgive me") — the close, informal way to say "forgive me" or "I'm sorry" to someone dear.
- 'We will definitely punish everyone.' Looking over firmly on the spot, spoken Russian compresses the whole thought into just 4 words, skipping the extra helper words English needs.
- 'This is from the hotel for the inconvenience.' Mikhail speaks wryly toward Denis, bringing things back to earth after the buildup.
- 'And it is already impossible to go back.' Denis responds smoothly at once and brings in "nevozmozhno" ("impossible") to make the meaning unmistakable.
