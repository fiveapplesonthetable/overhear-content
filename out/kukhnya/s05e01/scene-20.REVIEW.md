## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I did not understand anything myself.' Mikhail directs that point at once: Russian happily doubles the negative here — pairing "never" or "nothing" with "ne" ("not") — which is the only natural way to say it.
- 'Katya, please forgive me.' Denis stands firm steadily as they talk, making his position completely clear.
- 'Where did all these cockroaches come from?' Denis colors one vivid touch on cue: "tarakany" means "cockroaches" (from "tarakan"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'I meant well, understand.' Denis counters decisively to be clear, so Mikhail knows he is not just making small talk.
- 'It is not about the cockroaches at all.' Katya weighs in briskly before Denis, refusing to let the moment slip past.
- 'Love is not just beautiful words.' Katya picks one direct detail in earnest: "krasivyye" means "beautiful" (from "krasivyy"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'Love is genuine trust.' Katya shares that line plainly here: notice how Russian needs no word for "am", "is", or "are" in the present — placing the words side by side says it all.
- 'Katya, I honestly trust you.' Denis chimes in right away beside Mikhail, making his position completely clear.
- 'And it is no longer about you.' Katya leans on one telling touch on cue: "delo" means "matter", and it carries the main weight of what Katya is saying.
- 'Now I have doubts myself.' Katya voices this statement before Mikhail: the "-sya" ending on "somnevayus" ("doubt") turns the action inward or makes it happen between people naturally.
- 'It is better for us not to date for now.' Katya proceeds frankly on the spot, steering the talk right where she wants it.
- 'Forgive me, Denis.' Katya sharpens one practical touch on cue: "prosti" means "sorry, forgive me" (from "prostit"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'We will definitely punish everyone.' Mikhail hands over this update without pause: spoken Russian trims this down to just 4 words, dropping the extra helper words that English has to spell out.
- 'This is from the hotel for the inconvenience.' Mikhail jumps in wryly in this moment, to clear the air between them once and for all.
- 'And it is already impossible to go back.' Denis emphasizes this clear detail in earnest: "nevozmozhno" means "impossible", and it carries the main weight of what Denis is saying.
