## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Are you at it again?' Checking whether Denis at it again cuts straight through the tension between Barinov and Denis. Even in the middle of the drama, he keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
- 'Chef, we only do it after work.' Pointing out that they only do it after work gives Barinov a sobering reality check.
- 'There are bets there. Do you want a cut?' When Denis asks if there are bets there, Barinov has to answer fast. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'Fine, only after work.' Around the kitchen and room, Barinov sets a calm, everyday tone with Denis.
- 'That is it, she got really offended.' Noting that that is it she got really offended keeps Denis's side of the story simple and clear.
- 'She is not answering her phone.' Even though she is not answering her phone, Denis keeps a steady face in front of Barinov.
- 'How was I supposed to act?' Here wondering how he supposed to act was shows Denis looking for the real truth. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'Listen to me as a good lawyer.' With the shift and point in play, Kostya keeps this short reply brisk and direct.
- 'Formally, yes, you are her boyfriend.' Once formally yes Barinov is her boyfriend, Kostya can finally tell Barinov what he means. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
- 'But legally you are nobody to her.' Adding that legally Barinov is nobody fills in the missing piece for Barinov. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
- 'But there is definitely one solution.' Saying that there is definitely one solution is how Kostya sets the tone with Barinov.
- 'You need to marry her.' Since Barinov needs to marry, Kostya does not waste words on excuses. There is no "you" needed in the Russian here: one short word states the rule for everyone in the room.
- 'You love Katya, after all.' Mentioning that Barinov loves Katya after all moves the talk in a new direction. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
- 'The workday has already started!' Because the workday has already started, Barinov does not bother softening the blow for Denis.
