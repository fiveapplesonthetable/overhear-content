## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'So you told her that I knitted the shawl?' Conjunction phrase 'that is / so you mean' introduces Vika's incredulous question.
- 'Vika, well what difference does it make, a gift from her daughter-in-law!' In everyday Russian, 'difference' flows straight into 'daughter-in-law' without extra linking words.
- 'But we wanted to give her a fondue set!' Indeclinable neuter loanword 'fondue' follows the noun 'set'.
- 'Why, she does not even know what to do with it!' Max argues that a knitted shawl suited his mother much better.
- 'Perhaps you got a little hungry from the trip?' In spoken Russian, the words stay in their normal statement order and Vika's rising voice at the end turns the line into a question.
- 'I will not say no to your homemade treats!' In everyday Russian, 'house' flows straight into 'to refuse, decline, renounce' without extra linking words.
- 'Max praised you very much, especially your borscht!' Vika freezes when she hears that she is famous for her borscht.
- 'He even said that you cook better than I do.' Notice how Russian puts 'told' and 'better' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'So I also cook wonderfully in our house?' Once Mom steps out to unpack her jam, Vika glares at Max again.
- 'Confess, what else did you make up about me?' In everyday Russian, 'confess' flows straight into 'to about' without extra linking words.
- 'I only meant well, Mom is very pleased!' Idiom 'wanted as better' is the classic Russian excuse for well-intentioned blunders.
- 'Mom, let's go out to eat burgers today!' To avoid cooking borscht, Max hastily suggests going out for burgers.
