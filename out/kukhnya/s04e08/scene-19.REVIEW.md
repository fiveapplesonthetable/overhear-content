## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello, my dears, here I am at home!' Pointing out how dear he is at once, Max leans on "dorogiye" ("dear", a form of "dorogoy") to give the remark its edge.
- 'Vika, how did you forget Mom's shawl at work?' Turning smoothly on the spot, Max puts the question word "kak" right at the start so Vika hears the focus immediately.
- 'Mom, Vika knitted a new shawl for you today!' Max cuts in boldly across the room, to clear the air between them once and for all.
- 'And that old shawl fell apart completely.' Pointing to the kitchen to Denis, Max chooses "razvalilsya" ("to completely", from the root "razvalitsya") so Denis hears the exact meaning.
- 'Thank you very much, my goodness, what a beauty this is!' Mentioning the thank during the talk, Denis links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
- 'It is not hard for Vika at all, she knits very fast!' Max reacts earnestly with a nod, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'That is not true, in fact I knit very slowly.' Mentioning the untrue around Max, Vika brings in "nepravda" ("untrue, a lie") to make the meaning unmistakable.
- 'Vika, why, you simply have golden hands!' Turning to the hands on the spot, Denis uses "u tebya" (literally "by you") where English says "you have" or "your".
- 'Wait, and just what is this lying here?' Denis questions plainly what Max is actually getting at here.
- 'Mom, forgive me, someone is ringing the doorbell right now!' Focusing on the mum at once, Max chooses "mama" ("mum, mother") so Denis gets the point right away.
- 'Vika, Denis has come to the door, let him in.' Max pushes back earnestly at once and lands on "prishyol" ("came, have come", built on "priyti") to keep the statement grounded.
- 'Max's brother has come to see us, he dropped by to say hello!' Vika proceeds warmly during the talk, so Max knows she is not just making small talk.
