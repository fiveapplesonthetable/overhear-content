## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I heard that strange conversation with my own ears!' Instrumental plural 'with my own ears' emphasizes firsthand certainty.
- 'Boys, I am going to step out right now for a couple of hours.' Prefix on the future verb means driving off briefly.
- 'Vitya, please keep an eye on the kitchen here.' Perfective imperative 'look after' takes the preposition 'behind/after' plus instrumental.
- 'It does not look like they are going to fire Elena Pavlovna.' Impersonal predicate 'it does not resemble' introduces a clause.
- 'Be quiet, Lyova, do not shout so loudly!' Barinov hushes his sous-chef so the rest of the staff does not hear.
- 'Lyova, you did not understand, she does not know this.' Barinov assumes the owner is plotting behind Elena's back.
- 'They simply want to quietly replace her with another chef.' Impersonal third-person plural 'they want' expresses the owner's plan.
- 'Then we definitely need to warn her!' Perfective infinitive 'to warn' takes the feminine accusative pronoun 'her'.
- 'What is the use, after all you know her well.' Idiomatic genitive question 'what of sense' means 'what is the point?'.
- 'She will then simply walk out of here proudly on her own.' Barinov knows Elena is too proud to stay if she feels unwanted.
- 'And I really do not want her to leave.' Subjunctive conjunction with past-tense verb expresses a wish about someone else's action.
- 'She is not going anywhere, did you understand me, Lyova?' Determined to keep Elena close, Barinov prepares to defend her post.
