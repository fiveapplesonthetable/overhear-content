## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Elena Pavlovna, why did you abandon us like that?' By wondering why Elena abandon them like, Barinov presses Elena for a straight answer.
- 'We cannot manage in the kitchen here without you!' Declaring that they cannot manage in the kitchen here without Elena brings Barinov's temper right to the surface. Notice how he sticks to the formal "you", keeping a professional line between them.
- 'Oh come on, Viktor, just do not be modest.' Coming on Viktor just do not be modest is how Elena lays out the situation for Barinov. Even in the middle of the drama, she keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
- 'And I just came out to meet you and have a talk.' Noting that he just came out to meet Elena and have a talk keeps Barinov's side of the story simple and clear.
- 'You know, Vitya, I also wanted to talk with you.' Even though she also wanted to talk with Barinov, Elena keeps a steady face in front of Barinov. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
- 'Perhaps we can pleasantly combine our conversations?' Elena asks whether perhaps they can pleasantly combine their conversations, refusing to let the point slide. In Russian, she does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'Let's have dinner tomorrow evening at our restaurant!' Proposing that they have dinner tomorrow evening at their restaurant shows Elena is done holding back.
- 'Yes, of course, I will come with great pleasure!' Insisting that he will come with great pleasure shows Barinov is not backing down. Russian puts the key word right up front so his emotion lands on the first beat.
- 'Go into the kitchen, and I will breathe some more fresh air.' Mentioning that Elena should go into the kitchen and he will breathe some more fresh air moves the talk in a new direction.
- 'Lyova, can you imagine, she invited me on a date!' Declaring that can Elena imagines she invited him on a date brings Barinov's temper right to the surface.
- 'And that guy said that it was your car.' Since that guy said that it was Barinov's car, Lyova does not waste words on excuses. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
- 'Lyova, climb out quickly, we need to replace the window glass!' Between the dinner and the truth, Barinov delivers a sharp reaction. There is no "you" needed in the Russian here: one short word states the rule for everyone in the room.
