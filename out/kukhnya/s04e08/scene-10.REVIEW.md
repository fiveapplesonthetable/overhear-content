## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Elena Pavlovna, why did you abandon us like that?' Playful reproach hides why Barinov is actually standing in the parking lot.
- 'We cannot manage in the kitchen here without you!' In everyday Russian, 'kitchen' flows straight into 'to cope, manage' without extra linking words.
- 'Oh come on, Viktor, just do not be modest.' Putting the short word for 'not' right in front of the action word is all Russian needs to make a sentence negative, with no extra helper words like 'do' or 'did'.
- 'And I just came out to meet you and have a talk.' Notice how Russian puts 'how / as' and 'to talk' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'You know, Vitya, I also wanted to talk with you.' Notice how Elena switches from formal plural to warm informal 'Vitya'.
- 'Perhaps we can pleasantly combine our conversations?' In spoken Russian, the words stay in their normal statement order and Elena's rising voice at the end turns the line into a question.
- 'Let's have dinner tomorrow evening at our restaurant!' Elena wants to tell Viktor gently at dinner that she is moving away.
- 'Yes, of course, I will come with great pleasure!' Unaware of her real news, Barinov thinks he has scored a romantic date.
- 'Go into the kitchen, and I will breathe some more fresh air.' Here Chef Barinov anchors the thought on 'kitchen' and finishes on 'air', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'Lyova, can you imagine, she invited me on a date!' In everyday Russian, 'imagine' flows straight into 'date' without extra linking words.
- 'And that guy said that it was your car.' It turns out the car whose window Barinov smashed belonged to the young man only in his head—or rather, the young man already fled and left his car!
- 'Lyova, climb out quickly, we need to replace the window glass!' Now Barinov has to fix the broken car window before anyone notices.
