## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Guys, is nobody you know renting out a room?' Putting the short word for 'not' right in front of the action word is all Russian needs to make a sentence negative, with no extra helper words like 'do' or 'did'.
- 'What do we look like to you, a housing agency?' Senya initially waves the rookie away without thinking.
- 'Senya, he is looking for a room, why are you being slow?' Colloquial verb 'to be slow-witted' nudges Senya to connect the dots.
- 'Otherwise your red boat will definitely slip away!' Fedya reminds Senya that a tenant means instant boat money.
- 'Wait, there is a nice room in my apartment!' Senya immediately reverses course and calls Denis back.
- 'I will rent it to you for just twenty thousand.' Conveniently, the rent equals the exact price of the motorboat.
- 'Honestly speaking, that is a bit pricey for me.' Suffix on the adverb 'a bit expensive' softens the objection.
- 'After all, you will not be living with strangers!' Here Senya anchors the thought on 'to live' and finishes on 'people', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'I definitely will not kick you out onto the street at night.' In everyday Russian, 'at night' flows straight into 'to kick out, to evict, to drive out' without extra linking words.
- 'All right, I will think carefully about your offer.' Denis pairs 'good, well, all right' with 'offer, proposal; sentence' directly, since spoken Russian skips English filler words like 'the', 'a', and 'is'.
- 'And will your wife Marina not object to you?' Fedya knows that Marina actually runs Senya's household.
- 'I am the master of the house, whatever I ask for is how it will be!' Senya quickly downgrades 'as I say' to 'as I ask'.
