## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Kostya, I really need one glass of wine right now.' Realizing that she really needs one glass of wine right now changes how Eva handles the situation.
- 'I mean not for me, I never drink at work.' Eva tells Max that she means not for her she never drinks at work without dressing it up. Russian stacks "never" or "nothing" together with "not", doubling the refusal for extra weight.
- 'And when do you drink? At a café with the girls?' Here asking when Eva drinks shows Max looking for the real truth. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
- 'Yes, or if some handsome guy treats me.' Admitting that if some handsome guy treats shows Eva being honest for a change.
- 'And where do you usually go in the evening with your friends?' Asking where Eva usually go in the evening with Eva's friends cuts straight through the tension between Max and Eva. Even in the middle of the drama, he keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
- 'I will drop by and treat you, if I am handsome, of course.' Once he will drop by and treat Eva if he is handsome of course, Max can finally tell Eva what he means.
- 'Well, maybe you really are very handsome.' Adding that maybe Max really are very handsome fills in the missing piece for Max.
- 'Vika wants to play spies with me? Fine!' Questioning whether Vika wants to play spies with him is how Max tests Eva's story. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'Tonight our most important guests are in the dining room.' Since tonight their most important guests are in the dining room, Max does not waste words on excuses.
- 'Hello, Viktor Petrovich! Rodion Sergeyevich in person!' With the moment and room at stake, Rodion reacts without holding back.
- 'And I have come with a complete package of all documents.' Since he has come with a complete package of all documents, Rodion does not waste words on excuses.
- 'Come on, show us your new passport, Rodion.' Coming on show them Eva's new passport Rodion moves the conversation forward without extra drama. Notice how he sticks to the formal "you", keeping a professional line between them.
