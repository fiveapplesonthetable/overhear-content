## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Rodion Sergeyevich, where did you learn to cook so wonderfully?' Asking where Max learns to cook so wonderfully is how Barinov puts Max on the spot.
- 'I do not remember, Viktor Petrovich, I remember nothing at all.' Noting that he does not remember Viktor Petrovich he remembers nothing at all keeps Rodion's side of the story simple and clear. Russian stacks "never" or "nothing" together with "not", doubling the refusal for extra weight.
- 'My head forgot, but my hands remember everything well.' Even though his head forgot but his hands remember everything well, Rodion keeps a steady face in front of Barinov.
- 'We must definitely celebrate this, come with me quickly!' Saying that they must definitely celebrate this come with him quickly is how Barinov takes charge of the moment.
- 'Hi, Vika! How are things? I missed you a lot.' Wondering how things are is how Max puts Barinov on the spot. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'I have an important meeting with a girl tonight.' Admitting that he has an important meeting with a girl tonight shows Max being honest for a change. In Russian you say "by me" or "with us" rather than "I have", so the situation sits right with the person.
- 'With a very beautiful girl named Vika.' Around the moment and office, Max sets a calm, everyday tone with Barinov.
- 'Allow me to introduce: the star and face of our restaurant, Dmitry!' Insisting that Barinov should allow him to introduce the star and face of their restaurant Dmitry shows Max is not backing down. Russian puts the key word right up front so his emotion lands on the first beat.
- 'Very nice to meet you, and in person you are even younger, Kristina.' Since very nice to meet Barinov and in person Barinov is even younger, Max does not waste words on excuses. Notice how he sticks to the formal "you", keeping a professional line between them.
- 'All Kristinas are the most faithful and kind wives.' Mentioning that all are the most faithful and kind wives moves the talk in a new direction.
- 'Maybe I should not drink? I have not eaten properly today.' Wondering if maybe he should not drink shows Rodion caught off guard. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'You will have a snack at the same time! Rodion Sergeyevich, to you!' Barinov declares that Max will have a snack at the same time as the tension in the room peaks. Even in the middle of the drama, he keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
