## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Vika, hi, how are you doing?' Sasha greets Max's wife with breezy friendliness.
- 'And I decided to celebrate my wedding here.' The feminine past tense 'decided' introduces the perfective infinitive 'to celebrate'.
- 'That is great, isn't it?' Adding 'truth?' at the end of a statement asks 'right?' or 'isn't it?'.
- 'You want to celebrate your wedding here?' Stunned, Vika repeats the noun in the accusative case ('wedding?').
- 'Well yes, Misha and I are getting married.' Russian says 'we with Misha' to mean 'Misha and I'.
- 'We want to celebrate in a close circle.' In the prepositional phrase 'in a narrow circle', the noun 'circle' takes the locative ending '-u'.
- 'A banquet for approximately thirty people.' Putting the noun 'people' before 'for thirty' expresses an approximate headcount in Russian.
- 'Check, please, is Thursday free at your place?' The masculine short-form adjective 'free/available' agrees with 'Thursday'.
- 'I will look in our logbook right now.' Vika pretends to study the reservation book while making up her mind.
- 'Unfortunately, everything is booked this week.' The opener 'to regret' means 'unfortunately', and neuter 'all is occupied' shuts the door.
- 'So excuse me, please.' Vika switches to cold, formal plural 'excuse me'.
- 'We cannot help you with anything.' Because 'to help' takes the instrumental case of the means, 'with nothing' is 'nichem'.
