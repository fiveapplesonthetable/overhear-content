## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Kostya, hi, how glad I am!' Sasha spots her old friend behind the bar.
- 'Sasha, this is our new cook Denis.' Kostya introduces Denis using simple 'this is...'.
- 'Hi, Denis, very nice to meet you.' Literally 'very pleasant', the universal Russian phrase when being introduced.
- 'German Mikhailovich asked for an espresso.' When Max walks out of the kitchen, Kostya passes on the chef's order.
- 'Oh, Sashka, hi, how are things?' Max smiles warmly when he sees his ex-girlfriend.
- 'Everything is great, I am getting married!' For a woman, 'getting married' is 'going out behind a husband' ('zamuzh').
- 'Over there stands my fiancé.' The pointer word 'von' ('yonder / over there') directs Max's eyes across the hall.
- 'I sincerely congratulate you on the wedding!' Max offers a polite congratulation.
- 'Thank you, I congratulate you too.' Sasha has heard that Max and Vika finally got married as well.
- 'Lavrov, why are you standing around here?' Watching from across the room, Vika marches over to break up their chat.
- 'Do you have nothing at all to keep you busy?' Instrumental 'nechem' with the reflexive infinitive means 'nothing to occupy oneself with'.
- 'You do not have to hang around in the dining room.' Colloquial 'torchat' means 'to stick out' or 'to hang around idly'.
