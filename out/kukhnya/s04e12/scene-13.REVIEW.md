## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Wait, did you drag me out because of Sasha?' Denis uses one telling detail toward Max: "podozhdi" means "wait" (from "podozhdat"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'Max, are you even a normal person?' Denis lays out this point for Sasha: the conversational word "voobshche" ("in general, altogether") zooms out to sum up the bigger picture.
- 'I lied to Vika because of you!' Denis replies frankly with Max, so Max knows he is not just making small talk.
- 'Stop and let me out of the car.' Denis grounds one loaded element with Sasha: "mashiny" means "cars" (from "mashina"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'I do not want to take part in your little affairs.' Denis speaks up wryly beside Max, without trying to dress up how he really feels.
- 'Listen, what little affairs are you talking about?' Max tests bluntly while looking straight at Sasha here.
- 'Sasha asked for help, I could not refuse her.' Max grounds one vivid term right here: "poprosila" means "asked" (from "poprosit"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'Max, hi, forgive me for being so late.' Sasha sends that line facing Max: opening with the casual greeting "privet" ("hi") signals right away that Sasha and Max are on familiar terms.
- 'I do not know what to do with him.' Sasha stands firm firmly with Denis, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'Come on, get up, do you hear me!' Max features one practical term for Sasha: "slyshish" means "hear" (from "slyshat"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'Hey, who are these guys?' Denis states that point without pause: notice how Russian needs no word for "am", "is", or "are" in the present — placing the words side by side says it all.
- 'Boys, easy, he is completely drunk!' Sasha continues dryly with a nod, letting the remark speak for itself.
