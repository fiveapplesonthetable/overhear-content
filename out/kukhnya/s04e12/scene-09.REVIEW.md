## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello, Sasha, is everything okay with you?' Stepping onto the balcony so Vika won't wake up, Max answers cautiously.
- 'Max, I really need your help.' Feminine short-form 'nuzhna' agrees with the subject 'your help'.
- 'Can you come over to us right now?' The verb 'priyekhat' means 'to arrive / come by vehicle'.
- 'I am standing near the restaurant with Misha.' Preposition 'vozle' ('near / beside') takes the genitive case.
- 'He is drunk and wants to get behind the wheel.' Literally 'to sit behind the steering wheel', the standard Russian idiom for driving.
- 'I cannot handle him by myself.' Perfective reflexive 'spravitsya s' plus the instrumental means 'to cope with / handle someone'.
- 'Listen, it is actually the middle of the night outside.' Literally 'night in the yard', a classic Russian idiom for late at night.
- 'And what am I going to tell my Vika?' Max knows Vika will explode if he leaves at night to help Sasha.
- 'I beg you, I have no one else to turn to.' Negative pronoun with 'obratitsya' means 'there is no one else to turn to for help'.
- 'Well, Lyova, is there anything in there?' Meanwhile in Arcobaleno, Barinov finishes gutting the last fish.
- 'Well, it is actually good that there is nothing.' Lyova sighs with relief as he reads the criminal code on his phone.
- 'That carries up to seven years in prison!' In legal Russian, 'deprivation of freedom' means imprisonment.
- 'Maybe the diamonds are in Fedya's fish?' Realizing the diamonds must be in the crate Fedya took into Claude Monet, Barinov's eyes light up.
- 'Lyova, follow me, grab a flashlight!' Barinov leads Lyova toward the hole in the wall between the two kitchens.
