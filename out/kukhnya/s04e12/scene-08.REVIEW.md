## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'All right, halt, where are you carrying that?' Infinitive 'stoyat!' functions as a sharp military command: 'halt!'.
- 'Fedya, actually that is my fish!' Barinov tries to claim the crate in Fedya's hands.
- 'How is it yours when I ordered it!' Fedya refuses to hand over his crate.
- 'Chef, he placed his order before you.' Preposition 'do' plus the genitive pronoun 'tebya' means 'before you'.
- 'Fedya, for old friendship's sake, sell me the fish!' The dative phrase 'po staroy druzhbe' means 'for old times' sake / out of old friendship'.
- 'Chef, do not take offense, but I cannot.' Fedya secretly wants to gut his own crate for diamonds too!
- 'Chef, there is plenty of fish here, enough for everyone.' Dative plural 'vsem' with impersonal 'khvatit' means 'it will suffice for everyone'.
- 'And is it definitely from the same batch?' The phrase 'iz toy zhe partii' means 'from that very same batch'.
- 'Well yes, of course, take it all.' Barinov tells Lyova to haul the remaining crates inside.
- 'Timur, do you know about precious stones?' Once Barinov leaves, Senya approaches Timur with the stone from their fish.
- 'Of course, I studied to be a jeweler, after all.' In Russian, 'to study to become a [profession]' uses 'uchitsya na' plus the accusative.
- 'I inherited a little stone from my grandmother.' Senya invents a cover story about a family heirloom.
- 'Listen, sell it to me, I will give you a hundred thousand!' Timur plays along and offers a hundred thousand rubles on the spot.
- 'That means it is worth a full three hundred thousand!' Convinced the stone is a real diamond worth triple that, Senya snatches it back and runs inside!
