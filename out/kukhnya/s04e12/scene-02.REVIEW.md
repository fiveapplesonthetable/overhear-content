## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Chef, brother, take some good fish!' Timur greets Barinov warmly as 'brother' while opening his van.
- 'I do not need any, I already have some.' Barinov waves him off with 'not needed to me, by me there is'.
- 'Go sell it to the restaurant next door.' Chef Barinov pairs 'neighboring, next-door' with 'to sell' directly, since spoken Russian skips English filler words like 'the', 'a', and 'is'.
- 'They already took some for themselves.' Plural past 'they took' tells Barinov his rivals already bought a crate.
- 'You have not tasted fish like this yet.' Putting the short word for 'not' right in front of the action word is all Russian needs to make a sentence negative, with no extra helper words like 'do' or 'did'.
- 'Wait, it is not even fresh!' Inspecting the gills, Barinov calls out the fish as stale.
- 'What do you mean it is not fresh?' Timur acts deeply offended on the fish's behalf.
- 'They brought it from Yakutia just this morning.' Plural past 'brought' without 'they' is standard Russian for 'it was delivered'.
- 'She just got a little tired on the road.' Because 'fish' is feminine in Russian, Timur jokes that 'she got tired'.
- 'She flew for six hours on a plane.' In everyday Russian, 'hours' flows straight into 'airplane, aircraft' without extra linking words.
- 'She traveled for two days by dog sled!' Literally 'on dogs was making her way' to the airport in Yakutia.
- 'Listen, do not offend her, huh.' Timur talks about the dead fish as if her feelings might be hurt.
- 'I will give you one to try for free.' The phrase 'na probu' means 'as a sample' or 'for a try'.
- 'Fine, hold this, give the fish to Lyova.' Barinov accepts the free fish and hands it off to his kitchen helper.
