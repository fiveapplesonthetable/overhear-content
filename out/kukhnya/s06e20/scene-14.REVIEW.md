## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Dad agreed to babysit the child today.' Focusing on the child around the guest, Kostya lands on "rebyonkom" ("child", built on "rebyonok") to keep the statement grounded.
- 'Get ready, we will go to the movies.' Kostya speaks up lightly across the room, without trying to dress up how he really feels.
- 'He will steal the child without you here!' Talking about the child right here, the guest brings in "rebyonka" ("child", from "rebyonok") as the key Russian word here.
- 'Let us wait for the test result first.' Addressing the test result during the talk, Nastya uses the polite or group form "davayte" ("let's") to bring the guest into the plan.
- 'This swindler will demand a big ransom!' The guest opens up promptly without any doubt, so Nastya knows he is not just making small talk.
- 'Somehow I do not want to go to the movies.' Turning to the cinema in this beat, Nastya lands on "kino" ("cinema, movies") as the key everyday word here.
- 'I just want to stay at home.' Holding firm openly to the guest, Nastya uses the little word "prosto" ("simply, just") to make the point sound effortless.
- 'Let us go to the planetarium tomorrow.' The guest replies plainly with Nastya, testing Nastya's reaction in the middle of the exchange.
- 'You two are not going anywhere alone!' Nastya continues evenly right here and leans on "poydyote" ("will go", a form of "poyti") to give the remark its edge.
- 'Nastya, what on earth is the matter?' Mentioning the matter at once, Kostya drops in "voobshche" ("in general, altogether") to sum up the whole situation at once.
- 'I spoke with your grandmother.' Nastya speaks up firmly facing Kostya, to clear the air between them once and for all.
- 'She did not recognize Kostya's father in you.' Highlighting the father in the exchange, Nastya reaches for the Russian word "ottsa" ("father", built from "otets") right at the center of the line.
