## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I will not change your decision.' Because he will not change the inspector's decision, Mikhail treats the matter as settled.
- 'But his restaurant is worthy of receiving a star.' Explaining that his restaurant is worthy of receiving a star lays the cards on the table for the inspector.
- 'After all, he is a true culinary fanatic.' Mikhail reminds the inspector that after all he is a true culinary fanatic before the moment passes.
- 'Such a man will die at his work.' Claiming that such a man will die at his work sounds confident, even if the inspector has doubts.
- 'He truly does not spare himself here.' Pointing out that he truly does not spare himself here gives the inspector a sobering reality check.
- 'For his health he must not work right now.' Mikhail explains that for his health he must not work right now, keeping his voice calm and direct. Russian packs the whole idea of what is allowed or needed into one impersonal word at the front.
- 'Two months ago he was in a coma.' Realizing that two months ago he was in a coma changes how Mikhail handles the situation.
- 'But who can stop him?' Asking who stop him can is how Mikhail puts the inspector on the spot. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'Why should I believe you again?' Here asking why he believes Mikhail again should shows the inspector looking for the real truth. Notice how he sticks to the formal "you", keeping a professional line between them.
- 'Because this is the pure truth.' Admitting that because this is the pure truth shows Mikhail being honest for a change.
- 'Today this liar saved your life.' With the work and hotel in play, Mikhail keeps this short reply brisk and direct. Notice how he sticks to the formal "you", keeping a professional line between them.
- 'The battery in my phone simply died.' Over the work and the hotel, Mikhail answers the inspector without a second thought. In Russian you say "by me" or "with us" rather than "I have", so the situation sits right with the person.
