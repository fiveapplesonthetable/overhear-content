## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Well, is this a very unexpected twist?' Denis asks skeptically while looking straight at Nikita here.
- 'Oh, just shut up right now!' Nikita reacts naturally in the exchange and leans on "voobshche" ("at all") to give the whole sentence its punch.
- 'Hello, Mom, hi, we are working.' Turning to the mum with Nikita, the guest centers the thought on "rabotayem" ("we work, let's work", from "rabotat") without wasting a word.
- 'Yes, of course, we will meet as we agreed.' The guest reacts warmly during the talk, so Denis knows he is not just making small talk.
- 'Could you not face me one on one?' Denis replies readily right here and lands on "vyyti" ("to marry") as the key everyday word here.
- 'I did not want to dirty my hands on you.' Raising his hands without a pause, Nikita lets Denis see exactly where things stand.
- 'You are to blame for everything yourself!' Denis continues firmly with Nikita, testing Nikita's reaction in the middle of the exchange.
- 'Well, have you calmed down now?' The guest proceeds plainly in this beat and brings in "uspokoilis" ("have calmed down", from "uspokoitsya") as the key Russian word here.
- 'Your business is postponed for a little while.' Weighing in openly at once, the "-sya" ending on "otkladyvayutsya" ("are postponed") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'You are staying here to guard these two.' The guest speaks up steadily with a shrug, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'And we will drive back here soon.' The guest replies warmly in the exchange and puts the weight on "podyedem" ("to drive up", from "podyekhat") as the exchange unfolds.
- 'We need to get out of here urgently.' Pressing on smoothly on the spot, Denis leans on "nado" ("it is necessary, one needs to"), which carries the whole predicate in Russian without needing "it is".
