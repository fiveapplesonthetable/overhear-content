## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Viktor decided to open his own restaurant.' Because Viktor decided to open his own restaurant, Mikhail treats the matter as settled.
- 'He created his own signature menu.' With the soup and conversation in play, Mikhail keeps this short reply brisk and direct.
- 'Am I telling everything correctly?' Over the soup and the truth, Mikhail presses Barinov for a straight answer.
- 'Yes, this is my signature menu.' Claiming that this is his signature menu sounds confident, even if the inspector has doubts. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'You are simply a true hero, Viktor!' The inspector insists that Barinov is simply a true hero, leaving Barinov no room to argue. Notice how he sticks to the formal "you", keeping a professional line between them.
- 'He is great, he works every day.' By being great he works every day, Mikhail keeps his side of the story grounded.
- 'But he gets very badly tired.' Realizing that he gets very badly tired changes how Mikhail handles the situation.
- 'Which one of us needs the star?' Mikhail asks whether which one of them needs the star, refusing to let the point slide. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'Yes, I get tired, there is a lot of work.' Since he gets tired there is a lot of work, Barinov does not waste words on excuses.
- 'Of course, I understand you perfectly.' Saying that he understands Barinov perfectly is how the inspector sets the tone with Barinov. Even in the middle of the drama, he keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
- 'Because of fatigue Viktor has severe pains.' Saying that because of fatigue Viktor has severe pains is how Mikhail sets the tone with Barinov.
- 'That is why it was not he who cooked today.' Once that is why it was not he who cooked today, Mikhail can finally tell Barinov what he means.
