## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Viktor decided to open his own restaurant.' Underscoring the restaurant in this beat, Mikhail puts the emphasis on "restoran" ("restaurant") in the middle of the talk.
- 'He created his own signature menu.' Mikhail fires back honestly watching Barinov, without trying to dress up how he really feels.
- 'Am I telling everything correctly?' Mikhail responds calmly with Barinov and chooses "rasskazyvayu" ("to tell, to talk about", from the root "rasskazyvat") so Barinov hears the exact meaning.
- 'Yes, this is my signature menu.' Pointing out how auteur things are in this beat, Barinov links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
- 'You are simply a true hero, Viktor!' The inspector proceeds openly without holding back, without trying to dress up how he really feels.
- 'He is great, he works every day.' Mikhail answers directly beside the inspector and reaches for the Russian word "molodets" ("well done, good boy") right at the heart of the line.
- 'But he gets very badly tired.' Mikhail responds warmly on the spot and centers the thought on "ustayot" ("gets tired", from "ustavat") without wasting a word.
- 'Which one of us needs the star?' Mikhail presses openly to see how Barinov will answer on the spot.
- 'Yes, I get tired, there is a lot of work.' Turning to the work in the exchange, Barinov puts the weight on "raboty" ("of work", from "rabota") as the exchange unfolds.
- 'Of course, I understand you perfectly.' The inspector gently voices this confirmation in front of Barinov using "konechno" ("of course") — spoken with a soft "shn" sound in everyday Moscow Russian to mean "of course, certainly".
- 'Because of fatigue Viktor has severe pains.' Mikhail speaks up quietly without any doubt, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'That is why it was not he who cooked today.' Mikhail speaks naturally with the inspector and brings in "segodnya" ("today") to make the meaning unmistakable.
