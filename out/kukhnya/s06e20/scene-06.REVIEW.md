## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Believe me, I know what that is like.' Admitting that Denis should believe him he knows what that is like shows the inspector being honest for a change. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'Once I too was a chef.' Explaining that once he too was lays the cards on the table for Denis.
- 'Because of an injury I had to leave the profession.' The inspector mentions that because of an injury he had to leave the profession so Denis sees what is at stake.
- 'Unfortunately, such is our life.' Claiming that such is their life sounds confident, even if Denis has doubts.
- 'Maybe you will come to us once more?' Questioning whether maybe the inspector will come to them once is how Denis tests the inspector's story.
- 'Viktor will definitely surprise you today.' Denis explains that Viktor will definitely surprise the inspector today, keeping his voice calm and direct. Notice how he sticks to the formal "you", keeping a professional line between them.
- 'Yes, I will come with great pleasure.' Realizing that he will come with great pleasure changes how the inspector handles the situation.
- 'Did you and Katya break up because of me?' Here asking whether Denis and Katya break up because of him shows Nikita looking for the real truth.
- 'I wanted the two of you to get married.' Even though he wanted the two of the inspector to get married, Denis keeps a steady face in front of the inspector. Even in the middle of the drama, he keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
- 'Yes, you tried very hard.' Admitting that Denis tried very hard shows Nikita being honest for a change. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
- 'Especially that evening on the roof.' Over the wedding and the shift, Nikita answers Denis without a second thought.
- 'I made inquiries about you.' Once he made inquiries about Denis, Nikita can finally tell Denis what he means. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
