## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Misha, well, this is somehow wrong.' Weighing in plainly beside Mikhail, Barinov links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
- 'You deserved this star, after all!' Mikhail pushes back dryly across the room, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'How many inspections you and I have been through!' Emphasizing the inspections in this beat, Mikhail uses "inspektsiy" ("inspections", from "inspektsiya") to make the point stick.
- 'We cannot lose all of this right now.' Holding firm dryly around Nikita, Mikhail leans on "nelzya" ("it is not allowed, one must not"), which carries the whole predicate in Russian without needing "it is".
- 'Wait, he will suffocate like that!' Nikita follows up openly with a smile, grounding the moment in plain reality.
- 'I thought you would just beat him up.' Nikita speaks evenly in the exchange and centers the thought on "pobyote" ("you will beat up", from "pobit") without wasting a word.
- 'And you are planning to kill him!' Stepping in openly during the talk, the "-sya" ending on "sobirayetes" ("to intend") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'We did not agree on that.' Nikita fires back warmly without a pause, before Mikhail can change the subject.
- 'We did not make any agreement with you at all.' Mikhail counters warmly in the exchange and chooses "dogovarivalis" ("had an agreement", from the root "dogovarivatsya") so Nikita hears the exact meaning.
- 'You came here yourself, after all.' Mikhail jumps in lightly with a nod, making his position completely clear.
- 'Stop, you cannot do that at all!' Nikita continues dryly beside Mikhail, making his position completely clear.
- 'I will report this to the police right now!' Talking about the police in this beat, Nikita reaches for the Russian word "politsiyu" ("the police", built from "politsiya") right at the center of the line.
