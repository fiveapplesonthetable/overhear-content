## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Viktor, you were deceiving me today.' Because Barinov was deceiving him today, the inspector treats the matter as settled. Even in the middle of the drama, he keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
- 'Yes, but otherwise you would have left.' Explaining that otherwise the inspector would have left lays the cards on the table for the inspector.
- 'If I had not pretended back then.' Barinov mentions that if he had not pretended back then so the inspector sees what is at stake.
- 'I showed what I am capable of.' Claiming that he showed what he is capable sounds confident, even if the inspector has doubts.
- 'And you wanted to give me a star.' Pointing out that the inspector wanted to give him a star gives the inspector a sobering reality check.
- 'You passed yourself off as another person.' Around the conversation and inspector, the inspector sets a calm, everyday tone with Barinov. Notice how he sticks to the formal "you", keeping a professional line between them.
- 'What do you mean, as another person?' Wondering what the inspector means as another person is how Barinov puts the inspector on the spot. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'I am the chef, and this is my dish.' Since he is the and this is his dish, Barinov does not waste words on excuses.
- 'I cooked it, and you liked it.' Even though he cooked it and the inspector liked it, Barinov keeps a steady face in front of the inspector. Even in the middle of the drama, he keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
- 'Yes, of course, I am no angel at all.' Once he is no angel at all, Barinov can finally tell the inspector what he means.
- 'I am ashamed of this foolish performance.' Noting that he is ashamed of this foolish performance keeps Barinov's side of the story simple and clear.
- 'Are you not ashamed of destroying chefs' When Barinov asks if are the inspector not ashamed of destroying chefs, the inspector has to answer fast. Notice how he sticks to the formal "you", keeping a professional line between them.
