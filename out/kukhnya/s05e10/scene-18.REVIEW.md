- 'Do you even imagine what is going on in your hotel?' Russians say 'by me' or 'with us' where English says 'I have' or 'we have', without needing a separate word for 'have'.
- 'The commission refused to give an official report at all.' In everyday Russian, 'commission, inspection board' flows straight into 'conclusion, official report' without extra linking words.
- 'For your sake I committed a crime!' The meaning hinges on 'for the sake of' working together with 'crime' in spoken Russian.
- 'We have an excellent five-star hotel!' Here the Russian phrasing highlights 'at' before landing on 'stars'.
- 'Oh, look who is here! Have you already finished inspecting the hotel?' Notice how Russian expresses 'about' and 'hotel' with crisp inflection rather than helper words.
