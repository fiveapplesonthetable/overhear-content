## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'You definitely have three stars.' Explaining that Barinov definitely have three stars lays the cards on the table for Barinov. In Russian you say "by me" or "with us" rather than "I have", so the situation sits right with the person.
- 'I will try to stretch it to four.' The inspector mentions that he will try to stretch it to four so Barinov sees what is at stake.
- 'We need five!' By needing five, Mikhail raises the stakes in the room at once. Russian packs the whole idea of what is allowed or needed into one impersonal word at the front.
- 'Are you in your right mind?' Checking whether Barinov in Barinov's right mind cuts straight through the tension between the inspector and Barinov. Notice how he sticks to the formal "you", keeping a professional line between them.
- 'Do you even imagine what is going on in your hotel?' Asking whether Barinov even imagine what is going on in Barinov's hotel is how the inspector puts Barinov on the spot.
- 'The commission refused to give an official report at all.' Around the inspector and moment, the inspector sets a calm, everyday tone with Barinov.
- 'For your sake I committed a crime!' Over the point and the inspector, Mikhail lets his real feelings show. Even in the middle of the drama, he keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
- 'We have an excellent five-star hotel!' Saying that they have an excellent five star hotel is how Mikhail takes charge of the moment.
- 'Oh, look who is here! Have you already finished inspecting the hotel?' Wondering who here is cuts straight through the tension between Barinov and the inspector. Even in the middle of the drama, he keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
- 'My goodness, you must not walk! You have fractures everywhere.' Because his goodness Barinov must not walk, Mikhail does not bother softening the blow for Barinov. In Russian you say "by me" or "with us" rather than "I have", so the situation sits right with the person.
- 'Vitya, what fractures? I am fine!' Barinov asks whether he is fine, refusing to let the point slide.
- 'Vitya, did they give us five stars or not?' Checking whether they give them five stars or not cuts straight through the tension between Barinov and the inspector. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
