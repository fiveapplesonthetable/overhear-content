## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Excuse me, I will step away for a moment.' Notice how Russian expresses 'forgive' and 'will step away' with crisp inflection rather than helper words.
- 'I will join you later.' The meaning hinges on 'later' working together with 'will join' in spoken Russian.
- 'Vitya, I cannot go home!' The negative particle sits right before the verb meaning 'can' to negate the statement.
- 'We have a commission in the hotel.' Dmitry Nagiyev brings out the contrast between 'at' and 'us' in a few short words.
- 'Pull yourself together, I am telling you!' In Russian, the idea of 'pull yourself together' flows naturally into 'speak' without extra filler words.
- 'What are you allowing yourself?' The meaning hinges on 'what' working together with 'allow' in spoken Russian.
- 'How are you treating the guests?' the inspector raises the pitch on 'guests' to turn the phrase into a direct spoken question.
- 'He felt ill, I was trying to bring him to his senses.' The meaning hinges on 'to him' working together with 'consciousness' in spoken Russian.
- 'Are you alright? And why are you yelling at this nice man?' the inspector chooses the respectful plural 'you' alongside 'person' to keep a polite distance.
- 'Who gave you the right to raise your voice at him?' The meaning hinges on 'who' working together with 'voice' in spoken Russian.
- 'Calm down! Step away from me!' Notice the reflexive suffix on the verb meaning 'calm down', turning the action back on the subject.
- 'Excuse me!' An emphatic spoken line centered on the verb meaning 'excuse me'.
