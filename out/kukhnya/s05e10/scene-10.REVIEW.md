## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Excuse me, I will step away for a moment.' Mikhail gently drops this formal apology at the start of the beat using "prostite" ("forgive me") — a warmer, more earnest polite apology that literally asks someone to forgive you.
- 'I will join you later.' Jumping in warmly in the exchange, the "-sya" ending on "prisoyedinyus" ("join") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'Vitya, I cannot go home!' Nagiyev replies honestly with a smile, without trying to dress up how he really feels.
- 'We have a commission in the hotel.' Underscoring a commission at once, Nagiyev centers the remark on "komissiya" ("commission") without any extra fuss.
- 'Pull yourself together, I am telling you!' Replying calmly at once, the "-sya" ending on "soberis" ("pull yourself together") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'What are you allowing yourself?' The inspector checks eagerly to see how Mikhail will answer on the spot.
- 'How are you treating the guests?' Mentioning the guests right here, the inspector lands on "obrashchayetes" ("treat, handle", built on "obrashchatsya") to keep the statement grounded.
- 'He felt ill, I was trying to bring him to his senses.' Talking about the feeling before the inspector, Mikhail brings in "chuvstvo" ("feeling, emotion") to make the meaning unmistakable.
- 'Are you alright? And why are you yelling at this nice man?' The inspector inquires quietly what Mikhail is actually getting at here.
- 'Who gave you the right to raise your voice at him?' Talking about the right with Mikhail, the inspector chooses "pravo" ("right") so Mikhail gets the point right away.
- 'Calm down! Step away from me!' Reacting calmly in the exchange, spoken Russian compresses the whole thought into just 4 words, skipping the extra helper words English needs.
- 'Excuse me!' Nagiyev readily chooses this polite apology toward Mikhail using "izvinite" ("excuse me") — the respectful form of "excuse me" or "sorry" used when stepping in or apologizing.
