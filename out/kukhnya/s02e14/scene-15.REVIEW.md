## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Dmitry Vladimirovich, you have a visitor.' Pointing out that Nagiyev has a visitor gives Nagiyev a sobering reality check. Notice how he sticks to the formal "you", keeping a professional line between them.
- 'It is me again.' Vika explains that it is her again, keeping her voice calm and direct.
- 'You were not expecting me, hello again.' Realizing that Barinov was not expecting him hello again changes how Nagiyev handles the situation.
- 'Hello, Elena Pavlovna.' With the moment and office in play, Barinov keeps this short reply brisk and direct.
- 'You see, I feel guilty about this.' Even though she feels guilty about this, Vika keeps a steady face in front of Barinov.
- 'For sending Viktor Petrovich away for treatment.' With the moment and room in play, Nagiyev keeps this short reply brisk and direct.
- 'I wish someone would send me away like that.' Barinov explains that he wishes someone would send him away like, keeping his voice calm and direct.
- 'Well, his kitchen has been left unsupervised.' Once his kitchen has been left unsupervised, Vika can finally tell Barinov what she means.
- 'I could fill in for him while he is gone.' Adding that she could fill in for him while he is gone fills in the missing piece for Barinov.
- 'Oh, no, there is really no need.' Saying that there is really no need is how Barinov sets the tone with Nagiyev. Russian packs the whole idea of what is allowed or needed into one impersonal word at the front.
- 'We could not leave your boss short-staffed like that.' Since they could not leave Barinov's boss short staffed like, Vika does not waste words on excuses.
- 'Oh, that is not a problem at all.' Mentioning that that is not a problem at all moves the talk in a new direction. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'My sous-chef can easily cover for me.' Barinov reminds Nagiyev that his can easily cover for him before the moment passes.
