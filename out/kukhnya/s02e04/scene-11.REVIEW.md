## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Ladies and gentlemen.' Between the kitchen and the room, Barinov gives Vika a quick, grounded response.
- 'Chef and author Valdemar Barani.' Over the kitchen and the shift, Vika answers Barinov without a second thought.
- 'Gentlemen, I am not going to talk too much.' Barinov tells Vika that he is not going to talk too much without dressing it up.
- 'Otherwise it will not be interesting for you to read my book.' Vika tells Barinov that otherwise it will not be interesting for Barinov to read her book without dressing it up. Even in the middle of the drama, she keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
- 'Today I want to present my favorite dish to you.' Since today he wants to present his favorite dish to Vika, Barinov does not waste words on excuses. Notice how he sticks to the formal "you", keeping a professional line between them.
- 'But I am going to need an assistant.' Realizing that she is going to need an assistant changes how Vika handles the situation.
- 'Viktor Petrovich?' With the moment and room hanging in the air, Barinov puts Vika on the spot. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'With pleasure, of course.' Around the shift and meat, Vika sets a calm, everyday tone with Barinov.
- 'Our head chef just became a sous-chef.' With the shift and moment in play, Barinov keeps this short reply brisk and direct.
- 'It is better not to get in their way.' Vika mentions that it is better not to get in their way so Barinov sees what is at stake.
- 'As the saying goes.' Claiming that as the saying goes sounds confident, even if Vika has doubts.
- 'Just look at that.' With the shift and truth in play, Vika keeps this short reply brisk and direct.
- 'He wants to humiliate the Chef in public.' Barinov explains that he wants to humiliate the Chef in public, keeping his voice calm and direct.
