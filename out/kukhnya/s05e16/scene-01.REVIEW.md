## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'The car is nice, but the owner parked very badly.' Emphasizing the car before Max, Katya leans on "priparkovalsya" ("park", a form of "priparkovatsya") to give the remark its edge.
- 'You certainly cannot buy brains with daddy's money!' Pointing to the mind before Max, Katya reaches for the Russian word "papiny" ("dad's, father's", built from "papin") right at the center of the line.
- 'Women need surprises full of intrigue and romance.' Max weighs in right away without holding back, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'Even if they come from some mysterious stranger.' Mentioning the stranger in the exchange, Max lands on "tainstvennogo" ("mysterious", built on "tainstvennyy") to keep the statement grounded.
- 'Max Lavrov? Hi, let's hug right away!' Pressing on warmly to Max, the guest starts with "davay" ("come on, let's") to invite Max right into the action.
- 'Hi, Dasha, how glad I am to see you!' Max answers openly toward the guest, so the guest knows he is not just making small talk.
- 'You and I stayed good friends, remember?' Addressing the friends on the spot, the guest reaches for the Russian word "khoroshimi" ("good, nice", built from "khoroshiy") right at the center of the line.
- 'Yes, of course I remember, that did happen.' Underscoring the matter right here, Max chooses "konechno" ("of course") so the guest gets the point right away.
- 'Tell me honestly, Max, are you married now?' The guest questions point-blank about what Max just brought up.
- 'Why no, not at all, I am already divorced.' Making clear how divorced he is around the guest, Max lands on "razvedyon" ("divorced", built on "razvedyonnyy") to keep the statement grounded.
- 'Then why is there a wedding ring on your finger?' Making clear how wedding things are in front of Max, the guest links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
- 'It just will not come off at all, it is completely stuck!' Max replies plainly toward the guest, without trying to dress up how he really feels.
