## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Misha, be a realist, to her you are small fry.' Barinov counters decisively with Mikhail, without trying to dress up how he really feels.
- 'Vitya, in the past I froze on the street for five years.' Pointing to the street before Barinov, Mikhail brings in "ulitse" ("street", from "ulitsa") as the key Russian word here.
- 'Who would have thought that I would manage a hotel?' Dwelling on a hotel to Barinov, Mikhail puts the question word "kto" right at the start so Barinov hears the focus immediately.
- 'Tomorrow I will secretly invite her to a romantic dinner.' Mikhail replies steadily with a smile, shifting the mood of the conversation in a single beat.
- 'And at the table I will lay all my cards on the table!' Emphasizing the table to Barinov, Mikhail uses "stolom" ("the table", from "stol") to make the point stick.
- 'And where are you planning to invite her tomorrow?' Holding firm candidly in the exchange, Barinov puts the question word "kuda" right at the start so Mikhail hears the focus immediately.
- 'To our restaurant, but the main thing is I want to surprise her.' Mikhail weighs in bluntly with a smile, steering the talk right where he wants it.
- 'Wait, is that a real white truffle you have there?' Emphasizing how real things are at once, Barinov leans on "nastoyashchiy" ("real, genuine") to give the whole sentence its punch.
- 'Did you identify it right by its subtle scent?' Replying boldly around Barinov, Mikhail puts the question word "chto" right at the start so Barinov hears the focus immediately.
- 'No, I can see it with my eyes, close the door tightly right now!' Barinov counters quietly without any doubt, watching Mikhail's face to see how the news lands.
- 'Your daughter said that this is Eleonora's favorite delicacy.' Turning to the delicacy before Barinov, Mikhail chooses "delikates" ("delicacy") so Barinov gets the point right away.
- 'Hold it carefully, Vitya, tomorrow evening you will cook it for her!' Mikhail continues promptly before Barinov and leans on "prigotovish" ("will prepare", a form of "prigotovit") to give the remark its edge.
