## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I have heard about this German.' Saying that he has heard about this German is how Fedya sets the tone with German.
- 'A buddy of mine used to work for him.' Even though a buddy of his used to work for him, Fedya keeps a steady face in front of German. In Russian you say "by me" or "with us" rather than "I have", so the situation sits right with the person.
- 'Everywhere he works, he gets himself a snitch.' Admitting that everywhere he works he gets himself a snitch shows Fedya being honest for a change.
- 'That is a person who loves to gossip.' Saying that that is a person who loves to gossip is how Senya sets the tone with Fedya. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'Everyone loves to gossip.' Once everyone loves to gossip, Senya can finally tell Fedya what he means.
- 'We do not have rats in our kitchen.' Adding that they do not have rats in their kitchen fills in the missing piece for German.
- 'I am listening to you.' Explaining that he is listening to Fedya lays the cards on the table for Fedya. Notice how he sticks to the formal "you", keeping a professional line between them.
- 'I would like to help you.' Since he would like to help Fedya, Senya does not waste words on excuses. Even in the middle of the drama, he keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
- 'To settle into the new team.' With the exchange and work in play, Senya keeps this short reply brisk and direct.
- 'That is very interesting indeed.' Saying that that is very interesting indeed is how German sets the tone with Fedya. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'I could tell you what people think of you in the kitchen.' Because he could tell Fedya what people think of Fedya in the kitchen, Senya treats the matter as settled. Notice how he sticks to the formal "you", keeping a professional line between them.
- 'Reporting on colleagues is immoral.' Explaining that reporting on colleagues is immoral lays the cards on the table for Fedya.
