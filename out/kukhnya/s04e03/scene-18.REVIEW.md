## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Glad to see you free again, with a clear conscience.' With the truth and car in play, Timur keeps this short reply brisk and direct. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
- 'After all, I was the one who sold her car for a good price.' Even though after all he was the one who sold her car for a good price, Timur keeps a steady face in front of Barinov.
- 'She had to pay the colonel so that he would release you.' Admitting that she had to pay the so that he would release Barinov shows Timur being honest for a change. Russian packs the whole idea of what is allowed or needed into one impersonal word at the front.
- 'Wait, isn't he an acquaintance of hers?' By asking whether not he an acquaintance of hers, Barinov presses Elena for a straight answer. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'For that kind of money, he will be your acquaintance too.' Once for that kind of money he will be Barinov's acquaintance, Timur can finally tell Barinov what he means.
- 'I swear, you got lucky with Elena Pavlovna.' Explaining that he swears Barinov got lucky with Elena Pavlovna lays the cards on the table for Barinov. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
- 'Lena, I know everything. Both about the car and about the colonel.' Barinov tells Elena that someone he knows everything without dressing it up.
- 'I want to come back and work off every kopek.' Mentioning that he wants to come back and work off every kopek moves the talk in a new direction.
- 'And then I will buy you a car. May I come back?' Barinov asks whether he will buy Elena a car, refusing to let the point slide. Russian packs the whole idea of what is allowed or needed into one impersonal word at the front.
- 'You may. Only now as a regular line cook.' Around the plan and car, Elena sets a calm, everyday tone with Barinov. There is no "you" needed in the Russian here: one short word states the rule for everyone in the room.
- 'As a regular cook it will take me longer to earn enough for your car.' Because as a regular cook it will take him longer to earn enough for Elena's car, Barinov treats the matter as settled.
- 'That is alright, I am in no hurry.' Explaining that that is alright she is in no hurry lays the cards on the table for Barinov. Russian stacks "never" or "nothing" together with "not", doubling the refusal for extra weight.
