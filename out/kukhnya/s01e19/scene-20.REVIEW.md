## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'What are you thinking about, Max?' "O chyom", 'about what', is the question form; Kostya used it about Nastya's post. "Dumaesh'", 'you think', also covers 'you're thinking'.
- 'About what we have.' "O tom, chto", 'about that, which', stands in for 'about what'. "U nas est'" is 'we have'.
- 'And what do we have?' Vika just turns his words into a question. In Russian only the voice rising on "chto" makes it one.
- 'We have each other.' "My est' drug u druga" is literally 'we exist at each other'. It's the "drug drugu", 'each other', pair again, with "u" slotted in the middle.
- 'That's true.' "Eto pravda", 'this truth', needs no 'is'. "Pravda" is what Max asked, delighted, in the last scene.
- 'Many people are often troubled by doubts.' "Terzayut" means 'torment', a bookish word. "Mnogikh", 'many people', is the one being tormented, so it comes first.
- 'What doubts?' "Kakie", 'what kind of', ends in -ie because "somneniya" is plural. One doubt is "somnenie".
- 'About how much what you have means to you.' "Dorogo" means both 'dear' and 'expensive'. Here it's 'dear': "tebe dorogo", 'it is dear to you'.
- 'And does what you have mean a lot to you?' Vika swaps Max's "imeesh'", 'you have', for the everyday "u tebya est'". "Imet'" is more formal.
- 'A great deal.' "Ochen' dorogo", 'very dear', is a full answer on its own. In a shop the same two words mean 'very expensive'.
- 'To me too, Max.' I "mne", 'and to me', is a short way of saying 'it's dear to me too'. Here i means 'also'.
- 'I don't want to lose it.' "Poteryat'", 'to lose', is the one-time version of "teryat'". "Ne khochu", 'I don't want', comes right before it.
- 'You won't lose it.' "Poteryaesh'", 'you'll lose', is already future, so no 'will' is needed. Vika makes it a promise.
