## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Max, what are you doing here?' Word for word, it's the question Max already heard at home this morning. "Ty chto" at the start adds 'what, ...?' surprise.
- 'I am just standing and breathing air.' "Dyshu vozdukhom" is 'I breathe with air'; the -om is that 'with'. Max is pretending to get some fresh air.
- 'Are you following me again?' "Sledit'" za means 'to watch after', so to follow or spy on someone. Za "mnoy" is 'after me'.
- 'You have an important business meeting right now.' Zhe here means 'after all, don't you?'. It reminds someone of what they both know.
- 'Max, tell the truth, are you jealous?' "Chestno", 'honestly', comes from chest', 'honour'. Vika asked him the same "revnuesh'" after Igor left.
- 'I am not jealous of you at all, Vika.' In Russian you're jealous 'someone', with no 'of': "revnuyu tebya". "Sovsem ne" is 'not at all'.
- 'This is a meeting regarding the sale of the restaurant.' Po "povodu" means 'regarding', a bit formal. "Prodazha", 'sale', comes from "prodat'", 'to sell'.
- 'I am calm, go to your meeting.' "Spokoen" is the short form of "spokoyny", 'calm'. "Ezzhay", 'go', is for driving; on foot it would be "idi".
- 'Please drive my car home.' "Otgoni" comes from "gnat'", 'to drive or chase', and means to drive a car somewhere else. "Domoy" is 'home' as a direction; at home is "doma".
- 'And you will go in his car?' Russians travel 'on' a car: "na ego mashine". "Mashina" is the everyday word for a car.
- 'Yes, we will go together to negotiations.' "Peregovory", 'talks', is always plural and comes from "govorit'", 'to speak'. "Vmeste" means 'together'.
- 'It is time for me to go, bye, Max.' "Mne pora", literally 'to me it's time', announces you're leaving. Add "ekhat'" and it's 'time to drive'.
- 'Yes, of course, safe travels, Vika.' "Schastlivogo puti", literally 'of a happy road', is the standard send-off for any trip. Here Max is saying it through his teeth.
