## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Max, I'm a little worried.' "Perezhivayu", 'I worry', is the verb behind every "ne perezhivay" in this episode. "Nemnogo" means 'a little'.
- 'Why?' "Pochemu" asks for a reason; zachem, which comes up in a moment, asks for a purpose. Both translate as 'why'.
- 'What if it doesn't work out for us?' "Vdrug" usually means 'suddenly', but at the start of a worry it means 'what if'. "Ne poluchitsya" is 'won't work out'.
- 'Don't worry so much.' The same "ne perezhivay" opened the pep talk at the bar earlier. "Tak", 'so', makes it gentler.
- 'The main thing is that we're together.' "Glavnoe, chto", 'the main thing is that', is the pattern Kostya used for boats and oceans. "Vmeste" means 'together'.
- 'Yes, that's the main thing.' "Eto glavnoe", 'this is the main thing', with no 'is'. Vika agrees by repeating his word.
- 'For some people it's easier to lose everything.' "Komu-to", 'to someone', is "kto-to", 'someone', with the 'to' ending. "Proshche", 'easier', comes from "prosto", 'simple'.
- 'What for?' Zachem asks about the point of doing something. "Pochemu" asked about the cause.
- 'To understand how much it meant to them.' "Chtoby" here means 'in order to'. "Ponyat'" is 'to understand', the one-off version of "ponimat'".
- 'That's sad.' "Grustno", 'sad', describes a feeling or a situation. "Mne grustno" would be 'I feel sad'.
- 'Yes, but that's not about us.' "Pro nas", 'about us', is the everyday twin of "o nas". Max said "o nas" in the scene where he asked his question.
- 'We don't need to lose anything.' "Nam ne nuzhno", 'to us not needed', is 'we don't need to'. "Nichego ne" is the double negative again: 'nothing not'.
- 'That's right.' "Pravil'no", 'correct', comes from "pravilo", 'rule'. It's what a teacher says to a right answer.
