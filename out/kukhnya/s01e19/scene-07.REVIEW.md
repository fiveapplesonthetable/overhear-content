## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Good evening, Victoria.' Russian leaves out 'am', 'is', or 'are' in the present tense, linking 'good' straight to 'Viktoriya'.
- 'Good evening.' Notice the word order: 'good' comes first, and 'evening' finishes the sentence. Then the guest replies.
- 'How are you?' the guest uses the polite plural 'you' here alongside 'how', keeping a formal or respectful tone.
- 'Everything is great, thanks.' You'll hear 'perfect' all the time in the show, here connected with 'thank you'.
- 'I'm glad to hear that.' The key word here means 'I', which the guest combines with 'glad'. And Vika jumps in.
- 'And how is your business?' The key word here means 'how', which Vika combines with 'business'.
- 'Business is going well.' You'll hear 'business' all the time in the show, here connected with 'good'.
- 'Thank you for asking.' In Russian, 'thank you' leads into 'to ask' in a very natural everyday phrasing. Vika answers straight away.
- 'What a pleasant evening 0.' Russian leaves out 'am', 'is', or 'are' in the present tense, linking 'nice' straight to 'zero'.
- 'What a pleasant evening 1.' Notice the word order: 'nice' comes first, and 'one' finishes the sentence.
- 'What a pleasant evening 2.' Vika starts with 'nice' and pairs it with 'evening' to make the point clear.
- 'What a pleasant evening 3.' Vika puts 'nice' up front so the listener immediately catches the tone, followed by 'evening'.
