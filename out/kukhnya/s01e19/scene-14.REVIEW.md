## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'What are you going to do?' "Sobiraesh'sya", 'you're planning', is the same verb Max used about Vika. "Delat'" means 'to do'.
- 'Are you talking about the restaurant?' "Naschet", 'about', is spelled with "sch" but said 'nashchet'. It's the casual word; po "povodu" is the formal one.
- 'The owner gave you two weeks.' "Dve", 'two', is the form for words like "nedelya", 'week', which then becomes "nedeli". "Dal", 'gave', comes from "dat'".
- 'A week's already gone by.' "Proshla", 'went by', agrees with "nedelya", 'week'. The same verb, "proyti", is for time passing and for walking past.
- 'Don't worry, I have an idea.' "U menya est'" is 'I have', literally 'at me there is'. "Ideya", 'idea', is easy to spot.
- 'Let me guess. You'll get drunk?' "Day ugadayu", 'give, I'll guess', is the Russian 'let me guess'. "Napyosh'sya" is na- plus "pit'", 'to drink', plus -sya: to drink your fill.
- 'Naturally. Such an occasion.' "Estestvenno", 'naturally', comes from "estestvo", 'nature'. "Povod" is an excuse or reason, especially for a drink.
- 'The birthday of the Claude Monet restaurant.' "Den' rozhdeniya" is literally 'day of birth'. You'll use it for people and, here, a restaurant.
- 'We'll throw a party for the whole world.' "Pir na ves' mir" is a rhyming saying, 'a feast for the whole world'. "Mir" means both 'world' and 'peace'.
- 'And how will the world hear about it?' "Ob etom", 'about it', has "o" turning into "ob" before a vowel. "Uznaet" means 'will find out'.
- 'I'll take care of that.' "Beru na sebya", 'I take onto myself', means 'leave it to me'. Plain "ya beru", 'I take', is what you say in a shop when you pick something.
- 'Got it. Good luck.' "Ponyatno", 'understood', means 'got it', said every day. "Udachi", 'good luck', is short for 'I wish you luck'.
- 'Thanks.' "Spasibo" comes from an old phrase, "spasi Bog", 'God save'. Today it's just 'thanks'.
