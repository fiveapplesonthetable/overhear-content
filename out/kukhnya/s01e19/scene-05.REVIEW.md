## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Viktoria Sergeyevna, thank you for the tour.' "Spasibo" za is 'thanks for', and za changes "ekskursiya" to "ekskursiyu". Swap in "pomoshch'" and it's 'thanks for the help'.
- 'You're welcome, we're always glad to have guests.' "Pozhaluysta" does double duty, 'please' and 'you're welcome'. "Rady gostyam" is 'glad to guests': "gosti" becomes "gostyam".
- 'You have a very beautiful and cozy restaurant.' "U vas", 'at you', is 'you have' for a guest; to a friend it's "u tebya". "Uyutny", 'cozy', is a word Russians love for homes and cafes.
- 'Thank you, we try very hard every day.' "Staraemsya", 'we try hard', always ends in -sya, like "ulybaemsya", 'we smile'. "Kazhdy den'", 'every day', is a handy chunk to keep.
- 'And the cuisine here is truly wonderful.' "Deystvitel'no" means 'really, truly', a longer and more formal word than "pravda". "Prekrasnaya", 'wonderful', ends in -aya to match "kukhnya".
- 'Our head chef knows his craft.' "Shef-povar" is 'head chef': "shef" plus "povar", 'cook'. "Znaet svoyo delo", 'knows his own business', is high praise for anyone good at their job.
- 'It was a great pleasure meeting you.' "Mne bylo priyatno" is literally 'to me it was pleasant'. "Krayne", 'extremely', is very formal; in everyday talk you'd say "ochen'".
- 'Likewise, it was very pleasant for me too.' "Vzaimno", 'mutually', is a one-word polite reply to 'nice to meet you'. She adds "mne tozhe", 'me too', to sound warmer.
- 'I hope we'll see each other again soon.' "Uvidimsya" means 'we'll see each other', and the -sya carries the 'each other'. On its own it's a casual 'see you'.
- 'We'll be waiting for you at our restaurant.' "Budem zhdat'" is 'we will wait', built from "budem", 'we will be', and "zhdat'". "V nashem restorane", 'in our restaurant', shows the -e that comes after "v".
- 'Goodbye.' Do "svidaniya" is literally 'until the meeting'. It's the safe goodbye for anyone you call vy.
- 'Have a pleasant evening, Victoria Sergeyevna.' "Priyatnogo vechera" is a wish with 'I wish you' left out, which is why the endings are -ogo and -a. "Priyatnogo appetita", 'enjoy your meal', works the same way.
