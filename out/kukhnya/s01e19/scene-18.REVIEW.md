## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Vika, can I talk to you?' "Mozhno", 'may I', is the easy way into a serious talk. "Pogovorit'" is 'to have a talk', with the po- making it one conversation.
- 'Yes, of course. What happened?' "Konechno" means 'of course'; its ch is said like "sh", 'kaneshna'. "Chto sluchilos'" is what Kostya asked Max at the bar.
- 'I thought about us for a long time.' "O nas", 'about us', is how my, 'we', looks after "o". "Dolgo" means 'for a long time'.
- 'And what do you think?' I at the start is 'and', nudging him to go on. "Dumaesh'", 'you think', ends in -esh' for "ty".
- 'Vik, I don't want us to have an open relationship.' "Vik" is Vika cut short, the way friends call each other. "Chtoby" is how Russian says 'I want us to', literally 'so that we had'.
- 'Really?' "Pravda" means 'truth', and said as a question it's 'really?'. It's one of the most common replies you'll hear.
- 'Really. I don't like it.' "Mne ne nravitsya" is literally 'it doesn't please me'. Max said the same words about Igor's question.
- 'Then what do you want?' A here is 'then' or 'and so'. "Khochesh'", 'you want', comes from "khotet'", one of a handful of irregular verbs.
- 'I want to be only with you.' "S toboy", 'with you', is the "ty" word for 'you' after "s". "Tol'ko" means 'only'.
- 'Max, are you serious?' "Ty seryozno", 'you seriously', asks 'are you serious?' with no word for 'are'.
- 'Very serious.' Max answers with Vika's own word, "seryozno", plus "ochen'", 'very'. Repeating the key word is a natural way to say yes.
- 'Would you be my girlfriend?' "Ne protiv" means 'not against', so "ty ne protiv" is 'would you mind'. "Moey devushkoy" is 'my girlfriend', with the endings that come after "byt'".
- 'This is so unexpected...' "Neozhidanno" is the word Vika used for Max's flower, and now it fits much better. "Tak" means 'so'.
