## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Vika.' Max says her name alone, which already sounds serious. A name with no more words is a common way to begin a difficult talk.
- 'What?' The word 'chto' is spelled with a 'ch', but said 'shto'. It is the exception that surprises learners.
- 'I was thinking about us.' 'Thought' ends in the masculine 'l'. 'About us' is a small word and 'nas'.
- 'And what?' Two words, 'and what', mean 'so what?'. Vika is waiting.
- 'I don't want an open relationship.' 'I want' is an irregular word, so 'khochu' is worth learning whole. 'Open' is the plural of 'free', to match 'relationships'.
- 'Really?' The word is 'truth' used as 'really?'. It is a one-word question.
- 'Yes.' One word, 'da'. Max is sure of himself.
- 'I don't want it either.' 'Also' sits in front of the negative, so it's 'also not want'. Russian doesn't change the negative to say 'either'.
- 'I like you.' Russian says 'you to me please', with 'you' as the subject. The ending 'sya' is the usual one for this verb.
- 'I like you too.' The verb is left out, and it is just 'you to me too'. Vika echoes Max.
- 'Would you be my girlfriend?' The pattern is 'you not against to be', a polite way to ask. 'My girlfriend' takes the 'ey' and 'oy' endings after 'to be'.
- 'This is...' She starts with 'this', and the line stops. The dots show that she is lost for words.
