## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Does that mean yes?' "Znachit", 'means', is the same word that starts a sentence as 'so'. "Eto znachit da" is literally 'this means yes'.
- 'Yes, that means yes.' Vika simply hands his words back to him. It's the clearest yes there is.
- 'Really? I'm so glad!' "Rad", 'glad', has no ending because Max is a man. "Ya tak rad" is 'I'm so glad'.
- 'I'm very glad too.' "Rada" is Vika's form; Max said "rad" a line earlier. "Tozhe", 'too', goes before what it adds to.
- 'So now you're my girlfriend.' "Devushka" means 'girl' and also 'girlfriend'. "Moya", 'my', ends in -a to match it.
- 'Yes, and you're my boyfriend.' "Paren'", 'guy', is also 'boyfriend', just as "devushka" is both. Moy, 'my', has no ending to match it.
- 'No open relationship?' "Nikakikh", 'none at all', makes everything after it take the 'of' endings. So "svobodnye otnosheniya" becomes "svobodnykh otnosheniy".
- 'None. Just the two of us.' "My vdvoyom", 'the two of us', echoes Max's fear that Vika would leave with the buyer. Now it's good news.
- 'I've waited for this for a long time.' "Davno", 'for a long time', paired with the past ending, means 'I've been waiting ages'. "Etogo", 'for this', takes the 'of' ending after "zhdal".
- 'I know, Max.' "Ya znayu", 'I know', ends in -yu like "pomnyu", 'I remember'. Two words, and the warmest line in the scene.
- 'I feel really good right now.' "Mne khorosho", 'to me it's good', is the Russian 'I feel good'. "Mne plokho" is the opposite, 'I feel bad'.
- 'Me too.' "Mne tozhe", 'to me too', answers a "mne" line. After 'I'm glad' you'd say "ya tozhe" instead.
