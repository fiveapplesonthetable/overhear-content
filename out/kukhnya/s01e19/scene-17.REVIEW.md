## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'What are you doing here?' "Delaesh'", 'you do', is for someone you call "ty", here his wife. The owner sounds more scared than pleased.
- 'You're supposed to be in Monaco!' "Dolzhna", 'must', ends in -a because he's talking to a woman. To a man it's "dolzhen".
- 'But I came back!' "Vernulas'", 'came back', ends in -las' for a woman; a man says "vernulsya". The opening a means 'but'.
- 'You sent me there on purpose.' "Spetsial'no", 'specially', is how Russians say 'on purpose'. "Tuda", 'there', is where she was sent.
- 'Who is this woman?' "Eta", 'this', matches "zhenshchina", 'woman'; a man is "etot". "Kto eta zhenshchina" has no 'is' at all.
- 'I don't know anything.' "Ya nichego ne znayu" needs both "nichego" and ne: 'I nothing not know'. Two negatives are normal in Russian.
- 'A colleague told me everything.' "Mne", 'to me', comes first for weight. "Skazala", 'told', points to a woman as the one who told her.
- 'She works here and sees everything.' "Rabotaet" and "vidit" both end in -t, the ending for 'he' or 'she'. "Tut" is a casual 'here'.
- 'May I say something?' "Mozhno ya skazhu", 'allowed I'll say', asks for the floor. It's short and polite.
- 'Viktoria Sergeyevna is my girlfriend.' In writing a dash stands where English has 'is'. "Moya devushka", 'my girlfriend', is literally 'my girl'.
- 'We have a serious relationship.' "U nas s ney", 'at us with her', is 'she and I have'. It's the same pattern as Vika's "u nas s toboy", now in Max's mouth.
- 'Let your wife apologize to my girlfriend!' "Pust'", 'let', puts a demand on someone else. "Izvinitsya", 'apologize', is literally 'excuse oneself', hence the -sya.
- 'Let's go home, my dear.' "Rodnaya", 'dear', literally means 'my own, kin'. You heard "rodnoy" for a waiter at the start of the episode.
- 'Tomorrow we'll fly off to rest, okay?' "Poletim", 'we'll fly', comes from "letet'". "Otdykhat'", 'to rest', also covers going on holiday.
