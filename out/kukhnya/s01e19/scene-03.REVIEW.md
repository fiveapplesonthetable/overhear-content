## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'What are you doing here?' "Delaesh", 'you're doing', ends in -esh because these two are on "ty" terms. "Zdes'" is 'here', the spot you're in.
- 'Why aren't you at work?' "Chto" here means 'why', a casual shortcut you'll hear a lot. "Na rabote" is 'at work': after "na, rabota" becomes "rabote".
- 'Another interrogation.' "Opyat'" means 'again', with a sigh built in. "Dopros" is the very word Max threw at Vika last night, and now he's on the receiving end.
- 'Did you plan this or something?' Vy here is plural: Max means Kostya and Vika together. "Sgovorilis'" is 'you agreed between yourselves', and "chto" li on the end means 'or what?'.
- 'I'm on the second shift today.' "Vo vtoruyu smenu" is literally 'into the second shift'. "V" becomes "vo" here because "vtoruyu" starts with two consonants.
- 'Great. I'll go with you.' "Poedu" is 'I'll go' by car or bus; on foot it would be "poydu". "S toboy", 'with you', is the friendly 'you' after "s".
- 'Maybe you forgot,' "Mozhet", 'maybe', softens what Max is about to say. "Zabyl", 'forgot', is the form for a man, since he's talking to Kostya.
- 'Yeah, I remember.' "Ya pomnyu" is 'I remember', and Kostya says it before Max has to finish. "Pomnyu" ends in -yu, a usual ending for 'I'.
- 'I got fired.' "Menya uvolili" is literally 'me they fired', the standard way to say you lost your job. Swap in "ego" and it's 'he got fired'.
- 'Why didn't you stay at Vika's?' "U Viki" means 'at Vika's place': after "u", Vika becomes "Viki". The same "u" gives "u mamy", 'at Mum's'.
- 'She wouldn't let me in.' "Ne pustila", 'didn't let in', ends in -a because Vika is the one who refused. "Pustite", 'let me in', is what you'd shout at a locked door.
- 'She started making everything complicated.' "Uslozhnyat'", 'to complicate', is built on "slozhny", 'complicated'. Putting it before "nachala", 'started', sounds very conversational.
