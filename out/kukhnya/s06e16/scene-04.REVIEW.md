## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Listen, do you know such a famous singer?' Touching on a famous singer at once, Senya leans on "izvestnuyu" ("famous, well-known", a form of "izvestnyy") to give the remark its edge.
- 'Yes, of course, she is a superstar here.' Touching on a superstar with Katya, Senya reaches for "u nas" ("by us, at our place") to talk about what they share.
- 'Fresh green salad for the fourth table.' Senya jumps in steadily beside Katya, grounding the moment in plain reality.
- 'Alright, guys, bye everyone until tomorrow.' Pointing to the boys right here, Katya uses "rebyata" ("the boys, the lads") to carry the main weight of the thought.
- 'I have not said anything to Nikita yet.' Opening up plainly before Senya, Katya pairs the negative word with "ne" ("not"), since Russian naturally doubles its negatives for emphasis.
- 'Therefore it is better for us to keep our distance for now.' Katya chimes in earnestly without a pause, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'Just call him today and tell him.' Denis opens up readily before Katya and lands on "segodnya" ("today") as the key everyday word here.
- 'I do not want to say it over the phone.' Touching on the phone right here, Katya uses "telefonu" ("phone", from "telefon") to make the point stick.
- 'Once he arrives soon, then I will tell him.' Katya chimes in pointedly hearing Denis, leaving no doubt about where she stands.
- 'Good, agreed, I will wait a little.' Denis responds evenly beside Katya and chooses "dogovorilis" ("agreed, deal", from the root "dogovoritsya") so Katya hears the exact meaning.
- 'Can you add olive oil to the salad?' Pointing to the butter beside Denis, Senya leans on "mozhno" ("it is possible, one may"), which carries the whole predicate in Russian without needing "it is".
- 'Because there are no free waiters in the hall.' Senya replies promptly with Denis, refusing to let the moment slip past.
