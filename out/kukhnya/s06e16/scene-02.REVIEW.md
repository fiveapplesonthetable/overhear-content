## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'What is going on with all of you here?' Russians say 'by me' or 'with us' where English says 'I have' or 'we have', without needing a separate word for 'have'.
- 'Some strange fashion designer checked into the hotel.' Notice how Russian expresses 'some kind of' and 'drop by' with crisp inflection rather than helper words. Elena answers right away.
- 'Wow, I know him very well!' Here the Russian phrasing highlights 'nothing' before landing on 'know'.
- 'Well let us buy something from him.' The meaning hinges on 'come on' working together with 'anything' in spoken Russian.
- 'You will walk to work at the restaurant looking beautiful.' The grammar packs 'will' and 'go' into a tight, idiomatic kitchen exchange. Elena answers right away.
- 'One does not go to work in a dress like that.' Elena brings out the contrast between 'such' and 'a dress' in a few short words.
- 'I will marry you in it!' Notice how Russian expresses 'I' and 'will go' with crisp inflection rather than helper words.
- 'Hello, I will be glad to see you in the restaurant.' Spoken Russian keeps this brisk, leaning on 'hello' and finishing on 'restaurant'.
- 'Our restaurant is a contender for a high award.' Notice how Russian expresses 'our' and 'reward' with crisp inflection rather than helper words.
- 'And I am the head chef of this restaurant.' Here the Russian phrasing highlights 'and' before landing on 'restaurant'.
- 'My future wife is a big admirer of yours.' Notice how Russian expresses 'my' and 'admirer' with crisp inflection rather than helper words.
- 'I wanted to buy one of your dresses.' Here the Russian phrasing highlights 'I' before landing on 'dress'.
