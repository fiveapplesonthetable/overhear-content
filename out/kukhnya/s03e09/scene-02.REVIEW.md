## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Of course you can, let us go cure our hangover.' Adding that the guest can let them go cure their hangover fills in the missing piece for the guest. Russian packs the whole idea of what is allowed or needed into one impersonal word at the front.
- 'Hello, Nastya has a day off today.' Saying that Nastya has a day off today is how Kostya sets the tone with Barinov.
- 'I can see that she has a day off.' Since he can see that she has a day off, the guest does not waste words on excuses.
- 'I came specifically to see you.' Mentioning that he came specifically to see Barinov moves the talk in a new direction. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
- 'Then I am listening to you carefully.' Realizing that he is listening to Barinov carefully changes how Kostya handles the situation. Even in the middle of the drama, he keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
- 'This is none of my business, of course.' Because this is none of his business of course, the guest treats the matter as settled.
- 'Are you planning to marry Nastya?' Checking whether Barinov planning to marry Nastya cuts straight through the tension between the guest and Barinov. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
- 'Of course, we will definitely get married.' Kostya mentions that they will definitely get married so Barinov sees what is at stake.
- 'And just when will that happen?' Questioning whether just when will that happen is how the guest tests Barinov's story.
- 'Actually, we are saving up for the wedding right now.' Pointing out that they are saving up for the wedding right now gives Barinov a sobering reality check.
- 'And could you not just ask me for it?' Asking whether Barinov not just ask him for it cuts straight through the tension between the guest and Barinov. In Russian you say "by me" or "with us" rather than "I have", so the situation sits right with the person.
- 'We want to do everything ourselves.' Saying that they want to do everything themselves is how Kostya sets the tone with Barinov.
