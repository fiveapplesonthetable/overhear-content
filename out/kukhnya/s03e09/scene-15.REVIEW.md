## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hey there, hello, my dear Kostya!' Nastya speaks right away during the talk, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'Listen, pour me some water, I am so thirsty.' Pointing to the water in front of Kostya, Nastya reaches for the Russian word "vodichki" ("some water", built from "vodichka") right at the center of the line.
- 'Easy, I will pour some for you right away.' Stepping in smoothly beside Nastya, Kostya leans on "legko" ("easy"), which carries the whole predicate in Russian without needing "it is".
- 'Excuse me, what are you doing back here?' The guest prompts evenly while looking straight at Kostya here.
- 'Are you pulling my leg or what?' Kostya speaks up openly to the guest and lands on "prikalyvayeshsya" ("you are joking", built on "prikalyvatsya") to keep the statement grounded.
- 'What do you mean? Why did you walk back here?' Holding firm directly to Kostya, the guest puts the question word "zachem" right at the start so Kostya hears the focus immediately.
- 'I actually work here, I am the bartender.' Kostya opens up bluntly watching the guest, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'I am your new bartender since this morning.' Pointing to the bartender in this beat, Kostya brings in "barmen" ("bartender") to make the meaning unmistakable.
- 'I do not know, nobody told me anything.' Opening up directly in front of Kostya, the guest pairs the negative word with "ne" ("not"), since Russian naturally doubles its negatives for emphasis.
- 'Poor thing, you are working without any days off.' Nastya fires back at once during the talk, without trying to dress up how she really feels.
- 'Alright, I had better go home.' Nastya casually speaks this word of agreement toward the guest using "ladno" ("alright") — built from the old Russian root for harmony and order, used every day for "okay, fine".
- 'You will end up having problems because of me.' Focusing on the problems in the exchange, Nastya uses "u tebya" (literally "by you") where English says "you have" or "your".
