## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'How did you find me, Ainura?' Russian puts it as 'you how me found', which puts the question first. 'found' carries 'a' since Ainura is a woman.
- 'I have been working for you for a long time, after all.' 'At you I work' is the Russian for 'I work for you'. The term for 'for a long time' with the present is the Russian way to put 'have been working'.
- 'I know all your secret places.' 'Secret' comes from 'a secret'. 'Places' is a plural, and 'your' and 'all' agree with it.
- 'All right, and why did you come?' The term for 'why' here asks 'for what'. The Russian for 'came' ends in 'a' for a woman.
- 'Dear Chef, you need to return to the kitchen.' The expression for 'dear' is also the term for 'expensive'. 'To you necessary' is the Russian for 'you need'.
- 'You sound like my former mother-in-law right now.' 'Former' grows out of 'was'. 'Mother-in-law' is a word for a wife's mother, and Barinov has had three wives.
- 'Seriously, it is very hard for the cooks without you.' 'For the cooks' puts the 'am' ending on 'cooks'. 'Hard' is the same word as 'heavy'.
- 'They are not coping at all with the orders.' The Russian word for 'cope' ends in 'sya' and is followed by 'with'. 'Not at all' comes before the action.
- 'The new girl chef will soon fall completely ill.' 'Girl-chef' is two words joined by a hyphen. 'Fall ill' is a one-word future.
- 'Are you sure you are not deceiving me?' 'Exactly' is used here to mean 'for sure'. 'Deceive' is followed by 'me' in a changed form.
- 'When have I ever lied to you?' The Russian for 'when' opens the question. 'ever' adds a note of outrage.
- 'All right, let us drink to victory then.' 'Let's drink' is the 'we' form. 'To victory' is the toast form, and it asks for the movement ending.
- 'Chef, I am at work after all!' This is the same line as before, with the word 'zhe' for 'after all'. Ainura has said it twice, so Barinov has lost.
