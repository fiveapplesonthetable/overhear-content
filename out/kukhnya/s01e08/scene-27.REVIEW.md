## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'How did you find me, Ainura?' — an informal question using the past feminine verb for finding.
- 'I have been working for you for a long time.' — using the present tense to express an action continuing from the past.
- 'I know all your secret places.' — transitive present verb with an accusative plural object.
- 'Dear Chef, you need to return to the kitchen.' — dative modal construction followed by a reflexive infinitive.
- 'You sound right now like my former mother-in-law.' — a witty comparison introduced by the comparative conjunction.
- 'Seriously, it is very hard for the cooks without you.' — dative of the person experiencing difficulty with an adverb.
- 'They are not coping at all with orders.' — imperfective reflexive verb governing the instrumental preposition.
- 'The new female chef will soon fall completely ill.' — future perfective verb describing an imminent outcome.
- 'When have I ever lied to you?' — rhetorical question emphasizing past honesty.
- 'All right, let us drink to victory then.' Here Chef Barinov anchors the line on the word meaning 'all right' and builds the thought around 'then' and 'let us drink'.
- 'Chef, I am at work after all!' — short emphatic phrase rejecting a drink during working hours. Now listen to the full conversation once more.
