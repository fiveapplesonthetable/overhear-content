## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Dmitry, this was your stupid idea!' 'Was' gets 'a' since 'idea' counts as a she-word. 'Your' agrees with it.
- 'Stop shouting, it turned out a great show!' 'Enough' is followed by 'shouting'. 'Turned out' is a neuter form, since 'show' behaves as neuter.
- 'Rivals in life, rivals on screen.' 'Rivals' is borrowed from French. 'On the screen' ends in 'e', and the dash replaces 'are'.
- 'You just wanted to make me angry.' 'Make angry' comes from 'angry'. 'Wanted' ends in a consonant for a man.
- 'And it worked, you got angry perfectly!' 'At me it worked out' is how Russian says 'it worked for me'. 'Got angry' ends in 'sya'.
- 'Now that woman will be in my kitchen.' 'That' in front of 'woman' carries a sneer, like 'that woman there'. 'Will be' is the future of 'be', and 'in my kitchen' needs no other word for 'in'.
- 'She lost the bet and will honestly work the shift.' 'Lost' is the opposite of 'won', and it ends in 'a' for a woman. 'Honestly' comes from 'honour'.
- 'Viktor, get your cooks in order.' 'Bring into order' is the Russian for 'tidy up'. 'Your own' is a special word pointing back at Viktor.
- 'My cooks always work perfectly.' 'Always' is a very common word. 'Perfectly' is based on 'ideal'.
- 'I shouldn't feel ashamed before the guests.' 'To me shameful' puts the feeling outside. 'Before the guests' uses a word for 'in front of'.
- 'Do not worry, everything will be at the highest level.' 'At the highest level' is a set phrase for 'first-class'. 'Worry' is also 'live through'.
- 'Great, then I am calm about the restaurant.' 'Calm' is the man's short form. 'About' here is 'for', which shows what you are calm about.
- 'I will go meet our new worker.' 'I will go' and 'meet' stand side by side. 'Worker' is related to 'work'.
- 'Good luck in the kitchen, chef!' 'Luck' is in the 'of' form, as if 'I wish' were left out. It is a handy chunk.
