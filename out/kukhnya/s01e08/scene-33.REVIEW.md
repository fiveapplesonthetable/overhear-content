## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Max, I did stupid things today.' 'Did a lot of mischief' has a prefix that adds up the damage. 'Stupid stupidities' repeats the root, which is a way to stress it.
- 'Do not worry, all cooks make mistakes.' The Russian for 'make mistakes' is literally 'do mistakes'. 'Don't worry' is the Russian word for 'not' plus a command.
- 'But the chef shouted so loudly at me!' Russian shouts 'on' someone, so 'at me' is 'na' plus a changed form of 'me'. The term for 'so loudly' sits in front of the shouting.
- 'We cannot help making various mistakes.' 'Cannot not' is a double negative that means 'can't help'. 'Various' goes in front of 'mistakes'.
- 'Do you really think that this is normal?' 'Really' is literally 'truth', and Russians often start a question with it. 'Normal' stands alone, ending in 'o'.
- 'Mistakes are an important part of our life.' The dash and the word 'this' do the job of 'are', which Russian leaves out. 'Part' is a she-word, so 'important' and 'our' take the endings 'aya' and 'ey'.
- 'And what can they teach us?' 'Teach' takes the thing taught in the 'to' form, so 'what' becomes 'chemu'. 'Then' softens the question.
- 'They help us find the right path.' Russians help 'to' someone, so 'us' takes the 'to' form, which is an ending. 'Find' follows without a 'to' in front.
- 'So every blunder makes us stronger?' 'Blunder' is a word that means 'miss'. 'Stronger' is the comparative of 'strong'.
- 'That is precisely how we become true masters.' 'Become' ends in 'sya' and takes a 'with' form, which means 'masters' has the ending 'ami'. 'Precisely' sits in front of 'so'.
- 'Then I will not be so upset anymore.' 'No longer' is two small words, and the term for 'will not be' is the future of 'be' with a 'not'. 'Be upset' ends in 'sya'.
- 'The main thing is to remember your lessons and move forward.' The Russian for 'move' ends in 'sya', so it is literally 'move yourself'. 'Forward' means 'ahead'.
- 'Thank you for the support, you are a great friend.' 'Thank you for' takes the object directly. 'Great' is the word for top marks.
- 'Tomorrow we will show top class in the kitchen!' The Russian for 'we will show' ends in 'em', the ending for 'we'. 'Highest class' is a fixed phrase for first-rate, and it works for anything.
