## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Max, I did stupid things today.' Kostya's "natvoril", got up to, is from "tvorit", to create, and it is used for mischief. Then "glupostey" works like 'a heap of silly things'.
- 'Do not worry, all cooks make mistakes.' The friendly 'don't worry' is "ne perezhivay". And "delat oshibki", to make mistakes, matches the English almost word for word.
- 'But the chef shouted so loudly at me!' To shout at is "krichat na", with "na", on, before the person: "na menya", at me. And "tak gromko" is 'so loudly'.
- 'We cannot help making various mistakes.' Max's "ne mozhem ne", literally 'can't not', is how Russian says 'can't help'. The word "raznye", various, also means different.
- 'Do you really think that this is normal?' In a question, "pravda", truth, works as 'really?'. You'll hear "normalno", normal or fine, in Russian speech all day long.
- 'Mistakes are an important part of our life.' The dash plus "eto" stands in for 'are' between two things. A part, "chast", is a feminine word, so it is "vazhnaya chast".
- 'And what can they teach us?' In Russian you teach someone 'to' a thing, so 'what' becomes "chemu". The little "zhe" adds 'and so' to the question.
- 'They help us find the right path.' Help goes 'to' someone, so 'us' is "nam". And "put", path, is a masculine word although it ends in a soft t, which is why it's "pravilnyy put".
- 'So every blunder makes us stronger?' A "promakh", a miss, is what you call a shot that misses its target. Stronger, "silnee", grows out of "silnyy", strong.
- 'That is precisely how we become true masters.' Max's "imenno tak" means 'exactly so', 'precisely like that'. After "stanovimsya", we become, the masters take -ami: "nastoyashchimi masterami".
- 'Then I will not be so upset anymore.' Kostya's "ne budu rasstraivatsya" is 'I won't keep getting upset', a promise about the future in general. And "bolshe ne" means 'no longer'.
- 'The main thing is to remember your lessons and move forward.' The standard Russian phrase for moving on is "dvigatsya vperyod", to move forward. And "svoi uroki" is 'your own lessons'.
- 'Thank you for the support, you are a great friend.' Thanks for is "spasibo za", which turns "podderzhka", support, into "podderzhku". It's the same root as "podderzhivali" in Kostya's talk with Nastya.
- 'Tomorrow we will show top class in the kitchen!' A fixed phrase of praise, "vysshiy klass" means top class. Max's "pokazhem", we'll show, is a promise about tomorrow.
