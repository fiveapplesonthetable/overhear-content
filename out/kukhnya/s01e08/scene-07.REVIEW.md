## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I repeat for everyone, the order is already waiting!' 'I repeat' is a one-word present. 'For everyone' is two short words.
- 'Elena, they heard you perfectly well.' 'Heard' is the finished kind, so it means 'caught' what she said. 'You' is the informal one here, as Barinov uses it with her.
- 'Why then is nobody working?' 'Nobody' needs 'not' in front of the action. 'Then' is a very common little word.
- 'In my kitchen there is always strict order.' 'On my kitchen' is Russian for 'in my kitchen'. 'Strict order' is a phrase you hear all the time.
- 'You work too slowly for a restaurant.' 'Too slowly' is two words. 'For a restaurant' is the 'for' plus the 'a' ending.
- 'Every good dish needs its own time.' 'To every good dish' is the 'to' form of 'every good dish', because 'need' wants it. 'Its own' is a word that points back at the dish.
- 'Guests do not like to wait long for food.' 'Wait for food' has no word for 'for'.
- 'Our guests appreciate high taste.' 'Appreciate' stems from 'price'. 'Taste' is Russian for flavour and for good taste.
- 'I will show myself how one must work!' 'Myself' is the woman's form. 'How it should be done' is 'how needed to work'.
- 'Great, I will go calmly rest.' 'I will go' and 'I will rest' stand side by side, joined by nothing. 'Calmly' comes from 'calm'.
- 'Are you abandoning your kitchen to me?' 'Abandon' is literally 'throw'. 'Onto me' uses the word 'na'.
- 'I am sure that you will manage excellently.' 'Sure' is the man's short form. 'Manage' is the word from the bet.
- 'Return soon, otherwise I will take the restaurant!' 'Return' ends in 'sya'. 'Otherwise' is a handy short word.
- 'We will see who stays the winner here!' 'We will see' is the standard Russian phrase for saying 'time will tell'. 'Stay' is followed by 'with', so 'winner' ends in 'em'.
