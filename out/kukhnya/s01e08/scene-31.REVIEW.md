## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Kostya, this crazy workday has finally ended.' A working day is "rabochiy den", the normal way to say workday. Then "zakonchilsya" ends in -sya and has the 'he' shape to match "den".
- 'Yes, Nastya, real madness was happening in the kitchen.' Kostya's "tvorilos", was going on, comes from "tvorit", to create, and Russians use it for chaos. The madness, "bezumie", shares "bez", without, and "um", mind, with "bezumno", insanely.
- 'On the other hand, we supported each other great today.' Each other is "drug druga" when the other is on the receiving end; with a 'to' it becomes "drug drugu". And "podderzhivali", supported, is built on "derzhat", to hold.
- 'With you it is always easy and very pleasant to work.' Kostya says "s toboy", 'with you', to someone close. Both "legko", easy, and "priyatno", pleasant, end in -o, the way Russian says how something feels.
- 'You and I are simply wonderful loyal friends.' Here is "my s toboy" again, 'you and I', literally 'we with you'. And "vernye", loyal, comes from "vera", faith.
- 'Of course, between us there is only strong sincere friendship.' After "mezhdu", between, 'us' is "nami". The word "krepkaya", strong, is for firm friendships and also for strong tea or coffee.
- 'Some are sure that nothing can happen.' Nastya's "nekotorye" is 'some people'. And "uvereny", sure, is a shortened shape, used for how someone is feeling right now.
- 'They do not understand that everything has already begun.' Since "vsyo", everything, counts as an 'it', 'has begun' is "nachalos". Nastya is clearly not talking about the chef.
- 'Are you talking about us or about the chef?' In speech, "pro", about, is the everyday choice, and it gives "shef" an -a: "pro shefa". And "pro nas" is 'about us'.
- 'I am simply reflecting on life and feelings.' To reflect, "razmyshlyat", comes from "mysl", thought. After "o", about, "zhizn" becomes "zhizni" and "chuvstva" becomes "chuvstvakh".
- 'Then let us drink some delicious tea together.' Kostya says "chayu", a special shape of "chay" for 'some tea'; you'll also hear plain "chay". The word "vkusnogo", delicious, takes an 'of' ending to match it.
- 'With great pleasure I will sit here with you.' The phrase "s bolshim udovolstviem" is the standard warm yes. With "po-" in front, "posizhu" means 'I'll sit for a while'.
- 'I am insanely glad for our unexpected warm meeting!' You are glad 'to' something in Russian, so with "rad" the meeting becomes "nashey vstreche". And "bezumno", insanely, is the same word Max used to Vika.
