## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'All these people around are ungrateful scumbags.' The word "neblagodarnye" is "ne", not, plus "blagodarnye", grateful, the same root as "blagodaryu", I thank you. And "podonki" first meant the dregs at the bottom of a cup, which tells you how low he rates them.
- 'Chef, one should not think so badly about everyone.' With "nelzya", 'it is not allowed', nobody is named, so it sounds like a general rule. The little word "o", about, grows to "obo" before "vsekh", everyone, just to be easier to say.
- 'I taught them to cook the city's best dishes.' Barinov says "nauchil", and the "na-" in front means the teaching is done and dusted; the open-ended 'teach' is plain "uchit". Then "goroda", of the city, comes after the dishes: 'the best dishes of the city'.
- 'Every cook remembers your great lesson.' Lyova uses "vash", the polite 'your', to his boss. In "pomnit", remembers, you can hear the root of "pamyat", memory.
- 'Then why are they following someone else's orders now?' Literally 'listening to someone else's orders': "slushat" covers both listening and obeying. One word, "chuzhie", does the work of 'someone else's', and its opposite is "svoi", one's own.
- 'They are simply afraid of losing their job.' After "boyatsya", be afraid, Russian just adds 'to lose', so there's no 'of' and no '-ing'. A job here is "rabochee mesto", literally 'a working place'.
- 'A true master is never afraid of difficulties.' Whatever you fear gets an 'of' ending, so "trudnosti", difficulties, turns into "trudnostey". Notice "nikogda ne", literally 'never not': Russian needs both.
- 'You are the main culinary master for us.' Lyova's "samyy glavnyy" means 'the very main', with "samyy" piled on for extra weight. And "dlya nas" is 'for us'; "dlya" is the plain 'for' you'll use everywhere.
- 'Let them handle the complex banquet themselves now.' Put "pust", let, in front of a 'they' form and you get 'let them'. The banquet part, "so slozhnym banketom", is 'with the complex banquet': "so" is just "s", with, plus a vowel to ease the cluster of consonants, and after it "banket" takes the -om ending.
- 'Without your participation, chaos will break out in the kitchen.' After "bez", without, the words change to their 'of' shape: "vashego uchastiya". Russians use "nastupit", will set in, for night, winter or silence arriving, and here for chaos.
- 'That is great, let them see my strength!' Barinov's "vot i otlichno" is a dry 'well, that's just great'. Here "uvidyat" means 'they will see', and "pust" in front turns it into 'let them see'.
- 'I will stay by your side anyway.' Literally, "vsyo ravno", anyway, is 'all equal'. And "ryadom s vami" is 'next to you', with the respectful "vami" that Lyova keeps for his chef.
