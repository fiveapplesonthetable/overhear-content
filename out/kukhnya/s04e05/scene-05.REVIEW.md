- 'Dear colleagues, I ask for a minute of your attention!' A diminutive accusative noun softens the formal request for silence.
- 'I want to introduce our new sous-chef to you.' The dative plural pronoun marks the audience receiving the introduction.
- 'This is cook Lavrov Maksim Leonidovich.' Full official Russian style places the surname before the given name and patronymic.
- 'I ask everyone to welcome and respect him.' A classic Russian idiom pairs two infinitives to ask for a warm reception.
- 'Greetings to you, dear kitchen colleagues!' Max enjoys his new status with a formal first-person greeting verb.
- 'Cooks, bow low to our new sous-chef!' Fedya jokes using a plural reflexive imperative governing the dative case.
- 'Max, tell us honestly, what is going on here?' Fedya switches back to informal speech to ask how Max got promoted.
- 'How are you addressing the sous-chef right now?' Max playfully pulls rank using the reflexive verb for addressing someone.
- 'Oh, forgive us, respected Maksim Leonidovich!' Fedya mocks his formality by using Max's full name and patronymic.
- 'You have now risen to become one of our bosses!' A colloquial reflexive idiom describes climbing into the ranks of management.
- 'Yes, now I am your new boss.' Max confirms his promotion with a straightforward nominative predicate noun.
- 'Let's all quickly return to work.' A first-person plural exhortation brings the joking brigade back to their stations.
