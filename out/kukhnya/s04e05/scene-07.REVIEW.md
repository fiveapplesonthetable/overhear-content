## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Timur, is this crab definitely a real royal one?' Asking whether this crab definitely a real royal one is how Lyova puts Barinov on the spot.
- 'Of course, this is the very best royal crab!' Insisting that this is the very best royal crab shows Timur is not backing down. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'Look what noble eyes he has.' Explaining that what noble eyes he has lays the cards on the table for Barinov. In Russian you say "by me" or "with us" rather than "I have", so the situation sits right with the person.
- 'Even a real prince is not as royal as this.' Timur mentions that even a real prince is not as royal as this so Barinov sees what is at stake.
- 'Good, I am taking this fresh crab.' Claiming that good he is taking this fresh crab sounds confident, even if Barinov has doubts.
- 'Viktor Petrovich, are you not busy right now?' Here checking whether Barinov not busy right now shows Lyova looking for the real truth. Even in the middle of the drama, he keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
- 'Please help Timur with unloading the groceries.' Claiming that Barinov should help Timur with unloading the groceries sounds confident, even if Barinov has doubts.
- 'Oh, I cannot do that at all right now.' Mentioning that he cannot do that at all right now moves the talk in a new direction. Russian stacks "never" or "nothing" together with "not", doubling the refusal for extra weight.
- 'It is vitally necessary for me to go smoke right now.' Noting that it is vitally necessary for him to go smoke right now keeps Barinov's side of the story simple and clear.
- 'Ask that young cook over there instead.' Asking that young cook over there instead is how Barinov lays out the situation for Lyova.
- 'Viktor Petrovich, are you disobeying again?' By checking whether Barinov disobeying again, Lyova presses Barinov for a straight answer. Notice how he sticks to the formal "you", keeping a professional line between them.
- 'Lyova, let me unload everything myself.' Letting him unload everything himself is how Timur lays out the situation for Barinov. Starting with "davay" is the everyday Russian way to say "come on, let us do this".
