## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Stay calm, Your Royal Majesty, just stay calm.' Barinov addresses the giant crab mockingly using a royal title.
- 'That is what you are going to get now, Lyova.' Demonstrative particles express satisfaction as he prepares his prank.
- 'And how did you think you could joke with me?' A past-tense verb of thinking introduces the instrumental pronoun phrase.
- 'With us it is an eye for an eye, pal.' The classic biblical proverb uses the archaic Slavic poetic word for an eye.
- 'And of course, a big claw for a claw.' Barinov adapts the proverb to seafood using the feminine noun for a crab's claw.
- 'Right now I will quietly unplug this refrigerator.' A perfective future verb describes switching off the cooling unit.
- 'By morning the expensive crab will definitely spoil.' The preposition 'toward' with the dative marks the morning deadline.
- 'Viktor Petrovich, and what are you doing here?' Startled in the hallway, Barinov hears a question about his late presence.
- 'Why are you not sleeping at home yet?' The second-person plural verb maintains formal respect even late at night.
- 'Why, do they fine people who are awake too?' Barinov replies sarcastically using a substantivized active participle in the accusative.
- 'No, but it is already a very late hour.' A simple masculine time phrase notes how late the evening has grown.
- 'Fine, I am already heading home to my place.' In his haste to leave, Barinov forgets his signature lighter inside the fridge.
