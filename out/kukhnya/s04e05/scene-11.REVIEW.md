## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Stay calm, Your Royal Majesty, just stay calm.' Barinov proceeds steadily on the spot, steering the talk right where he wants it.
- 'That is what you are going to get now, Lyova.' Barinov continues honestly on the spot, bringing things back to earth after the buildup.
- 'And how did you think you could joke with me?' Barinov opens up frankly at once and uses "shutit" ("to joke") to carry the main weight of the thought.
- 'With us it is an eye for an eye, pal.' Focusing on the friend in the exchange, Barinov reaches for "u nas" ("by us, at our place") to talk about what they share.
- 'And of course, a big claw for a claw.' Barinov chimes in boldly during the talk, making his position completely clear.
- 'Right now I will quietly unplug this refrigerator.' Highlighting the fridge in the exchange, Barinov uses "kholodilnik" ("fridge") to carry the main weight of the thought.
- 'By morning the expensive crab will definitely spoil.' Bringing up the morning at once, the "-sya" ending on "isportitsya" ("to spoil, go bad") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'Viktor Petrovich, and what are you doing here?' Lyova presses frankly about what Barinov just brought up.
- 'Why are you not sleeping at home yet?' Lyova opens up smoothly in this beat and chooses "yeshchyo" ("more, still") so Barinov gets the point right away.
- 'Why, do they fine people who are awake too?' Weighing in naturally around Lyova, Barinov puts the question word "chto" right at the start so Lyova hears the focus immediately.
- 'No, but it is already a very late hour.' Lyova reacts quietly watching Barinov, making his position completely clear.
- 'Fine, I am already heading home to my place.' Barinov reacts briskly before Lyova and lands on "ukhozhu" ("am leaving", built on "ukhodit") to keep the statement grounded.
