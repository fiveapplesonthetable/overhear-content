## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'German Mikhailovich, may I come in to see you?' Max continues firmly during the talk and brings in "mozhno" ("may, can") to make the meaning unmistakable.
- 'Yes, Lavrov, come in quickly, how are things?' Jumping in smoothly in front of Max, German puts the question word "kak" right at the start so Max hears the focus immediately.
- 'By the way, I fired one cook today.' Max continues evenly with a nod, without trying to dress up how he really feels.
- 'Here is his letter of voluntary resignation.' Bringing up the statement to German, Max reaches for the Russian word "sobstvennomu" ("own, proper", built from "sobstvennyy") right at the center of the line.
- 'Lavrov, you are simply growing in my eyes!' Discussing the eye around Max, German uses the little word "prosto" ("simply, just") to make the point sound effortless.
- 'And what is this gathering by my office?' German prompts curiously where this conversation is heading next.
- 'The cooks are asking to allow them to do sports.' Addressing the cook in this beat, Max brings in "zanimatsya" ("to be busy with") to make the meaning unmistakable.
- 'Interesting, what kind of sports at work?' Discussing the sport in the exchange, German keeps normal statement word order here, turning the line into a question for Max purely through rising pitch.
- 'Ordinary light gymnastics in the back courtyard.' Max replies calmly in their talk, before German can change the subject.
- 'After all, people stand by the stove all day.' Highlighting the stove in the exchange, Max reaches for the Russian word "tselyy" ("whole, all") right at the heart of the line.
- 'Well all right, I do not object at all.' Reacting dryly in front of Max, German adds "sovsem" ("completely, at all") so the statement leaves zero room for doubt.
- 'Only make sure they do not turn gymnastics into smoke breaks!' German reacts steadily watching Max, watching Max's face to see how the news lands.
