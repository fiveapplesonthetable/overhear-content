## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Lev, today showed you still have much to learn.' Dative pronoun 'to you' combines with an existential verb and dative pronoun 'to what'.
- 'Viktor Petrovich will now take the position of sous-chef.' Notice the inverted Russian word order with the direct object 'position' first.
- 'I understand everything and completely agree.' Short masculine predicate adjective expresses full agreement.
- 'All right, Lyova, do not be too upset.' Imperfective negative reflexive imperative offers gentle comfort.
- 'Hey, do you have a light right now?' In spoken Russian, the literal noun for fire is used to ask for a cigarette light.
- 'Whoa, my lighter, where did you find it?' Barinov recognizes the lighter he dropped the night before.
- 'Right where you left it yesterday, in the refrigerator.' Prepositional case reveals that Lyova knew the culprit all along.
- 'Wait, and why did you stay silent then?' The prefixed perfective past verb means keeping quiet and holding one's tongue.
- 'Why say anything, after all they would have fired you right away.' Subjunctive particle with the plural past tense expresses a hypothetical consequence.
- 'Take my money for this big fine.' Touched by Lyova's nobility, Barinov insists on paying the twenty-thousand-ruble fine himself.
- 'Lena, please put Lyova back in his position.' Barinov goes straight to Elena Pavlovna with a warm singular imperative.
- 'I was the one who unplugged the refrigerator on purpose yesterday.' Cleft structure 'it is I' places full blame on himself.
- 'Deal, but now under your personal responsibility.' Preposition 'under' with the accusative seals their agreement.
