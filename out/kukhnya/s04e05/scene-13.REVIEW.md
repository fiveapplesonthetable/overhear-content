## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Lev, the important guests are already waiting in the hall.' Notice how Russian puts 'important' and 'hall' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'Please take out our royal crab quickly.' Elena uses a polite everyday Russian courtesy expression that sets the tone of the conversation right away.
- 'Good, right now we will quickly make the broth.' Lyova prepares the spiced cooking liquid before opening the fridge door.
- 'Ugh, Lyova, what a terrible smell all around!' Barinov feigns shock using an expressive interjection of disgust.
- 'Elena Pavlovna, we have a big problem.' Russians say 'by me' or 'with us' where English says 'I have' or 'we have', without needing a separate word for 'have'.
- 'What happened here in the kitchen, Lev?' Here Elena anchors the thought on 'happened' and finishes on 'kitchen', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'It seems our royal crab has gone completely bad.' In spoken Russian, the verb for disappearing also means spoiling of food.
- 'It does not just seem so, Lev, it has rotted!' Elena pairs 'it seems' with 'rotted' directly, since spoken Russian skips English filler words like 'the', 'a', and 'is'.
- 'Maybe this crab should just take a shower?' Barinov cracks a joke until Elena glares at him to be quiet.
- 'Lev, why was this refrigerator unplugged?' Here Elena anchors the thought on 'why' and finishes on 'is switched off', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'I cannot even believe it, I did not unplug it!' Lyova is genuinely bewildered and spots Barinov's lighter inside the fridge.
- 'I did not expect this from you at all.' Putting the short word for 'not' right in front of the action word is all Russian needs to make a sentence negative, with no extra helper words like 'do' or 'did'.
- 'I will deduct twenty thousand from your salary!' Seeing how distraught Elena is, Barinov begins to regret his prank.
