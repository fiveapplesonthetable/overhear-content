- 'Vitya, why did you take the ticket from the guest?' Mikhail anchors the line on 'proper name', while 'retrieved' completes the thought.
- 'I thought that you gave him my ticket!' Spoken Russian keeps this brisk, leaning on 'I' and finishing on 'ticket'. Mikhail answers right away.
- 'Whenever you think, things only get worse for everyone!' In Russian, the idea of 'when' flows naturally into 'to become' without extra filler words.
- 'Sorry, Misha, I really did not know that.' The negative particle sits right before the verb meaning 'sorry' to negate the statement.
- 'Now I am going to have serious problems because of you.' Mikhail brings out the contrast between 'now' and 'at' in a few short words. Chef Barinov answers right away.
- 'Then let me give him this ticket right here.' Chef Barinov anchors the line on 'and', while 'ticket' completes the thought.
- 'And you will simply return mine to me quietly!' Chef Barinov brings out the contrast between 'and' and 'you' in a few short words.
- 'I already returned your ticket a long time ago!' The meaning hinges on 'yes' working together with 'ticket' in spoken Russian. Chef Barinov answers right away.
- 'You really returned it? Is it lying in my pocket?' The '-l' ending on the verb meaning 'to give back' places the action in the masculine past tense.
- 'But now I have big problems because of you.' Russians say 'by me' or 'with us' where English says 'I have' or 'we have', without needing a separate word for 'have'.
- 'Why then should I be so kind to you?' Spoken Russian keeps this brisk, leaning on 'why' and finishing on 'kind'. Chef Barinov answers right away.
- 'Misha, wait, do not take both tickets at once!' An emphatic spoken line centered on the verb meaning 'wait'.
