## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Today for lunch there will be your favorite pilaf.' Fedya favors this sharp word in reply: "lyubimyy" means "favourite", and it carries the main weight of what Fedya is saying.
- 'Your pilaf always turns out simply wonderful.' Fedya shapes this statement on cue: Russian says "u tebya" (literally "at you" or "by you") where English uses "you have" or "your", keeping the phrasing close and informal.
- 'Look, what kind of blue paint is this?' Denis questions curiously as Fedya tries to explain the situation.
- 'An acquaintance from the bank brought me this yesterday.' Fedya uses one sharp element with Katya: "znakomyy" means "familiar", and it carries the main weight of what Fedya is saying.
- 'He works at the bank transporting money.' Fedya weighs in quietly in their talk, to clear the air between them once and for all.
- 'They put such paint in bags in case of theft.' Fedya pushes back boldly beside Katya, testing Katya's reaction in the middle of the exchange.
- 'And how am I supposed to work now? The shower broke!' Denis picks one concrete choice in earnest: "slomalsya" means "broke down" (from "slomatsya"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'Take the key, you can take a shower in my hotel room.' Katya jumps in boldly with a nod, so Fedya knows she is not just making small talk.
- 'Katya, did you move into our hotel?' Fedya challenges promptly in front of Denis without any preamble.
- 'Yes, there is a small renovation at my home right now.' Katya grounds one colorful touch toward Fedya: "nebolshoy" means "small, modest", and it carries the main weight of what Katya is saying.
- 'The plus is that my mom is the owner of the hotel.' Katya directs the reply right away: notice how Russian needs no word for "am", "is", or "are" in the present — placing the words side by side says it all.
- 'Cool, you are lucky to have a mom like that!' Fedya answers briskly without holding back, without trying to dress up how he really feels.
