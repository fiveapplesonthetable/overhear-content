## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello, young man, and who are you anyway?' Asking whether young man and who are Max anyway puts Max right on the defensive. Notice how she sticks to the formal "you", keeping a professional line between them.
- 'I am Maxim Lavrov, a cook in your restaurant.' Noting that he is Maxim Lavrov a cook in Eleonora's restaurant keeps Max's side of the story simple and clear.
- 'And where is Katya right now? Probably in the kitchen?' Asking where Katya right now is cuts straight through the tension between Eleonora and Max.
- 'Yes, she is downstairs, should I pass anything on to her?' Max asks whether she is downstairs should he passes anything, refusing to let the point slide. Russian packs the whole idea of what is allowed or needed into one impersonal word at the front.
- 'No, there is no need to pass anything on to her right now.' Since there is no need to pass anything on to her right now, Eleonora does not waste words on excuses. There is no "you" needed in the Russian here: one short word states the rule for everyone in the room.
- 'Then I will go back downstairs to work.' Explaining that he will go back downstairs to work lays the cards on the table for Eleonora.
- 'Chef, there are simply no more tickets for this match!' Once there are simply no more tickets for this match, Timur makes sure Eleonora hears every word. Russian puts the key word right up front so his emotion lands on the first beat.
- 'Timur, please get me at least one ticket!' By getting him at least one ticket, Max raises the stakes in the room at once. Russian puts the key word right up front so his emotion lands on the first beat.
- 'Would you like me to get you a rare fish instead?' Here asking whether Eleonora likes him to get Eleonora a rare fish instead shows Timur looking for the real truth.
- 'Even getting a giant beast is much easier right now!' Saying that even getting a giant beast is much easier right now is how Timur takes charge of the moment. Russian puts the key word right up front so his emotion lands on the first beat.
- 'What do I need fish for when today is the main derby?' Asking what he needs fish for when today is the main derby cuts straight through the tension between Max and Eleonora. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'Sorry, my friend, here I am powerless to help.' Saying that his friend here he is powerless to help is how Timur sets the tone with Eleonora.
