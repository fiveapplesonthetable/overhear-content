## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Forgive me.' By forgiving him, Max keeps his side of the story grounded.
- 'They are perfectly fine.' Realizing that they are perfectly fine changes how Fedya handles the situation.
- 'I just made an assumption.' Noting that he just made an assumption keeps Rodion's side of the story simple and clear.
- 'Why are you even listening to me in the first place?' Asking why Fedya even listening to him in the first place is is how Max puts Fedya on the spot. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
- 'And what if he does not show up?' Wondering if what if he does not show up shows Fedya caught off guard.
- 'He will show up, where else would he go?' Questioning whether he will show up where else would he go is how Rodion tests Fedya's story. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'He needs the cash, which means he will show up.' Once he needs the cash which means he will show up, Max can finally tell Fedya what he means.
- 'Konstantin, hello there.' With the situation and point in play, Fedya keeps this short reply brisk and direct.
- 'Do you happen to drink beer, by any chance?' Asking whether Fedya happens to drink beer by any chance is how Rodion puts Fedya on the spot. Notice how he sticks to the formal "you", keeping a professional line between them.
- 'Hello, Rodion Sergeyevich.' Between the situation and the shift, Max gives Fedya a quick, grounded response.
- 'Rodion Sergeyevich, who drinks beer outside in the winter?' Questioning whether who drinks beer outside in the winter is how Fedya tests Max's story.
- 'Well, one can see.' Rodion reminds Fedya that one can see before the moment passes.
- 'Tell me who your friend is.' Telling him who Fedya's friend is cuts straight through the tension between Max and Fedya. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
