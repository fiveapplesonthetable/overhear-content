## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'And I had to go buy a brand-new batch.' Max explains that he had to go buy a brand new batch, keeping his voice calm and direct.
- 'You have been given a lot more authority now.' Realizing that Max has been given a lot more authority now changes how Barinov handles the situation. In Russian you say "by me" or "with us" rather than "I have", so the situation sits right with the person.
- 'There are excellent snails in the fridge, I checked them myself.' Noting that there are excellent snails in the fridge he checked them himself keeps Timur's side of the story simple and clear.
- 'Before we manage to sell it all.' Over the situation and the shift, Max answers Barinov without a second thought.
- 'Chef, where are you heading off to?' By asking where Max heading off is, Barinov presses Max for a straight answer. Even in the middle of the drama, he keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
- 'There will be at most one and a half people here today.' Saying that there will be at most one and a half people here today is how Timur sets the tone with Barinov.
- 'I will be back in the evening.' Once he will be back in the evening, Max can finally tell Barinov what he means.
- 'Wait, where is everybody?' Here asking where everybody is shows Barinov looking for the real truth. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'Two huge barges.' Around the situation and shift, Timur sets a calm, everyday tone with Barinov.
- 'Oh, come on now.' Because Barinov should come on now, Max treats the matter as settled.
- 'I do not get it, what kind of nonsense is this?' Asking whether he does not get it what kind of nonsense is this puts Max right on the defensive. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'Oh, take it easy.' By telling Barinov to take it easy, Timur keeps his side of the story grounded.
- 'Excuse me, please, how will you manage to seat us?' By asking how Barinov manages to seat them will, Max presses Barinov for a straight answer. Russian expresses having something as "by me is", keeping the phrase short and grounded.
