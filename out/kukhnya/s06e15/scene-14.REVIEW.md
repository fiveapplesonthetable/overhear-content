## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Excuse me, but the dishes are identical.' Claiming that the dishes are identical sounds confident, even if Barinov has doubts. Even in the middle of the drama, she keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
- 'Fine, then you will write the resignation letter yourself.' Pointing out that fine then Elena will write the resignation letter themselves gives Elena a sobering reality check.
- 'Chef, I respect you very much, of course.' Respecting Barinov very much of course moves the conversation forward without extra drama.
- 'But you peeked, and Lyova did not.' Realizing that Barinov peeked and Lyova did not changes how Elena handles the situation. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
- 'Therefore Lyova won in this bet.' Noting that therefore Lyova won in this bet keeps Elena's side of the story simple and clear.
- 'I saw myself how Senya was cutting.' Even though she saw herself how Senya was cutting, Elena keeps a steady face in front of Barinov.
- 'I did not see anything through there!' Because he did not see anything through there, Barinov does not bother softening the blow for Elena. Russian stacks "never" or "nothing" together with "not", doubling the refusal for extra weight.
- 'It is unpleasant when your student cooks better.' Saying that it is unpleasant when Barinov's student cooks better is how Elena sets the tone with Barinov. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
- 'By the way, you did not tell Nikita either.' Once Barinov did not tell Nikita either, Sveta can finally tell Barinov what she means.
- 'Sveta, we really need to talk seriously.' Between the truth and the moment, Sveta gives Barinov a quick, grounded response. There is no "you" needed in the Russian here: one short word states the rule for everyone in the room.
- 'Let us talk later, meet my parents.' Talking later meet her parents together is how Sveta lays out the situation for Barinov. Starting with "davay" is the everyday Russian way to say "come on, let us do this".
- 'Mom, Dad, and this is my Denis.' Since Mom dad and this is her Denis, Sveta does not waste words on excuses.
