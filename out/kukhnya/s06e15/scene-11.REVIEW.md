## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Viktor Petrovich, how could you do that?' Following up earnestly in front of Barinov, Lyova puts the question word "kak" right at the start so Barinov hears the focus immediately.
- 'You are simply afraid to work without me!' Lyova weighs in bluntly watching Barinov, to clear the air between them once and for all.
- 'Lyova, do you have a new suit today?' Talking about a new suit around Lyova, Barinov reaches for the Russian word "kostyum" ("suit") right at the heart of the line.
- 'Leave me alone, Lyova, I am cooking an important dish.' Highlighting how important he is beside Lyova, Barinov leans on "vazhnoye" ("important", a form of "vazhnyy") to give the remark its edge.
- 'You cooked one dish and you are already a chef?' Lyova checks plainly while looking straight at Barinov here.
- 'I will easily cook any dish of yours!' Touching on the dish at once, Lyova chooses "prigotovlyu" ("will prepare", from the root "prigotovit") so Barinov hears the exact meaning.
- 'I will cook it with my eyes closed no worse!' Turning to his eyes in front of Barinov, Lyova leans on "zakrytymi" ("closed, classified", a form of "zakrytyy") to give the remark its edge.
- 'Right with your eyes closed and no worse?' Barinov questions briskly before Lyova can change the subject.
- 'Fine, deal, right now we both cook blindfolded!' Barinov steps in readily beside Lyova and uses "dogovorilis" ("agreed, deal", from "dogovoritsya") to make the point stick.
- 'If you cook worse, you will work for free!' Barinov pushes back naturally before Senya and brings in "prigotovish" ("will prepare", from "prigotovit") as the key Russian word here.
- 'I bet a thousand rubles on the chef!' Senya follows up dryly without holding back, shifting the mood of the conversation in a single beat.
- 'Give us the blindfolds, I am ready to start the bet.' Focusing on the blindfolds right here, Lyova leans on "povyazki" ("blindfolds", a form of "povyazka") to give the remark its edge.
