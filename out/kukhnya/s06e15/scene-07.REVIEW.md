## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Why does your application start with a lowercase letter?' By asking why Kostya's application start with a lowercase letter, Barinov presses Kostya for a straight answer.
- 'That is a capital letter, it just looks like that.' Explaining that that is a capital letter it just looks like lays the cards on the table for Barinov. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'Go and quickly rewrite everything all over again.' Going and quickly rewrite everything all over again is how Barinov lays out the situation for Kostya.
- 'Are you kidding me, this is already the third letter!' Lyova insists that are Barinov kidding him this is already the third letter, leaving Barinov no room to argue.
- 'If you want to be a chef, learn to write documents!' Saying that if Kostya wants to be a learn to write documents is how Barinov takes charge of the moment. Russian puts the key word right up front so his emotion lands on the first beat.
- 'Did you think of who to introduce your father to today?' Asking whether Barinov thinks of who to introduce Barinov's father to today cuts straight through the tension between Kostya and Barinov. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'I have two options right now.' Admitting that he has two options right now shows Kostya being honest for a change.
- 'Look, you two are together at work too.' Saying that Barinov two are together at work is how Kostya sets the tone with Barinov. Even in the middle of the drama, he keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
- 'Hello, Dad, should I pour for you as usual?' When Kostya asks if dad should he pours for Barinov as usual, Barinov has to answer fast. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
- 'I was walking nearby and decided to visit my son.' Adding that he was walking nearby and decided to visit his son fills in the missing piece for Barinov.
- 'Alright, I will not bother you two.' Noting that Alright he will not bother Barinov two keeps Lyova's side of the story simple and clear. Notice how he sticks to the formal "you", keeping a professional line between them.
- 'I will go sit quietly at a table alone.' Since he will go sit quietly at a table alone, Lyova does not waste words on excuses.
