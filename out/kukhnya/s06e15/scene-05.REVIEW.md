## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Maybe he was just sitting in the dark?' Focusing on the darkness right here, Kostya uses the little word "prosto" ("simply, just") to make the point sound effortless.
- 'Maybe he was just saving electricity at home?' Kostya prompts promptly if there is more going on behind the scenes.
- 'Does it not seem to you that your father is lonely?' Bringing up the father on the spot, Nastya brings in "kazhetsya" ("it seems, I think", from "kazatsya") as the key Russian word here.
- 'We definitely need to introduce him to someone.' Leaning in quietly on the spot, Nastya leans on "nado" ("it is necessary, one needs to"), which carries the whole predicate in Russian without needing "it is".
- 'I wonder, and who will we introduce him to?' Kostya quizzes briskly whether Nastya really thought this through.
- 'Of course, to some good, kind woman.' Bringing up the woman with Kostya, Nastya reaches for the Russian word "zhenshchinoy" ("a woman", built from "zhenshchina") right at the center of the line.
- 'Good morning, respected Eleonora Andreyevna!' Nastya readily shares this morning greeting in one quick breath using "dobroye utro" ("good morning") — literally "kind morning", with the neuter ending matching "utro" ("morning").
- 'Kostya, one must say the full hello properly.' Eleonora fires back plainly hearing Kostya, testing Kostya's reaction in the middle of the exchange.
- 'There is no such short word in the language.' Showing how short things are to Kostya, Eleonora centers the thought on "korotkogo" ("short, brief", from "korotkiy") without wasting a word.
- 'No, she and Dad will not get along.' Touching on the dad around Eleonora, the "-sya" ending on "soydutsya" ("to converge") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'I would say a language barrier will get in the way.' Kostya follows up sharply toward Nastya, grounding the moment in plain reality.
- 'Wait for me, I need to go to the restroom.' Underscoring the toilet beside Kostya, Nastya brings in "tualet" ("toilet") to make the meaning unmistakable.
