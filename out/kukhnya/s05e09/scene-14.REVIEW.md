## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello.' With the point and visit in play, Barinov keeps this short reply brisk and direct.
- 'Alright, dirty the plates, then go wash them.' Over the point and the visit, Elena answers Barinov without a second thought.
- 'Will you not have breakfast with me?' Checking whether Barinov not have breakfast cuts straight through the tension between Elena and Barinov.
- 'No, did you forget? I am on a diet.' Asking whether Elena forgets is how Barinov puts Elena on the spot. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
- 'I would rather have some water.' Admitting that he would rather have some water shows Barinov being honest for a change.
- 'And did you lose much in one day?' By checking whether Barinov loses much in one day, Elena presses Barinov for a straight answer.
- 'Yes, that is not the limit yet.' Once that is not the limit yet, Barinov can finally tell Elena what he means. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'By the way, let us record all of this.' Proposing that they record all of this is how Elena lays out the situation for Barinov. Starting with "davay" is the everyday Russian way to say "come on, let us do this".
- 'We will photograph you every day.' Saying that they will photograph Barinov every day is how Elena sets the tone with Barinov.
- 'And your belly will get smaller.' Since Barinov's belly will get smaller, Elena does not waste words on excuses. Russian expresses having something as "by me is", keeping the phrase short and grounded.
- 'Let us do it.' Adding that they should do it fills in the missing piece for Elena. Starting with "davay" is the everyday Russian way to say "come on, let us do this".
- 'See how dangerous it is to joke with me!' Declaring that Elena should see how dangerous it is to joke with him brings Barinov's temper right to the surface.
