## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Did you break something?' Leaning in earnestly in this beat, Vika keeps normal statement word order here, turning the line into a question for Kostya purely through rising pitch.
- 'Me? No, I did not break anything.' Kostya questions straight out why things suddenly took this turn between them.
- 'Nobody saw a thing at all. Right, Max?' Focusing on the honestly to Max, Kostya leans on "voobshche" ("at all") to give the whole sentence its punch.
- 'Kostyan, sorry. I promised Vika not to lie.' Max chimes in smoothly at once and puts the weight on "obeshchal" ("promised", from "obeshchat") as the exchange unfolds.
- 'That is not true.' Max cuts in earnestly toward Kostya, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'There was cognac there, and it was cheap.' Highlighting the cognac during the talk, Max leans on "deshyovyy" ("cheap") to give the whole sentence its punch.
- 'Vika, listen, I really have changed.' Focusing on the honestly before Vika, the "-sya" ending on "izmenilsya" ("have changed") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'Let me help you, huh?' Max checks evenly whether Vika really thought this through.
- 'Alright.' Vika warmly voices this word of agreement as they meet using "ladno" ("alright") — built from the old Russian root for harmony and order, used every day for "okay, fine".
- 'Great! Then I will quickly run to change clothes.' Pushing back dryly with Vika, the "-sya" ending on "pereodetsya" ("change clothes") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'Five minutes, and do not go anywhere, okay?' Max tests openly whether Vika really thought this through.
- 'Hey, who are you?' Stepping in plainly in front of Vika, Max links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
