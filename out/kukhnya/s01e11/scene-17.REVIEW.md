## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'What do you want to order with us today?' Russians say "u nas", "at us", for "with us", meaning at our place. Nastya uses the polite "vy": "chto vy khotite zakazat".
- 'Bring me any dish to your taste.' The man uses "ty" with a waitress, which is rude. "Lyuboe" is "any at all". "Na tvoy vkus" is "to your taste".
- 'Good, I will choose the best for you.' "Vyberu", "I will choose", is the verb in Senya's "ya vybral samy luchshy tovar". "Luchshee" is "the best thing". Nastya keeps the "vy": "dlya vas".
- 'Why do you not take a seat nearby?' "Pochemu by tebe ne" is "why wouldn't you", a soft-sounding suggestion. "Prisest" is "to sit down for a moment". "Ryadom" is "nearby".
- 'Excuse me, but I am at work right now.' Russians are "na rabote", "on work", not "in" it. "Izvinite" is the polite "excuse me", not the casual "izvini". Nastya refuses gently.
- 'I am your customer and I pay for everything.' "Klient" is "client" in Russian letters. "Plachu za vsyo" is "I pay for everything", from "platit". The man says it as a threat.
- 'I am obliged only to bring your dishes.' "Obyazana", "obliged", has the woman's -a. "Tolko", "only", is Louis's word in "tolko bely", "only white". "Prinosit" is "to bring".
- 'You were the first to make pretty eyes at me.' The idiom is "stroit glazki", "to build little eyes", meaning "to flirt". "Pervaya", "first", has a woman's ending. It's an accusation.
- 'I was simply smiling politely at you, as at everyone.' "Ulybalas", "was smiling", has the woman's -la and the -sya. "Kak vsem" is "as to everyone": the smile is part of the job.
- 'Do not leave, sit with me a little.' "Ne ukhodi" is "don't go away", with u-, "away". "Posidi" is "sit a while", with po- for a short stay. "So mnoy" is "with me".
- 'I do not have the right to sit here.' "Ne imeyu prava" uses "imet", a formal verb for "have" that is rare in everyday speech. "Pravo", "right", becomes "prava" after the "ne".
- 'You must fulfill any requests of mine.' "Dolzhna", "must", has the woman's -a, since he is addressing her. "Prosby", "requests", grows from "prosit", "to ask for".
- 'Excuse me, I am going to the kitchen right now.' "Izvinite" again, with the polite form. "Poydu", "I will go", is the on-foot future. "Na kukhnyu" is "onto the kitchen".
