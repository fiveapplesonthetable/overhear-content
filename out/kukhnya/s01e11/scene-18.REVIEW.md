## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello, did you like our restaurant today?' "Pleased to you" is the Russian for "did you like", and the verb agrees with the restaurant, which is masculine. Vika opens with a standard question. She is polite and calm.
- 'Please forgive us, for the love of God.' The little word "uzh" softens the request, like "please do". "For God's sake" begins with the word "radi" that you heard in "for pleasure". The husband sounds sheepish.
- 'Did something happen with your wife?' "Something" is "what" with "-to" on the end. "With your wife" shows the "with" in the ending. Vika is puzzled by the apology, and she tries to be tactful.
- 'My wife told me everything in detail.' "In detail" is the single word you heard in "I can explain in detail". The husband puts "wife" first, as if she were the main character. He is the quiet half of the couple.
- 'Did she get poisoned by some dish of ours?' "Some" has "-to" on the end of "which". "Got poisoned" has the -las ending of a woman, with the -sya. Vika's worst fear has come true, or so she thinks.
- 'No, she was not poisoned by food at all.' "Not at all" is the little word you know, "vovse ne". The food is in the "by means of" form. The husband cuts the worry short. He is quick to clear their name.
- 'Why then did she feel so ill?' The "then" is the little "zhe" again. "To her became so bad" is the pattern for feeling ill. Vika sounds relieved, and she wants the real reason.
- 'She is pregnant, already in the third month.' Russians are "on the third month", which sounds odd to an English ear. "Pregnant" is a short form, and it ends in -a for a woman. The husband says it quietly, not boasting.
- 'So she just has bad morning sickness?' "Toksikoz" is a medical term, "toxicosis", that Russians use for morning sickness. "So" begins the question. Vika understands, and her relief shows in her voice.
- 'Yes, the smells of food make her nauseous.' "From the smells" uses the small word for "from" or "because of". The smell of the kitchen is too much for her. It is a standard complaint of early pregnancy.
- 'We were very worried about her health.' "Worried" is in the plural, because Vika speaks for the whole restaurant. The term for health is the one Senya used for the chef. She is gracious, and her voice is warm.
- 'Thank you for your understanding and care.' The husband uses "za", what Russian calls "for". The word for "care" is the one that Vika used in the banquet scene. His thanks are formal, but sincere.
- 'So this woman is definitely not the critic.' "So" is the little word that literally means "means". "Definitely not" is two words. Vika crosses a suspect off her list. She is more relieved than disappointed.
