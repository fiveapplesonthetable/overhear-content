## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Chef, attention! An emergency situation in the kitchen.' "Chrezvychaynaya situatsiya" is the long official phrase for an emergency, literally "extraordinary situation". Kostya opens with "Shef, vnimanie!", like an announcement.
- 'Kostya, what happened in the dining room?' "Chto sluchilos" is the everyday "what happened". And "v zale", "in the hall", is how restaurant staff say "in the dining room".
- 'A suspected restaurant critic has been spotted!' "Obnaruzhen predpolagaemy kritik" is "discovered, a supposed critic", the wording of a police report. Kostya puts "obnaruzhen" first, before the critic.
- 'She is sitting at table seven.' Russians sit "za", "behind", a table: "za sedmym stolikom". "Stolik" is the little-table word, the one used for café and restaurant tables. "Sedmoy" is "seventh".
- 'And I have a salad rejection!' "U menya otkaz", "at me a refusal", is how Russian says "I have". "Otkaz ot salata" is "refusal from the salad", a dish sent back.
- 'How, a rejection? My perfect salad?' Barinov repeats "otkaz" as a question: "Kak otkaz?" "Idealny" is "ideal" with a Russian ending, and it means "perfect".
- 'She sent the plate back, chef.' "Vernula" is "returned", with -la because she did it. "Nazad", "back", is the word Barinov used in "polchasa nazad", "half an hour ago".
- 'Which table? Definitely the seventh?' "Kakoy stolik?" is "which table?" And "tochno" is "exactly" or "for sure": "tochno sedmoy?", "definitely the seventh?" Barinov wants to be sure.
- 'Yes, a strict lady in glasses.' "Dama", "lady", is a formal word borrowed from French. Glasses are always plural, "ochki", so "in glasses" is "v ochkakh". "Strogaya" is "strict".
- 'What exactly did she not like?' "Chto ey ne ponravilos" is "what to her did not please", the same pattern as "mne nravitsya", "I like it". "Imenno" is "exactly".
- 'She said the sauce was too sour.' "Slishkom", "too", is the word Senya used when the little stove heated "slishkom medlenno", "too slowly". "Kisly" is "sour".
- 'This is an author recipe of haute cuisine!' "Avtorsky retsept", "author's recipe", is the Russian for a signature dish. And haute cuisine is "vysokaya kukhnya", "high kitchen". Barinov is wounded.
- 'Chef, she has already opened a notepad.' "Otkryla" is "opened", with -la because the woman did it. "Uzhe" is "already". And "bloknot" is the notepad from the last scene.
- 'I will personally prepare her a dessert!' "Lichno", "personally", is from "litso", "face": in person. "Ey", "to her", is one small word. Barinov's answer is food: "desert".
