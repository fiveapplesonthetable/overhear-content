## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Max, why are you slacking off here?' "Prokhlazhdaeshsya" means "cooling yourself off", from "prokhlada", "coolness", a relative of "kholod", "cold". Russians use it for someone taking it easy.
- 'What else am I to do, chef?' Russian says "chto mne eshchyo delat", "what to me still to do". "Delat" is "to do", in the dictionary form. It's the question of a worker who has been given no job.
- 'I was suspended from work today.' "Otstranili" is "they moved aside", with nobody named: the -li ending says "they" without saying who. "Ot raboty" is "from work".
- 'Who suspended you? Me?' Barinov repeats the word, now as "otstranil", with -l for a single man. The pause between "kto" and "ya" is the joke. He cannot bear that anyone else makes decisions in his kitchen.
- 'Viktoria Sergeyevna told me to sit here.' First name plus father's name, Sergeyevna, is the polite way to name the art director. And "skazala sidet", "said to sit", needs no "that" between.
- 'Since when do you listen to her?' Russian says "s kakikh por", "from which times", for "since when". "Slushaesh", "listen", means "obey" here, as in English "listen to your mother".
- 'In this kitchen, only I am the boss!' "Glavny", "main", is the word from "kto samy glavny". "Tolko ya", "only I", puts the stress on him. "Na etoy kukhne" is "on this kitchen".
- 'She promised to fire me, chef.' "Obeshchala", "promised", ends in -la, because a woman made the promise. "Uvolit", "to fire", is the word Alisa used when she threatened Lyova. "Menya" is "me".
- 'Only I can fire cooks!' "Uvolnyat", "to fire", here is the form for doing it again and again, as a power. "Povarov" is "povara", "cooks", as the ones being fired. "Mogu" is "I can".
- 'Now march to the kitchen!' "A nu" is a push, something like "come on, now". "Marsh" is a military "march". "Na kukhnyu" is "to the kitchen", the same as in Vika's order.
- 'A pile of orders is waiting for you there.' "Kucha", "heap", is everyday Russian for "lots". The order slips are "cheki", "checks". "Dozhidaetsya" is "waits until you come", from "zhdat", "to wait".
- 'Thank you, chef, I am running!' "Begu" is "I run", said in the middle of the movement. Russians say "begu!" for "I'm on my way!". Max is delighted.
- 'Wait, where are you carrying that pan?' "Ponyos" is "nyos", "carried", with po- for setting off, so Barinov catches him in the act. "Skovoroda", "pan", becomes "skovorodu", the thing carried. "Stoy" is "stop".
- 'To the workstation, chef!' "Na rabochee mesto" is literally "to the working place". "Rabochee" comes from "rabota", "work". Max keeps hold of the pan, and the scene ends.
