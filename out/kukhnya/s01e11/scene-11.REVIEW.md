## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Sasha, come over here for a second.' The "come up" word is the walking-up one. Vika uses the casual "ty" with the waitress, and she is in a hurry, so the command is short.
- 'What happened, Victoria Sergeyevna?' Sasha uses first name and father's name, because she is talking to the manager. "What happened" is the phrase you know by now. She is polite, and a little nervous.
- 'Who is sitting at the fifth table?' The table gets an ordinal, "the fifth little table". Russians sit "behind" it. Vika asks like a detective.
- 'A rather strange man is sitting there.' "Rather" is one small word. The describing word and the noun for "man" match each other. Sasha answers truthfully, and she is not impressed by what she saw.
- 'Why is he not ordering anything?' Vika's question goes to the heart of the matter. "Nothing not orders" has the double negative. She is quick, and she is paying attention.
- 'I do not know, this is Nastya's guest.' The phrase "of Nastya" is shown by an ending on the name. Sasha hands the problem to her colleague. She is glad that it is not hers.
- 'Did she already approach him?' The woman's -la on "came up" shows that Nastya did it. "To him" has an "n" added to "him", as Russian does after small words. Vika follows the trail.
- 'Yes, she just gave him the menu.' "Just now" is two small words. "Gave" has the woman's ending. Sasha reports, and she keeps it short. Nobody wants to be blamed.
- 'Is he carefully studying our hall?' "Studies" is the school word. Vika thinks the man is observing the room, and she is not wrong. His eyes are on everything but the menu.
- 'He is constantly writing in his notebook.' "In his own notebook" uses the "own" word again. "Constantly" is the one from "regular guests". Sasha's report is the clue that Vika wanted to hear.
- 'This looks very much like an inspection.' "Similar to" is the same pattern as "do I look like you?". The term for "inspection" grows from "check". Vika draws her conclusion at once.
- 'Should I call Nastya to you?' Russian says "to me to call?" for "shall I call?". "To you" is polite. Sasha offers to fetch her colleague, which is helpful.
- 'Yes, call her right now.' The command "call" is for summoning someone. "Straight away" is two small words. Vika ends the scene with an order. She will question Nastya herself.
