## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Viktor Petrovich, we have an urgent problem!' Vika uses first name and father's name, which is her formal register for the chef. "Urgent" is the word that began the whole banquet. She means business.
- 'Vika, do not shout in the kitchen.' It's "ne krichi", the shouting command that Alisa used on her father. Barinov is the one who shouts the most, so the irony is plain. "In the kitchen" has the "on" word.
- 'The critic at the table refused the salad.' "Refused from the salad" is the pattern with "from". The ending -sya shows that the critic did the refusing himself. Vika reports the news in her usual crisp voice.
- 'Why? My salad was fresh.' Russian has a past of "to be": "byl". Barinov uses it here, though the present has none. He is hurt, and the short sentence shows it.
- 'He says that he does not eat grass.' The salad was sorrel, and "grass" is the critic's word for it. "Doesn't eat" is short and flat. Vika quotes him word for word.
- 'He demands hot soup right now.' "Demands" covers a firm request. The critic orders and does not ask. Vika reports the demand, hoping the chef will give in.
- 'Let him make a normal order first.' "Let him" is the familiar "pust". "Normal" is borrowed. Barinov's pride is hurt, and the critic is not getting special treatment.
- 'Give him the soup from table one!' "Give" is the polite plural command, "otdayte". "From table one" uses the small word for "from". Vika's idea is to take the first table's soup and give it to the critic.
- 'Are you out of your mind, Vika?' Russian says "are you in your own mind?", with the "own" word. It's a standard idiom for "have you lost it". Barinov uses "ty" with her, and he does not hold back.
- 'Another person is waiting for this soup.' The Russian puts the doer at the end: "this soup waits another person". It is a usual Russian word order for emphasis. Barinov defends the other customer.
- 'But the critic will write a terrible review!' The term for "review" is related to "response". Vika uses it as a threat. She has seen what a bad review can do.
- 'I have had enough of these whims.' Russian says "from me is enough", with "enough" being a single word. "Whims" take the "of" ending. Barinov has reached his limit.
- 'Let him wait for soup in turn.' "In turn" uses the word "po", meaning "by", and what Russian calls "queue". Barinov's rule is the first-come rule. It's the fairest rule, and the most inconvenient.
