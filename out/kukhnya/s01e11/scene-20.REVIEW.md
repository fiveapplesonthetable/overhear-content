## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Vitya, we have an urgent problem!' Vitya is the short, friendly form of Viktor; Vika knows the chef very well. "Srochnaya", "urgent", ends in -aya to match "problema".
- 'Vika, do not shout in the kitchen.' "Ne krichi" is the command Alisa gave her father: "Papa, ne krichi na povarov". Barinov is the one who shouts the most. "Na kukhne" is "on the kitchen".
- 'The critic at the table refused the salad.' "Otkazalsya ot salata" is "refused from the salad". Here it ends in -lsya, the man's form of Vika's "otkazalas" about the woman guest.
- 'Why? My salad was fresh.' "Byl", "was", is the past of "to be", which Russian drops in the present. "Svezhy" is "fresh". Barinov is hurt.
- 'He says that he does not eat grass.' "Travu" is "trava", "grass", the critic's word for the salad. "Ne est" is "doesn't eat". Vika quotes him word for word.
- 'He demands hot soup right now.' "Trebuet", "demands", is a firm word: the critic orders and does not ask. "Goryachy sup" is "hot soup".
- 'Let him make a normal order first.' "Pust" is "let him", followed by "sdelaet", "he will make". "Snachala" is "first". "Normalny" is easy.
- 'Give him the soup from table one!' "Otday", "give away", is the plain command for "ty", the "you" Vika uses with the chef. "S pervogo stola" is "from the first table".
- 'Are you out of your mind, Vika?' Russian says "ty v svoyom ume?", "are you in your own mind?", with "svoyom" for "own". It's the standard "have you lost it". Barinov uses "ty" with her.
- 'Another person is waiting for this soup.' The Russian puts the waiting person last: "etot sup zhdyot drugoy chelovek", "this soup waits another person". The word order puts weight on him.
- 'But the critic will write a terrible review!' "Otzyv", "review", is related to "otozvatsya", "to respond". "Napishet" is "will write".
- 'I have had enough of these whims.' Russian says "s menya khvatit", "from me is enough". "Kaprizy", "whims", becomes "kaprizov" after it.
- 'Let him wait for soup in turn.' "Po ocheredi" is "in turn", from "ochered", "a queue". "Pust zhdyot" is "let him wait". Barinov's rule is first come, first served.
