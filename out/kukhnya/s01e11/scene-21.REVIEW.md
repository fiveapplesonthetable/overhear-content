## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Your soup, buddy, enjoy your meal.' The usual Russian for "bon appetit" is "of pleasant appetite", with the "of" ending. "Buddy" is the same warm word as in the bar scene. Barinov serves the man himself.
- 'Viktor Petrovich, what are you doing?' Vika uses the polite "vy", with his full name and father's name. The question is a rhetorical one. She can see what he is doing.
- 'I am doing what I must do.' "That what" is the Russian frame for "what". "Must" is "dolzhen". Barinov is calm, which is more alarming than a shout. He has principles, and he is following them.
- 'But an important critic is sitting right there!' The little "zhe" is the "after all". Vika points at the table. The critic has been sitting without soup for some minutes.
- 'I do not care who is sitting there.' Russian says "to me all the same", which is the idiom for "I don't care". The word for "same" is built on "equal", and it comes back in the last line. Barinov is clear.
- 'This guest ordered soup earlier.' "Earlier" means "before" in time. The guest at the bar had ordered first. Barinov explains it as simple justice. He is a man of rules.
- 'The critic is very angry, he will leave!' "Angry" is in a short form, and it comes from the Russian for "wicked". "Will leave" is a short future with the "away" prefix. Vika's voice is rising.
- 'Let him leave if he does not know how to wait.' "Let him" and "know how to" are two words you have heard. Barinov dares the critic to go. The chef will not move for anyone.
- 'You are risking our restaurant's reputation.' In Russian, you risk "with" something, so the reputation takes the "with" ending. "Reputatsiya" is borrowed. Vika's voice is serious. This is her real fear.
- 'Even if he were three times a critic!' "Be he" is a way to say "even if he were". "Three times" is one word. Barinov exaggerates, and it is part of the charm.
- 'I serve all guests in turn.' "Serve" is the "serve" of the banquet. "To all guests" shows "to" in both words. "In turn" again. He repeats his rule, like a man defending a creed.
- 'In my kitchen everyone is equal!' "Equal" is the plural short form, and it is the root of "all the same". Barinov ends the argument on a principle. It is the best line of the scene.
