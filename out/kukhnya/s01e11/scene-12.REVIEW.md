## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Nastya, how long have you had this guest?' Russian has two words for "long". One looks back from now, "since long ago", and the other measures a stretch of time. Barinov uses the first, with "at you".
- 'Which guest exactly, chef?' Nastya plays for time, or perhaps she really doesn't know which one he means. "Exactly" is the little word you know. It's a natural reaction.
- 'That strange man in the dark jacket.' "Dark" is the dark of the dark chocolate. "In a jacket" shows the "in" in the ending. Barinov describes the man from the kitchen door, and the picture is vivid.
- 'You could say, quite a long time.' "It is possible to say" is the Russian for "you could say". That "possible" is the opposite of "not allowed". Nastya hedges. She has been talking to him a while.
- 'What has he already managed to order?' "Managed" means "had time to", the very thing the stove didn't have. Barinov wants details, and he is half checking his own memory.
- 'Nothing at all so far, only water.' The little word for "so far" is the one that also means "while" and "bye". "Completely nothing" is a double negative. Nastya answers in short, flat words.
- 'He is writing something the whole time.' "All the time" is two words. And "something" is "what" with a "-to" on the end, the same trick as "somewhere". Nastya describes what she sees.
- 'Is he some famous writer?' "Famous" literally means "known". The writer is "one who writes", built from the verb. Barinov guesses. He has no better idea.
- 'But you were talking about that girl!' The little "zhe" is the "but you know". Nastya points out what the chef said earlier, and she sounds confused. The chef changes his mind a lot.
- 'It turns out I have two critics now?' "It comes out" is the usual Russian for "it turns out". Barinov works it out aloud, and the sum is bad. Two critics are twice as many as he can feed.
- 'What should I do now, chef?' The question has no word for "should". The plain form "to do" carries it. Nastya asks for orders. She is a waitress, and she wants to be useful.
- 'Immediately take him our signature menu.' The prefix on "take" means "away to". "Signature" echoes the one in "signature meat". Barinov's cure for a critic is a menu, as it is for everything.
- 'I will do everything in the best way.' "In the best form" is the idiom that Lyova used. Nastya promises. She will do as she's told, with her usual calm.
