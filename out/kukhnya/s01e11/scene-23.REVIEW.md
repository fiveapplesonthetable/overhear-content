## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Did you see what just happened in the hall?' Senya asks with "ty", as friends do. "Videl", "saw", has the man's -l. "Proizoshlo" is a more formal "happened" than the "sluchilos" you heard at the bar.
- 'Our bartender boldly stepped up to the critic's table.' "Smelo" is "boldly". "Podoshyol", "walked up", has po- for "up to". "Stolik" is a small table, the word for tables in a restaurant.
- 'This critic was rudely insulting our Nastya again.' "Obizhal" is a repeated action, and the form shows it. "Nashu Nastyu", "our Nastya", is how Russians speak of someone they're fond of. Senya is outraged.
- 'Kostya did not start tolerating such insolent behavior.' "Ne stal terpet", "did not become tolerating", is the Russian for "didn't put up with it". "Povedenie", "behaviour", comes from "vesti sebya", "to lead yourself". Fedya is full of admiration.
- 'What, did the guest order a drink from the bar now?' Senya's "chto" here is a surprised "what". "Napitok", "a drink", is what Kostya gave the hungover guest on the house. "Iz bara" is "from the bar".
- 'Exactly, he received an order to the fullest program!' "Po polnoy programme", "by the full programme", is the idiom for "the works". "Tochno" is "exactly". The critic got more than he ordered.
- 'Kostya poured a glass right onto his suit!' The prefix vy- in "vylil", "poured", means "out". "Stakan" is the tall glass, and "kostyum" is "suit". Senya says it with relish.
- 'Our quiet bartender turned out to be a real brave hero.' "Okazalsya" is "turned out to be", and after it "hero" becomes "geroem". "Khrabry" is "brave", and "tikhy" is "quiet". Fedya admires him.
- 'Now this important critic sits completely wet.' "Mokry" is "wet". "Ves", "whole", means "completely" here: "ves mokry", "all wet". Senya has an eye for the picture: the great man dripping at his table.
- 'But the chef will be terribly angry because of the scandal.' "Iz-za", "because of", is the small word that blames something. "Uzhasno", "terribly", comes from "uzhas", "horror". Fedya sees the trouble coming.
- 'Viktoria is already quickly running into the dining hall.' "Bezhit", "is running", is the on-foot word. "Obedenny zal" is the "lunch hall", from "obed", "lunch". Senya follows the story with his eyes.
- 'A very swift dismissal definitely threatens Kostya right now.' Russian says "Koste grozit uvolnenie", "to Kostya threatens a dismissal", with Kostya in the "to" form. "Grozit" is a strong word. Fedya has no illusions.
- 'At least he courageously protected his beloved girl!' "Zato" means "but on the plus side", and Russians use it to turn a complaint around. "Zashchitil", "protected", is related to "zashchita", "defence". Senya finishes on the bright side.
