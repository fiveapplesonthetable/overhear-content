## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Runt, are you still standing here?' "Oguzok" is the chef's nickname for Max, a cut of meat from the rear of the animal. "Vsyo yeshchyo" is "all still", Russian for "still".
- 'Chef, please, do not humiliate me anymore.' "Ne nado" is "not necessary", the Russian way to say "don't". "Unizhat'" is "to humiliate", from "nizkiy", low: to make someone low.
- 'Tomorrow morning come to work on time.' "Zavtra utrom" is "tomorrow in the morning". "Prikhodi" is "come" for something that repeats. "Vovremya" is "on time".
- 'Are you letting me stay to work in the restaurant?' "Ostavlyayete" is "you leave", used for "you keep". Max says "vy" to his boss, and "menya" is "me".
- 'Chef, but why did you change your mind?' "Peredumali" is "pere-", over, plus "dumat'", to think: to think again. It ends in "-li" because Max says "vy".
- 'Do not ask foolish questions, or I'll change my mind!' "A to" is "or else", literally "and that". "Peredumayu" is "I will change my mind", one word.
- 'Chef, are you really letting him stay?' "Pravda" is "truth", used for "really". "Yego" is "him", and Ainura says "vy" to the chef.
- 'I bet that he will run away on his own!' "Poporil" is "bet", the family of "posporim". "Sbezhit" is "will run away", from "bezhat'", to run. "Sam" is "himself".
- 'In a week this guy will leave on his own.' "Cherez nedelyu" is "through a week", for "in a week". "Paren'" is the casual "guy", and "uydyot" is "will leave".
- 'Every person has the right to a chance.' "Imeyet pravo" is "has the right", with the formal "imet'" for "have". "Pravo" also means "law".
- 'To some, life itself gives a second chance.' "Nekotorym" is "to some". "Sama zhizn'" is "life itself", with "sama" the feminine of "sam". "Vtoroy" is "second".
- 'I will definitely prove my culinary mastery.' "Obyazatel'no" is "definitely", from "obyazatel'nyy", obligatory. "Masterstvo" has "master" inside it, as in "mastera".
- 'In short, our life is full of surprises.' "V obshchem" is "in general", family of "obshchego" in "a lot in common". "Polna" is the feminine "full", to match "zhizn'".
- 'And the main adventure is only just beginning!' "Nachinayetsya" has a "self" on the end, so the adventure "begins itself". "Glavnoye" is "main", as in "main instrument".
