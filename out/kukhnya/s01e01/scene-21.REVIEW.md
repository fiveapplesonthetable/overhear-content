## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Chef, the festive dessert is already ready!' "Prazdnichnyy" comes from "prazdnik", holiday. "Gotov" is the short "ready", and it comes from the same root as "to cook".
- 'Attention everyone! The guest is tasting the newcomer's dish.' A woman guest is "gost'ya", the same word with a feminine ending. "Blyudo novichka" is "dish of newcomer", with a short "of" ending on "novichok" and no 's.
- 'Come here, sweet little doggy! Taste the dessert.' "Sobachka" is "sobaka", dog, with a small ending that makes it a pet name. "Milaya" is "sweet", the feminine of "milyy". "Poprobuy" is a command built on "probovat'", to try.
- 'She doesn't even look at the plate.' A dog is "ona", she, because "sobachka" is a feminine word. "Smotret' na" is "to look at", and "tarelku" takes -u.
- 'Chef, that is unfair! After all, it is a dog.' "Nechestno" is "ne" plus "chestno", honestly, the word from "frankly speaking". "Eto zhe sobaka" has the "zhe" for "after all".
- 'The world is not ideal at all, young man.' "Mir" means both "world" and "peace". "Ideal'en" is the short form of "ideal'nyy". The chef lectures Max with "young man".
- 'Let's make a bet, chef!' "Davayte" is literally "give", but before another word it means "let's", and it's one of the most useful words in Russian. "Posporim" comes from "spor", an argument or bet.
- 'What will we bet about?' "O chyom" is "about what", as before. "My budem sporit'" is "we will bet", the future "will be" plus "to bet".
- 'An ordinary person will like my hot dish.' "Obychnomu cheloveku" is "to an ordinary person", both words with the "to" ending, because the person is the one pleased. "Goryacheye" is "hot".
- 'Good! You will prepare a dish for me.' "Dlya menya" is "for me". Where English has one "me", Russian has several forms, and this is the "for" one.
- 'If I like it, you stay here.' "Yesli mne ponravitsya" is "if it pleases me". "Ostayosh'sya" is "you stay", in the present, which Russians use for firm promises.
- 'If not, you will leave here forever.' "Navsegda" is "na" plus "vsegda", always: forever. "Otsyuda" is "from here", the partner of "syuda", "to here".
- 'I agree to this bet!' A man says "soglasen", and a woman would say "soglasna". "Na etot spor" is "to this bet".
- 'We will see what kind of cook you are!' "Posmotrim" is "we will see". "Kakoy iz tebya povar" is literally "what from you cook", Russian for "what kind of cook you'll turn out to be".
