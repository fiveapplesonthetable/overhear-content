## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Angelina, how are our reservations today?' 'Chto u nas s' is literally 'what at us with', and it's how Russians ask 'how are we doing with'. 'Rezervy' are the bookings, borrowed from 'reserve'.
- 'Dmitry Vladimirovich, all tables are already booked.' First name plus father's name is the respectful way to address the owner. 'Stoliki' is 'little tables', with 'ik' making it small, and 'zanyaty' means 'taken'.
- 'Really? Are there no seats left at all?' 'Pravda' means 'truth', and Russians say it to mean 'really?'. 'Sovsem ne' is 'not at all', and 'mest' means 'of seats', after the word for 'remained'.
- 'Yes, there are no more free tables.' 'Bolshe net' is 'no more', with 'net' meaning 'there is not'. 'Svobodnykh stolikov' is 'of free tables', in the form it takes after 'net'.
- 'The guests will start arriving any minute.' 'S minuty na minutu' is literally 'from minute to minute'. 'Nachnut' is 'will begin', and 'prikhodit' means 'to come, on foot'.
- 'Look at that! It turned out just like I said.' 'Ty smotri' is Russian for 'look at that', and the 'ty' makes it a friendly nudge. 'Vyshlo' is 'came out', and 'tak, kak' is 'just as'.
- 'Our restaurant is very popular right now.' 'Populyaren' is 'popular', and a bare ending suits a masculine word like 'restoran'. There is no word for 'is'.
- 'People love us just the way we are!' 'Lyudi' is 'people', and it's irregular: 'chelovek' is one person and 'lyudi' is many. 'Imenno takimi' means 'exactly as such'.
- 'Go prepare additional tables.' 'Idi gotov' is two orders side by side, 'go, prepare', with no word for 'and'. 'Dopolnitelnye' means 'additional'.
- 'We will place two more tables by the window.' 'Dva stola' is 'two tables', and 'stol' takes an 'a' after the number two. 'U okna' is 'by the window', and 'okno' becomes 'okna' after 'u'.
- 'Great, today we have a full house!' 'U nas' is 'at us', so 'we have', and 'polny zal' is 'a full hall'. Nagiyev's 'otlichno' is 'excellent'.
- 'I will quickly organize everything right now.' 'Organizuyu' looks like the English, and it works the same way. It says 'I', 'will' and 'organize' in one word, and 'seychas' can mean 'now' or 'in a moment'.
- 'Wonderful, the evening promises to be successful!' The evening 'promises', as if it were a person. 'Udachnym' comes from 'udacha', luck, so something successful is something lucky.
