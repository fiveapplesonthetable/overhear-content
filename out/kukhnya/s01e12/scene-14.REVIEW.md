## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Nastya, are you ready to start our loud performance?' 'Gotova' is 'ready', with an 'a' because Kostya is talking to a woman. A man would be 'gotov'. 'Gromky' means 'loud' and 'spektakl' is a show or a play.
- 'Yes, only do not say overly rude words.' 'Ne govori' is 'don't say', and 'slishkom' means 'too'. After a 'don't', the words take a new ending, so 'grubykh slov' means 'of rude words'.
- 'Hey, pour me strong tea right now!' 'Ey' is the hey you call across a room. 'Naley' is 'pour!', and 'krepky' is 'strong', and is the word used for tea or coffee, rather than 'silny'.
- 'Pour it yourself, I do not listen to a foolish waitress!' Take 'ofitsiant', waiter, and add 'ka', and you get the waitress. Kostya's 'sama', yourself, takes an 'a' because Nastya is a woman.
- 'Kostya, after all we agreed to act completely differently!' 'Dogovarivalis' ends like a 'sya' word, so it's 'we agreed with each other'. 'Igrat' means both 'to play' and 'to act', and 'inache' is 'differently'.
- 'Shut up, redhead, do not disturb me working peacefully!' 'Zatknis' is a very rude 'shut up', and not something to say to anyone outside a play. 'Ryzhaya' is 'ginger-haired', with an 'aya' ending for a woman. 'Ne meshay' is 'don't disturb'.
- 'Well, did our cunning plan work perfectly?' 'Nu chto' is a 'so, then?' that Russians use to open a question. 'Khitry' is 'cunning'. 'Srabotal' means 'it worked', and the 's' at the front means 'it worked out'.
- 'Do not touch me and never come near me again!' 'Ne trogay' is 'don't touch'. 'Nikogda bolshe ne' means 'never again', with 'nikogda' and 'ne' side by side. That is correct in Russian.
- 'Nastya, wait, I was trying for our sake!' 'Postoy' is 'wait', and it's made from 'stoy', 'stand', with 'po' at the front. 'Starayalsya' is 'I tried hard', and 'radi nas' is 'for the sake of us'.
- 'You hurt me deeply with your mean words.' 'Obidel' means 'offended' or 'hurt feelings', not 'harmed the body'. The 'with' in 'with your mean words' is in the endings: 'svoimi zlymi slovami'.
- 'That was only an act for the strict management!' Act and game share one word in Russian, and it's feminine, so 'was' picks up an 'a' too.
- 'I do not care, I do not want to see you.' Word for word, 'to me everything is equal', and that's Russian for 'I don't care'. The second half is a plain 'I don't want to see you'.
