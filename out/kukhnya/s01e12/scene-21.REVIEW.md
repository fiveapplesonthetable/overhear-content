## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'This cigarette already tastes bitter.' The bitter-tasting action comes from the adjective 'bitter'. Cigarette is a feminine noun, so 'this' becomes 'eta'.
- 'Just throw it out quickly into the trash bin.' The 'it' is 'her', since cigarettes are feminine in Russian. Objects have a gender, and the little word for 'it' follows suit.
- 'All right, I want to go to the bathroom.' 'Khochu' is 'I want', and the thing he wants follows it straight, 'to go'. 'Tualet' is the same as the English word, borrowed ages ago from French.
- 'Wait, there is fire over there, friends!' 'Fire' is the flame kind. 'Friends' is an odd plural, because one friend is a different-looking word.
- 'The neighboring restaurant is burning right now!' 'Neighbouring' is built from 'neighbour'. 'Burns' is a short action.
- 'Are you sure that this is a real fire?' 'Uveren' is 'sure', with a man's bare ending. 'Pozhar' is a fire as a disaster, and 'ogon' is a flame, which is why Fedya asks if it's the serious kind.
- 'Of course, look at this black smoke.' 'Look at' is built from an order plus the little word for 'at'. 'Black' goes in front of 'smoke', as in English.
- 'People are running out of the building onto the street.' 'Vybegayut' is 'they run out', from 'vy', 'out', and 'begat', 'run'. 'Na ulitsu' is 'onto the street', and the 'u' ending shows direction.
- 'We urgently need to tell the boss about this.' Needing is expressed with 'to us', so the people who need go in the 'to' form. The boss gets a 'u' because he is the one told.
- 'Yes, he must know about this fire.' 'About' here is a casual word. 'Must' is short, and 'know' follows in its basic form.
- 'Drop the cigarette and let's go inside faster.' 'Poshli' looks like 'they went', but in speech it's an invitation, 'let's go'. Say it to friends. 'Brosay' is an order, and it can mean 'drop' or 'throw'.
- 'I am running after you, open the door.' 'After you' is literally 'behind you', in the form with 'oy' on the end. The order 'open!' is a friend's order.
