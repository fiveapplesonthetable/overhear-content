## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'You probably just got a defective turnstile.' Saying that Max probably just got a defective turnstile is how Barinov sets the tone with Max.
- 'Fedya, what happened to your gloves?' Over the truth and the award, Barinov presses Max for a straight answer. Russian expresses having something as "by me is", keeping the phrase short and grounded.
- 'Look, they tore! Fedya, put on new ones.' Saying that Fedya put on new ones is how Barinov takes charge of the moment. Russian puts the key word right up front so his emotion lands on the first beat.
- 'What is wrong? Do you not know where the new ones are?' By asking what wrong is, Barinov presses Max for a straight answer.
- 'I really do not know where the new ones are.' Since he really does not know where the new ones are, Max does not waste words on excuses.
- 'And these new gloves have disappeared somewhere too!' Insisting that these new gloves have disappeared somewhere shows Barinov is not backing down. Russian puts the key word right up front so his emotion lands on the first beat.
- 'I love our chain's new management so much!' Saying that he loves their chain new management so much is how Barinov takes charge of the moment.
- 'Good for you, what a good boy you are!' Declaring that good for Max what a good boy Max is brings Barinov's temper right to the surface. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
- 'Now you will be the best employee of this month.' Explaining that Max will be the best employee of this month lays the cards on the table for Max. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
- 'Viktor Petrovich, tell me, have you finished already?' Questioning whether tell her have Barinov finished already is how Vika tests Barinov's story.
- 'No, I can gladly continue some more.' Claiming that he can gladly continue some sounds confident, even if Max has doubts.
- 'No need, please come to the office right now.' Noting that Barinov should need please come to the office right now keeps Vika's side of the story simple and clear. Russian packs the whole idea of what is allowed or needed into one impersonal word at the front.
