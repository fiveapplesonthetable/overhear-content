## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Chef, forgive us, I really did not mean to.' Max responds wryly in this moment, grounding the moment in plain reality.
- 'And I do not care at all anymore.' Barinov features one key touch toward Nagiyev: "sovershenno" means "quite, completely", and it carries the main weight of what Barinov is saying.
- 'We have nothing at all to feed the guests in the hall.' Barinov phrases the comeback in earnest: adding "sovsem" ("completely, at all") pushes the statement all the way, leaving zero room for doubt.
- 'It is all over, our restaurant is finished.' Barinov fires back steadily hearing Nagiyev, saying out loud what both of them already know.
- 'That means I will not have to tolerate you anymore.' Barinov chooses one practical element in earnest: "pridyotsya" means "you'll have to" (from "priytis"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'And you too, I am going home right now.' Barinov delivers the comment with candor: the "-sya" ending on "seychas" ("now") turns the action inward or makes it happen between people naturally.
- 'There is nothing to feed the guests, we must do something fast.' Max fires back sharply to be clear, steering the talk right where he wants it.
- 'Do not think that we are skimping on electricity.' Nagiyev stresses one vivid detail toward Max: "elektrichestve" means "on electricity" (from "elektrichestvo"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'We are the true elite of Moscow's restaurant business.' Nagiyev aims the thought without pause: notice how Russian needs no word for "am", "is", or "are" in the present — placing the words side by side says it all.
- 'We are trying to create a special warm atmosphere here.' Nagiyev opens up right away on the spot, saying out loud what both of them already know.
- 'Dima, and when will they finally bring us food?' Max leans on this sharp word toward Barinov: "nakonets" means "at last, finally", and it carries the main weight of what Max is saying.
- 'Do I need to invest money in the restaurant first?' Max states the thought beside Nagiyev: the single Russian word "nuzhno" ("it is needed, one has to") sets up the whole statement without needing a separate word for "it is".
