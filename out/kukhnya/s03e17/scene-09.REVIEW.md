## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Who are you hiding from right here, Max?' Katya checks openly about what Max just brought up.
- 'I am not hiding from anyone at all.' Max reacts quietly in the exchange and leans on "pryachus" ("to hide", a form of "pryatatsya") to give the remark its edge.
- 'Chef just gave me urgent work.' Touching on the job in this beat, Max uses the little word "prosto" ("simply, just") to make the point sound effortless.
- 'I need to urgently check the electricity here.' Max reacts right away toward Katya, testing Katya's reaction in the middle of the exchange.
- 'And why do you have to be the one to do that?' Katya pushes back decisively to Max and puts the emphasis on "dolzhen" ("has to, must") in the middle of the talk.
- 'Because I studied to be an electrician in Voronezh.' Focusing on an electrician during the talk, Max uses "elektrika" ("electrician", from "elektrik") to make the point stick.
- 'Alright then, go ahead and fix it in front of me.' Katya answers wryly around Max, without trying to dress up how she really feels.
- 'Now I have really gotten myself into trouble.' Max follows up naturally in this beat and centers the remark on "seryozno" ("seriously") without any extra fuss.
- 'Well, you will not understand anything anyway.' Weighing in naturally on the spot, Max pairs the negative word with "ne" ("not"), since Russian naturally doubles its negatives for emphasis.
- 'Let us see what looks the most harmless in here.' Max pushes back bluntly toward Katya, keeping the focus on what actually matters here.
- 'Whoa, and is that all of your work?' Highlighting the job around Max, Katya leans on "rabota" ("job") to give the whole sentence its punch.
- 'One fuse is alive, now we will check the others.' Focusing on the fuse on the spot, Max reaches for the Russian word "predokhranitel" ("fuse") right at the heart of the line.
