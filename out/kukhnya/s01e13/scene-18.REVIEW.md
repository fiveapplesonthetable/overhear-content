## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Why did you come back so angry?' 'Vernulas'' means 'came back', with an 'as' ending for a woman. 'Takaya zlaya' is 'so angry', and both end in 'aya'.
- 'Nastya just saw me in here!' 'Tolko chto' is 'just now', not 'only what'. 'Videla' is 'saw', and it ends in 'a' because Nastya is a woman.
- 'She simply lives in this apartment.' The word for 'apartment' changes its last letter after 'in', so 'kvartira' becomes 'kvartire'. Max says it as casually as he can, as though it were obvious.
- 'Did you invite our whole restaurant here?' She says 'whole' in front of 'our restaurant', as if he had invited everybody, and she means it as a dig.
- 'In my opinion, you are exaggerating greatly now.' 'Po-moemu' is 'in my opinion', built like 'po-russki', 'in Russian'. 'Preuvelichivaesh' means 'you exaggerate', and it comes from 'velik', great.
- 'My reputation at the restaurant will be ruined.' 'Budet razrushena' is 'will be ruined', and it ends in 'a' because 'reputatsiya' is feminine. The word 'razrushit' means 'to destroy'.
- 'Nastya is a kind girl and knows how to keep quiet.' 'Umeet' means 'knows how', so it's about a skill. 'Molchat' is 'to be silent'. Max is soothing.
- 'Waiters always discuss the management's personal life.' 'Ofitsianty' are waiters, and 'obsuzhdayut' means 'discuss'. 'Nachalstva' means 'of the management', and it's a collective word.
- 'No one will discuss you, calm down.' 'Ne stanet' is 'will not begin', and it works as 'won't'. 'Uspokoysya' is 'calm yourself'.
- 'I will never come here again.' 'Bolshe nikogda ne' is 'any more never not', a triple negative. 'Pridu' is 'I will come', for someone arriving on foot.
- 'Perhaps it is already time for you to go to work.' 'Pozhaluy' is 'perhaps' or 'I suppose', and it softens a suggestion. 'Tebe pora' is 'to you time'.
- 'We must leave the building separately.' 'Razdelno' is 'separately', and it comes from the word for 'divide'. 'Iz doma' is 'out of the house', with 'a' at the end of 'dom'.
- 'I agree, you go first, Vika.' 'Soglasen' is 'agree', with a man's bare ending. 'Pervoy' is 'first', and the 'oy' ending is for a woman.
