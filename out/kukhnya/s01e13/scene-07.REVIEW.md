## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Viktor Petrovich, you have a broken mirror here.' 'U vas tut' is 'at you here', and it's the polite way to say 'you have'. 'Slomannoe' means 'broken' and ends in 'oe' to go with 'zerkalo', mirror.
- 'Well, would you look at that!' 'Nado zhe' is an old idiom meaning 'well I never!' It's made out of 'nado', 'necessary', and the little 'zhe' that adds feeling. Barinov is laying a trap.
- 'Didn't you notice?' Barinov uses the formal 'you' for his ex-wife, and it sounds icy. A man shouting at his ex rarely sounds so polite.
- 'That yours is too?' 'Tozhe' means 'also', and 'u vas tozhe' is 'at you also'. In other words, 'yours too'.
- 'Mine? How? Where?' Three question words in a row: 'u menya' is 'at me', so 'mine', then 'kak' is 'how' and 'gde' is 'where'. Eleonora is fumbling.
- 'Right there.' 'Vot' points, 'zhe' adds weight, and 'nu' is a 'well'. All three are little words, and together they say 'look, right there!'.
- 'Now we're even.' 'Kvity' is the same word as the English 'quits', and it means the same. 'Teper' is 'now', and 'my' is 'we'.
- 'Kostya, did you see?' The word for 'did you see' ends in 'l' because Kostya is a man; the same question to a woman would end in 'la'. The real action is the whisper, with no more words than that.
- 'He smashes the mirror on her car.' 'Razbivaet' is 'smashes' or 'breaks into pieces', while 'slomat' is 'to break' something like a mechanism. 'Ee mashiny' is 'of her car'.
- 'And she acts like she had nothing to do with it.' 'Tipa' is a slangy 'like' or 'sort of', the sort teenagers use. 'Ne pri delakh' is slang for 'not involved'.
- 'I am sure.' The word for 'sure' ends in 'a' because Nastya is a woman, and a man would say it without the 'a'. Two words are enough, 'ya uverena'.
- 'This won't just blow over.' 'Prosto tak' is 'just like that'. 'Zakonchitsya' means 'will end', with 'sya' because things end by themselves.
