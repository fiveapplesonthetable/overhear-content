## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Why didn't you tell me you were leaving?' 'Skazala' ends in 'a' because Vika is a woman. 'Uezzhaesh' is 'you're leaving', and the 'u' at the front means 'away' for something that goes by vehicle.
- 'Let's talk later.' 'Davay' begins a suggestion. 'Pozzhe' is 'later', and 'pogovorim' is 'let's have a talk'. Vika wants the talk to happen, but not now.
- 'No, we're talking now!' It's the same 'pogovorim' as before, and the only thing that changes is 'seychas', 'now'. Max copies her words, which is a good way to answer.
- 'Let me explain what's going on.' The phrase for 'what's going on' is literally 'in what is the matter'. It's a set expression, so learn it as a lump and use it whenever you want an explanation.
- 'You've been avoiding me all day!' 'Running around' is how Russian says 'avoiding', so she says 'you run from me'. English uses 'have been' for this, but Russian just uses the present.
- 'I need to think.' 'Podumat' is 'to think for a bit', and the 'po' at the front means a short time. 'Mne nado' is 'to me it's necessary'.
- 'We'll talk in three days when I get back.' The prefix 'pri' means 'arrive', and the rest is the verb for flying. So the word says 'I'll arrive by air', which a plane trip needs.
- 'Vika, good morning!' 'Utro' is a neuter word, so 'good' becomes 'dobroe'. Max is trying to start again with a smile.
- 'I brought you coffee.' The word order is 'to you coffee brought', and Russian is perfectly happy with that. 'Prinyos' is 'brought', and 'kofe' never changes its ending.
- 'I hate you!' The word for 'hate' is a very strong word, and the object, 'you', is in the form it takes after it. Russians keep it for real anger.
- 'You don't like coffee?' Max is baffled. 'Lyubish' is 'you like' or 'you love', depending on what's coming. It's the only everyday word for 'like'.
- 'What the hell coffee!' 'Kakoy' plus a noun gives a scornful 'what do you mean, ...!'. 'K chyortu' means 'to the devil', a mild way of dismissing the coffee.
- 'I missed the Milan trip because of you!' 'Ne poletela' is 'didn't fly', with an 'a' for a woman. 'Iz-za tebya' is 'because of you', the same 'iz-za' that means 'because of' in lots of arguments.
