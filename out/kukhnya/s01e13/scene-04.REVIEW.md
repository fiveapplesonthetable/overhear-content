## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Let's have you walk from here.' The 'do' that begins the walking word means 'all the way to', so she tells him to walk until he arrives. 'Otsyuda', from here, is the 'from' partner of 'syuda', to here.
- 'We do not need to appear at work together.' 'Nam ne nado' is literally 'to us not necessary', the usual way to say 'we don't need to'. 'Poyavlyatsya', to appear, ends in 'sya', as if 'to show oneself'.
- 'You are absolutely right, Vika.' 'Sovershenno', absolutely, is a stronger 'completely'. 'Prava', right, ends in 'a' because Vika is a woman.
- 'It could cause damage to my reputation.' 'Nanesti uron' is 'to inflict damage', two words that go together, and 'uron' is damage. Damage is done 'to' something, so 'moey reputatsii' has the 'to' ending.
- 'Max, are you sure that this is right?' A man would be 'uveren', with a bare ending. 'Pravilno', correct, grows from the same root as 'pravy', right.
- 'We will see.' It's a single word, 'posmotrim', which is 'we'll look'. Russians say 'we'll see' like this, as if looking were the answer.
- 'I do not want extra questions from the boss.' After 'ne khochu', I don't want, the questions take the 'of' form: 'lishnikh voprosov', literally 'of extra questions'. 'Ot shefa' is 'from the boss', and 'ot' sits in front of the person who sends the thing.
- 'No one will find out about our secret romance.' 'Nikto ne uznaet' is 'nobody will find out', with the double no. 'Roman' means both 'romance' and 'novel', and 'tayny' means 'secret'.
- 'In this restaurant people really love gossip.' 'Spletni' is gossip, the plural of 'spletnya', a single piece of it. 'Lyudi', people, is an odd plural: one person is 'chelovek'.
- 'I will keep quiet and just cook food.' 'Budu molchat' is 'I will be silent', the future made with 'budu' and the basic form. 'Gotovit' is 'to cook', and 'edu' is 'food' with the ending that goes with 'cook'.
- 'Then you go first, and I will go later.' 'Idi' is the order 'go', and 'poydu' is 'I will go', from the same verb with 'po' added. 'Pervym' has the 'ym' ending of 'as the first one'.
- 'Good, see you in the kitchen in ten minutes.' 'Cherez' is 'through', and Russians use it for 'in' when talking about time: 'cherez desyat minut' is 'in ten minutes'. After 'ten', the word for minute takes a special plural, 'minut'.
