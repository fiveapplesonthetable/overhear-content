## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Let's have you walk from here.' The 'do' tucked into the front of the walking word means 'all the way to', so Vika isn't saying 'walk', she's saying 'walk until you get there'. The word for 'from here' is a single word.
- 'We do not need to appear at work together.' The word for 'appear' ends in 'sya', which means 'show oneself'. Russians use 'no need' in front of it to say 'let's not'.
- 'You are absolutely right, Vika.' 'Sovershenno' means 'perfectly' or 'entirely'. 'Prava' ends in 'a' because Max is saying it about a woman.
- 'It could cause damage to my reputation.' 'Nanesti uron' is 'to inflict damage', two words that go together, and 'uron' is damage. 'Reputatsii' ends in 'i' after 'my'.
- 'Max, are you sure that this is right?' 'Uveren' is 'sure', with a man's bare ending. 'Pravilno' means 'correct', and it comes from the same family as 'right' in the sense of 'pravy'.
- 'We will see.' It's a single word, 'posmotrim', which is 'we'll look'. Russians say 'we'll see' like this, as if looking were the answer.
- 'I do not want extra questions from the boss.' 'Lishnikh voprosov' has the ending that follows 'don't want'. 'Ot shefa' is 'from the boss', and 'ot' sits in front of the person who sends the thing.
- 'No one will find out about our secret romance.' 'Nikto ne uznaet' is 'nobody will find out', with the double no. 'Roman' means both 'romance' and 'novel', and 'tayny' means 'secret'.
- 'In this restaurant people really love gossip.' The word for 'gossip' is always plural in Russian, so there is no way to say 'one gossip'. 'People' is the irregular plural of 'person'.
- 'I will keep quiet and just cook food.' 'Budu molchat' is 'I will be silent', the future made with 'budu' and the basic form. 'Gotovit' is 'to cook', and 'edu' is 'food' with the ending that goes with 'cook'.
- 'Then you go first, and I will go later.' The same action comes twice, once as an order and once as a future. 'First' ends in 'ym' because it means 'as the first'.
- 'Good, see you in the kitchen in ten minutes.' 'Cherez' is 'through', and Russians use it for 'in' when talking about time: 'cherez desyat minut' is 'in ten minutes'. After 'ten', the word for minute takes a special plural, 'minut'.
