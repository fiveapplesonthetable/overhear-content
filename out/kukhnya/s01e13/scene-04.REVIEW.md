## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Let's have you walk from here.' The 'do' that begins the walking word means 'all the way to', so she tells him to walk until he arrives. 'From here' is one word.
- 'We do not need to appear at work together.' The verb 'poyavlyatsya' carries the 'sya' ending of 'show yourself'. Put 'ne nado' in front, and you have the Russian 'let's not'.
- 'You are absolutely right, Vika.' The 'perfectly' word is an adverb, and Max uses it to tease. 'Right' has the ending for a woman.
- 'It could cause damage to my reputation.' 'Nanesti uron' is 'to inflict damage', two words that go together, and 'uron' is damage. 'Reputatsii' ends in 'i' after 'my'.
- 'Max, are you sure that this is right?' A man would be 'uveren', with a bare ending. 'Pravilno', correct, grows from the same root as 'pravy', right.
- 'We will see.' It's a single word, 'posmotrim', which is 'we'll look'. Russians say 'we'll see' like this, as if looking were the answer.
- 'I do not want extra questions from the boss.' 'Lishnikh voprosov' has the ending that follows 'don't want'. 'Ot shefa' is 'from the boss', and 'ot' sits in front of the person who sends the thing.
- 'No one will find out about our secret romance.' 'Nikto ne uznaet' is 'nobody will find out', with the double no. 'Roman' means both 'romance' and 'novel', and 'tayny' means 'secret'.
- 'In this restaurant people really love gossip.' You can't say 'one gossip' in Russian, since the word is always plural. 'People' is an irregular plural, because one person looks different.
- 'I will keep quiet and just cook food.' 'Budu molchat' is 'I will be silent', the future made with 'budu' and the basic form. 'Gotovit' is 'to cook', and 'edu' is 'food' with the ending that goes with 'cook'.
- 'Then you go first, and I will go later.' One movement appears twice, first as an order and then as a future. 'Pervym' has the 'ym' ending of 'as the first one'.
- 'Good, see you in the kitchen in ten minutes.' 'Cherez' is 'through', and Russians use it for 'in' when talking about time: 'cherez desyat minut' is 'in ten minutes'. After 'ten', the word for minute takes a special plural, 'minut'.
