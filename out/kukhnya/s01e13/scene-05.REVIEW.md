## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hi to everyone.' 'Vsem' means 'to everyone'. Max says 'privet vsem', 'hi to all', and there's no 'I say'.
- 'Boss, excuse me, I simply overslept.' 'Prospal' means 'overslept', made from 'spat', to sleep, with 'pro' meaning 'through'. 'Izvinite' is the polite form for a boss.
- 'Such a thing will not happen again.' 'Povtoritsya' means 'will repeat itself', which is how Russian says 'will happen again'. 'Bolshe' is 'any more', and Max sounds quite grown up here.
- 'Today you are working in the stockroom.' 'Rabotaesh' is 'you work', and Russian uses it for 'you are working' and 'you will work'. 'Na sklade' is 'in the warehouse', with 'na' again, and it's the usual way to say it.
- 'Oversleep one more time and I will fire you.' There's no 'if' in it. 'Esche raz prospish' is 'one more time you oversleep', and the dash is the 'then'. This is how Russian makes a threat short.
- 'But boss, I am an excellent cook.' With no 'am' it's just 'I excellent cook', which makes it sound blunt. 'Otlichny' is a word you'll hear for 'excellent' and for the top mark in school.
- 'A cook must arrive at work on time.' 'Vovremya' is 'on time' and is made of 'v', in, and 'vremya', time. 'Dolzhen' is 'must', and 'prikhodit' is 'to arrive on foot'.
- 'I promise to never be late again.' 'Bolshe nikogda ne' is 'any more never not', which is a triple negative and absolutely correct. 'Opazdyvat' is 'to be late'.
- 'I have said my word.' 'Svoyo slovo' is 'my own word', neuter to match 'slovo'. It's a set phrase, a bit like saying 'my word is final'.
- 'Victoria Sergeyevna, why are you so silent?' Barinov uses the polite 'vy' with Vika here, in front of the staff. 'Molchite' is 'you are silent'. 'Tak' means 'so'.
- 'It is necessary to go to bed earlier to not oversleep.' 'Lozhitsya spat' is literally 'lay yourself to sleep', and it means 'go to bed'. 'Chtoby' means 'in order to', and 'prospat' is 'to oversleep'.
- 'Here you see, she agrees with me.' 'Vot vidite' is a pointing 'you see'. 'Soglasna' is 'in agreement', and it ends in 'a' because it is about a woman. 'So mnoy' is 'with me'.
