## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'And I have a surprise for you!' Insisting that she has a surprise for the guest shows Nastya is not backing down.
- 'Could it be that our guest finally moved out?' Checking whether it be that their guest finally moved out cuts straight through the tension between Kostya and the guest. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'No, you did not guess right at all.' Noting that the guest did not guess right at all keeps Nastya's side of the story simple and clear. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
- 'Tell me, is everything ready in the room already?' Questioning whether tell her is everything ready in the room already is how Nastya tests the guest's story.
- 'Yes, everything is ready, come on in!' Because everything is ready come, the guest does not bother softening the blow for Kostya. Russian puts the key word right up front so his emotion lands on the first beat.
- 'This is all his excellent work.' Saying that this is all his excellent work is how Nastya sets the tone with the guest. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'It is big, just like you wanted.' Once it is big just like the guest wanted, Nastya can finally tell the guest what she means.
- 'Only I wanted to buy my own television.' Adding that only he wanted to buy his own television fills in the missing piece for the guest.
- 'And I do not want anything at all from him!' Declaring that he does not want anything at all from him brings Kostya's temper right to the surface. Russian packs the whole idea of what is allowed or needed into one impersonal word at the front.
- 'I do not get it, where did you get the money?' Questioning whether he does not get it where did the guest gets the money is how Kostya tests the guest's story.
- 'They were stolen from you on the metro!' Kostya declares that they were stolen from the guest on the metro as the tension in the room peaks. Russian expresses having something as "by me is", keeping the phrase short and grounded.
- 'Or did they not steal all your money?' Here asking whether they not steal all the guest's money shows Kostya looking for the real truth. In Russian you say "by me" or "with us" rather than "I have", so the situation sits right with the person.
