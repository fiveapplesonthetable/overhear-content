## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I borrowed the money for this television.' Between the point and the money, Nastya gives the guest a quick, grounded response.
- 'I borrowed them from my dad!' Between the money and the point, Nastya delivers a sharp reaction. Russian puts the key word right up front so her emotion lands on the first beat.
- 'Son, you should have used your head first.' Noting that son Kostya should have used Kostya's head first keeps the guest's side of the story simple and clear.
- 'There is no need to swing a hammer around right away.' The guest explains that there is no need to swing a hammer around right away, keeping his voice calm and direct. Russian packs the whole idea of what is allowed or needed into one impersonal word at the front.
- 'And did you think before coming here?' Here checking whether the guest thinks before coming here shows Kostya looking for the real truth. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
- 'There is nothing but trouble from you in the house!' Because there is nothing but trouble from the guest in the house, Kostya does not bother softening the blow for the guest.
- 'Do not bring your mother into this, do you hear me?' Checking whether not bring Kostya's mother into this do Kostya hears him cuts straight through the tension between the guest and Kostya. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'Your mother was a wonderful woman.' Adding that Kostya's mother was a wonderful woman fills in the missing piece for Kostya. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
- 'Why then did she choose a man like you?' Kostya asks whether why then did she choose a man like the guest, refusing to let the point slide.
- 'Doctor, who hit you so hard like that?' Asking whether doctor who hit the guest so hard like puts the guest right on the defensive. Even in the middle of the drama, she keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
- 'My own father hit me like that.' Mentioning that his own father hit him like moves the talk in a new direction. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'Honestly speaking, he hit me for a good reason.' Kostya reminds the guest that speaking he hit him for a good reason before the moment passes.
