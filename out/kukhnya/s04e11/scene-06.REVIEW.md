## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Irochka, may I have you for a second?' Leaving out the verb, 'possible you for a second?' is a polite way to call a server over.
- 'Why do you not correct me?' Squinting at her badge, Nagiyev realizes her name is not Ira.
- 'You are Nastya, after all!' The emphatic particle acknowledges her real name.
- 'I have already gotten tired of correcting you.' After 'tired', Russian uses the imperfective infinitive 'to correct'.
- 'But I am pleased that you remembered.' Literally 'to me it is pleasant', using the dative pronoun.
- 'Do not get smart, I read it on your badge.' The colloquial verb means 'to act smart' or 'be a smart-aleck'.
- 'Call our sommelier over to us.' Like 'kino', the French loanword 'sommelier' is indeclinable in Russian.
- 'It is time for us to drink some good wine.' The partitive genitive 'of good wine' means 'some good wine'.
- 'Our sommelier is not here today.' Because he is absent, 'sommelier' is in the genitive case functionally before 'is not'.
- 'What do you mean not here, where is he?' Nagiyev frowns at the bad timing.
- 'He did not come in to work again.' Going in to work for a shift is expressed as 'exiting onto work'.
- 'Then bring us the best bottle yourself.' The feminine pronoun 'sama' stresses that Nastya should bring it herself.
- 'An excellent choice, I will bring it right away!' Nastya uses the classic sommelier phrase to flatter the boss.
