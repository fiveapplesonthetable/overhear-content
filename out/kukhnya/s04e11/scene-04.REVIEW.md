## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Oh, what just happened?' The lights go pitch-black right in the middle of dinner service.
- 'I just pressed a button back there.' The verb 'to press' takes the preposition 'on' plus the accusative case.
- 'And everything suddenly switched itself off.' The reflexive verb means the power shut off on its own.
- 'You are poking your fingers where you should not!' Nagiyev scolds the staff using the instrumental plural 'with fingers'.
- 'Make sure there is light in here in half an hour!' Starting a sentence with 'so that' plus a past-tense verb gives a stern boss's ultimatum.
- 'Go on quickly, I told you!' Nagiyev snaps his fingers to send the staff running.
- 'And you owe a fine for this!' Literally 'from you a fine', this short phrase is how a boss announces a penalty.
- 'And you, create an atmosphere for us.' Turning to Denis at the piano, Nagiyev asks for romantic music in the candlelight.
- 'Play a beautiful melody for us.' The perfective command asks the pianist to strike up a tune.
- 'All right, I will play for you right now.' Denis sits down at the keys and begins to play.
- 'That is definitely a short circuit.' In Russian, a 'short circuit' is literally a 'short closing'.
- 'Quickly bring candles to the tables!' The plural command 'carry/bring' takes the accusative destination 'onto the tables'.
