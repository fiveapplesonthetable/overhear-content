## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Chef, maybe that is enough already, huh?' The impersonal verb 'it suffices' is the everyday way to say 'that's enough!'.
- 'I do not understand what you are trying to achieve.' The reflexive verb 'to strive for / try to achieve' governs the genitive case ('of what').
- 'This does not bring you any pleasure, after all.' Because the verb is negated, 'pleasure' takes the genitive singular ending '-ya'.
- 'This is red wine, Nastya.' Taking a sip from the glass, Kostya states the only obvious fact he knows.
- 'And is that all that you know?' Nastya stares at him in dismay.
- 'I said as much as you poured.' The correlative pair 'how much... that much...' jokes about the tiny tasting pour.
- 'You have to name the year and the country.' A real sommelier has to identify the vintage and country of origin.
- 'Nastya, you started all this in vain.' The short adverb 'zrya' means 'in vain' or 'for nothing'.
- 'I will mess up in front of him again.' Kostya predicts disaster at the evening test.
- 'And Nagiyev will fine me again.' The perfective future verb means 'will fine'.
- 'In your opinion, do I think about our family in vain?' The adverb 'po-tvoyemu' means 'in your opinion' or 'according to you'.
- 'Well, that is not at all what I meant to say.' Literally 'not this I wanted to say', the standard Russian way to say 'that's not what I meant'.
