## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'You must bring your appearance closer to the person's.' The audiobook uses formal vocabulary with the preposition 'to' plus the dative case.
- 'Look who has returned from Turkey!' Country names in '-iya' take the genitive ending '-ii' after the preposition 'from'.
- 'How was your vacation, what did you bring me?' In Russian, asking 'how did you rest?' is the standard way to ask 'how was your vacation?'.
- 'Hey, what is that on your face?' Barinov stares in disbelief at Lyova's upper lip.
- 'What, are they really real?' After tugging on Lyova's moustache, Barinov realizes it is not glued on.
- 'Why did you grow a moustache, Lyova?' When talking about hair, a beard, or a moustache, the verb 'to let go' means 'to let it grow out'.
- 'Mom says a moustache suits me very well.' When clothes or hairstyles look good on someone, Russian says they 'go/walk' to that person in the dative.
- 'And have you finished your foolish war?' Lyova asks whether Barinov and German have finally made peace.
- 'Of course, we finished it a long time ago.' Barinov lies without blinking using the adverb 'long ago'.
- 'Are you deceiving me again, chef?' Opening the fridge to find a prank trap, Lyova catches Barinov red-handed.
- 'People called me from here every day.' The accusative time phrase 'every day' needs no preposition.
- 'You have not finished anything at all!' Fronting 'nothing' puts strong emphasis on Lyova's disappointment.
- 'Chef, please hear me out.' Lyova pleads with his boss not to stoop to petty pranks.
- 'You should not behave like that.' Combining 'not needed' with 'to lead oneself' means 'one shouldn't behave that way'.
