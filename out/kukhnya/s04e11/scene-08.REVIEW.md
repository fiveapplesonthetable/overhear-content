## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Here at last the light has come back on!' When the electricity returns, the dining room lights up brightly.
- 'Well then, shall we go to my place now?' Nagiyev leans across the table expecting his romantic evening to continue.
- 'No, I still have important business.' In Russian, 'dela' in the plural means 'business', 'errands', or 'things to do'.
- 'And what about the casting for the show?' The phrase 'and how about...?' brings up the real reason she agreed to dinner.
- 'You have come to the wrong person for that.' Literally 'that for you is not to me', an idiomatic way to say 'that's not my department'.
- 'I am just the host on there!' The present active participle from 'to lead' acts as the noun for 'TV host'.
- 'Where are you going, wait!' His date throws her napkin down and marches out of the restaurant.
- 'Thank you, you cheered an old man up.' Turning to Denis at the piano, Nagiyev praises his soulful music.
- 'Thank you, I tried very hard.' Denis stands up respectfully by the piano.
- 'Who are you at our place, anyway?' Nagiyev does not even recognize his own kitchen cook out of uniform.
- 'I work here as a cook.' Professions after 'work as' take the instrumental case ('povarom').
- 'You will also be a musician at our place.' After the future of 'to be', the new job title 'musician' also takes the instrumental case.
- 'I will put you on half-time pay.' In Russian labor terms, 'stavka' is a full-time salary rate, so 'polstavki' is a half-time position.
- 'Thank you very much, Dmitry Vladimirovich!' Denis beams at earning a second paycheck playing the piano he loves.
