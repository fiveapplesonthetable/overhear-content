## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Chef, what are you doing here?' Lyova cuts in quietly in this beat and brings in "delayete" ("are doing", from "delat") as the key Russian word here.
- 'A fine aroma, isn't it?' Addressing the smell in the exchange, Barinov keeps normal statement word order here, turning the line into a question for Lyova purely through rising pitch.
- 'But the restaurant dining room is on the other side!' Lyova follows up firmly on the spot, leaving no doubt about where he stands.
- 'What are you doing all this for?' Lyova counters decisively before Barinov and uses "delayete" ("are doing", from "delat") to make the point stick.
- 'Relax, Lyova, everything is great.' Jumping in plainly beside Lyova, Barinov leans on "otlichno" ("great, excellent"), which carries the whole predicate in Russian without needing "it is".
- 'It is a real French restaurant, after all.' Barinov proceeds honestly facing Lyova, refusing to let the moment slip past.
- 'I am creating the atmosphere of France for them.' Talking about the atmosphere on the spot, Barinov puts the weight on "atmosferu" ("atmosphere", from "atmosfera") as the exchange unfolds.
- 'Choose an ironic tone in conversation.' Emphasizing an ironic tone in the exchange, Lyova centers the remark on "ironichnyy" ("ironic") without any extra fuss.
- 'Chef, you are behaving like a child.' Lyova jumps in decisively without holding back, bringing things back to earth after the buildup.
- 'Well, and what will you do next?' Lyova answers plainly in this beat and leans on "sdelayete" ("will do", a form of "sdelat") to give the remark its edge.
- 'I do not know myself yet.' Barinov politely drops this casual farewell while facing Lyova using "poka" ("bye") — the quick everyday word for "bye for now" between people on close terms.
- 'Maybe you will release cockroaches in there too?' Lyova challenges skeptically while looking straight at Barinov here.
- 'Lyova, you got so much smarter with that moustache!' Discussing the moustache in front of Lyova, Barinov brings in "usami" ("moustache") to make the meaning unmistakable.
- 'That is simply a brilliant idea!' Noting how excellent things are in this beat, Barinov uses the little word "prosto" ("simply, just") to make the point sound effortless.
