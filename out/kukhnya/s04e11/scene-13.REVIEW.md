## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'It seems I have found us a new sommelier.' Nastya uses the feminine past tense 'found' to pitch her idea.
- 'He does not drink and does not smoke.' Two present-tense verbs contrast Kostya with the fired alcoholic sommelier.
- 'Handsome and a very good family man.' The noun for 'family man' is built directly from the word for 'family'.
- 'It is our bartender Kostya.' Nastya reveals her candidate with a bright smile.
- 'Are you pitching your own husband to me?' Nagiyev raises an eyebrow at the obvious family nepotism.
- 'He smashed three bottles today!' Nagiyev reminds her of the crash at the bar minutes ago.
- 'But at his own expense, after all!' The idiom 'at one's own expense' uses the preposition 'za' plus the accusative.
- 'You are a kind and generous person, after all.' Nastya switches to shameless flattery.
- 'And the best TV host in the whole country!' The genitive singular 'of the country' follows the superlative 'best host'.
- 'Fine, be ready by evening.' Flattery works like magic on Nagiyev, who agrees to test Kostya 'toward evening'.
- 'I will set up a wine tasting for you.' The perfective future verb means 'I will arrange/stage a tasting'.
- 'Nastya, I do not know anything about wines!' The reflexive verb with 'in' plus the prepositional plural means to be knowledgeable about a subject.
- 'The main thing is to say 'excellent choice' on time.' The adverb 'on time' stresses timing over actual wine expertise.
- 'Your wife and child are sure that you will succeed.' The plural short-form adjective 'confident/sure' leaves Kostya no way out.
