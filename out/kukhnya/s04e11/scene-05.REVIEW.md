## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Oh, you are standing next to each other and not fighting.' The reflexive verb 'fighting each other' takes the second-person plural ending.
- 'Could it be you decided to make a truce?' The question particle at the start expresses hopeful disbelief: 'can it really be that...?'.
- 'He deprived the entire restaurant of electricity!' The verb 'to deprive' takes an accusative person/place and a genitive thing deprived ('of electricity').
- 'Is that really an act of truce?' The noun 'truce' is built from the roots 'between' and 'peace'.
- 'Vitya, are you in your right mind at all?' Vika turns on Viktor in exasperation.
- 'You will not prove a thing!' Smirking, Barinov uses the perfective future of 'to prove'.
- 'What happened in the kitchen?' A cook runs out from Arcobaleno shouting for the boss.
- 'There is a live snake in your kitchen!' German's counter-prank is revealed: a live snake slithering across Barinov's floor.
- 'There you go, an eye for an eye.' The biblical proverb uses the old poetic Slavic word for 'eye'.
- 'Well, you will pay for this yet!' Literally 'you will dance by me yet', this idiom means 'I'll make you sorry for this!'.
- 'Let's go back into our dining room.' German strolls away satisfied with his revenge.
- 'I will not leave it like this!' Barinov vows to get even using the perfective future of 'to leave'.
