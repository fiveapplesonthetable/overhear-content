## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Oh, you are standing next to each other and not fighting.' Admitting that Barinov is standing next to each other and not fighting shows Vika being honest for a change. Notice how she sticks to the formal "you", keeping a professional line between them.
- 'Could it be you decided to make a truce?' Here checking whether it be Barinov decided to make a truce shows Vika looking for the real truth. In Russian, she does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'He deprived the entire restaurant of electricity!' With the situation and plan at stake, German reacts without holding back.
- 'Is that really an act of truce?' Checking whether that really an act of truce cuts straight through the tension between German and Barinov. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'Vitya, are you in your right mind at all?' By asking whether Barinov in Barinov's right mind at all, Vika presses Barinov for a straight answer. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
- 'You will not prove a thing!' Saying that German will not prove a thing is how Barinov takes charge of the moment.
- 'What happened in the kitchen?' Around the shift and conversation, Barinov wants to know where German really stands. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'There is a live snake in your kitchen!' Because there is a live snake in Barinov's kitchen, Vika does not bother softening the blow for Barinov. In Russian you say "by me" or "with us" rather than "I have", so the situation sits right with the person.
- 'There you go, an eye for an eye.' Because there Barinov goes an eye for an eye, German treats the matter as settled.
- 'Well, you will pay for this yet!' Once German will pay for this yet, Barinov makes sure German hears every word. Russian expresses having something as "by me is", keeping the phrase short and grounded.
- 'Let's go back into our dining room.' Proposing that they go back into their dining room is how German lays out the situation for Barinov.
- 'I will not leave it like this!' Barinov insists that he will not leave it like this, leaving German no room to argue.
