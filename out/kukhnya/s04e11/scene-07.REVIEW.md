## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'By copying facial expressions, you gain control.' The verbal adverb ending in '-ya' means 'while copying' or 'by copying'.
- 'Can you imagine what German thought up?' Asking 'do you imagine?' is the natural spoken way to say 'can you believe...?'.
- 'He planted a live snake on us!' The prefix 'pod-' on 'threw' means to secretly plant or slip something in.
- 'What should we do to him?' In Russian, doing something 'to him' uses the preposition 'with' plus the instrumental case.
- 'Do nothing at all, chef.' Using the infinitive echoes the exact grammar of Barinov's question.
- 'He will be waiting for a dirty trick from you.' The verb 'to wait' takes the genitive case when waiting for a general thing like trouble.
- 'No, that is too simple for him.' Barinov rejects the peaceful advice with the degree adverb 'too'.
- 'Not simple at all, dear chef.' Lyova strokes his moustache in the exact same rhythm as Barinov.
- 'Why are you doing that right now?' Barinov notices that Lyova is mirroring his every move.
- 'And what am I doing?' Lyova blinks innocently while copying Barinov's stance.
- 'You are getting on my nerves really badly!' The transitive verb means 'you are making me nervous'.
- 'Well, do not get so worked up, chef.' Notice the pair: 'nervirovat' means to make someone else nervous, while 'nervnichat' means to feel nervous yourself.
- 'What, are you mocking me?' The verb 'to mock/taunt' governs the preposition 'nad' ('over') plus the instrumental case.
- 'Just get out of here already!' Losing his temper completely, Barinov chases his sous-chef out of the office.
