## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I do not know how to approach him to make peace.' Kostya tells Ilya that he does not know how to approach him to make peace without dressing it up.
- 'Maybe I should invite him out for a beer, huh?' Wondering if maybe he should invite him out for a beer huh shows Ilya caught off guard. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'Well, maybe.' Over the shift and the surprise, Kostya answers Ilya without a second thought.
- 'Wait, do you seriously want to make peace with him?' Here asking whether Kostya seriously want to make peace with him shows Ilya looking for the real truth. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
- 'Well yeah, that is why I am asking you.' Because yeah that is why he is asking Ilya, Kostya treats the matter as settled.
- 'Listen, what does he like in general?' Asking what he like in general cuts straight through the tension between Ilya and Kostya.
- 'Oh, he likes all kinds of things.' Kostya mentions that he likes all kinds of things so Ilya sees what is at stake.
- 'Actually, this will be even better.' Claiming that this will be even better sounds confident, even if Kostya has doubts.
- 'He is going to love this.' Being going to love this is how Kostya lays out the situation for Ilya.
- 'You are welcome.' Ilya explains that Kostya is welcome, keeping his voice calm and direct.
- 'Thanks, Kostyan.' With the conversation and peace in play, Kostya keeps this short reply brisk and direct.
- 'Alright, I am off.' Noting that Alright he is off keeps Ilya's side of the story simple and clear.
- 'Just a little token.' Around the conversation and peace, Kostya sets a calm, everyday tone with Ilya. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
