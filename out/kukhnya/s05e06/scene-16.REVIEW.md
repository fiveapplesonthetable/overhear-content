## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Little bunny, where are you going?' Pointing to the bunny with Max, Nagiyev reaches for the Russian word "zaychonok" ("little bunny") right at the heart of the line.
- 'Far away from you and your children!' Max opens up quietly with Nagiyev, refusing to let the moment slip past.
- 'Just listen to me, sweetheart!' Nagiyev speaks up plainly without a pause, bringing things back to earth after the buildup.
- 'What was that just now?' Holding firm openly to Vika, Nagiyev puts the question word "chto" right at the start so Vika hears the focus immediately.
- 'Dima, forgive me.' Vika plainly gives this personal apology as they meet using "prosti" ("forgive me") — the close, informal way to say "forgive me" or "I'm sorry" to someone dear.
- 'I told Max that this is your child.' Vika responds promptly around Max, steering the talk right where she wants it.
- 'What did you do?' Nagiyev jumps in frankly on the spot and puts the weight on "sdelala" ("have done, did", from "sdelat") as the exchange unfolds.
- 'There was nothing between you and me!' Jumping in firmly with Vika, Nagiyev reaches for "u nas" ("by us, at our place") to talk about what they share.
- 'I just wanted him to leave me alone.' Vika responds honestly without a pause, without trying to dress up how she really feels.
- 'How was there nothing at all?' Weighing in evenly before Vika, Nagiyev pairs the negative word with "ne" ("not"), since Russian naturally doubles its negatives for emphasis.
- 'I am very sorry.' Holding firm naturally beside Nagiyev, Vika leans on "zhal" ("it is a pity"), which carries the whole predicate in Russian without needing "it is".
- 'Great, now there will not be a wedding either.' Nagiyev pushes back bluntly around Vika, making his position completely clear.
