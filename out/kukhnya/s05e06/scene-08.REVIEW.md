## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'You should quit cards, you do not know how to play.' Barinov speaks up evenly toward Eleonora, steering the talk right where he wants it.
- 'Practice with Katya here.' Barinov proceeds earnestly across the room, to clear the air between them once and for all.
- 'Take your hand off!' Turning to the hand to Eleonora, Barinov centers the thought on "ruku" ("hand", from "ruka") without wasting a word.
- 'How could you drag our child to an underground casino?' Bringing up her child in this beat, Eleonora leans on "mozhno" ("it is possible, one may"), which carries the whole predicate in Russian without needing "it is".
- 'I wanted to save her from depression.' Barinov responds evenly with Eleonora, bringing things back to earth after the buildup.
- 'It perks you up.' Barinov replies candidly at once and chooses "bodrit" ("invigorates") so Eleonora gets the point right away.
- 'Hello, come on in.' Eleonora naturally extends this formal greeting with Barinov using "zdravstvuyte" ("hello") — the polite Russian greeting that literally wishes the other person good health.
- 'Who is this now?' Barinov inquires curiously if there is more going on behind the scenes.
- 'Doctor of psychological sciences.' Discussing the doctor beside Barinov, the doctor chooses "psikhologicheskikh" ("psychological", from the root "psikhologicheskiy") so Barinov hears the exact meaning.
- 'Sweetheart, please talk with the doctor, he will help you.' Eleonora speaks up candidly with a shrug, keeping the focus on what actually matters here.
- 'My daughter does not need a doctor.' Barinov chimes in calmly on the spot, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'You certainly need him more, but you can wait.' Eleonora jumps in promptly during the talk and chooses "poterpish" ("will wait, endure", from the root "poterpet") so Barinov hears the exact meaning.
