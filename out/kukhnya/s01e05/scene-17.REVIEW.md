## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Kostya, wait, I cannot do this.' "Podozhdi" is 'wait' for someone you call "ty". "Ya tak ne mogu", 'I so not can', means 'I can't do it like this'.
- 'What happened, Nastya, what is wrong?' "Chto sluchilos" is the standard 'what happened?'. "Chto ne tak" is literally 'what not so', with no 'is' at all.
- 'I wanted to get back at Maxim through you.' Nastya uses Max's full name, Maksim, which sounds colder. "Otomstit", 'to take revenge', puts 'to' on the person you're getting back at: "otomstit Maksimu".
- 'Max fully deserves a good lesson, you know.' "Zasluzhivaet", 'deserves', takes the genitive here, so it's "khoroshego uroka". "Znaesh" at the end works like the English 'you know'.
- 'I cannot accept such a sacrifice.' "Zhertva" means both 'sacrifice' and 'victim', and the context decides. Here it's "zhertvu", with -u, as the thing she won't accept.
- 'And what are we to do now?' "Chto zhe nam delat" is literally 'what then to us to do', with "nam", 'to us', in place of 'we'. The little "zhe" adds a note of 'so then'.
- 'Let us simply tell everyone about us.' "Davay", from 'give', turns what comes next into 'let's': "davay skazhem". "Vsem", 'to everyone', comes from "vse", 'all', and pro "nas" means 'about us'.
- 'And in reality there is nothing?' "Na samom dele", literally 'on the very deed', means 'actually' or 'in reality'. "Nichego net" is 'there is nothing'.
- 'Yes, this will be only our secret.' "Sekret" sounds almost English. "Budet", 'will be', fills the slot that 'is' leaves empty in the present.
- 'Fine, let Max be slightly jealous.' "Pust", 'let', is 'let', like in 'let him'. "Porevnuet" has "po-" on the front, which here means doing something for a little while.
- 'Kostya, thank you, you are simply the best!' "Klassnyy", 'great', is casual and built on "klass". "Samyy klassnyy" turns it into 'the very best'.
- 'For you I am ready for anything.' "Dlya tebya", 'for you', goes first for emphasis. "Gotov na vsyo", with "na" and the accusative, is 'ready for anything'.
- 'My order is ready, I'll run back to work!' "Gotov" here describes "zakaz", a masculine noun, so it has the same bare form Kostya used about himself. "Pobegu", 'I'll run', is the perfective of "bezhat".
