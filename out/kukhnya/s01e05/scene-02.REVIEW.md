## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Nastya, is everything serious between you and Max?' Russian says 'at you with Max everything serious', with no 'is' at all. "U vas" is plural here, because Sasha means the two of them.
- 'Yes, yesterday we even kissed.' The same kissing verb, but now it's "potselovalis", with "po-" in front. That makes it one kiss, done and finished, where Kostya's "tselovalis" sounded more like an ongoing activity.
- 'Really? And how did that happen?' "Pravda" literally means 'truth', and said with a rising voice it's 'really?'. "Proizoshlo" ends in -o to agree with "eto", 'it'.
- 'It was incredibly romantic!' "Neveroyatno" is "ne", 'not', in front of "veroyatno", 'probably', so: improbably, unbelievably. "Romantichno" you can guess.
- 'Did you really fall in love with him?' "Neuzheli" is a surprised 'can it really be?'. In Russian you fall in love 'into' someone: "vlyubilas v nego", and the -las tells you it's a woman who fell.
- 'He is such a kind and attentive guy.' "Takoy" before a description means 'such a'. "Dobryy" and "vnimatelnyy" both end in -yy, the usual ending when the adjective describes a man, here "paren", 'guy'.
- 'After all, you have only known each other one week.' "Ved", tucked in the middle, is 'after all', a gentle 'come on'. "Nedelya", 'week', becomes "odnu nedelyu" when it says how long.
- 'True love knows no time.' It sounds like a song lyric, and Nastya means it. After "ne znaet", 'doesn't know', the word for 'time' shifts from "vremya" to "vremeni".
- 'Imagine what beautiful children we will have!' "Predstavlyaesh" is literally 'do you imagine?', used like 'just imagine!'. 'We will have' is "u nas budut", 'at us will be'.
- 'Are you already thinking about children?!' "Uzhe", 'already', carries all of Sasha's shock. After "o", 'about', "deti", 'children', turns into "detyakh".
- 'Of course, we will create a wonderful family.' "Konechno" is spelled with a ch but said 'koneshno'. "Semya", 'family', becomes "semyu" because it's the thing they'll create.
- 'And does Max know about your plans?' The one question Nastya has dodged. After "o", both words take plural endings: "o tvoikh planakh".
- 'Soon we will definitely discuss everything.' "Obsudim" is 'we will discuss', with the same -im as "sozdadim" in her last line. "Obyazatelno", 'definitely', is a word Russians say all the time to promise something.
