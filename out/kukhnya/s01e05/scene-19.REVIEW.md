## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Sasha, well, did you tell Max?' The two words 'well' and 'what' are a casual 'so?'. 'Told' ends in 'la' because Sasha is a woman, and 'to Max' needs the ending 'u'.
- 'Yes, I said that I forgot him.' Both 'said' and 'forgot' end in 'la', because Sasha is speaking. Russian doesn't need the word 'that' to be different from 'what'.
- 'And how did he react to that?' 'React' is almost the English word. 'To that' is two tiny words.
- 'He was very surprised and upset.' Both feeling words end in 'sya' and 'l', which means 'got surprised' and 'got upset'. The words 'very' and 'strongly' stand together for 'really'.
- 'I even felt a little sorry for him.' 'Sorry' is 'pity', and Russian says 'to me pity him became'. 'Even' sits at the front.
- 'Just do not tell anyone the truth.' 'Don't tell' is 'not' and then the command. The truth takes an 'u' ending because it's what's being told.
- 'Of course, no one will find out anything.' 'No one nothing not will find out' is a three-part negative, and Russian wants every part.
- 'Thank you, you are a true loyal friend!' 'True' and 'loyal' are both describing words, and they end in the same way as 'friend'. 'True' also means 'real'.
- 'Listen, maybe we should go somewhere?' 'Maybe' here is two words, a casual 'or perhaps'. 'Somewhere' is 'where' with 'nibud' on the end, which means 'anywhere at all'.
- 'Where exactly do you suggest going?' 'where to' is the one for movement. 'Exactly' sits beside it, and 'suggest' is followed by 'to go'.
- 'Let us go to the movies together.' 'cinema' is the same short word as the one for 'film', and it never changes. 'Together' is a handy one-word adverb.
- 'This is needed for the believability of our plan.' The long word is made from 'truth' and 'like', so it is 'truth-likeness'. That is how Russians say 'believability'.
- 'Great idea, let us go tonight!' 'This evening' is two words, with the 'om' ending on 'evening'. 'Let's go' ends in 'yom'.
- 'Agreed, then let us meet after work.' The first word ends in 'sya' and means 'we've reached an agreement'. 'After work' is the word 'after' and the word for work with a different end.
