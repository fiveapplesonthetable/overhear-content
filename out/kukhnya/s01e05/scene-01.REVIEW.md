## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Kostya, hi. Why are you angry?' "Pochemu" is the everyday 'why'. "Serdishsya" is the 'you' form of the verb "serditsya", 'to be angry', and the -sya on the end belongs to the verb, so it stays on in every form.
- 'Back off, or I will break your nose!' "Otvali" is a rude 'get lost', not something to say to your boss. Then Russian says 'I will break to-you the nose': "tebe", 'to you', does the work of 'your'.
- 'What for? What did I do to you?' "Za chto", two short words, is what you say when you're blamed and can't see why. The second question has the same "tebe" in the middle: "chto ya tebe sdelal".
- 'Yesterday you kissed my girl!' In Russian you kiss 'with' someone: "tselovalsya s moey devushkoy", and after "s" the words take the endings -oy. "Devushka" covers both 'young woman' and 'girlfriend'.
- 'It was a completely accidental misunderstanding.' Max goes for long, official-sounding words. "Sluchaynoe" comes from "sluchay", 'a chance' or 'an occasion', and "nedorazumenie" is the standard word for a misunderstanding.
- 'You were kissing right in the restaurant!' Now the 'you' is "vy", Max and Nastya together, and the verb turns plural: "tselovalis". "Pryamo" means 'right' or 'straight', and "v restorane" takes -e because it says where.
- 'I wanted to sleep all day yesterday.' "Ves den", 'the whole day', needs no little word in front, just as in English. "Khotel" is the man's form of 'wanted'; Nastya would say "khotela".
- 'By the way, technically she is not yours yet.' "Kstati" is 'by the way', perfect for slipping in an awkward point. "Poka" here means 'yet' or 'for now', and the same word is also an everyday 'bye'.
- 'For me that does not matter.' Word for word: 'for me this not has meaning'. "Dlya menya" means 'for me', and "ne imeet znacheniya" is a ready-made way to say something doesn't matter.
- 'There is nothing between me and Nastya!' After "mezhdu", 'between', both names take instrumental endings: "mezhdu mnoy i Nastey". "Nichego net" uses two negatives, and in Russian that's correct.
- 'You betrayed our friendship, Max.' "Druzhba", 'friendship', is built on "drug", 'friend'. Here it's "druzhbu", with -u, because it's the thing being betrayed.
- 'I will prove that she means nothing to me.' "Dokazhu" is 'I will prove'. Then she is "bezrazlichna mne", 'indifferent to me', with "bez", 'without', stuck on the front.
- 'We will see how you prove it.' "Posmotrim", 'we'll see', is what you say when you don't believe a word. Max's "dokazhu" comes back as "dokazhesh", the form for 'you'.
