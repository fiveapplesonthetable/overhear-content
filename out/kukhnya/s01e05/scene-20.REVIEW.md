## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Fedya, please help me with the orders.' "Pozhaluysta", 'please', also means 'you're welcome'. After "s", 'with', the plural takes -ami: "s zakazami".
- 'What are you saying? I can't hear well.' "Slyshu" is from "slyshat", 'to hear', which is different from "slushat", 'to listen'. "Plokho", 'badly', is the opposite of "khorosho".
- 'I am saying that I ask for forgiveness.' "Proshu proshcheniya", literally 'I ask for forgiveness', is a more formal apology than "izvini". "Proshchenie" becomes "proshcheniya" after "proshu".
- 'Colleagues, could you be quieter in the kitchen, please?' "Mozhno", 'may one', is the all-purpose way to ask permission. "Potishe", with "po-", means 'a bit quieter'.
- 'Max wants to tell us something important.' "Chto-to vazhnoe" is 'something important', and "vazhnoe" takes -oe to match "chto-to". Fedya says it loudly so the whole kitchen will listen.
- 'Fedya, forgive me for everything.' "Izvini" is the "ty" command, for a friend or an equal; to a boss it would be "izvinite". "Za vsyo", 'for everything', has "za", the word you apologise 'for' with.
- 'You're an excellent, experienced cook, and I'm not.' "Opytnyy" comes from "opyt", 'experience'. "A ya net", 'and I not', gets by without any 'am'.
- 'Help me quickly prepare these dishes.' "Pomogi mne" is literally 'help to me', and Russian always helps 'to' someone. "Prigotovit", 'to prepare', comes straight after with no 'to' word.
- 'I heard everything very well.' "Uslyshal" means 'I heard' as in 'I caught it', the one-off partner of "slyshat". It answers his own "plokho slyshu" from a moment ago.
- 'But I do not believe you at all.' "Verit" takes the dative, so it's 'I believe to you': "tebe ne veryu". "Sovsem ne" means 'not at all'.
- 'In your words there is no real sincerity.' "Net", 'there is no', puts what's missing into the genitive, so it's "iskrennosti". "V tvoikh slovakh", 'in your words', takes the -akh ending for a place.
- 'Come on, repeat everything once more louder!' "Razok" is a cosy form of "raz", 'one time'. "Gromche", 'louder', is the comparative of "gromko".
- 'I will not repeat anything anymore!' "Bolshe ne" means 'not anymore'. "Ne budu povtoryat" is 'I won't be repeating', with "budu", 'I will be', in front.
- 'Alright, calm down, I will help now.' "Ladno", 'okay then', settles things. "Uspokoysya", 'calm down', ends in -sya, so it's literally 'calm yourself'.
