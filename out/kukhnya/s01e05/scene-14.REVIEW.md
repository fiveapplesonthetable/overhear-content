## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Kostya, hi, are you very busy right now?' "Zanyat" is 'busy' for a man; for a woman it's "zanyata". "Silno", literally 'strongly', often just means 'very'.
- 'Hi, Nastya, no, I am completely free.' "Sovsem", 'completely', is the word that made Max's "sovsem ne". "Svoboden", 'free', is the man's form.
- 'Then come with me into the quiet room.' "Poydyom" means 'let's go', with the 'we' ending -yom. "V tikhuyu komnatu" is a direction, so both words end in -u.
- 'Wait a second, where exactly are we going?' "Kuda" asks 'where to', for movement. If you just want to know where something is, you say "gde".
- 'I wanted to ask you seriously for a long time.' "Davno khotela", 'long wanted', covers 'I've been wanting for a long time'. "Khotela" has the woman's -a.
- 'I will listen to you with pleasure now.' "S udovolstviem", 'with pleasure', is a polite yes to any offer. "Vyslushayu" is 'hear out', listening all the way through.
- 'Do you want us to start dating?' "Khochesh", "chtoby my nachali" is 'you want so-that we started', with "nachali" in the past after "chtoby". "Vstrechatsya" is the dating verb once more.
- 'Nastya, I just got a little confused.' "Rasteryalsya" contains "teryat", 'to lose', with -sya, so roughly 'I lost myself'. "Prosto nemnogo", 'just a little', softens it.
- 'Or do you not want this at all?' After "ne khochesh", "eto" changes to "etogo". It's the same shift that turned "shutka" into "shutki" after 'didn't understand'.
- 'Of course I want to, I like you very much!' "Khochu" alone means 'I want to'. Then "ty mne nravishsya", 'you to me are pleasing', is a plain 'I like you'.
- 'I am very glad to hear this from you.' "Rada" is the woman's 'glad'; Kostya would say "rad". "Ot tebya", 'from you', uses "ot", which takes the 'of' form.
- 'Now it is time for us to return to the guests.' "Pora", 'it's time', is followed by a plain verb. "K gostyam", 'to the guests', is "k" with a plural ending -am.
