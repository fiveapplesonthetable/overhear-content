## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Nastya, stop being so deeply upset!' The opening word is a command meaning 'stop', and the last one ends in 'sya', which makes it really 'stop upsetting yourself'. Sasha adds 'so strongly' in the middle.
- 'It hurts me very much, he broke my heart.' Russian puts the feeling outside you: 'to me painful'. 'broke' is the one you'd use for smashing a glass, and the heart is what got smashed.
- 'Do you know how many more guys you will still have?' The question word for 'how many' opens the second half. 'You will have' comes out as 'at you will be', which is how Russian builds 'have'.
- 'I do not need others, I liked Max.' Both halves put Nastya in the 'to me' slot: 'to me not needed others' and 'to me was pleasing Max'. So in Russian, Max is the one doing the pleasing.
- 'Is it really worth falling apart over every one?' The line opens with a surprised 'really?', and you'll hear it a lot. The last long word, ending in 'sya', is literally 'to kill yourself', as in 'to torment yourself'.
- 'I am afraid that they will deceive me again.' 'they will deceive' has no one named, so it sounds like 'people will'. 'Again' is a handy small word.
- 'You are kind, beautiful, and very smart.' Three describing words in a row, each ending in 'aya' because they describe a woman. The last one comes from 'mind'.
- 'Then why did he forget me so quickly?' 'Why' is a word you can put at the front of any complaint. 'Forgot' ends in a consonant because it's about Max.
- 'Because he is a foolish and fickle guy.' 'because' is pieced together from 'why', so the question and the answer sound alike. 'Fickle' comes from 'wind', so he blows with it.
- 'I want him to understand his mistake.' Russian doesn't say 'I want him to understand'. It says 'I want so-that he understood', with the past form of the word after a small linking word. 'His own' is a different word from plain 'his'.
- 'Now those are the right thoughts, my friend!' The word for a woman's friend is not the one for a man's friend, and Sasha uses the female one. The first two words work like 'now we're talking'.
- 'Thank you so much for the warm support.' Russian can put 'to you' after 'thanks' to make it personal. 'Huge' is the usual phrase for saying 'big thanks', and 'warm' works for kind help just as in English.
