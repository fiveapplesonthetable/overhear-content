## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Nastya, stop being so deeply upset!' "Perestan", 'stop', is followed by the 'to' form, here "rasstraivatsya", 'to get upset'. Its -sya makes it 'stop upsetting yourself'.
- 'It hurts me very much, he broke my heart.' "Mne bolno" is 'to me painful'. "Razbil" is the verb for smashing a glass, and like Kostya's nose, it's "mne serdtse", 'to me the heart'.
- 'Do you know how many more guys you will still have?' After "skolko", 'how many', "paren" becomes "parney". "U tebya budet" is 'at you will be', the future of 'you have'.
- 'I do not need others, I liked Max.' Both halves start with "mne", 'to me'. "Mne nravilsya" Maks is literally 'to me was pleasing Max', so in Russian it's Max who does the pleasing.
- 'Is it really worth falling apart over every one?' "Stoit" means 'it's worth'. "Ubivatsya" is literally 'to kill yourself', but people use it for grieving too hard.
- 'I am afraid that they will deceive me again.' "Boyus", 'I'm afraid', also ends in -s, a short -sya. "Obmanut" has no named subject, which makes it 'people will lie to me'.
- 'You are kind, beautiful, and very smart.' All three adjectives end in -aya, because they describe a woman. "Umnaya", 'smart', comes from um, 'mind'.
- 'Then why did he forget me so quickly?' "Tak bystro" is 'so quickly'. "Zabyl" ends in a consonant because Max did the forgetting.
- 'Because he is a foolish and fickle guy.' "Pochemu" asks 'why' and "potomu chto" answers 'because', and the two sound alike. "Vetrenyy", 'fickle', comes from "veter", 'wind'.
- 'I want him to understand his mistake.' Russian says 'I want so-that he understood': "khochu", "chtoby on ponyal". After "chtoby" the verb goes into the past form.
- 'Now those are the right thoughts, my friend!' "Vot eto uzhe" works like 'now that's more like it'. "Podruga" is a female friend; a male friend is "drug".
- 'Thank you so much for the warm support.' "Spasibo tebe", 'thanks to you', makes it personal. "Ogromnoe", 'huge', is how you turn "spasibo" into 'thanks so much'.
