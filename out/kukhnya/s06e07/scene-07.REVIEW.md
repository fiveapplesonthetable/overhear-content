## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Are you having a private party here?' Russians say 'by me' or 'with us' where English says 'I have' or 'we have', without needing a separate word for 'have'.
- 'Actually we wanted to sit quietly with the girls.' The grammar packs 'actually' and 'girls' into a tight, idiomatic kitchen exchange.
- 'Alright, I will go then, I will not bother you.' Notice how Russian expresses 'all right' and 'to disturb' with crisp inflection rather than helper words. Nastya answers right away.
- 'What a fine fellow, he just got up and left!' An emphatic spoken line centered on the verb meaning 'took'.
- 'He always knows how to disappear so suddenly.' The meaning hinges on 'he' working together with 'to disappear' in spoken Russian.
- 'Forgive me for reminding you of that again.' The '-a' ending on the past-tense verb for 'reminded' marks a female speaker or subject. Nikita answers right away.
- 'Work is over, and you are still here?' Nikita anchors the line on 'work', while 'here' completes the thought.
- 'Are you making extra revenue for our bar today?' In Russian, the idea of 'are doing' flows naturally into 'bar' without extra filler words.
- 'We wanted to sit in female company.' Spoken Russian keeps this brisk, leaning on 'we' and finishing on 'companies'. Nastya answers right away.
- 'Katya, come on, Nikita is our boss!' Nastya anchors the line on 'Katya', while 'boss' completes the thought.
