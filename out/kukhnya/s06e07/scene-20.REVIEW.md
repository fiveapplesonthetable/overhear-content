## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'So you think I am unattractive, got it?' Pointing out how unattractive she is around Lyova, Elena keeps normal statement word order here, turning the line into a question for Lyova purely through rising pitch.
- 'Hello, yes, chef, I am listening to you.' Lyova fires back sharply toward Barinov, steering the talk right where he wants it.
- 'Well alright, let me tell her everything.' Lyova pushes back candidly during the talk and brings in "khorosho" ("good, fine") to make the meaning unmistakable.
- 'Hello, respected Elena Pavlovna.' Lyova briskly directs this formal greeting right on cue using "zdravstvuyte" ("hello") — the polite Russian greeting that literally wishes the other person good health.
- 'I really did go take the medical tests in the chef's place.' Lyova speaks evenly in this moment, shifting the mood of the conversation in a single beat.
- 'Forgive us, please, for this deception.' Mentioning the trick beside Barinov, Lyova centers the thought on "prostite" ("excuse me", from "prostit") without wasting a word.
- 'Yes, all the best, goodbye.' Touching on the meeting during the talk, Lyova brings in "svidaniya" ("meeting", from "svidaniye") as the key Russian word here.
- 'So you did not go to the hospital because of an illness?' Barinov quizzes plainly how Lyova expects that to work in practice.
- 'No, on the contrary, my test results are excellent!' Insisting on how excellent things are right here, Lyova brings in "otlichnyye" ("excellent", from "otlichnyy") as the key Russian word here.
- 'And why did you suddenly ask about that?' Reacting directly in the exchange, Lyova puts the question word "pochemu" right at the start so Barinov hears the focus immediately.
- 'Oh no, nothing, everything is very good.' Barinov counters earnestly with a sigh, leaving no doubt about where he stands.
- 'What a strange evening it has turned out to be today!' Talking about a strange evening to Barinov, Lyova brings in "strannyy" ("strange, odd") to make the meaning unmistakable.
