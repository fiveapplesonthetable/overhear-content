## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Gulya, help yourself to fresh pastries, please.' Lyova proceeds promptly with a nod, refusing to let the moment slip past.
- 'No, thank you very much, I will clean up now.' Touching on the thank at once, Gulya brings in "bolshoye" ("big, very much", from "bolshoy") as the key Russian word here.
- 'Wait, there is no need to clean anything right now.' Speaking dryly during the talk, Lyova pairs the negative word with "ne" ("not"), since Russian naturally doubles its negatives for emphasis.
- 'I have wanted to tell you for a long time.' Lyova cuts in boldly in their talk, refusing to let the moment slip past.
- 'Lev Semyonovich, I have a husband and children in Kyrgyzstan.' Highlighting a husband at once, Gulya chooses "muzh" ("husband") so Lyova gets the point right away.
- 'Really? And I did not know that.' Checking on the honestly during the talk, Lyova keeps normal statement word order here, turning the line into a question for Gulya purely through rising pitch.
- 'Forgive me, please, goodbye then.' Lyova promptly delivers this informal apology for Gulya using "izvini" ("sorry") — the casual singular apology you use with someone you know well.
- 'I will never take the pastries again!' Bringing up the pastries on the spot, Gulya reaches for the Russian word "pirozhnyye" ("pastries, cakes", built from "pirozhnoye") right at the center of the line.
- 'I will work hard, do not fire me!' Gulya replies smoothly in front of Ainura and centers the thought on "uvolnyayte" ("fire", from "uvolnyat") without wasting a word.
- 'Lyova, I was the one who stole those pastries!' Ainura reacts frankly with a sigh, testing Lyova's reaction in the middle of the exchange.
- 'Fire me, she has a husband and five children!' Highlighting the children on the spot, Ainura leans on "detey" ("children", a form of "rebyonok") to give the remark its edge.
- 'I was not going to fire anyone!' Holding firm plainly in the exchange, the "-sya" ending on "sobiralsya" ("was going to") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'I simply wanted to get to know you better.' Lyova responds steadily beside Ainura, shifting the mood of the conversation in a single beat.
