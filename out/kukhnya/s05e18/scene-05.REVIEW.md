## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Good morning, Katya, hello to you.' With the point and surprise in play, Denis keeps this short reply brisk and direct. Speaking with the close, informal "you" strips away any polite distance between them.
- 'Oh, what a mess you have here.' Pointing out that what a mess Denis has here gives Denis a sobering reality check. In Russian you say "by me" or "with us" rather than "I have", so the situation sits right with the person.
- 'This junk should have been thrown out long ago.' Katya explains that this junk should have been thrown out long ago, keeping her voice calm and direct.
- 'Listen, I hid two tickets in there.' Realizing that he hid two tickets in there changes how Denis handles the situation.
- 'Those are tickets for tonight's concert.' Noting that those are tickets for tonight concert keeps Denis's side of the story simple and clear. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'And you just threw them away.' Even though Katya just threw them away, Denis keeps a steady face in front of Katya.
- 'Oh, Denis, forgive me, please.' Around the point and surprise, Katya sets a calm, everyday tone with Denis.
- 'I would have gone with great pleasure.' Saying that she would have gone with great pleasure is how Katya sets the tone with Denis.
- 'But I have the evening shift today.' Once she has the evening shift today, Katya can finally tell Denis what she means.
- 'Will you get them out of there yourself?' By asking whether Denis gets them out of there themselves, Katya presses Denis for a straight answer. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
- 'Forgive me once again, alright?' Over the surprise and the kitchen, Katya presses Denis for a straight answer. In Russian, she does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'Alright, no big deal, I will get them myself.' Since Alright no big deal he will get them himself, Denis does not waste words on excuses.
