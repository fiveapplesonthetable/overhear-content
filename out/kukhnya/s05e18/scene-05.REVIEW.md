- 'Oh, what a mess you have here.' Katya steps in at once facing Denis, refusing to let the moment slip past.
- 'This junk should have been thrown out long ago.' Katya stresses one practical element in reply: "vykinut" means "to throw out", and it carries the main weight of what Katya is saying.
- 'Listen, I hid two tickets in there.' Denis stands firm decisively with a sigh, before Katya can change the subject.
- 'Those are tickets for tonight's concert.' Denis opens up frankly in their talk, grounding the moment in plain reality.
- 'And you just threw them away.' Denis frames this key detail toward Katya: "vykinula" means "threw out" (from "vykinut"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'Oh, Denis, forgive me, please.' Katya sends this statement in earnest: dropping in "pozhaluysta" ("please") keeps the request polite on the surface even in a brisk exchange.
- 'I would have gone with great pleasure.' Katya continues right away across the room, to clear the air between them once and for all.
- 'But I have the evening shift today.' Katya picks one memorable term toward Denis: "vecherneye" means "evening" (from "vecherniy"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'Will you get them out of there yourself?' Katya shapes that thought in earnest: Russian keeps the exact word order of a normal statement here, turning it into a question for Denis purely through rising pitch at the end.
- 'Forgive me once again, alright?' Katya tests candidly what Denis is actually getting at here.
- 'Alright, no big deal, I will get them myself.' Denis frames this telling expression with Katya: "strashnogo" means "terrible, serious" (from "strashnyy"), giving the line its exact edge.
