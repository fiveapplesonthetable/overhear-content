## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Tell me, when will Viktoria Goncharova be discharged?' Max drops the comeback with candor: putting the question word "kogda" right at the front of the line lets the doctor hear the point of the question on the very first beat.
- 'She was discharged early this morning.' The doctor pushes back bluntly with a shrug, before Vika can change the subject.
- 'It seems her sister picked her up.' The doctor centers this telling detail right here: "kazhetsya" means "it seems, I think" (from "kazatsya"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'Hi, where were you all morning?' Vika drops the remark without fuss: opening with the casual greeting "privet" ("hi") signals right away that Vika and Max are on familiar terms.
- 'I went to the hospital, I wanted to pick you up.' Max holds firm bluntly as things stand, watching the doctor's face to see how the news lands.
- 'Max, nothing is going to work out between us.' Vika focuses on one practical word at once: "poluchitsya" means "will come out", and it carries the main weight of what Vika is saying.
- 'Alright, I understand everything perfectly.' Max sends this update on cue: tacking on "ladno" ("okay, fine, deal") softens the edge and checks that the doctor is on board.
- 'But you cannot forbid me to see our daughter.' Max proceeds frankly before Vika, bringing things back to earth after the buildup.
- 'And I had no intention of forbidding it.' Vika chooses one loaded choice on cue: "zapreshchat" means "to forbid", and it carries the main weight of what Vika is saying.
- 'I will support my family no matter what.' Max holds firm bluntly without any doubt, making his position completely clear.
- 'Thank you for the assembled baby crib.' Vika replies wryly during the talk, watching the doctor's face to see how the news lands.
- 'I will always be right there for you both.' Max grounds one classic element at once: "vsegda" means "always", and it carries the main weight of what Max is saying.
