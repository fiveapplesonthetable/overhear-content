## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Tell me, when will Viktoria Goncharova be discharged?' Looking over plainly during the talk, Max puts the question word "kogda" right at the start so the doctor hears the focus immediately.
- 'She was discharged early this morning.' The doctor opens up bluntly with a smile, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'It seems her sister picked her up.' Highlighting the sister beside Vika, the doctor uses "rodnaya" ("dear, darling", from "rodnoy") to make the point stick.
- 'Hi, where were you all morning?' Holding firm candidly with Max, Vika puts the question word "gde" right at the start so Max hears the focus immediately.
- 'I went to the hospital, I wanted to pick you up.' Max chimes in bluntly during the talk, so Vika knows he is not just making small talk.
- 'Max, nothing is going to work out between us.' Vika weighs in calmly before Max and uses "poluchitsya" ("will come out") to carry the main weight of the thought.
- 'Alright, I understand everything perfectly.' Max smoothly gives this word of agreement to Vika using "ladno" ("alright") — built from the old Russian root for harmony and order, used every day for "okay, fine".
- 'But you cannot forbid me to see our daughter.' Max counters frankly without any doubt, keeping the focus on what actually matters here.
- 'And I had no intention of forbidding it.' Vika continues plainly in front of Max and lands on "zapreshchat" ("to forbid") as the key everyday word here.
- 'I will support my family no matter what.' Turning to his family on the spot, Max lands on "semye" ("family", built on "semya") to keep the statement grounded.
- 'Thank you for the assembled baby crib.' Vika naturally chooses this word of thanks for Max using "spasibo" ("thank you") — the everyday word for "thank you", which grew out of the old blessing "spasi Bog" ("God save you").
- 'I will always be right there for you both.' Max proceeds dryly beside Vika and centers the remark on "vsegda" ("always") without any extra fuss.
