## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'This Spartak does not even know how to play here.' Claiming that this Spartak does not even know how to play here sounds confident, even if Alisa has doubts.
- 'This is the key to Vika's apartment.' Pointing out that this is the key to Vika apartment gives Alisa a sobering reality check. Russian drops "is" in the present, setting the two halves of the thought side by side.
- 'You need to go there and assemble the crib.' Saying that Alisa needs to go there and assemble the crib is how Tanya sets the tone with Alisa.
- 'Tomorrow she is being discharged from the maternity hospital.' Realizing that tomorrow she is being discharged from the maternity hospital changes how Tanya handles the situation.
- 'Wait, what do I have to do with this?' Asking what he has to do with this is how Barinov puts Alisa on the spot. In Russian, he does not need helper words like "do" or "did"—rising pitch alone turns it into a question.
- 'She is not asking her husband for anything.' Once she is not asking her husband for anything, Tanya can finally tell Alisa what she means.
- 'We are swamped in the kitchen today.' Admitting that they are swamped in the kitchen today shows Barinov being honest for a change. Russian expresses having something as "by me is", keeping the phrase short and grounded.
- 'I can see just how swamped you are.' Saying that she can see just how swamped Alisa is is how Tanya sets the tone with Alisa. In Russian you say "by me" or "with us" rather than "I have", so the situation sits right with the person.
- 'Dad, assemble the crib, please.' Around the office and work, Alisa sets a calm, everyday tone with Barinov.
- 'You are the very best dad.' Adding that Barinov is the very best dad fills in the missing piece for Barinov. Russian expresses having something as "by me is", keeping the phrase short and grounded.
- 'Shelves also need to be put up there, will you do it?' When Tanya asks if shelves also need to be put up there will Alisa does it, Alisa has to answer fast. Russian packs the whole idea of what is allowed or needed into one impersonal word at the front.
- 'And do the floors not need washing there too?' Checking whether the floors not need washing there cuts straight through the tension between Barinov and Alisa.
