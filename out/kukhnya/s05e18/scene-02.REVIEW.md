## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'This Spartak does not even know how to play here.' Barinov counters briskly with Tanya and centers the thought on "umeyet" ("knows how to", from "umet") without wasting a word.
- 'This is the key to Vika's apartment.' Mentioning the flat with a nod, Tanya sets the direction for the rest of the talk with Barinov.
- 'You need to go there and assemble the crib.' Addressing the crib in front of Barinov, Tanya lands on "krovatku" ("baby crib", built on "krovatka") to keep the statement grounded.
- 'Tomorrow she is being discharged from the maternity hospital.' Touching on the maternity hospital with Barinov, Tanya uses "vypisyvayut" ("they discharge", from "vypisyvat") to make the point stick.
- 'Wait, what do I have to do with this?' Barinov asks openly about what Tanya just brought up.
- 'She is not asking her husband for anything.' Discussing the husband around Barinov, Tanya brings in "muzha" ("husband", from "muzh") as the key Russian word here.
- 'We are swamped in the kitchen today.' Addressing the kitchen on the spot, Barinov reaches for "u nas" ("by us, at our place") to talk about what they share.
- 'I can see just how swamped you are.' Tanya proceeds evenly with a smile, steering the talk right where she wants it.
- 'Dad, assemble the crib, please.' Alisa lightly sends this courtesy word in the exchange using "pozhaluysta" ("please") — the all-purpose Russian politeness word that covers both "please" and "you're welcome".
- 'You are the very best dad.' Explaining how best Tanya is in front of Tanya, Alisa links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
- 'Shelves also need to be put up there, will you do it?' Tanya asks earnestly about what Barinov just brought up.
- 'And do the floors not need washing there too?' Addressing the floors right here, Barinov puts the emphasis on "poly" ("floors") in the middle of the talk.
