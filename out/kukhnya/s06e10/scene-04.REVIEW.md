## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'For my own money you want to send me to a mental clinic?' Katya brings out the contrast between 'for' and 'my' in a few short words.
- 'Leave my office immediately, you are fired!' Katya anchors the line on 'immediately', while 'fired' completes the thought.
- 'As I understand it, she did not agree again.' Notice the reflexive suffix on the verb meaning 'to agree', turning the action back on the subject. Katya answers right away.
- 'Sveta, hi, may I have you for one minute?' Katya anchors the line on 'Sveta', while 'a minute' completes the thought.
- 'Listen, is there something going on between you and Denis?' Katya chooses the respectful plural 'you' alongside 'there is' to keep a polite distance.
- 'And what difference does that even make to you?' Sveta anchors the line on 'and', while 'difference' completes the thought. Katya answers right away.
- 'Since I am asking, that means it makes a difference to me.' Notice how Russian expresses 'time' and 'difference' with crisp inflection rather than helper words.
- 'First of all, it is none of your personal business at all.' The meaning hinges on 'first of all' working together with 'business' in spoken Russian.
- 'And second of all, yes, he and I are dating.' Notice the reflexive suffix on the verb meaning 'are dating', turning the action back on the subject.
- 'What, now you will tell your mom to fire me?' Sveta anchors the line on 'what', while 'to fire' completes the thought.
- 'If you are coming to my birthday, we are gifting money.' The grammar packs 'if' and 'money' into a tight, idiomatic kitchen exchange.
- 'Alright, as girls you can chip in a bit less.' Spoken Russian keeps this brisk, leaning on 'all right' and finishing on 'to rent out'.
