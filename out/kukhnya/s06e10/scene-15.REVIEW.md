## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Senya, Sveta and I want to drink to you.' Denis speaks candidly with a sigh, making his position completely clear.
- 'We wish you strong health and great love.' Underscoring the health beside Katya, Denis uses "krepkogo" ("strong", from "krepkiy") to make the point stick.
- 'Here take this, this gift is from Sveta and me.' Bringing up the gift on the spot, Denis chooses "podarok" ("gift, voucher") so Katya gets the point right away.
- 'Senya, accept congratulations from Nikita and me too.' Focusing on the congratulations facing Senya, Katya keeps Senya's attention locked on what matters.
- 'Hand over the envelope, take our joint gift.' Focusing on the envelope around Senya, Katya uses "obshchiy" ("shared") to carry the main weight of the thought.
- 'Thank you, friends, let us drink to my health!' Mentioning his health during the talk, Senya uses the polite or group form "davayte" ("let's") to bring Katya into the plan.
- 'Listen, it seems she is not very jealous of me.' Denis chimes in quietly without any doubt, testing Senya's reaction in the middle of the exchange.
- 'Sveta, please come closer and hug me.' Denis speaks earnestly during the talk and reaches for the Russian word "blizhe" ("close, near", built from "blizkiy") right at the center of the line.
- 'Tell me honestly, does this mean something to you?' Stepping in firmly around Denis, Katya keeps normal statement word order here, turning the line into a question for Denis purely through rising pitch.
- 'For me this means nothing at all.' Katya weighs in right away toward Denis, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'I will better go to the bar and drink some more wine.' Focusing on the bar in front of Senya, Katya centers the remark on "yeshchyo" ("more, still") without any extra fuss.
- 'Raise your glasses, the party continues at the bar counter!' Touching on the glasses in front of Katya, the "-sya" ending on "prodolzhayetsya" ("continues") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
