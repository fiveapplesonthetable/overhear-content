## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Thank you for saving me.' The question word for 'what' comes right at the start, followed directly by 'me'.
- 'What a fool you are.' The question opens with the interrogative word for 'what a' and builds toward 'fool'.
- 'Do you think these are funny jokes?' Vika turns a statement into a question purely through rising intonation on the word meaning 'think', pairing it with 'ridiculous' and 'jokes'.
- 'Vika, forgive me, please.' Max leads with the direct command form meaning 'sorry' to make the point directly in conversation.
- 'I really screwed up badly.' The verb for 'screw up' is in the masculine past tense, marked by the '-l' ending.
- 'I simply do not want to lose you.' Max uses the informal singular 'you' here with 'to lose', as friends and close colleagues do.
- 'Do I have even a small chance?' Max uses the classic Russian possession phrasing — literally 'at me' rather than a verb meaning 'to have', combining 'there is' with 'short' and 'chance'.
- 'I will do everything that you say.' Max uses the perfective verb meaning 'I'll do' to focus on the single completed result, pairing it with 'will you say'.
- 'I no longer believe your words.' Vika puts the word for 'more' together with 'believe' and 'word' in a crisp, natural spoken turn of phrase.
- 'But I am telling you the truth.' Max chooses the imperfective verb meaning 'am telling' for the ongoing action, followed by 'truth'.
- 'You need to change.' Vika uses the informal singular 'you' here with 'to change', as friends and close colleagues do.
- 'I promise, I will change.' The reflexive ending on the verb for 'to change' turns the action back onto the subject.
- 'Once again, forgive me.' Max leads with the direct command form meaning 'sorry', pairing it with 'more' and 'time'.
