- 'He writes that he cannot live.' Vika chooses the imperfective verb meaning 'is writing' for the ongoing action, followed by 'can' and 'to live'.
- 'He writes that life has no meaning.' The negative particle sits directly before the verb for 'is writing' to negate the action.
- 'And he asks not to blame for anything.' Vika chooses the imperfective verb meaning 'is asking for' for the ongoing action, followed by 'to blame'.
- 'What an idiot he is after all.' Vika delivers a punchy one-word reaction built around the Russian word meaning 'idiot'.
- 'This is another stupid joke.' Russian omits the present-tense verb 'to be' here, linking 'another' directly with 'silly', combining 'another' with 'silly' and 'joke'.
- 'I do not believe a single word of his.' Vika chooses the imperfective verb meaning 'believe' for the ongoing action, followed by 'alone' and 'word'.
- 'He thinks that I am stupid?' Russian forms this yes-or-no question without a question word, relying on rising pitch on 'silly'.
- 'I will go and tell him everything to his face.' Vika uses the perfective verb meaning 'go' to focus on the single completed result, pairing it with 'I'll tell' and 'face'.
- 'He made me really angry.' The phrasing opens with 'very much' before bringing in 'strongly' and 'to anger' to make the point clear.
- 'I will cause a scandal for him right now.' Vika expresses this naturally by combining the word for 'now' with 'to arrange' and 'scandal'.
