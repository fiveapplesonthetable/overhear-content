## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Well then, Victor, do you surrender?' The guest proceeds decisively beside Barinov and centers the thought on "sdayoshsya" ("give up, surrender", from "sdavatsya") without wasting a word.
- 'Russians do not surrender, especially when playing against oligarchs.' Emphasizing the oligarchs around the guest, Barinov chooses "oligarkhov" ("oligarchs", from the root "oligarkh") so the guest hears the exact meaning.
- 'Do not call me an oligarch.' The guest answers bluntly across the room, making his position completely clear.
- 'Then what should I call you?' Barinov fires back sharply before the guest and uses "nazyvat" ("to call, to name") to carry the main weight of the thought.
- 'The meat king of Russia?' Discussing the meat king to the guest, Barinov keeps normal statement word order here, turning the line into a question for the guest purely through rising pitch.
- 'Alright then, let us show our cards.' The guest directly sends this word of agreement for Barinov using "ladno" ("alright") — built from the old Russian root for harmony and order, used every day for "okay, fine".
- 'I have a flush.' Talking about a flush in the exchange, the guest brings in "flesh" ("flush") to make the meaning unmistakable.
- 'You and I have not seen each other in a long time.' Pressing on readily during the talk, the "-sya" ending on "videlis" ("have seen each other") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'And you still have not learned to play poker.' Barinov fires back plainly toward the guest, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'I have four of a kind.' Touching on a kind with the guest, Barinov lands on "kare" ("four of a kind") as the key everyday word here.
- 'How much meat will you have to sell to settle up with me?' Turning to the meat on the spot, Barinov leans on "nado" ("it is necessary, one needs to"), which carries the whole predicate in Russian without needing "it is".
- 'Whole truckloads of meat!' Barinov answers wryly across the room, testing the guest's reaction in the middle of the exchange.
