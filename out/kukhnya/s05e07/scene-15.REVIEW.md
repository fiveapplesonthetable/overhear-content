## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Girls, order, do not be shy.' Vika continues steadily with a shrug, without trying to dress up how she really feels.
- 'Give the bill to Lavrov.' Talking about the counting in front of Barinov, Vika chooses "schyot" ("counting, account") so Barinov gets the point right away.
- 'Well then, did you lose?' Following up calmly with Mikhail, Barinov puts the question word "chto" right at the start so Mikhail hears the focus immediately.
- 'He will come right back and finish eating.' Mikhail weighs in frankly without holding back, so Barinov knows he is not just making small talk.
- 'There are still a hundred and twenty grams left here.' Pointing to the gram at once, Barinov chooses "grammov" ("gram", from the root "gramm") so Mikhail hears the exact meaning.
- 'Nikolai Maryanovich, are you in here?' Opening up promptly in this beat, Mikhail links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
- 'Nikolai Maryanovich, is everything alright with you?' Mikhail quizzes curiously why things suddenly took this turn between them.
- 'Why is he not answering?' Barinov pushes back firmly before Mikhail and brings in "otvechayet" ("is responsible", from "otvechat") as the key Russian word here.
- 'Open the door, please!' Mikhail politely chooses this courtesy word in one quick breath using "pozhaluysta" ("please") — the all-purpose Russian politeness word that covers both "please" and "you're welcome".
- 'He got sick from your bet.' Barinov answers pointedly on the spot, so Mikhail knows he is not just making small talk.
- 'Break the lock faster!' Talking about the lock during the talk, Mikhail centers the thought on "bystreye" ("faster", from "bystro") without wasting a word.
- 'Call an ambulance!' Turning to the help at once, spoken Russian compresses the whole thought into just 3 words, skipping the extra helper words English needs.
