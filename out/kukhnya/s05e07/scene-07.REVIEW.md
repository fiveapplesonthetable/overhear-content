## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'There is an idea to hold a 'Month of Provence' Saying that there is an idea to hold a of Provence is how Barinov sets the tone with Eleonora.
- 'Whoever eats the entrecote does not have to pay for it.' Since whoever eats the entrecote does not have to pay for it, Barinov does not waste words on excuses.
- 'Too bad our moustached generator of random ideas does not know.' Mentioning that too bad their moustached generator of random ideas does not know moves the talk in a new direction.
- 'The point of any promotion in a restaurant is to make a profit.' Mikhail reminds Barinov that the point of any promotion in a restaurant is to make a profit before the moment passes.
- 'And not to engage in charity.' Around the bet and kitchen, Mikhail sets a calm, everyday tone with Barinov.
- 'Vitya, seriously, it is some kind of strange promotion.' Explaining that seriously it is some kind of strange promotion lays the cards on the table for Barinov.
- 'People will eat up all our meat for free.' Eleonora mentions that people will eat up all their meat for free so Barinov sees what is at stake.
- 'Do you think it is so easy to eat two kilograms of meat?' Here asking whether Eleonora thinks it is so easy to eat two kilograms of meat shows Barinov looking for the real truth. Using the informal "you" shows how direct and unfiltered their kitchen relationship is.
- 'Let us make a bet.' Realizing that they should make a bet changes how Barinov handles the situation. Starting with "davay" is the everyday Russian way to say "come on, let us do this".
- 'Do you like losing?' Asking whether Barinov likes losing cuts straight through the tension between Mikhail and Barinov.
- 'Return the keys and the car documents.' Mentioning that Barinov should return the keys and the car documents moves the talk in a new direction.
- 'You have won the chance to go for a ride with a charming cook.' Noting that Eleonora has won the chance to go for a ride with a charming cook keeps Barinov's side of the story simple and clear. Even in the middle of the drama, he keeps the polite plural "you" that workplace Russian expects.
