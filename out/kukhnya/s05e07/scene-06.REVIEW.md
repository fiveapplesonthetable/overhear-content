## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Katya, forgive me, please, that turned out awkward.' Senya jumps in boldly around Barinov, saying out loud what both of them already know.
- 'And hiding my bra in your socks was convenient?' Lyova centers one direct detail on cue: "lifchik" means "bra", and it carries the main weight of what Lyova is saying.
- 'Did your Marinka forgive you?' Lyova states that line in earnest: Russian keeps the exact word order of a normal statement here, turning it into a question for Barinov purely through rising pitch at the end.
- 'Ever since she went on vacation, I cannot catch a breath around her.' Senya weighs in openly in their talk, before Lyova can change the subject.
- 'Chef, we urgently need to do something with this meat.' Lyova uses one key word at once: "myasom" means "meat" (from "myaso"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'Its shelf life is one month.' Lyova lays out this warning at once: notice how Russian needs no word for "am", "is", or "are" in the present — placing the words side by side says it all.
- 'Lyova, I owe you money, after all.' Barinov pushes back wryly to settle things, grounding the moment in plain reality.
- 'There is no rush.' Lyova centers one loaded piece toward Senya: "spekhu" means "hurry, rush" (from "spekh"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'No, Lyova, no.' Barinov puts the comment on cue: spoken Russian trims this down to just 3 words, dropping the extra helper words that English has to spell out.
- 'Barinov always pays his debts.' Barinov weighs in wryly on the spot, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'No need, do not thank me.' Barinov picks one familiar piece on cue: "blagodari" means "thank" (from "blagodarit"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'Thank you very much, Chef.' Lyova sends the thought without fuss: the everyday word "spasibo" ("thank you") grew out of an old blessing meaning "save you", and it anchors the whole line here.
