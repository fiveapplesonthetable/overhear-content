## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I cannot manage the whole kitchen!' Russian says 'manage with the kitchen', and the 'ey' on 'kitchen' means 'with'. 'Ne smogu' is the future, 'I will not be able'.
- 'You will definitely do absolutely great!' 'Obyazatelno' grows out of 'obligatory', so it's a strong 'definitely'. 'At you it will turn out' is Russian for 'you'll manage'.
- 'I have never replaced the chef before.' 'Never before not' is the triple that Russian uses. 'Zamenyal' is 'replaced', in a man's past form.
- 'Because you are our sous-chef, that is super-chef!' Senya's joke only works because 'sous' sounds a bit like 'super', so he makes the word mean 'super-chef'. The two little words 'after all' and 'that is' set it up.
- 'But the chef always shouts so loudly.' 'Always' goes right near the start, which is where a nervous Lyova wants it. 'Shouts' is one word with no 'he'.
- 'And you simply shout at everyone louder.' 'Krichi' is an order, 'shout!', and 'gromche' means 'louder'. The 'e' ending is the same one that makes 'faster'.
- 'I completely do not know how to shout terribly.' 'Umeyu' is 'I know how to', so it's about skill. 'Strashno' means 'terribly', from 'strashny', scary.
- 'Come on, try to shout loudly at me.' The 'come on' is literally 'give'. 'Shout at' has a 'na' in front that adds the feel of a job done.
- 'Senya, go cut these vegetables immediately!' 'Idi rezat' is 'go cut', two verbs side by side with no 'and'. 'Ovoshchi' is vegetables.
- 'Very weak, Lyova, we need more anger!' 'Slabo' is 'weakly', and Russians say it as a cutting 'too feeble'. 'Nuzhno bolshe zlosti' is 'it's needed more of anger'.
- 'All right, now I will gather my strength.' Literally, 'I will gather myself with strengths', which is how Russians say 'I'll pluck up courage'. The 'sya' is the 'myself'.
- 'You are our very best head chef today!' 'Samy luchshy' is 'the very best', which is overkill on purpose. 'Glavny povar' is 'head cook'.
- 'Yes, I can do it, I definitely can!' 'Smogu' is 'I will be able', the future of 'to be able'. 'Tochno' means 'exactly' and 'definitely'.
- 'Now I see a real chef!' 'I see' is a single short word. The ending on 'real' changes because the chef is the one seen.
