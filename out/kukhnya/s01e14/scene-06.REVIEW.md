## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I cannot manage the whole kitchen!' 'Upravlyat', to run or manage, takes the 'with' form, so 'vsya kukhnya' becomes 'vsey kukhney', ending in 'ey'. 'Ne smogu' is a future, 'I won't be able to'.
- 'You will definitely do absolutely great!' 'Obyazatelno' is the adverb from 'obyazatelny', obligatory, and in speech it's a firm 'definitely'. 'U tebya poluchitsya' is 'it'll work out at you', so 'you'll manage'.
- 'I have never replaced the chef before.' Literally, 'I never before not replaced'. Russian keeps both negatives, 'nikogda' and 'ne'. 'Zamenyal' is the man's past form, since it's Lyova.
- 'Because you are our sous-chef, that is super-chef!' 'Ved' is 'after all', the word for reminding someone of the obvious. 'To est' is literally 'that is', and Russians use it just like English 'i.e.'.
- 'But the chef always shouts so loudly.' Russian says 'shouts so strongly', 'tak silno krichit'; 'silno' is the all-purpose intensifier for noise and pain alike.
- 'And you simply shout at everyone louder.' 'Gromko' is loud, and 'gromche' is louder; its opposite works the same way, 'tikho', quiet, and 'tishe', quieter. You shout 'na vsekh', onto everyone.
- 'I completely do not know how to shout terribly.' 'Ne umeyu' is 'I don't know how', about a skill, not about being allowed. 'Strashno' is from 'strakh', fear, so 'frighteningly'.
- 'Come on, try to shout loudly at me.' 'Davay', literally 'give', is the everyday 'come on'. 'Nakrichat na menya' has 'na' twice: once as the prefix, for one complete telling-off, and once as 'at'.
- 'Senya, go cut these vegetables immediately!' 'Idi rezat' is 'go cut', two verbs side by side, the second in its basic form. 'Eti ovoshchi' is 'these vegetables', plural all the way.
- 'Very weak, Lyova, we need more anger!' 'Slabo' alone is a verdict, 'weak!'. After 'bolshe', more, the noun takes its 'of' form, so 'zlost', anger, becomes 'zlosti'.
- 'All right, now I will gather my strength.' 'Soberus s silami' is literally 'I'll gather myself with strengths', the set phrase for pulling yourself together. The final 's' on 'soberus' is the 'myself'.
- 'You are our absolute best head chef today!' 'Samy luchshy' is 'the very best', with 'samy' doubling up on an already top word. 'Glavny povar' is literally 'main cook'.
- 'Yes, I can do it, I definitely can!' 'Smogu' is the same verb as the 'ne smogu' from his first line, now without the 'ne'. 'Tochno' means 'exactly', and here 'for sure'.
- 'Now I see a real chef!' 'Vizhu' is 'I see'. The chef is the one being seen, so 'nastoyashchy shef' becomes 'nastoyashchego shefa', with 'ego' and 'a' endings for a man.
