## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Here everything is in French.' The little 'po' plus the ending 'i' gives 'in French' as an adverb. The same trick gives 'in Russian' and 'in Italian'.
- 'For our chef this is not a problem.' 'Problema' is the English word, with an 'a' because Russians treat it as feminine.
- 'Right, Maximilian?' Elena borrows 'pravda', 'truth', as a tag, like 'isn't it?'. She puts him on the spot.
- 'Si.' It's an Italian 'yes', written in Russian letters. Max has chosen the wrong foreign language, which is the whole joke.
- 'Si? Idiot.' Louis echoes the word as a question and then insults him. 'Idiot' is the English word, with a Russian look.
- 'Si is in Italian.' A dash stands in for 'is'. 'Po-italyanski', in Italian, is built with the same 'po' and 'ski' as 'in French'.
- 'In French it will be oui.' Louis uses the word 'will be', although English would use 'is'. Russians sometimes use the future for what a word 'turns out to be'.
- 'Yes, of course, I know this.' 'I know' is one word, with the 'ya' in front. 'Konechno' is 'of course' and it's pronounced 'konyeshno'.
- 'Maximilian, do you understand the French language?' Elena says 'French language' with both words in full, where Russians would usually leave out the second. 'Understand' is the polite form.
- 'Of course, I understand French perfectly.' Here 'French' stands alone, and it's an adjective that does duty as the name of the language. Russians do this all the time.
- 'Well, what will you do now?' 'Will do' is made of two words, a form of 'be' plus the basic form of 'do'. That is the usual way for a long action in the future.
- 'I will read this recipe and prepare the dish.' 'Read' has a 'pro' in front, which means 'read through to the end'. Max promises to finish the job.
- 'This will be very funny.' Louis is calm. 'Will be' is the future again, and 'funny' has no 'is' in front.
