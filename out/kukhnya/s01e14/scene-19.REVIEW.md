## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Alright, we will figure this out.' 'Razberemsya' is the 'we will' form of the word Kostya used with Lyova, razbiratsya, to sort out. 'Ladno' is the shrugging 'okay then'.
- 'Everything will be fine.' Literally 'everything will be well': khorosho, 'well', rather than the word for 'good'. 'Vse budet khorosho' is one of the most useful sentences in Russian.
- 'What should we do now?' 'Chto nam delat' is literally 'what to us to do'. 'Nam', to us, names the people who must act, and delat stays in its basic form.
- 'Let's go.' 'Poekhali' comes from the word for going by vehicle. The first man in space said it as his rocket lifted off, and now it means 'let's get going' with anything.
- 'Where are we going?' 'Kuda' is 'where to'. If you're asking where something is, the word is gde; if you're asking where someone is heading, it's kuda.
- 'We are not going anywhere.' Nikuda ne, 'nowhere not'. Russian needs both negatives here.
- 'It means that we are starting.' 'Eto znachit, chto' is literally 'this means, what'. The same chto that asks 'what' also works as 'that', joining two halves.
- 'Now I understand everything.' Ponyal is past, 'I understood', but Russians say ya ponyal for 'I get it' now. The 'l' ending fits Ilya, a man; Sasha would say ponyala.
- 'Then let's work.' 'Davayte rabotat': the group 'let's', then the action in its basic form, the one ending in 't'. 'Togda', then, ties it to what Max just said.
- 'Yes, back to work.' Za rabotu again, the rallying cry from the last scene, literally just 'to work'.
- 'We have little time.' 'U nas malo vremeni', literally 'at us little of time'. After malo, little, vremya takes the 'of' ending and becomes vremeni.
- 'We must be in time.' 'Uspet' means 'to manage to do something in time', a single word with no neat English match. 'Dolzhny', must, is the plural form for 'we'.
- 'Of course we will be in time.' 'Uspeem' is 'we'll make it', the 'we' form of uspet. Russian puts a comma after konechno, so it opens the answer like 'of course, yes'.
- 'Then we start right now.' 'Nachinaem' is 'we start', and said to a group it works like 'let's start'. 'Pryamo seychas' is literally 'straight now'.
