## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Diana, you smell wonderful as always!' 'Kak vsegda' is 'as always'. 'Pakhnete', you smell, ends in 'ete', the 'vy' ending, because Nagiyev is polite with a guest.
- 'Dmitry, you are so funny.' She pairs his full first name with the polite 'vy', friendly but proper. 'Vy takoy smeshnoy' is literally 'you such funny', no 'are'.
- 'By the way, I already met the head chef.' 'Kstati' is 'by the way'. You get acquainted 's', with, someone, so 'shef-povar' takes the 'with' ending 'om'. 'Poznakomilas' has the 'a' of a woman.
- 'Oh, Lord, and what do you make of him?' 'Gospodi' is the old calling form of 'Gospod', 'the Lord', kept alive in this exclamation. 'Kak on vam?' is literally 'how is he to you?'.
- 'He is super, just such a handsome man!' 'Krasavchik' shares its root with 'krasivy', handsome, and the 'chik' makes it playful, like 'a real looker'. 'Super' is borrowed and never changes.
- 'Who are you talking about anyway?' 'O kom' is 'about whom': 'kto', who, turns into 'kom' after 'o'. 'Vobshche', literally 'in general', here gives the 'anyway'.
- 'He is so sweet and very sociable!' 'Obshchitelny', sociable, is from 'obshchatsya', to chat or mix with people. 'Mily' is 'sweet' or 'nice' about a person.
- 'Probably, he is in a good mood today.' 'V khoroshem nastroenii' is 'in a good mood'. After 'v', in, both words change their endings: 'khoroshee' becomes 'khoroshem' and 'nastroenie' becomes 'nastroenii'.
- 'I was gifted a book with French recipes.' 'Mne podarili' is literally 'to me they gifted', with no one named; Russian uses the 'they' form for this. 'Knigu', book, takes 'u' as the thing given.
- 'This is a very interesting gift.' 'Podarok', gift, comes from the same verb as 'podarili' in the line before. 'Interesny' ends in 'y' to match it, a masculine noun.
- 'Could he cook for us?' 'Mog by' is 'could', the past tense 'mog' plus 'by', and it softens a request. 'Dlya nas' is 'for us'.
- 'If he is in such a mood, yes.' Here's the same mood phrase again, now with 'takom', the 'in' form of 'takoy', such. 'Esli' is 'if'.
- 'I want him to cook here!' Literally, 'I want so that he cooked here'. After 'chtoby' the verb takes the past form, 'gotovil', though she means the future.
- 'All right, he will do everything for you.' 'Sdelaet' is 'will do', a future in one word. 'Dlya vas' is 'for you', the polite 'vy' again.
