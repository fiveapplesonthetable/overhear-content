## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Louis, why are you crying bitterly again?' 'Gorko plachesh' is 'cry bitterly', and 'gorko' comes from 'gorky', bitter. 'Opyat' means 'again'.
- 'Chef, a terrible drama happened to me.' 'U menya sluchilas'' is 'at me happened', which Russians use for events. 'Strashnaya' is 'terrible', from 'strakh', fear.
- 'Stop this foolish noise right now!' The order 'stop' is the same one used for ending a conversation, a harsh one. 'Foolish noise' makes Louis's crying sound like a stupid thing.
- 'My tender heart is broken into pieces.' 'Razbito' means 'broken' and it ends in 'o' to match 'serdtse', neuter. 'Na kuski' is 'into pieces'.
- 'Quickly wipe your tears and make desserts.' Tears are always plural in Russian, so there's no word for a single tear in this form. Two orders sit side by side, 'wipe' and 'make'.
- 'I cannot bake sweet cakes right now.' 'Pech' is 'to bake', and 'pirozhnye' are small cakes, which look like a describing word but work as a name. 'Ne mogu' is 'I can't'.
- 'Our important guests have been waiting for desserts for a long time!' 'Davno zhdut' is 'for a long time wait', and Russian uses the present for 'have been waiting'. Barinov keeps it short.
- 'All right, chef, I will start making creams.' 'Nachnu' is 'I will begin'. 'Gotovit' is 'to make' and 'kremy' is 'creams', in the plural.
- 'And stop your constant empty complaints.' 'Zhaloby' means 'complaints', and it is always plural. 'Pustye' is 'empty', which Russians use for 'meaningless'.
- 'Hey, cooks, why are you all standing around?' 'Povara' is the plural of 'povar', and it ends in 'a', which is the odd form for masculine words. 'Vy vse' is 'you all'.
- 'We are already starting to prepare orders quickly.' Louis answers with 'we', not 'I', to include the whole kitchen. The word for 'orders' is plural, and it is what the cooks make.
- 'Move lively, sluggards, let us begin working together!' 'Zhivee' is 'more alive', so 'faster', and 'lentyai' is 'lazybones'. 'Druzhno' means 'in a friendly, united way' and comes from 'drug', friend.
- 'Work quickly, guests are waiting for their orders!' 'Rabotaem' is 'we work', and Russians use it for 'let's work'. 'Svoi' is 'their own'.
