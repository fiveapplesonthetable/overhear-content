## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'All right, go on, get to work.' Leaning in promptly to Fedya, spoken Russian compresses the whole thought into just 3 words, skipping the extra helper words English needs.
- 'Thank you, Chef.' Fedya evenly directs this word of thanks at this moment using "spasibo" ("thank you") — the everyday word for "thank you", which grew out of the old blessing "spasi Bog" ("God save you").
- 'Forgive me for earlier.' Barinov naturally chooses this informal apology in the exchange using "izvini" ("sorry") — the casual singular apology you use with someone you know well.
- 'It turned out to be a dumb joke.' Bringing up a dumb joke in front of Barinov, the "-sya" ending on "poluchilas" ("turned out") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'Well, forgive me too if anything was wrong.' Barinov pushes back lightly facing Fedya, steering the talk right where he wants it.
- 'Why did you call me over to you?' Fedya proceeds decisively beside Barinov and puts the weight on "pozval" ("invited", from "pozvat") as the exchange unfolds.
- 'And why does this always happen on my shift?' Mentioning his shift right here, Barinov puts the question word "pochemu" right at the start so Fedya hears the focus immediately.
- 'Wait, what is wrong with him?' Fedya checks briskly why things suddenly took this turn between them.
- 'Spartak lost again, when will they ever...' Barinov jumps in plainly to Fedya and centers the thought on "proigral" ("lost", from "proigrat") without wasting a word.
- 'Learn how to play football.' Pressing on smoothly beside Barinov, the "-sya" ending on "nauchitsya" ("to learn") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'Why am I surrounded exclusively by invalids?' Barinov checks deadpan if there is more going on behind the scenes.
- 'You say your beard is lucky, huh!' Emphasizing how lucky things are to Barinov, Fedya puts the emphasis on "schastlivyy" ("lucky") in the middle of the talk.
- 'It does not help you at all!' Reacting boldly to Fedya, Barinov uses "u tebya" (literally "by you") where English says "you have" or "your".
