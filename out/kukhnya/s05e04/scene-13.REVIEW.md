## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Have you completely lost your mind?' Spoken Russian keeps this brisk, leaning on 'that' and finishing on 'flew off'.
- 'The guests are laughing at you.' Kostya brings out the contrast between 'on' and 'you' in a few short words.
- 'Ladies and gentlemen, there is no need to laugh.' The negative particle sits right before the verb meaning 'to laugh' to negate the statement.
- 'I come from an ancient Scottish clan.' Chef Barinov brings out the contrast between 'I' and 'from' in a few short words.
- 'In our clan it is customary to wear kilts.' The grammar packs 'at' and 'in skirts' into a tight, idiomatic kitchen exchange.
- 'Everything is fine. I told them I am a Highlander.' Notice how Russian expresses 'all' and 'Highlander' with crisp inflection rather than helper words. Kostya answers right away.
- 'You are not a Highlander. You are an old man disgracing both yourself and the hotel.' Kostya addresses the listener with the informal singular 'you' alongside 'hotel'.
- 'Viktor Petrovich, are you aware that we have strict rules?' Russians say 'by me' or 'with us' where English says 'I have' or 'we have', without needing a separate word for 'have'.
- 'well, non-traditional room service is prohibited?' Mikhail raises the pitch on 'of room service' to turn the phrase into a direct spoken question. Chef Barinov answers right away.
- 'What is that lady doing here?' Chef Barinov brings out the contrast between 'what' and 'this' in a few short words.
- 'You can always win it back! I cannot.' Mikhail brings out the contrast between 'you' and 'always' in a few short words.
- 'Right now I am fulfilling another wish.' Chef Barinov brings out the contrast between 'I' and 'now' in a few short words.
- 'Not yours.' Chef Barinov anchors the line on 'not', while 'your' completes the thought.
