## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Listen, not a bad place. Was it tasty?' The guest wonders eagerly if there is more going on behind the scenes.
- 'A good restaurant, and a good head chef.' Discussing a good head with Barinov, Elena lands on "shef-povar" ("head chef") as the key everyday word here.
- 'You bet it is.' Reacting evenly in front of the guest, spoken Russian compresses the whole thought into just 4 words, skipping the extra helper words English needs.
- 'Viktor Barinov.' Elena continues firmly with a shrug, keeping the focus on what actually matters here.
- 'Wait, is that not the Barinov you were seeing?' The guest responds boldly in the exchange and brings in "podozhdi" ("wait", from "podozhdat") as the key Russian word here.
- 'And the two of you are back together...' Replying naturally beside Elena, the guest links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
- 'And is that not him by any chance?' The guest checks candidly before Elena can change the subject.
- 'Let us get out of here.' Elena jumps in naturally around the guest and leans on "otsyuda" ("from here") to give the whole sentence its punch.
- 'Lena, it is just a joke.' Returning to a joke with Elena, Barinov uses the little word "prosto" ("simply, just") to make the point sound effortless.
- 'I lost at cards.' Barinov follows up calmly in this moment, to clear the air between them once and for all.
- 'How familiar does that sound to me? Lost at cards.' Highlighting the cards with Barinov, Elena chooses "znakomo" ("familiar", from the root "znakomyy") so Barinov hears the exact meaning.
- 'You know, I almost believed that you had changed.' Pushing back evenly in the exchange, the "-sya" ending on "izmenilsya" ("have changed") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
