## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'When you cannot solve a problem, you only have one way out.' Russians say 'by me' or 'with us' where English says 'I have' or 'we have', without needing a separate word for 'have'.
- 'To forget it forever and' The grammar packs 'to forget' and 'and' into a tight, idiomatic kitchen exchange.
- 'try to live on.' In everyday Russian, 'to try / attempt' flows straight into 'further, on' without extra linking words.
- 'Defendant Denis Andreyevich Krylov, you are accused of infidelity.' In Russian, the idea of 'defendant' flows naturally into 'infidelity' without extra filler words.
- 'But it is not my fault!' Spoken Russian keeps this brisk, leaning on 'but' and finishing on 'at fault'.
- 'Right now it is going to get even better.' Notice how Russian expresses 'now' and 'better' with crisp inflection rather than helper words.
- 'Arousal denied.' Here the Russian phrasing highlights 'initiation' before landing on 'to refuse'.
- 'My stomach cramped up.' Senya brings out the contrast between 'belly' and 'seized' in a few short words.
- 'I need the toilet, it is a total nightmare in there.' Senya anchors the line on 'I', while 'hanger' completes the thought.
- 'Could you not hold it for at least an hour?' Here the Russian phrasing highlights 'to hold on' before landing on 'an hour or so'.
- 'At least five minutes.' The meaning hinges on 'at least' working together with 'five' in spoken Russian.
- 'When our housemates are not home, we will be running all night.' Putting the short word for 'not' right in front of the action word is all Russian needs to make a sentence negative, with no extra helper words like 'do' or 'did'.
