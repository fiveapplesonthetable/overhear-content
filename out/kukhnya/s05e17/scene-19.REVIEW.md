- 'Are you asking for trouble again, young fellow?' Turning to the boyfriend at once, the guest uses "naryvayeshsya" ("are asking for it", from "naryvatsya") to make the point stick.
- 'I did not ride up here in a freight elevator.' Mentioning a freight elevator with the guest, Max centers the thought on "gruzovom" ("freight", from "gruzovoy") without wasting a word.
- 'Max, please, let's just leave instead.' Vika counters boldly with a shrug, bringing things back to earth after the buildup.
- 'You are not going anywhere, give birth calmly.' Max responds firmly during the talk and uses "poydyosh" ("will you go", from "poyti") to make the point stick.
- 'Guys, clear the ward of these citizens!' Emphasizing the citizen in this beat, the guest uses "grazhdan" ("citizen", from "grazhdanin") to make the point stick.
- 'Who wants foam stuffed in their mouth here?' Max prompts earnestly whether the guest really thought this through.
- 'Hello, Bunny, where are you giving birth right now?' Turning to the sweetheart to Max, the guest brings in "rozhayesh" ("to give birth", from "rozhat") as the key Russian word here.
- 'In what other maternity hospital?' Turning to the maternity hospital before Max, the guest keeps normal statement word order here, turning the line into a question for Max purely through rising pitch.
- 'Wait, what traffic jams, I gave you a flashing light!' The guest replies earnestly with a smile, before Max can change the subject.
- 'Alright, I am coming to you right now, damn!' Addressing the darn at once, the guest brings in "chyort" ("darn, heck") to make the meaning unmistakable.
- 'That is it, stand down, this ward is canceled.' Turning to the stand during the talk, the "-sya" ending on "otmenyayetsya" ("is canceled") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'Call the doctor, my wife is giving birth!' Max follows up sharply in this moment, without trying to dress up how he really feels.
