## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'It turns out all the wards really are occupied.' Touching on the hospital room before the doctor, the "-sya" ending on "okazyvayetsya" ("to turn out to be") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'Excuse me, is this room free?' Max casually drops this formal apology at the start of the beat using "prostite" ("forgive me") — a warmer, more earnest polite apology that literally asks someone to forgive you.
- 'No, this room is occupied too.' Highlighting how busy things are in front of Max, the doctor uses "zanyata" ("busy, occupied", from "zanyatyy") to make the point stick.
- 'And who is giving birth here, the television?' Pointing to the television with the doctor, Max puts the question word "kto" right at the start so the doctor hears the focus immediately.
- 'This room is for someone from the mayor's office.' The doctor cuts in steadily facing Max, making his position completely clear.
- 'They told me to prepare everything and wait.' The doctor reacts earnestly with Max and uses "podgotovit" ("to prepare") to carry the main weight of the thought.
- 'Viktoria Sergeyevna, our room is already ready!' Bringing up his room beside the doctor, Max links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
- 'How are you not ashamed of yourselves?' Max wonders openly before the doctor can change the subject.
- 'You have a minister's wife waiting in the hallway!' Pointing to a minister in this beat, Max puts the weight on "ministra" ("minister", from "ministr") as the exchange unfolds.
- 'Forgive us, we did not know that you had arrived.' The doctor steps in at once facing Max, grounding the moment in plain reality.
- 'Please, come in and make yourselves comfortable.' The doctor smoothly directs this courtesy word with Vika using "pozhaluysta" ("please") — the all-purpose Russian politeness word that covers both "please" and "you're welcome".
- 'Max, what on earth is going on here?' Vika opens up calmly during the talk and leans on "proiskhodit" ("is going on") to give the whole sentence its punch.
- 'Walk ahead and do not ask questions.' Underscoring the questions beside Vika, Max uses "voprosov" ("questions", from "vopros") to make the point stick.
