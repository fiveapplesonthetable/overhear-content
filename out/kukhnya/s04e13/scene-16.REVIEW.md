## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Katya, will you have some hot tea right now?' In spoken Russian, the words stay in their normal statement order and Chef Barinov's rising voice at the end turns the line into a question.
- 'No, Dad, I do not want anything.' Katya declines quietly with a negative pronoun and first-person singular verb.
- 'I am not offended at you at all.' Putting the short word for 'not' right in front of the action word is all Russian needs to make a sentence negative, with no extra helper words like 'do' or 'did'.
- 'I do not work there anymore, they fired me.' An impersonal plural past verb expresses being fired by management.
- 'What do you mean fired, what a scoundrel he is!' Barinov pretends to be shocked and indignantly insults German.
- 'Remember, you can always work with me.' Notice how Russian puts 'remember' and 'to work' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'Chef, our whiskered friend completed the mission!' Senya barges in without noticing Katya and brags about the cat.
- 'Senya, what nonsense are you talking, drink some tea!' Barinov desperately tries to silence him with a colloquial idiom meaning talking nonsense.
- 'Chef, I am talking about the cat!' Oblivious to Barinov's signals, Senya names the cat explicitly.
- 'Dad, what a deceiver you really are!' Katya pieces everything together and confronts her father with an exclamatory determiner.
- 'Chef, did I ruin your surprise or what?' Senya realizes too late why Katya just ran out of the room.
- 'Get out of here, I cannot stand the sight of you!' Barinov chases Senya out in frustration after his scheme backfires.
