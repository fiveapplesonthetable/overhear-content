## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Denis, get this cat out of here quickly!' Katya proceeds honestly with a smile, without trying to dress up how she really feels.
- 'Katya, where did a cat even come from here?' German uses this clear piece toward Katya: "voobshche" means "at all", and it carries the main weight of what German is saying.
- 'I myself know absolutely nothing!' Katya shapes this warning around Denis: Russian happily doubles the negative here — pairing "never" or "nothing" with "ne" ("not") — which is the only natural way to say it.
- 'What is going on over there with you right now?' German tests frankly as Katya tries to explain the situation.
- 'Hurray, I finally caught this cat!' Denis picks this sharp detail with German: "nakonets" means "at last, finally", and it carries the main weight of what Denis is saying.
- 'Wait, there is a phone number on the collar here.' Denis jumps in honestly with a nod, before Katya can change the subject.
- 'We can call and find out whose cat it is.' Denis jumps in evenly to settle things, steering the talk right where he wants it.
- 'Thank you, Denis, let me call right now.' German picks one telling element with Katya: "spasibo" means "thank you", and it carries the main weight of what German is saying.
- 'Wait, why is my cell phone ringing?' Katya phrases that answer in earnest: putting the question word "pochemu" right at the front of the line lets Denis hear the point of the question on the very first beat.
- 'German Mikhailovich, this is some kind of cruel setup!' Katya continues plainly as they talk, so German knows she is not just making small talk.
- 'Honestly, this is not my cat!' Katya uses one pointed detail with Denis: "chestnoye" means "honest" (from "chestnyy"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'I do not want to listen, you are fired!' German colors one clear touch right here: "slushat" means "to listen to", and it carries the main weight of what German is saying.
