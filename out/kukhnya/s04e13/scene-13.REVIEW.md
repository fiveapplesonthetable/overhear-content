## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Everyone remember once and for all!' Vika addresses the waiters with a plural imperative and a fixed adverbial idiom.
- 'Everything in my life is great.' She insists she is fine using a predicative adverb of high quality.
- 'Take your flowers back and leave!' Two plural imperatives express her pride before she learns the truth.
- 'A courier brought this bouquet, not you?' The masculine past verb agrees with the subject noun meaning courier.
- 'Chef, I have important information again.' Out in the courtyard, Senya approaches Barinov with a second piece of gossip.
- 'About Katya again, speak quickly!' Barinov prompts him with an imperative and a comparative adverb.
- 'Tonight at Monet there is a dog party.' The possessive adjective for dog modifies the masculine nominative noun for holiday.
- 'A rich lady is throwing a banquet for her little dog.' The dative feminine phrase marks the pampered beneficiary of the party.
- 'And what does my Katya have to do with this?' Barinov uses an idiomatic prepositional question meaning what does she have to do with it.
- 'They appointed Katya responsible for this banquet.' The plural past verb governs an accusative object and an instrumental adjective.
- 'Get out of here if you have nothing useful!' Barinov pretends the tip is useless so he does not have to pay Senya.
- 'Hello, Timur, do you remember your debt?' As soon as Senya leaves, Barinov calls Timur to put his plan into motion.
