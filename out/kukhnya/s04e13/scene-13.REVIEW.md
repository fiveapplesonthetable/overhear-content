## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Everyone remember once and for all!' Notice how Russian puts 'remember' and 'forever' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'Everything in my life is great.' She insists she is fine using a predicative adverb of high quality.
- 'Take your flowers back and leave!' Two plural imperatives express her pride before she learns the truth.
- 'A courier brought this bouquet, not you?' The masculine past verb agrees with the subject noun meaning courier.
- 'Chef, I have important information again.' Out in the courtyard, Senya approaches Barinov with a second piece of gossip.
- 'About Katya again, speak quickly!' Here Chef Barinov anchors the thought on 'again' and finishes on 'quickly', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'Tonight at Monet there is a dog party.' In everyday Russian, 'today' flows straight into 'celebration' without extra linking words.
- 'A rich lady is throwing a banquet for her little dog.' Senya pairs 'rich, wealthy' with 'little dog, doggy' directly, since spoken Russian skips English filler words like 'the', 'a', and 'is'.
- 'And what does my Katya have to do with this?' In spoken Russian, the words stay in their normal statement order and Chef Barinov's rising voice at the end turns the line into a question.
- 'They appointed Katya responsible for this banquet.' Here Senya anchors the thought on 'appointed' and finishes on 'banquet', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'Get out of here if you have nothing useful!' Barinov pretends the tip is useless so he does not have to pay Senya.
- 'Hello, Timur, do you remember your debt?' As soon as Senya leaves, Barinov calls Timur to put his plan into motion.
