## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello, Katya, things turned out foolishly between us.' Barinov follows up dryly in the exchange and reaches for the Russian word "poluchilos" ("worked out", built from "poluchitsya") right at the center of the line.
- 'I cannot talk right now, bye.' Katya responds promptly to Barinov and lands on "govorit" ("to talk") as the key everyday word here.
- 'Excuse me, please, go ahead.' Katya naturally offers this polite apology while facing German using "izvinite" ("excuse me") — the respectful form of "excuse me" or "sorry" used when stepping in or apologizing.
- 'You already worked in our restaurant.' Focusing on his restaurant in front of Katya, German brings in "restorane" ("restaurant", from "restoran") as the key Russian word here.
- 'Moreover, that was quite recently.' Stepping in boldly to Katya, German adds "sovsem" ("completely, at all") so the statement leaves zero room for doubt.
- 'Yes, that is all true, I worked here.' Katya opens up evenly around German, watching German's face to see how the news lands.
- 'Back then I worked under my father.' Turning to her father to German, Katya uses "rabotala" ("worked", from "rabotat") to make the point stick.
- 'He was the head chef here back then.' Emphasizing the head in front of Barinov, Katya puts the weight on "shef-povarom" ("head chef", from "shef-povar") as the exchange unfolds.
- 'Then you resigned of your own accord.' German speaks pointedly watching Katya, grounding the moment in plain reality.
- 'May I find out the reason for this wish?' Focusing on the wish before Katya, German chooses "zhelaniya" ("wish") so Katya gets the point right away.
- 'We simply did not get along in character.' Dwelling on the character to German, Katya uses the little word "prosto" ("simply, just") to make the point sound effortless.
- 'That is not surprising, come with me.' German opens up wryly with a shrug, so Katya knows he is not just making small talk.
