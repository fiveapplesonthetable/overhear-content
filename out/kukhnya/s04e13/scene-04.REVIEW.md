## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello, Katya, things turned out foolishly between us.' Barinov picks this sharp element in earnest: "poluchilos" means "worked out" (from "poluchitsya"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'I cannot talk right now, bye.' Katya states this point facing German: the "-sya" ending on "seychas" ("now") turns the action inward or makes it happen between people naturally.
- 'Excuse me, please, go ahead.' Katya continues lightly with Barinov, keeping the focus on what actually matters here.
- 'You already worked in our restaurant.' German underscores one sharp element in reply: "restorane" means "restaurant" (from "restoran"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'Moreover, that was quite recently.' German brings the comment beside Barinov: adding "sovsem" ("completely, at all") pushes the statement all the way, leaving zero room for doubt.
- 'Yes, that is all true, I worked here.' Katya answers evenly in this moment, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'Back then I worked under my father.' Katya focuses on this clear element at once: "rabotala" means "worked" (from "rabotat"), giving the line its exact edge.
- 'He was the head chef here back then.' Katya weighs in pointedly with a shrug, so German knows she is not just making small talk.
- 'Then you resigned of your own accord.' German steps in pointedly toward Barinov, refusing to let the moment slip past.
- 'May I find out the reason for this wish?' German picks this direct piece at once: "zhelaniya" means "wish", and it carries the main weight of what German is saying.
- 'We simply did not get along in character.' Katya states the comeback briskly now: the little word "prosto" ("simply, just") works like a shrug in the sentence, making a big claim sound effortless.
- 'That is not surprising, come with me.' German responds wryly to be clear, grounding the moment in plain reality.
