## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I apparently misunderstood you.' Vika uses a parenthetical adverb meaning apparently and a feminine past-tense verb.
- 'Do you want to celebrate your dog's party?' The feminine genitive phrase modifies the accusative noun for celebration.
- 'Yes, I want to close the whole restaurant.' The guest states her demand with a perfective infinitive and an accusative determiner.
- 'We and our guests need comfort.' Dative plural pronouns mark who needs comfort in this impersonal clause.
- 'He has a strong allergy to lactose.' explaining why the cappuccino was actually for the dog using an accusative prepositional phrase.
- 'We do not hold such events.' Vika tries to decline politely using a negated first-person plural verb.
- 'But Dima Nagiyev said something completely different.' The guest invokes the restaurant owner's name to overrule Vika.
- 'He promised that everything will be fine.' A past-tense verb of promising introduces a future-tense clause.
- 'I wanted to come to you the day before yesterday.' The temporal adverb for the day before yesterday specifies when she originally planned to visit.
- 'But he said that you are getting divorced.' The reflexive plural present verb means getting divorced.
- 'Excuse me, please, for a minute.' Overcome with emotion, Vika excuses herself using an accusative time phrase.
- 'Nastya, if you cry, I will fine you!' Vika warns empathetic Nastya using a conditional clause with perfective future verbs.
- 'Why are you standing there, get to work quickly!' Vika orders the staff back to their stations with a brisk prepositional command.
