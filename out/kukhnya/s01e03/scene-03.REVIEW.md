## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I cannot kill this goose.' "Ya ne mogu" is "I not can". "Etogo gusya" puts -ogo on "this" and -ya on "goose", the object shape for a living creature.
- 'Maybe you will try once more?' "Mozhet" is a clipped "maybe", and "yeshchyo raz" is "once more". "Poprobuyesh'", you'll try, is the "ty" form of Max's "poprobuyu".
- 'No, I definitely cannot do this.' "Smogu" is the finished partner of "mogu": "I'll manage", one time, as opposed to being able in general. "Tochno" is "definitely".
- 'Let us suddenly scare him in the dark!' "Davay" plus a "we" verb, here "napugayem", gives "let's". "Rezko" is "sharply", and "v temnote" is "in the darkness".
- 'Kostya, this is complete nonsense.' "Kakoy-to" is "some kind of": the -to makes "kakoy" vague. "Polnyy bred" is "full nonsense", and "bred" also means a fever's raving.
- 'Birds have a very weak heart.' "U ptits" is "at birds", the usual way to say "have". The "d" in "serdtse", heart, is silent, so it sounds like "sertse". "Slaboye" is "weak".
- 'See, he is standing and hissing loudly.' "Vidish'" is "you see". "Shipit", hisses, sounds like what it means, and Russian says the goose "stoit", stands.
- 'I urgently need a real specialist.' "Mne nuzhen" is "to me needed", masculine to match "spetsialist". "Srochno" is "urgently", and "nastoyashchiy" is "real".
- 'Ainura, help us solve the problem!' "Pomogi nam", help us, puts "we" in its "to" shape, because Russians help "to" someone. "Reshit'" means both "decide" and "solve".
- 'What needs to be done with this bird?' "Chto nuzhno sdelat'" is "what is needed to do". "Ptitsa", bird, becomes "s etoy ptitsey", with -ey after "s", with.
- 'The goose needs to be removed quickly and neatly.' "Ubrat'" means to take away and also to tidy up, so it makes a gentle way to say "get rid of". "Akkuratno" is "neatly".
- 'A thousand rubles, and you will never see him.' After "tysyacha", thousand, comes "rubley", the "of" plural of "rubl'". "Nikogda ne uvidite" is "never not will see".
- 'Deal, here is your money!' "Dogovorilis'", we've agreed, is the word that seals a deal. "Den'gi", money, is always plural, so "your" is "tvoi".
