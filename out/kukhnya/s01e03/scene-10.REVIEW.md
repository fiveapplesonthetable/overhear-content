## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Oguzok! Where is my fresh goose?' Barinov opens with the nickname. "Gde" is "where", and "svezhiy" is "fresh".
- 'I have just finished it off!' "Prikonchil" contains "konets", end, so it's "brought to an end". "Tol'ko chto" is "only what", meaning "just now".
- 'Why are you so suspiciously clean?' "Podozritel'no" is "podozritel'nyy", suspicious, with the -o of a how-word. "Chistyy" is "clean".
- 'There is not a drop of blood on you!' "Net ni kapli krovi" is "there isn't not a drop of blood": "ni" means "not even". "Kaplya" and "krov'" both take the "of" form.
- 'I did not cut it at all.' "Sovsem ne" is "not at all". "Rezal", cut, ends in -l for a man, and "yego" is the goose.
- 'And how did you kill it?' "Kak" is "how". "Ubil", killed, is the past of "ubit'", the verb from the goose scene.
- 'Chef, I simply strangled it!' "Zadushil", strangled, is "dushit'", to choke, with "za-" for finishing it, related to "dukh", breath. "Prosto" is "simply".
- 'What a nightmare! A real Othello in the kitchen!' "Kakoy koshmar!" is "what a nightmare!". "Otello" strangles his wife in Shakespeare, so the chef is joking.
- 'Drag your bird to the sous-chef's table!' "Tashchi" is "drag", said of anything heavy. "Su-shefu", to the sous-chef, takes -u for "to", and "svoyu ptitsu" is "your own bird".
- 'Lyova, start preparing the goose quickly!' "Nachinay" is the "ty" command "start". "Gotovit' gusya" is "to cook the goose", with -ya on the bird.
- 'Understood, Chef, I am turning on the oven.' "Vklyuchayu", I switch on, also means "I include", and it works for any switch. "Dukhovka", oven, comes from "dukh", hot air.
- 'And I will take care of an important compliment.' "Zaymus'", I'll take care, ends in -s'. What you take care of changes its ending, so it's "vazhnym komplimentom". A chef's "kompliment" is a free little dish.
- 'I saved the bird and my job.' "Spas", saved, shares its root with "spasibo", once "spasi Bog", "may God save you". "Svoyu rabotu" is "my own job".
