## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Sasha, come here quickly!' Russians say "go here" for "come here", when the target is a place. "Skoreye" is "faster", the word you'd use to hurry someone along.
- 'What happened, Nastya?' This is the question to ask whenever something has gone wrong. Sasha puts the name on the end.
- 'Look, a live goose is sitting in the box!' Russians say the goose "sits" in the box, as they do for anything that has been put in a container. "Zhivoy", alive, is where "quick!" comes from.
- 'Where did this goose come from?' The verb is literally "took itself", and Russians say it when something turns up out of nowhere. "Otkuda" means "from where".
- 'The chef wanted to prepare a dish out of it.' The goose is "he" in Russian, because the word is masculine. "Out of him" gets an extra "n" on the front of "him".
- 'Did you decide to save this bird?' "Save" sits behind "spasibo", thanks, which began as "God save". "Ptitsa" is "bird".
- 'Yes, I cannot abandon him.' The same word means to throw and to abandon. Nastya is a vegetarian, and she speaks of the goose as "him".
- 'The chef will be furious if he finds out!' "In fury" is two short words. "Finds out" is "know" with "u-" on the front.
- 'I will hide the goose in a safe place.' You can see the pieces in "safe": "bez" plus "opasnoye", without danger.
- 'Just do it very carefully.' "Carefully" is the describing word "careful" with its ending swapped to "-o". Sasha is nervous for her friend.
- 'The guests almost found him.' "Barely not" is the Russian for "almost". And the guests come last, since they are the news.
- 'Carry him away from the kitchen right now!' The "u-" in front of "carry" means "away", the same as in "to leave". "From the kitchen" has its own ending.
- 'I will find a warm corner for him.' "For him" gets the added "n" again. "Warm" and "corner" are two easy words to hold onto.
- 'And return to work quickly!' "Return" ends in "-sya", the little "self": "return yourself". "To work" ends in -e after "to".
