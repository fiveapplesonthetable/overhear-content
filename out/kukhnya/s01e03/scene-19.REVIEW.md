## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Chef, excuse us for this noise.' "Izvinite nas" is "excuse us", a polite command. The reason follows "za": "za etot shum", for this noise.
- 'Do not worry, this pistol was not real.' "Ne bespokoytes'", don't worry, hides "bez pokoya", without peace. "Ne nastoyashchiy" is "not real".
- 'Here is generous money for these inconveniences.' "Neudobstva" is "ne" plus "udobstva", comforts. "Den'gi" is plural, so "shchedryye", generous, is plural too.
- 'Thank you, we are glad to help our guests.' "Rady", glad, is plural for "we". "Nashim gostyam" is "to our guests", because Russians help "to" someone.
- 'Oguzok, come here right now!' The nickname again. "Idi syuda" is "go to here", and "pryamo seychas" is "right now".
- 'Chef, do you want to fire me?' "Khotite", you want, is the polite form. "Uvolit'" is "to fire", and "menya" is "me".
- 'Let us sum up your first work week.' "Podvedyom itogi" is "let's bring up the totals", the set phrase for summing up. "Nedelya", week, becomes "nedeli" for "of the week".
- 'On the first day you almost poisoned a guest.' Russians say "v pervyy den'", in the first day. "Chut' ne otravil" is "barely not poisoned", and "gost'" becomes "gostya".
- 'On the second day you started a brawl in the bar.' It's "vo vtoroy", with "vo" instead of "v" before "vt". "Ustroil draku" is "arranged a fight".
- 'You angered our chief art director.' "Razozlil", angered, has "zloy", angry, inside. "Glavnogo art-direktora" ends in -ogo and -a as the person angered.
- 'And today you were almost shot!' "Tebya chut' ne zastrelili" is "you barely not they shot". With no subject, the -li verb leaves the shooter unnamed.
- 'Chef, I promise to work much better.' This promise is built like the English one: "obeshchayu", I promise, plus "rabotat'", to work. "Namnogo", by a lot, goes in front of "luchshe", better, the way "much" does.
- 'Looks like you fit right into the team!' "Pokhozhe" is "it looks like", from "pokhozhiy", similar. "Vpisyvayesh'sya" is "you write yourself in", the Russian "fit in".
- 'Let us head quickly to work in our kitchen.' "Poshli" is a past form used as "let's go". "Na nashu kukhnyu" is "onto our kitchen", with -u for heading there.
