## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Vika, please forgive me for everything.' "Prosti" is the "ty" form of "forgive". "Za vsyo" is "for everything", the "za" of reasons.
- 'Why do I always trust the wrong people?' "Veryu" means both "I believe" and "I trust", and it takes the "to" form: "tem lyudyam". "Ne tem", not those, is the Russian "the wrong ones".
- 'Every person can make a mistake.' "Sovershit' oshibku" is "to commit a mistake", a slightly formal phrase. "Oshibka" becomes "oshibku" as the object.
- 'I myself am to blame for this situation.' "Vinovata", to blame, is built from "vina", fault, with a woman's -a. "Sama", myself, also ends in -a.
- 'So that means it is true, you are together?' "Tak znachit" is "so it means", for putting two and two together. "Vy" is plural here, for both of them.
- 'Vadim, what are you doing here?' "Chto ty zdes' delayesh'?" is "what you here do?". Russian has no -ing form, so the plain present does the job.
- 'I came to talk with you right now.' "Prishyol", came, is a man's form and means he came on foot. "Pogovorit' s toboy" is "to talk with you", the "ty" form.
- 'And this guy must leave immediately!' "Paren'" is the ordinary word for a guy. "Dolzhen", must, has a man's ending, and "uyti" is "to go away".
- 'Choose your words when talking with her!' Max now says "ty" to Vadim: "vybiray" and "govorish'". "S ney" is "with her".
- 'You are talking to a beautiful girl.' "Razgovarivayesh'", you're talking, is from "razgovor", a conversation. "S krasivoy devushkoy" has -oy on both words after "s".
- 'What did you just say to me, pup?' "Shchenok" is a puppy, and it's an insult for a young man. "Ty chto" at the start is the challenge.
- 'I told you: step away from her!' "Otoydi" is the command "step away". "Ot neyo" is "from her", where "yeyo" picks up an "n" after "ot".
- 'Max, stop, he has a gun!' "Prekrati" is "stop it". "U nego yest' pistolet" is "at him there is a pistol", the full "have" pattern with "yest'".
