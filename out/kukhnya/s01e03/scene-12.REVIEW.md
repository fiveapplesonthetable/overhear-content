## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Lyova, show me this goose.' "Pokazhi" is the "ty" command "show". "Etogo gusya", this goose, takes the creature's object endings, -ogo and -ya.
- 'Here it is, chef, I already finished.' "Vot on" is "here he is", because "gus'" is masculine. "Zakonchil", finished, ends in -l for a man.
- 'Why is it so strangely small?' "Chego" is spoken Russian for "why". "Stranno", strangely, is the adverb of "strannyy", strange, and "malen'kiy" is "small".
- 'It turned out like that because of feathers.' "Iz-za" is "because of", usually for something bad. "Per'ya", feathers, becomes "per'yev" after it, and "tak poluchilos'" is "so it turned out".
- 'It turned out that it has very thick feathers.' "Okazalos'", it turned out, is a handy way to open news. "U nego" is "at him", the goose's "have", and "gustyye" is "thick".
- 'We bought a huge bird after all!' "Zhe" gives the "after all". "Kupili" is "we bought", and "ogromnuyu ptitsu", a huge bird, ends in -u as the thing bought.
- 'I was surprised myself when I was plucking it.' "Sam udivilsya" is "myself was surprised", with -sya on the verb. "Oshchipyval", was plucking, comes from "shchipat'", to pinch.
- 'There is very little meat here for the guests.' "Malo", little, is followed by "myasa", the "of" form of "myaso". "Dlya gostey" is "for the guests".
- 'What should we do with it now?' "Chto nam delat'?" is "what to us to do?". "S nim", with him, gets the added "n" after "s".
- 'Prepare a special sauce and a beautiful side dish.' "Prigotov'" is the command "prepare". "Sous" and "garnir", side dish, both came from French.
- 'Alright, chef, I will do everything quickly.' "Sdelayu", I'll do, is the finished partner of "delayu", so it means "I'll get it done". "Bystro" is "quickly".
- 'The guests must be pleased with our banquet!' "Dolzhny byt'" is "must be". "Dovol'ny" means "pleased", and what you're pleased with takes -om: "nashim banketom".
