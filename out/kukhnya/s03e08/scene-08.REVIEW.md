## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Max, why did you put that on right now?' Ainura anchors the line on 'Max', while 'put on' completes the thought.
- 'Well our chef took me back into the kitchen.' Spoken Russian keeps this brisk, leaning on 'so' and finishing on 'took'.
- 'Now I am a cook here again.' Notice how Russian expresses 'I' and 'cook' with crisp inflection rather than helper words.
- 'What cook, the chef said that you are my assistant.' Ainura addresses the listener with the informal singular 'you' alongside 'assistant'.
- 'Wait, so I am a cleaner now?' Notice how Russian expresses 'wait' and 'cleaner' with crisp inflection rather than helper words. Ainura answers right away.
- 'I am the cleaner, and you are the cleaner's assistant.' Here the Russian phrasing highlights 'cleaner' before landing on 'assistant'.
- 'And what do you want, they told me to take you, I took you.' The '-l' ending on the verb meaning 'took' places the action in the masculine past tense.
- 'And what you will work as in my kitchen is up to me to decide.' Chef Barinov addresses the listener with the informal singular 'you' alongside 'to decide'.
- 'Or do you not want to be a cleaner?' The negative particle sits right before the verb meaning 'want' to negate the statement.
- 'No, I do not want to at all.' In Russian, the idea of 'no' flows naturally into 'want' without extra filler words.
- 'Well why did you not say so right away, I am promoting you!' Notice how Russian expresses 'come on' and 'promote' with crisp inflection rather than helper words.
- 'You will be a food delivery courier, that is all, the conversation is over.' Here the Russian phrasing highlights 'will' before landing on 'finished'.
