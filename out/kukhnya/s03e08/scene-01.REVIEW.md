## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Vitya, you can go wherever you want.' Vika fires back boldly in the exchange and reaches for the Russian word "khochesh" ("do you want", built from "khotet") right at the center of the line.
- 'If, of course, football is dearer to you than I am.' Stressing how dearer she is around Barinov, Vika links the Russian words directly in the present with no word for "am", "is", or "are" needed.
- 'Alright, darling, as you say, so it will be.' Barinov speaks sharply around Vika, steering the talk right where he wants it.
- 'Dmitry Vladimirovich, do you have a minute?' Highlighting a minute during the talk, Vika leans on "minutka" ("minute, moment") to give the whole sentence its punch.
- 'I hope that we have a whole life.' Talking about a whole life with Vika, Nagiyev reaches for "u nas" ("by us, at our place") to talk about what they share.
- 'Our chef fired the food delivery courier.' Vika chimes in wryly toward Barinov, making her position completely clear.
- 'He said that his dishes are eaten only in the restaurant.' Addressing the restaurant with Vika, Barinov chooses "restorane" ("restaurant", from the root "restoran") so Vika hears the exact meaning.
- 'How to deal with him, I do not understand.' Holding firm earnestly on the spot, the "-sya" ending on "borotsya" ("to fight") marks an action that unfolds mutually or turns back on the speaker.
- 'I also do not understand whether we are dating or not.' Nagiyev cuts in openly without any doubt, watching Vika's face to see how the news lands.
- 'After all, you and I went to the movies three times.' Mentioning the cinema at once, Nagiyev lands on "kino" ("cinema, movies") as the key everyday word here.

- 'Yesterday, when you fell fast asleep at the movies.' Nagiyev fires back lightly beside Vika, without trying to dress up how he really feels.
