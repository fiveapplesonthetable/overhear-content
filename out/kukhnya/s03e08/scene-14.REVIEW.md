## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Dima, maybe we will eat a little later?' Vika shapes that point in earnest: Russian keeps the exact word order of a normal statement here, turning it into a question for Max purely through rising pitch at the end.
- 'We will eat later, when we actually get hungry.' Vika weighs in frankly as things stand, so Nagiyev knows she is not just making small talk.
- 'Dima, let us not rush right now.' Vika features one sharp term on cue: "toropitsya" means "hurry, rush", and it carries the main weight of what Vika is saying.
- 'After all, we will still have time for everything.' Vika hands over that remark around Nagiyev: the phrase "u nas" literally means "by us" or "at our place", the standard Russian way to talk about what a group or couple shares.
- 'Alright, I will probably drive home right now.' Max pushes back quietly in their talk, watching Nagiyev's face to see how the news lands.
- 'That kid ruined everything for me again!' Max stresses one direct detail in reply: "malchishka" means "boy, kid", and it carries the main weight of what Max is saying.
- 'Hello, may I book a train ticket to Voronezh?' Max hands over this statement with ease: the single Russian word "mozhno" ("it is possible, one may") sets up the whole statement without needing a separate word for "it is".
- 'I need one night ticket for today.' Max fires back briskly with a shrug, shifting the whole mood of the room in a single beat.
- 'Yes, I definitely need fast delivery.' Max uses this direct expression with Nagiyev: "obyazatelno" means "surely, without fail", and it carries the main weight of what Max is saying.
- 'Wonderful, you made a simply wonderful shot!' Nagiyev makes this answer with warmth: using "u vas" (literally "by you") is how Russian politely asks or talks about what someone has on their side.
- 'Well then, friends, shall we walk on calmly?' Nagiyev questions bluntly why things suddenly took this turn between them.
- 'Be more careful, you are such a big joker!' Nagiyev emphasizes this sharp touch toward Vika: "ostorozhneye" means "more carefully", and it carries the main weight of what Nagiyev is saying.
