## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Dima, maybe we will eat a little later?' Pushing back firmly in the exchange, Vika keeps normal statement word order here, turning the line into a question for Max purely through rising pitch.
- 'We will eat later, when we actually get hungry.' Vika proceeds frankly with a sigh, making her position completely clear.
- 'Dima, let us not rush right now.' Vika pushes back openly to Max and puts the emphasis on "toropitsya" ("hurry, rush") in the middle of the talk.
- 'After all, we will still have time for everything.' Touching on the time at once, Vika reaches for "u nas" ("by us, at our place") to talk about what they share.
- 'Alright, I will probably drive home right now.' Max follows up quietly beside Vika, bringing things back to earth after the buildup.
- 'That kid ruined everything for me again!' Bringing up the boy around Vika, Max leans on "malchishka" ("boy, kid") to give the whole sentence its punch.
- 'Hello, may I book a train ticket to Voronezh?' Mentioning a train at once, Max leans on "mozhno" ("it is possible, one may"), which carries the whole predicate in Russian without needing "it is".
- 'I need one night ticket for today.' Max replies briskly beside Nagiyev, grounding the moment in plain reality.
- 'Yes, I definitely need fast delivery.' Bringing up the delivery before Nagiyev, Max lands on "obyazatelno" ("surely, without fail") as the key everyday word here.
- 'Wonderful, you made a simply wonderful shot!' Pushing back earnestly with Max, Nagiyev uses the little word "prosto" ("simply, just") to make the point sound effortless.
- 'Well then, friends, shall we walk on calmly?' Nagiyev questions bluntly whether Max really thought this through.
- 'Be more careful, you are such a big joker!' Turning to a big joker in the exchange, Nagiyev centers the remark on "ostorozhneye" ("more carefully") without any extra fuss.
