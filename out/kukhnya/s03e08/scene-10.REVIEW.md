## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hi, consider that I have reserved this seat.' Here the Russian phrasing highlights 'hello' before landing on 'reserved'.
- 'Do not go anywhere, I will be back soon.' The meaning hinges on 'nowhere' working together with 'will return' in spoken Russian.
- 'Please get acquainted, this is my Viktor Petrovich.' Notice the reflexive suffix on the verb meaning 'meet', turning the action back on the subject. the guest answers right away.
- 'Very nice to meet you, we are so glad to see you.' the guest brings out the contrast between 'very' and 'pleasant' in a few short words.
- 'At least a decent chef has finally appeared in Moscow.' The '-l' ending on the verb meaning 'appeared' places the action in the masculine past tense.
- 'Tell me, and what do you do in general?' Spoken Russian keeps this brisk, leaning on 'say' and finishing on 'do'.
- 'What do you have there, elephants?' Chef Barinov raises the pitch on 'elephants' to turn the phrase into a direct spoken question.
- 'Pay no attention, Viktor Petrovich is joking.' The grammar packs 'not' and 'jokes' into a tight, idiomatic kitchen exchange.
- 'Well, let us start our match quickly.' Notice how Russian expresses 'come on' and 'match' with crisp inflection rather than helper words. Chef Barinov answers right away.
- 'In general, by the way, how long do you play this golf?' Here the Russian phrasing highlights 'at all' before landing on 'to play'.
- 'To complete everything, it takes five hours.' the guest brings out the contrast between 'so that' and 'everything' in a few short words.
- 'But you are beginners, we will play a short version and finish in two hours.' the guest chooses the respectful plural 'you' alongside 'will finish' to keep a polite distance.
