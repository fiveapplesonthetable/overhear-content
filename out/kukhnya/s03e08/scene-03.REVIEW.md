## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'You are here like a silent reproach from the past.' Underscoring how silent Max is in the exchange, Nagiyev leans on "proshlogo" ("past", a form of "proshlyy") to give the remark its edge.
- 'Tell me, do you want to fire me?' Pushing back frankly in front of Nagiyev, Max keeps normal statement word order here, turning the line into a question for Nagiyev purely through rising pitch.
- 'Yes, I want to very much, but I cannot.' Nagiyev follows up wryly watching Max, letting the remark speak for itself.
- 'After all, I gave my honest word.' Talking about the word with Max, Nagiyev leans on "chestnoye" ("honest", a form of "chestnyy") to give the remark its edge.
- 'Maxim, go work in the kitchen instead.' Bringing up the kitchen during the talk, Nagiyev reaches for the Russian word "kukhnyu" ("kitchen", built from "kukhnya") right at the center of the line.

- 'Chef, you look very good today.' Max opens up candidly in this beat and brings in "segodnya" ("today") to make the meaning unmistakable.
- 'Did you dress up so nicely for a wedding?' Speaking frankly to Barinov, Max keeps normal statement word order here, turning the line into a question for Barinov purely through rising pitch.
- 'No, I dressed up for a funeral.' Barinov pushes back earnestly beside Max, so Max knows he is not just making small talk.
- 'Vitya, why is your cook walking around in the hall?' Emphasizing the hall on the spot, Nagiyev lands on "povar" ("chef, cook") as the key everyday word here.
- 'Because I kicked him out of the kitchen.' Underscoring the kitchen to Nagiyev, Barinov uses "kukhni" ("kitchen", from "kukhnya") to make the point stick.
