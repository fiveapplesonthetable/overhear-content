## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Comrade Captain, why did you let him go just now?' Notice how Russian puts 'comrade' and 'let go' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'Turn on your head at last and think!' The senior detective scolds his partner with two singular imperatives.
- 'This young guy is just a small-time courier.' In everyday Russian, 'young' flows straight into 'courier, errand boy' without extra linking words.
- 'Through him we will get to the big fish!' the inspector pairs 'to go out, to get out' with 'fish' directly, since spoken Russian skips English filler words like 'the', 'a', and 'is'.
- 'Look, he went into this expensive restaurant.' Sitting in their car outside Claude Monet, they watch Denis enter.
- 'Wait, and who else is that in the courtyard?' They spot supplier Timur unloading boxes by the service door.
- 'And here the serious suppliers have arrived here!' The detective misinterprets Timur's grocery delivery as a cartel meeting.
- 'We have hit the trail of a major syndicate.' the inspector pairs 'attacked, mugged' with 'big' directly, since spoken Russian skips English filler words like 'the', 'a', and 'is'.
- 'But we do not have direct evidence yet.' Russians say 'by me' or 'with us' where English says 'I have' or 'we have', without needing a separate word for 'have'.
- 'That means we need to quietly infiltrate inside.' Russian expresses feelings and needs by saying 'to me it is needed' or 'to me it seems', putting the person in the 'to me' form.
- 'I propose naming our secret operation Hurricane!' The eager lieutenant suggests a dramatic code name.
- 'Let's go into the dining room, we will find out everything there now!' The two detectives adjust their jackets and enter the dining room.
