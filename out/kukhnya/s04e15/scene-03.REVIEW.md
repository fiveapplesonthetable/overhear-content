## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Good morning, Lyova, pour me some coffee.' Chef Barinov uses a polite everyday Russian courtesy expression that sets the tone of the conversation right away.
- 'Good morning, Viktor Petrovich, how was your celebration?' Lyova greets his boss politely before spotting Eva in the doorway.
- 'Viktor Petrovich, she is a very young girl after all!' Lyova whispers in shock using an emphatic particle and feminine noun phrase.
- 'Quiet, otherwise Viktoriya Sergeyevna will definitely kill me!' Even after the breakup, Barinov dreads Vika or Elena finding out.
- 'Oh, excuse me, please, hello everyone!' Eva breezes into the kitchen right in front of Lyova's mother.
- 'Viktor Petrovich, who is this young woman?' Vera Ivanovna looks inquiringly from Eva to Chef Barinov.
- 'Oh, this is my beloved niece who arrived!' Notice how Russian puts 'favourite, beloved' and 'came, arrived' together in a short, direct phrase where word endings show how the ideas connect.
- 'She came to visit me for a short while.' Here Chef Barinov anchors the thought on 'came, arrived' and finishes on 'guests', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'Why did you never speak about her before?' In everyday Russian, 'why' flows straight into 'spoke, said' without extra linking words.
- 'She flew in from distant Vladivostok for two days!' Barinov picks the farthest city in Russia to explain why nobody has met her.
- 'Vera Ivanovna, excuse us, we are in a terrible hurry!' He cuts the conversation short with a first-person plural present verb.
- 'Lyova, hurry up, it is time for us to go to the kitchen!' Barinov hustles his sous-chef out the front door.
