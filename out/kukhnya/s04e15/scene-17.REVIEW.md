## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Lyova, why did you even let her in here?' In the hallway, Barinov whispers angrily using a masculine past verb.
- 'And what could I do, Chef?' Lyova defends himself with a masculine past modal verb.
- 'Vera Ivanovna, where do you keep your wineglasses?' In the living room, Eva prepares to pour wine for a toast.
- 'The glasses are in the right cupboard, sweet Evochka.' Vera Ivanovna beams at her son's supposed fiancée.
- 'Tell me, please, how did you two meet?' Lyova uses a polite everyday Russian courtesy expression that sets the tone of the conversation right away.
- 'It was my first day at the restaurant.' Here Eva anchors the thought on 'first' and finishes on 'restaurant', keeping the spoken rhythm brisk and natural.
- 'I look, and a solid man is standing there with a cigar!' Since Lyova never smokes cigars, Barinov panics immediately.
- 'Lyova, you are not watching the time at all!' Putting the short word for 'not' right in front of the action word is all Russian needs to make a sentence negative, with no extra helper words like 'do' or 'did'.
- 'We are running late, Katya invited all of us!' He invents an urgent invitation to Claude Monet.
- 'Why did you stage all this in the house?' Outside on the stairs, Barinov confronts Eva about her prank.
- 'Lyova, go take a walk until ten o'clock at night!' Barinov banishes both of them so Vera Ivanovna never learns the truth.
- 'And do not show up in this house anymore!' Chef Barinov pairs 'house' with 'anymore' directly, since spoken Russian skips English filler words like 'the', 'a', and 'is'.
