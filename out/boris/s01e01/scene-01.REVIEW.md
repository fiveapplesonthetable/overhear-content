## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Actually, I'm the intern.' The first word gently corrects her assumption. The article meaning 'the' has this form before a masculine noun beginning with an s followed by another consonant. The intern's job title has the same singular form for a man or a woman.
- 'I just wanted...' The past form of 'want' softens the beginning of his explanation. The final word means 'only' or 'just', but Arianna interrupts before he can say what he wanted.
- 'No, you don't want anything.' Italian puts a negative before 'want' and the word for 'nothing' after it. The two work together; English usually chooses 'not anything' in this sentence.
- 'You stay right here, quietly.' The description 'still' takes its ending from the man being addressed. The two words at the end literally mean 'in silence'; together they tell him not to speak.
- 'And you wait for instructions, okay?' Italian places the thing being awaited directly after 'wait', without a word for 'for'. These are instructions, rather than just one order; the ending on that word tells us there is more than one.
