## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello, I'm Paquita Salas, and I'm an agent.' The same form of am introduces both her name and her job. There is no word for an before agent here: in a simple job description like this, Spanish usually leaves it out.
- 'Paquita is brilliant.' The borrowed word describes someone exceptionally good at something. The word for a before it is masculine here, although she is talking about a woman. This praise word can be used that way; it does not refer to a break or a crack in an object.
- 'Everyone loves Paquita very much.' Everyone is literally all the world, so the verb is singular. The little word before Paquita marks a person as the object of loves. It does not mean that the love is moving towards her.
- 'I shouldn't say it myself, but everyone adores me.' The opening literally says it is bad that I say it. The short word for it points to the boast that follows. Me is emphasized once at the beginning of the boast, and another me still sits just before adores. This doubling puts the spotlight on Paquita herself.
- 'We've been in business for more than twenty-five years.' Spanish uses a present-tense verb with the length of time to say how long something has continued. More than introduces the number; it does not mean exactly twenty-five.
- 'We started out as a modelling agency.' Started describes a completed event. This particular we form is also used for we start. Her story about the agency's beginning tells us that she means the past.
- 'Little by little, we've grown.' The repeated word for little makes the phrase mean gradually. The rest literally says we have gone growing. Spanish uses that pattern for gradual progress up to now.
- 'We're experts at creating success.' The word for experts has an ending meaning more than one, to match we. After experts at, Spanish uses the basic form meaning to create. The final word means success, rather than an exit from a building.
- 'At the moment, we represent Macarena García.' Earlier, this same word introduced the years they have been in business. Here it means represent or manage a client. The little word before Macarena marks the person they represent, and now means at the present time.
- 'Has Maca called?' She uses a short name for Macarena. Has plus called asks whether the call has happened. There is no separate word for she: the name at the end tells us who she is asking about.
- 'No, Maca hasn't called.' The first no is the answer. The second makes the statement negative and sits before has called. Called keeps the same ending here; it does not change to a feminine form because Maca is a woman.
- 'Acting is a profession.' The unconjugated form meaning to act names the activity itself. English uses acting here. Unlike the earlier job description about herself, this sentence includes a word for a before profession.
- 'We try to work with people who understand that very clearly.' Try followed by a small linking word and to work describes an attempt. People is grammatically singular in this Spanish sentence, so the verb after who is singular too. That points back to acting being a profession.
