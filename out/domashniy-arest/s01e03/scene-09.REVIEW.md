## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'We've been going out for a month already.' Russian uses the present tense with 'already' for something that started in the past and is still going on. Then he sighs.
- 'I'm tired of it already.' Literally 'to me it has become boring already'. Slavik is copying Arkady's bragging from the kitchen.
- 'And how did you two meet?' Literally 'how did you with her get acquainted?'. The self-action verb form at the end of the verb means 'each other'.
- 'She was the one who came up to me!' Word for word: 'it is she who got acquainted with me'. Putting 'it is she' first puts all the stress on her.
- 'She was running after me.' Literally 'she ran around me' — feminine past tense, ending in 'a'. In the kitchen Arkady said she circled him like a shark.
- 'All right, you'll have a girlfriend too.' Slavik pats his classmates on the shoulder like an older man.
- 'You've got plenty of years ahead of you.' Literally 'what are your years?' — a set phrase older people say to comfort someone young. Coming from a ten-year-old, it sounds ridiculous. His classmate Dimon grabs the phone.
- 'Hold on, let me have a look! Attention, everyone!' Dimon holds the screen up to the other boys and calls Slavik by his father's nickname, Samson.
- 'Who are you lying to, Samson?' The question word is in the 'to whom' form. Dimon doesn't believe a word of it.
- 'Who needs you?' Literally 'to whom are you needed?', with the masculine short form because Slavik is a boy.
- 'Give back the phone!' A direct command. Then Slavik proudly says her name.
- 'Her name is Lyolya.' Literally 'they call her Lyolya'. Dimon laughs in his face.
- 'Come on! I don't believe you.' In the original scene the boys make a crude schoolyard joke that his only girlfriend is his own hand. Slavik gets angry.
- 'You're just jealous!' Literally 'to you all it is envious simply'. Then he defends her.
- 'She's very beautiful.' Dimon sees a way to test his story.
- 'Well, since you're so cool, maybe you'll introduce us?' Here the little word for 'time' means 'since' or 'if'. And Dimon issues the dare.
- 'Bring her to Dimon's party tonight!' Dimon is hosting a party at his flat that evening, and the boys dare Slavik to bring Lyolya along. Slavik tries to back out.
- 'She isn't like that.' Adding 'by me' is a warm, possessive way of saying 'my girl isn't that sort'.
- 'She doesn't go to parties.' The verb is the habitual 'goes', for something someone does or doesn't do in general.
- 'In short, Samson, tonight you bring her to Dimon's place.' The first word literally means 'shorter' and is how Russian speakers say 'bottom line'.
- 'Or else you'll get a new nickname! Weakling!' The two little words at the start mean 'otherwise' or 'or else'. In the show the boys threaten him with a much ruder school nickname.
