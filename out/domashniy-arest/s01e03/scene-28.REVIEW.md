## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Right, let's go over it again.' Literally, 'let's repeat'. Lyolya recites her lines like an actress.
- 'I'm your girlfriend, we've been dating for a month.' The self-action verb form for 'meeting each other' is the standard way to say 'dating' or 'going out'. And she remembers the most important detail for Slavik's pride.
- 'I ask you all the time: may I do this, may I do that.' The word for 'may I' literally means 'it is possible' or 'it is allowed'.
- 'Uh-huh.' That is how Slavik nods in agreement, and then Lyolya adds the final part of the deal.
- 'And after ten minutes I leave.' After numbers from five upwards, 'minutes' takes the plural 'of' form, with no ending at all. She gives the nervous boy a pat on the shoulder.
- 'Don't be afraid, everything will be fine.' Then they step inside the flat, and Dimon stares in disbelief.
- 'Guys, Samson's here!' At school the boys call Slavik 'Samson' after his father and their surname, Samsonov. Slavik steps forward proudly.
- 'Meet her, Dim. This is my girlfriend Lyolya.' The command for 'get acquainted' is singular because he is speaking to Dimon first. Lyolya turns on the charm.
- 'I've always dreamed of meeting my boyfriend's friends.' Because Lyolya is a woman, the past tense 'dreamed' ends in 'a'. Then she looks around the room.
- 'And is there anything to drink?' Literally, 'and to drink is there what?' Dimon answers.
- 'Only beer.' After all, it's a schoolboys' party. Lyolya shrugs.
- 'Beer it is. Oh, I forgot.' Repeating a noun with 'then' in the middle is a handy Russian idiom for 'so be it, that'll do'. Then she remembers her role.
- 'Darling, may I drink a tiny drop of beer?' She calls the ten-year-old 'darling' and uses the diminutive 'little drop', followed by 'of beer' in the 'of' form. Slavik plays it cool in front of his stunned friends.
- 'Yes.' One word is all he needs.
- 'Thank you, baby.' Every boy in the room is speechless.
