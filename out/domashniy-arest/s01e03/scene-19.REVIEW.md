## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Well, and what is all this?' A classic angry opening question. Nina thinks he is looking at the tools on the table.
- 'What? You bought everything yourself.' The little word after 'you' means 'after all', and 'yourself' is masculine for Samson. Samson waves that away.
- 'That's not what I mean.' Word for word: 'yes I am not about this'.
- 'Who was that guy in the corridor?' Literally 'who is this type in the corridor'. Russian drops 'was' and uses the location ending '-e' after 'in'. Nina works out whom he means.
- 'Which one? You mean Danya?' Adding the two little words at the end turns a name into 'do you mean Danya?'.
- 'Well, he's Anikeev's lawyer.' The surname takes the '-a' ending to mean 'of Anikeev'. Samson mocks the sweet names and compliments he heard in the hall.
- 'Danya, Danyusha!' Both are affectionate short forms of the name Danila.
- 'Look how beautiful you've become!' Both 'what a' and 'beautiful' have feminine endings for Nina. Nina rolls her eyes and calls her husband by his surname.
- 'What's wrong with you, Samsonov? We're just classmates.' The word for 'classmates' is built from 'one' and 'class': people from the same class. Samson does not buy it.
- 'Classmates, right!' He repeats the word sarcastically.
- 'I don't understand how you could be with him.' Literally 'how you with him could', with the feminine past ending '-la'. Nina is baffled.
- 'Could what?' In casual speech, 'what' often appears in its 'of' form.
- 'He was like a girlfriend to me.' Notice the feminine word for a female friend: she means Danila was like one of the girls at school, never a boyfriend. Samson scoffs.
- 'A girlfriend! We know that kind of girlfriend.' In the second sentence the noun is in the object form plural, which for animate nouns looks just like the plural 'of' form. Then he makes a threat.
- 'I'm telling you seriously.' Word for word: 'I to you seriously speak'.
- 'Let him just show up here one more time!' The first word makes a third-person command: 'let him appear'.
- 'Stuttering won't be his only problem with me! So there.' Literally 'he with me not only to stutter will'. And he finishes with a satisfied 'there!'. Nina sees a way to put her husband's temper to use.
- 'What?' She pauses for a second as the idea clicks.
- 'Go to Anikeev in just that mood.' After 'with', both 'such' and 'mood' take the 'with/as' ending. And after 'towards', Anikeev's surname takes the '-u' ending. Then the practical reminder.
- 'Check the soldering iron.' A brisk command at the end. Remember, they only want to scare Arkady into telling where the forty million roubles are.
