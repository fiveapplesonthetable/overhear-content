## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'What are you doing in here, Marina Sergeyevna?' They always use the polite 'you' with each other. Marina quickly invents an excuse.
- 'I dropped in to apologise.' The verb at the end is feminine past, meaning 'stepped in briefly on the way'. Arkady raises an eyebrow.
- 'For what, exactly?' Literally 'for what is this?'. Marina thinks of their quarrel in the courtyard the day before.
- 'Yesterday I said too much to you.' The prefix on the verb means piling up a lot of words, and the last word means 'extra' or 'things I shouldn't have said'. Arkady enjoys this.
- 'Ah, yes. You ought to be ashamed.' Literally 'to you it must be shameful'. That is too much for Marina's pride.
- 'I'm not ashamed. I'll go.' Literally 'to me not shameful'. She edges toward the door, still hiding the bug behind her back, and Arkady gets suspicious.
- 'Stop! What are you doing in my room?' Then he guesses she might be stealing.
- 'Did you take something of mine?' He uses the plural past form because he addresses her with the polite 'you'.
- 'Well, show me your hands. What is behind your back?' After the preposition 'behind', 'back' takes the 'with/as' ending.
- 'I didn't take anything.' Remember that Russian uses double negation: 'nothing not took', with the feminine past ending in 'a'.
- 'Show me your right hand! Well?' Reluctantly, Marina holds out her hand with the small foil packet the agents used to disguise the bug. Arkady's face lights up with smug delight.
- 'Aha! So that's what this is about.' An everyday idiom: literally 'here it is in what the matter is'. He completely misunderstands why she has that packet.
- 'No, it isn't what you thought!' Notice how 'yes no' together just means an emphatic 'no, not at all!'. Marina cannot explain the bug without giving herself away.
- 'What is there to think about? Everything is clear.' In the show Arkady launches into a pompous speech, convinced that Marina is in love with him and came to seduce him.
- 'I am glad to help you.' The little particle after 'I' means 'would', and 'glad' is masculine because Arkady is a man.
- 'But unfortunately you are not my type.' Word for word: 'not in my taste'. Then the doorbell rings.
- 'Unfortunately, I'm busy.' Again, a masculine short form.
- 'Mishkin has come to see me. Business!' He thinks his expensive Moscow lawyer has arrived for the evening hearing, and waves Marina out of the room.
