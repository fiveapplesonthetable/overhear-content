## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Well, have you calmed down? Where's the money?' The verb has the masculine past ending plus the reflexive tail at the end. Arkady gives the same answer as before.
- 'I'm telling you, I don't have any money.' After 'there isn't', both 'any' and 'money' take the genitive plural ending. Then he warns both of them.
- 'They're tricking you!' Literally 'they are deceiving you', using the third-person plural with no subject to mean 'Pasha is playing you'. Samson decides to act tough and grabs Arkady's bandaged finger.
- 'Right, that's it. I'm squeezing your finger right now!' He barely touches it, but Arkady screams anyway.
- 'No, don't! Don't, it hurts!' Literally 'to me it is painful'. That is enough to make big Samson lose his nerve.
- 'Okay, that's enough. I can't do this.' He lets go at once and sits down, looking upset himself. Nina is furious with her husband.
- 'Vanya, what is wrong with you?' She tells him he didn't even squeeze Arkady's finger.
- 'He just started getting scared, and you stopped!' Both verbs are masculine past forms, because both Arkady and Samson are men. And then Samson blurts out the one thing he shouldn't say.
- 'Well, what am I supposed to do?' Literally 'what to me to do?'.
- 'We agreed to just scare him!' He says it right in front of the man tied to the chair. Nina can't believe her ears.
- 'Vanya, are you stupid?' A short masculine adjective.
- 'Why did you just say that in front of him?' Notice the little preposition before 'him': it means 'in the presence of', 'in front of'. And Nina knows the game is up.
- 'Now he has worked everything out!' Literally 'now he already everything understood'. Arkady's fear vanishes on the spot.
- 'I get it now!' The exact same masculine past verb: 'I understood everything'.
- 'Untie me, please.' He starts politely, addressing the two of them with the plural command. When they don't move, he gets impatient.
- 'Samson, I'm tired.' Literally 'I got tired'.
- 'I still have to go to court today!' Remember, the evening hearing with Judge Berg is today. That's when Nina steps forward.
- 'Listen here, four-eyes!' She uses Samson's old schoolyard nickname for Arkady, from the word for glasses.
- 'If he's a pushover, I'm not.' The word for 'pushover' literally means 'a rag'—someone soft and spineless. And Nina reminds him of her job.
- 'I'm a medical worker.' She works as a nurse at the hospital, and she has brought something from work.
- 'This right here is a scalpel.' She holds up a real surgical blade in front of Arkady's face.
- 'I'm going to cut off your ear right now!' Literally 'to you right now I will cut off the ear'. Arkady still thinks they are bluffing.
- 'Oh, two losers!' He uses the borrowed English word 'loser' with a Russian plural ending after 'two'.
- 'Untie me, fast!' Here he uses the plural past tense as a barked order—a bossy Russian way to say 'get it done this instant!'. As he jerks his head and mocks her, the scalpel slips and nicks his ear.
- 'What is this? Is this blood?' He feels something warm on his neck, and all three of them freeze.
