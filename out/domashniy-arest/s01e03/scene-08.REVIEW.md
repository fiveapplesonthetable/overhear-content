## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Well then, did you scare him?' The prefix on the verb makes it single-action form: did you actually manage to frighten him? Samson boasts about his terrible threats.
- 'I told him everything!' Literally, 'I to him everything said', masculine past tense.
- 'I told him I'd cut his ear off.' Just as in English, Russian keeps the future tense inside 'told him that I will cut off'. And he didn't stop at the ear.
- 'And take his eye out with a spoon.' The first word is 'spoon' in the 'with/as' form, which means 'with a spoon, using a spoon'. Nina wants to know the result.
- 'And what did he do?' Literally, 'and what he?'. Three tiny words do the whole job. Samson looks deflated.
- 'Nothing! He just sits there smiling.' Both verbs are in the present tense, making the picture vivid: he sits and smiles. Nina hears Samson's sheepish tone.
- 'And did you try to scare him in that voice?' Now the verb 'scared' has no prefix, so it is ongoing-action form: 'how you were trying to scare him'. 'In that voice' uses the 'with/as' form again. Samson defends himself.
- 'No, I scared him properly, sternly!' Both adverbs end in 'o', the usual ending for Russian words that tell how you do something. Nina thinks of the three-room flat with the balcony.
- 'There it is, our new flat, Samson, do you understand?' Literally, 'here she is, our new flat', because the word for 'flat' is feminine. It is right within reach.
- 'And you can't do a thing!' Again, Russian uses a double negative: 'nothing to do not can'.
- 'You're talking some kind of childish nonsense.' Literally, 'carrying some kind of baby babble', the idiom Russian uses for talking nonsense. Samson throws his hands up.
- 'Well, what are you suggesting?' Present tense, second person singular.
- 'That I really cut his ear off?' After 'that, in order that', the verb is in the past-tense form. And then he hands the problem over to his wife.
- 'You're the clever one here!' Literally, 'you after all among us are so clever', feminine ending on both words.
- 'Tell me what to do, and I'll do it.' Notice the pair: the basic verb form 'what to do', and then the future 'I will do it'. Nina takes him at his word.
- 'What to do? Let's go to the shop.' And in a few scenes we'll see what kind of shop she means.
