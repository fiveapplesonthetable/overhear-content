## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Where's the dog? Where is our Lyutik?' Putting the word for 'where' after 'dog' makes the question sound sharp and urgent. Samson has no idea why this stranger is in their corridor.
- 'Right, Slavik, who is this? What happened?' The verb 'happened' has the neuter past ending because 'what' is grammatically neuter. Judge Berg answers for the boy.
- 'Is this your son?' He uses the polite plural 'your' with the parents.
- 'He stole our dog!' The noun for 'dog' and the word for 'our' both change their '-a' ending to '-u' as the direct object. In the show Berg adds that it was for blackmail. Samson stares at his son.
- 'A dog? Why did you steal a dog?' Repeating 'dog' in the object case means 'what do you mean, a dog?'. Frightened Slavik blurts out that Uncle Arkasha asked him to, and he didn't do it on purpose. Samson can hardly believe his ears.
- 'Uncle Arkasha asked you?!' Children in Russia call grown-up family friends 'Uncle' or 'Aunt' plus their short first name.
- 'Give back the dog!' Samson gives Slavik a cuff on the head and tells him to hand the little dog over right now. Polina hugs her beloved Lyutik, and Judge Berg draws himself up.
- 'For your information, I am the chairman of the court.' The first two words literally mean 'between other things', the everyday idiom for 'by the way' or 'for your information'. After 'chairman', 'court' takes the 'of' ending. And he gives them a warning.
- 'Say thank you that the dog was found.' The verb 'was found' is literally 'found itself', with the self-action verb form at the back. Otherwise, things would have been much worse for Slavik.
- 'Otherwise he would have ended up in a colony!' The two little words at the start mean 'or else', and the particle after them makes it 'would have'. By a 'colony', he means a juvenile detention colony.
- 'Actually, I haven't decided yet. Let's go!' The last word is literally 'went' in the plural past, which Russians use as a brisk 'let's go!'. Berg and Polina leave with Lyutik, and Samson slowly walks into Arkady's room.
- 'What's up, Vanya?' Arkady is still sitting at the table, not yet realising what just happened in the hall. Samson's voice is dangerously quiet.
- 'Did you decide to make my son a criminal?' Literally, 'to make a criminal out of my son'. Both 'my son' after 'out of' and 'criminal' as the animate object take the '-a' ending. Arkady blinks.
- 'I don't understand.' Literally, 'I didn't understand', in the masculine past tense. That is the last straw for Samson.
- 'What did you not understand? What were you doing?!' He steps right up to Arkady, furious that Arkady dragged little Slavik into stealing a judge's dog. Arkady backs away.
- 'Vanya, listen! Listen to me!' Two urgent commands from the single-action form 'to listen'. But Samson doesn't listen.
