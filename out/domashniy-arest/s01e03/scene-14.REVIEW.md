## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Judge Berg speaking.' Literally 'Judge Berg, I am listening' — a standard formal way to answer the phone.
- 'Hello, Ilyusha, hi. It's Anikeev.' Arkady uses the informal greeting 'hi' and the pet name 'Ilyusha'. Berg immediately puts up a wall.
- 'Hello. I recognised your voice. What did you want?' Notice that Berg answers with the formal 'hello' and calls Arkady by the polite 'you'. Arkady complains.
- 'Why are you so unfriendly?' Literally 'not-affectionate'.
- 'That's not very neighbourly.' Adverbs built with this prefix and suffix mean 'in the manner of': here, 'like a neighbour'. Berg reminds him of the law.
- 'I'm not allowed to talk to you at all.' Literally 'to me with you at all to communicate is forbidden'.
- 'Good day.' Literally 'of everything good' — a polite, final goodbye. Arkady stops him before he hangs up.
- 'Wait, don't hang up!' Literally 'don't put down the receiver'.
- 'I have a business offer.' Now Arkady lays out the deal.
- 'You'll release me on a travel ban.' He uses the future-tense form: 'you will let me out'.
- 'You won't owe me anything.' Literally 'obliged to me by nothing'. Notice how Berg now drops the formal 'you' too.
- 'Interesting. I don't owe you anything anyway.' That little pair of words in the middle means 'even so, as it is'. So Arkady reminds him of a favour.
- 'Really?' An everyday expression of disbelief.
- 'And who got your mother a flat?' Literally 'made a flat for your mum', with 'your mum' in the 'to/for' form.
- 'Under the Affordable Housing programme! Did you forget?' That's the town housing scheme for people in need. Berg defends himself.
- 'My mother had a right to the flat. She is a pensioner.' 'A right to' uses the preposition 'onto' plus the object-form. Arkady points out the catch.
- 'Half the town had the right here!' Arkady pairs the verb for 'had' with 'right' in the direct-object form.
- 'But she didn't stand in the queue.' Ordinary people wait years on the housing waiting list. Then Arkady tries to blackmail the judge.
- 'And what if they find out about this in court?' Literally 'if about this they find out on the court'. Berg turns the threat right back on him.
- 'Then I'll give you two more years!' Judge Berg uses the informal singular 'you' here with 'years', as friends and close colleagues do.
- 'For property fraud.' After all, Arkady was the mayor who signed the papers! Berg hangs up, and suddenly Golovkin, who has heard every word in the room, speaks up in shock.
- 'Did you hand out waiting-list flats through connections?' 'Through connections' — pulling strings for friends — is a classic Russian phrase.
- 'I've been standing in the queue for fifteen years!' Russian uses the present tense for an action that started in the past and is still going on. Arkady has just confessed his own housing fraud to a lawyer who has been waiting in that very queue for fifteen years! Arkady quickly sends him out.
- 'Leave, please. I really need to think.' And Golovkin leaves very quietly.
- 'Goodbye. I'll be in touch with you.' Literally 'I will connect with you'.
