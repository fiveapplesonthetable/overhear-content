## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Nina, look what I found! A fishing rod!' The noun for 'fishing rod' has the object-form '-u' ending because it's the object of 'found'. Nina is not impressed.
- 'It's the first time I've seen anyone scare people with a fishing rod.' Notice the 'with/as' ending on 'with a fishing rod'. Samson tries to justify his choice.
- 'There's fishing line and a sharp hook here. It's very painful!' Nina sees right through him.
- 'Samson, I don't understand. Don't you want the flat?' Remember Pasha promised them a three-room flat with a balcony if they scare the truth out of Arkady. Samson answers honestly.
- 'I want a fishing rod. Both that one and the other one!' Both pronouns are feminine object-form to match 'fishing rod'. Nina shuts that down with one word.
- 'No you can't!' Literally 'it is not allowed'. Then Samson voices his real doubt.
- 'And what if there is no flat?' With negative 'will not be', Russian puts 'this flat' into the 'of' form.
- 'With an attitude like that, of course there won't be.' The word for 'mood' or 'attitude' is in the 'with/as' form after 'with'.
- 'Stop it! We will have the flat.' Literally 'there will be by us a flat'. Samson picks up a tiny tool from the shelf.
- 'All right. Shall we show this to Anikeev?' The future-tense form 'we will show' asks 'shall we show?'. Nina rolls her eyes.
- 'Have you lost your mind? He won't be scared!' Literally 'have you stepped down from your mind?' — a very common everyday idiom.
- 'Right, we are not joking, understood? Find a scarier tool.' The last word is the comparative form: 'scarier' or 'more frightening'. Then Samson spots a big pair of garden shears.
- 'Oh, look, cheap and effective!' Word for word: 'both cheap and angry' — a famous Russian idiom for something inexpensive that really does the job.
- 'And at the same time I'll trim the branches in the yard.' Practical to the end: even when buying a tool to frighten the mayor, Samson is thinking about yard work.
