## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'This needs to be put in Anikeev's room.' Literally, 'this is necessary to put'. Anikeev's surname takes the 'of' ending, meaning 'room of Anikeev'. Captain Zavarzin reassures her.
- 'It's simple, it's on a sticky pad.' He calls the little bug 'he' because the Russian word for a bug is masculine. The last word comes from the verb 'to stick', so it means a sticky pad or Velcro. Lavrov tells her where it goes.
- 'Stick it at head height, in a quiet spot.' He uses the polite plural command form because they address Marina respectfully. 'At the level of the head' uses the 'of' form of 'head'. Marina sees an obvious problem.
- 'How will I do that?' A single-action form in the present form always points to the future: 'how will I get that done?'
- 'He's in the room all the time.' Russian needs no word for 'is' in the present tense, just 'he all time in room'. Zavarzin points out that even someone under house arrest leaves his room.
- 'But he goes out to the bathroom, to the kitchen.' Notice how 'in the room' used the location ending, while 'to the bathroom' and 'to the kitchen' use the object ending for movement towards a place.
- 'What, am I supposed to wait by the door?' Literally, 'to me what, to wait by the door?' Using 'to me' with an action verb is how Russian asks 'am I supposed to do this?'. Suddenly she wonders how she got into this mess.
- 'I've never done anything like this.' Because Marina is a woman, the past-tense verb ends in 'a' before the self-action verb form. And Russian stacks negatives: 'never not did'.
- 'What am I doing here?' Four simple everyday words. Then she sighs and gives in.
- 'Fine, let me try.' Literally, 'let's I will try', a polite, natural way to offer to give something a go. She reaches for the bug, but Lavrov stops her.
- 'Wait, that's a sample.' That first bug was only for demonstration.
- 'And here is yours.' He hands her a small square foil packet instead. Marina stares at it in disbelief.
- 'What is this? Why are you giving me this?' Literally, 'for what to me this?'. To make sure nobody suspects a listening device if it is found in her pocket, they have hidden it inside a small foil wrapper.
- 'There's the same kind of bug inside.' Literally, 'there inside is such-same little beetle'. In Russian, a listening bug is affectionately called a 'little beetle'. Zavarzin explains the disguise.
- 'Marina Sergeyevna, the packaging is camouflage.' He addresses her by first name and father's name, completely deadpan.
- 'It doesn't raise suspicion.' Literally, 'does not call out suspicions', in the plural 'of' form after a negative. Lavrov adds a helpful remark.
- 'Everyone has one of these.' Literally, 'such a thing is at everyone'. Marina has the last word.
- 'I don't.' Literally, 'at me there isn't', the exact mirror of 'at everyone there is'.
