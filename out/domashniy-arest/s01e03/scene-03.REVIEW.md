## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'It's cold in here! They can't even put a heater in.' The word he uses for 'heater' is the everyday word for a stove. Major Lavrov tells him to be patient, because they have plugged in an electric radiator.
- 'Quiet! Everything will warm up in a moment.' A moment later the heater starts working.
- 'Oh, it's warm at last! Thank goodness. Now I'll make some tea too.' And with that, Zavarzin switches on the electric kettle at the same time—and trips the fuse, plunging the room into darkness.
- 'What, did you leave your head in Moscow?' After 'in', the name Moscow takes the location ending.
- 'I've told you a hundred times!' Literally 'I to you a hundred of times said'.
- 'You can't turn on the heater and the kettle together.' The first word means 'it is not allowed' or 'one cannot', followed by the basic verb form. Once the power is back on, Zavarzin aims their directional microphone at the windows of the communal flat.
- 'Just a moment, Comrade Major, everything's fine. Switching on the microphone.' Literally 'we switch on'.
- 'Ours is the second floor, right? Go on, let's listen.' First they catch a snippet of Marina telling her ex on the phone that she doesn't want to see him, and then they hear the old neighbour, Klavdia Viktorovna, discussing her digestion with a friend.
- 'Hello, Tanechka! At my age that's normal.' The word for 'hello' here is the one used only when answering the telephone.
- 'I've already eaten prunes. Nothing helps!' The verb 'ate' has the feminine past ending '-a', because Klavdia is a woman. Lavrov has heard quite enough about prunes.
- 'Right, can you cut out all this noise?' Literally 'can you remove all this noise?'.
- 'We only need Anikeev.' Literally 'to us is needed only Anikeev', with the masculine short form of 'needed'.
- 'Comrade Major, you can barely hear anything here. There won't be any quality anyway.' After 'there won't be', the noun for 'quality' goes into the 'of' form. So Lavrov makes the call.
- 'Then we need to plant a bug.' The Russian word for a listening bug literally means 'little beetle'.
- 'Understood. Marina Sergeyevna will help us.' Remember that at the end of the last episode, Marina reluctantly agreed to help the security service.
