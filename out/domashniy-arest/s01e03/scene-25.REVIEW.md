## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Has the bleeding stopped yet?' Literally 'did the blood stop there already?'. The word for 'blood' is feminine, so the past verb ends in the feminine form.
- 'Look how much blood came out!' After 'how much', 'blood' takes its 'of'-form ending. Samson winds the bandage so tightly round Arkady's neck and chin that Arkady can hardly breathe.
- 'Samson, don't choke me.' Even tied to a chair, Arkady sounds more annoyed than frightened now. Samson rushes to make him feel better.
- 'Arkasha, here's some hot tea.' Literally 'hold hot tea'—and since Arkady's hands are tied, Samson holds the mug for him.
- 'And eat a jam sandwich.' Literally 'a sandwich with jam', because sweet things are good for shock. Then Samson turns on Nina, while putting Arkady's glasses back on for him.
- 'Nina, how could you?' The past verb has the feminine ending because he is speaking to his wife.
- 'Cutting a living person with a knife!' Notice 'with a knife' is a single word in the 'with/as' form, the form for the tool you use. Nina apologises and says Arkady provoked her, while Arkady tastes the sandwich and frowns.
- 'Wait, what is this, apricot?' He is tied to a chair with a bandaged head, and he's complaining about the flavour of the jam.
- 'I hate apricots.' Then he makes a polite request.
- 'Do you have raspberry?' Literally 'is there by you raspberry?'. Samson checks the kitchen shelf.
- 'No raspberry.' Because it is absent, 'raspberry' switches to the 'of' form.
- 'There's cherry jam, do you want some?' Using 'will you?' on its own is the everyday Russian way to offer food or drink. Arkady gives his royal approval.
- 'Yes. Bring your cherry.' Just then the door opens, and Pasha—the man who promised Samson and Nina the three-room flat—walks in.
- 'Well, did he confess?' One short masculine past verb. Arkady can't see Pasha clearly behind him, or pretends not to.
- 'Samson, who is that?' Then he gives Samson an order as if Samson were his butler.
- 'I want him to leave.' After 'so that'—which is how Russian says 'want someone to do something'—the verb always goes into the past tense. Samson awkwardly explains the situation to Pasha.
- 'Things went wrong for us.' Literally 'by us everything went not so'.
- 'Nina nearly cut his ear off.' There is that 'nearly didn't' pattern again: 'almost cut off'. Pasha doesn't care at all.
- 'So what?' Cold and smooth as ever.
- 'He is supposed to suffer, and we need the information.' Even big Samson is shocked by how cold Pasha is.
- 'You can't torture a living person like that!' Samson only ever meant to scare Arkady, not hurt him. And now Arkady sees his chance to break Pasha's deal completely.
- 'Then untie me!' He shouts to Samson and Nina, and then threatens Pasha.
- 'I'll tell the police everything!' Literally 'I will say everything in the police'.
- 'I'll say that you hired people to torture me.' And then he turns the threat on Samson and Nina's reward.
- 'And you'll never see that flat!' Literally 'not to see to you of the flat'—a vivid Russian idiom for 'you can kiss that flat goodbye'. Pasha and Samson square up to each other, and Samson has had enough.
- 'We don't need a flat like that! Get out of here!' Samson throws away the dream of the three-room flat rather than torture a man for Pasha, and Pasha leaves empty-handed.
