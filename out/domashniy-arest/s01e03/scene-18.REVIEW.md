## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Please, don't chase me away.' He starts with 'please' and a negative command: 'don't drive me out'.
- 'I know it's my fault.' Word for word: 'I know that I am guilty'. The short adjective at the end is masculine because Edik is a man. Then he shows his peace offerings.
- 'This is an electric guitar for Lyosha.' After 'for', the boy's name takes the 'of' ending.
- 'And for you I brought Chilean wine and berries.' Literally 'to you I took'. 'Wine' is neuter in Russian, so the adjective 'Chilean' ends in '-oye'. Marina is not won over.
- 'Edik, please leave.' She uses his short name, Edik, and the informal command 'go away'. Then Edik looks down at the table and sees the small foil wrapper with the hidden bug inside.
- 'What is this?' Three short words of pure shock. Marina tries to brush it off.
- 'It's not what you think.' Literally 'this is not that which you thought'. And she tries to push him out the door.
- 'That's it, get out.' Literally 'all, go away'. Now the dean is furious.
- 'What do you mean, get out?' Word for word: 'what means, go away?'.
- 'I demand an explanation, Marina.' In Russian he says 'explanations' in the plural, in the 'of' case after the verb 'demand'. Marina fires right back.
- 'What explanation? Am I your wife?' Remember, the dean is married and fired her from the university to save his own skin. At the worst possible moment, Arkady strolls in.
- 'Eduard Vladimirovich, hello.' Arkady greets the dean politely by name and patronymic, delighted by the drama.
- 'And what about Alla Yevgenyevna?' That is the dean's wife. Arkady loves reminding him that he is a married man.
- 'Hello. Leave Alla Yevgenyevna alone!' Literally 'leave Alla Yevgenyevna in peace'. Then he tells Arkady to mind his own business.
- 'Don't meddle in our private life.' Literally 'it is not necessary to climb into our personal life'. Then he connects Arkady with the foil packet on the table.
- 'Marina, is this for him?' Since Marina cannot reveal that the packet holds a secret-service microphone, she makes up a story that also hurts Edik.
- 'Edik, I wanted to cheat on you.' 'I wanted' has the feminine '-a' ending. The verb 'to betray' or 'cheat on' takes the 'to' case: 'to you'.
- 'With him, to take revenge.' 'With' takes the 'with'-form form of 'he', which starts with an 'n' after a preposition. Then she adds an insult for Arkady.
- 'But he is too disgusting, and I changed my mind.' The prefix on the verb at the end means 're-think' or 'change one's mind'. Arkady's pride cannot take that.
- 'I'm disgusting?' He drops the feminine ending and uses the masculine '-iy' for himself.
- 'Marina, listen, I was the one who turned you down!' Even while arguing about an imaginary affair, they stay on the formal 'you'. The dean has heard enough.
- 'That's it, I'm leaving.' Present tense for an action happening right now.
- 'The child isn't to blame.' An idiomatic phrase meaning 'the child has nothing to do with this'.
- 'Give this to Alexei.' He uses Lyosha's full name, Alexei, in the 'to' case, and leaves the guitar behind. Arkady spots an opportunity.
- 'And what about the wine and fruit?' After 'with', 'fruit' is in the 'with/as' form plural. The dean shoves the bag into Arkady's arms.
- 'You need this right now.' Literally 'this to you now is necessary'. And he storms out while Marina calls after him.
- 'Edik, wait! I'm begging you.' Two short pleas, but the door is already shut.
