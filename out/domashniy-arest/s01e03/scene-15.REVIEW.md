## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Danka Golovkin, goodness!' In this moment, 'Danka' is the familiar schoolyard form of Danila. She turns to her husband Samson, whose real name is Vanya.
- 'Vanya, this is Danka Golovkin, do you remember him?' In spoken Russian, names ending in a vowel often lose that final vowel when you call someone: 'Vanya' becomes 'Van'. Samson grumbles.
- 'Of course I remember. Your school friend.' Samson looks at him with immediate suspicion, so Nina sends Samson on to their room.
- 'Vanya, go on, I'll come right now.' 'I will come' is the future-tense form. Then she turns back to Danya.
- 'Listen, we haven't seen each other in a hundred years, right?' Just as in English, 'a hundred years' means 'ages'. The verb has the self-action verb form, which here means 'each other'. Danya gives the exact number.
- 'Nineteen years.' Exact and lawyerly. Nina smiles.
- 'Really? Listen, you've changed so much, you've blossomed!' Both past-tense verbs are masculine, because she is speaking to a man. The second verb literally means 'came into bloom'. Danya returns the compliment.
- 'And you haven't changed at all.' Now the same verb has the feminine past ending before the self-action verb form suffix, because he is speaking to Nina.
- 'You're just as beautiful!' Literally 'such the same beauty'. Nina is delighted, and notices something else.
- 'Thank you, thank you! And you stutter less.' Then she realises that might sound tactless.
- 'Sorry, I didn't mean to offend you.' 'I didn't want' has the feminine past ending.
- 'And what are you doing here?' In response, Danya points back towards Arkady's door.
- 'I'm the lawyer for that neighbour. For Anikeev.' The preposition 'by, at' with the 'of' form is a natural way to say whose lawyer you are. Nina is impressed.
- 'You're a lawyer? That's great, well done!' That last word literally means 'fine fellow' and is used for 'good job!' or 'well done!'. From inside their room, Samson calls out impatiently.
- 'Nina, well, are you coming? I'm waiting!' So they say a quick goodbye.
- 'Yes, Vanya, I'm coming now! Well, Danya, bye.' And Danya replies warmly.
- 'Well, take care, bye, Nina.' Golovkin puts 'bye' up front and finishes with 'bye' to make the point unmistakable.
