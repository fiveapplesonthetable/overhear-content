## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Danya, I thought you had a cottage.' 'Danya' is the short direct-address form of Daniil.
- 'And you have a real house!' Golovkin brushes off the praise modestly.
- 'Come on, Anton, you call this a house.' Then Samson inspects the woodwork more closely.
- 'And did you do the renovation yourself?' Golovkin immediately guesses what's coming.
- 'What, are the seams uneven everywhere?' Samson points right at the wall planks.
- 'Oh, look, you have gaps over there!' To express possession, Russian uses the preposition 'by' plus the 'of me/you' pronoun followed by 'gaps'.
- 'I'm a builder, I know my stuff.' That self-action verb form literally means 'I sort things out, I understand a subject well'. Nina steps in politely.
- 'Well I like it, Danya.' Notice the 'to'-form 'to me'.
- 'Good job, it's very cosy.' Then Samson opens his shirt collar and looks down at his chest.
- 'What is this I have here?' Nina glances at the mark.
- 'A bruise.' Samson looks closer and recognizes the shape.
- 'That's a bruise from a steering wheel!' After 'from', the noun 'steering wheel' is in the 'of' form.
- 'In what car? Did you wreck the car or what?' Remember, in Episode 4 Golovkin actually hit drunken Samson with his car! As Nina freezes, Golovkin invents a second heroic story on the spot.
- 'You caught bandits!' Golovkin blurts out.
- 'Who robbed the ATM on Garibaldi Street.' Remember the exploded ATM from Scene 1! Samson looks at Nina suspiciously.
- 'Nina, what is he saying?' Nina plays along with the lie.
- 'No, he's telling it all right!' Notice that 'yes no' at the start simply means an emphatic 'no!'. Encouraged, Golovkin elaborates.
- 'They jumped into a car and drove off.' And according to Golovkin, Samson gave chase.
- 'You drove after them in the car.' The verb for 'drove' is in the masculine past tense, marked by the '-l' ending.
- 'And rammed them head-on!' Literally 'and right into the forehead!'. Golovkin acts amazed that Samson forgot.
- 'Don't you remember anything at all?' Flattered again, Samson pretends it's coming back to him.
- 'I'm trying to recall it.' Samson uses the adverb for 'sort of' right alongside the verb meaning 'am recalling'.
- 'So I wrecked the car, then?' Golovkin waves it away.
- 'And the car is insured, don't be upset.' The negative particle sits directly before the verb for 'is insured' to negate the action.
