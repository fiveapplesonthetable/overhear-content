## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'What was I drinking? Fizzy wine!' The word Samson uses for sparkling wine comes from the verb 'to fizz' and is in the direct-object form after the verb 'was drinking'.
- 'Do I have to breathe? Breathe into the tube?' Literally 'it is necessary to breathe', followed by the preposition 'into' with the direct-object form of 'little tube'.
- 'Let me go, please! Let me go!' He uses the polite plural command of the single-action form 'to let go'.
- 'Three and a half per mille?' Per mille is parts per thousand of blood alcohol — three and a half is an enormous reading. Notice the 'with/as' form after 'with': 'three with a half'.
- 'I have seven loans!' After numbers from five upwards, Russian nouns take the plural 'of' form. As the police lead him away, Samson calls out to his lawyer friend Danila Golovkin.
- 'No, don't! Danya, come on, tell them!' Danila tries to intervene, while back at the communal flat Arkady and Ilona stare at the television screen.
- 'Look at the news!' Arkady points at the screen.
- 'A terrible crash happened at the Society for the Blind!' The preposition for 'by' or 'at' takes the 'of' form of 'society', and 'of the blind' is the plural 'of' form of the adjective used as a noun.
- 'At huge speed the car crashed into the building!' Notice how 'on huge speed' uses the location form, while 'into the building' uses the direct-object form for motion into a place.
- 'A fire started there! Fire engines and police are everywhere!' Literally 'fire machines and police'. Ilona buries her face in her hands.
- 'Well, what can I tell you, Arkasha?' She uses the future-tense form 'what will I say to you' and the warm diminutive of Arkady's name.
- 'This is a total nightmare!' And she regrets turning down Mayor Pasha Osipenko.
- 'What a fool I am!' Literally 'here I am a fool'.
- 'I should have worked for Osipenko.' Literally 'it was necessary to work on Osipenko' — working for a boss uses the preposition 'on' with the direct-object form.
- 'Easy, easy! Not everything is lost yet!' Literally 'quietly, quietly', using the short passive form 'lost' in the neuter form.
