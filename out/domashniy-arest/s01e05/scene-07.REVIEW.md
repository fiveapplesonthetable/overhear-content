## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Come on, Uncle Grisha, we did our job fine.' The prefix on the verb means working through a task from start to finish. Uncle Grisha stops on the landing and glares at him.
- 'Did you hear what she said? I'm not afraid of you!' Remember that the verb 'to be afraid' takes the 'of' form: not afraid of you.
- 'And people should be afraid of us! Nobody is afraid of us!' For a debt collector, that is a professional disaster. Kostik tries to defend himself.
- 'She is afraid, I saw it.' Uncle Grisha counts up their week's results.
- 'We've been working for a week already! And nobody has paid us any money.' Notice the double negative: 'nobody did not give back money'.
- 'And why is that? Because you're a coward!' Literally 'and all why?'. Kostik looks hurt.
- 'And what should I do?' Literally 'and what to me to do?'. Grisha reminds him of their division of labour.
- 'We agreed: I do the scaring, and you illustrate it!' Uncle Grisha is the talker, and Kostik is supposed to be the muscle.
- 'But I was illustrating! I was making a scary face!' And Kostik pulls his funny frown again to show how scary he looked.
- 'You can make a scary face at your wife!' Literally 'on your own wife you will make'.
- 'Here you have to take action!' Grisha asks for concrete instructions.
- 'And what should I have done?' Adding the past neuter 'was' to 'it is necessary' makes it 'should have'.
- 'Anything at all! You could have broken a wardrobe.' With the conditional particle 'would', the past form means 'you could have broken'.
- 'Then they will understand that we are serious people.' Suddenly Kostik gets fired up and turns back toward the stairs.
- 'Then let's go, let's go back! I'll turn everything upside down!' But Uncle Grisha is in no hurry to skip a meal.
- 'No! Right now we'll go and have lunch.' Intimidation can wait until after lunch.
