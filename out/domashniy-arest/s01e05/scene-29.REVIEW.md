## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Yes, Viktor Stepanovich, I'm listening to you.' Pasha uses his most respectful tone. On the other end of the line, the governor is sitting in a hot steam bath and shouting.
- 'I warned you! You lost everything again!' Earlier in the episode the governor warned Pasha to watch out for Ilona, and now Ilona has turned Pasha's Blind Society trap into a triumph for Samson.
- 'I can even picture your face right now!' In the show the governor adds a string of unprintable comparisons.
- 'Do you hear me or not?' Pasha sits stiffly at his desk, speaking into the speakerphone.
- 'I'm listening, Viktor Stepanovich, I'm listening to you.' His tired tone only makes the governor angrier.
- 'Listen, don't do me a favour here!' The noun for 'a favour' is in the 'of' form after the negative command.
- 'I'm not doing that.' Then the governor sums up the whole election.
- 'Any monkey will win this election!' With all the administrative resources of city hall, winning should be automatic. So he asks Pasha a pointed question.
- 'What, are you worse than a monkey?' After the comparative word 'worse', 'monkey' takes the 'of'-form ending 'than a monkey'. Pasha answers literally.
- 'No, I'm better than a monkey.' That sounds suspiciously like backtalk to the governor.
- 'What are you doing over there now? Are you joking with me, or what?' In the show he uses a colourful Russian verb meaning 'are you being cheeky with me?' Pasha quickly backs down.
- 'No, I'm not joking, Viktor Stepanovich.' notice how the consonant in 'joke' changes to a 'ch' sound in the 'I' form.
- 'Then don't joke with me right now!' Literally, 'there, and one must not joke with me now'.
- 'You can't be trusted with anything! Are you listening to me?' Literally, 'to you nothing is possible to entrust'.
- 'I'm listening.' Suddenly the governor notices the echo on the line.
- 'What do you mean, listening? Are you talking to me on speakerphone?' Literally, 'by loud connection' — the standard Russian phrase for speakerphone.
- 'Quickly take the receiver in your hands! The receiver!' Even when nobody is in the room, Pasha isn't allowed the dignity of speakerphone. Pasha scrambles to pick up the handset.
- 'I'm listening right by my ear.' After the preposition 'near' or 'right by', 'ear' takes the 'of'-form ending. Then the governor turns to his bath attendant, who is whipping his back with birch branches.
- 'What, are you steaming yourself? Harder, come on!' While the bath attendant beats the governor with leafy birch branches, Pasha has to hold the receiver pressed to his ear and listen to every slap and grunt.
- 'Yes, Viktor Stepanovich.' Humiliated in his own mayor's chair, Pasha can only say yes.
