## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Do you think I want to?' She looks around the messy room in dismay.
- 'We can't stay here.' Literally 'to us it is not allowed to remain here'.
- 'Come on, get dressed.' Just then, Arkady steps into the doorway.
- 'Listen, you don't need to go anywhere.' Notice the double negative: 'not needed to go nowhere'.
- 'I repeat once again, I solved your problem.' He holds up his bare left wrist.
- 'See, no watch?' In Russian, the word for a wristwatch is always plural, and after 'there is no' it takes the genitive plural.
- 'And your debt is gone too.' Literally 'and of your debt also there is not'. Marina looks at him coldly.
- 'And I don't believe you!' The verb 'to believe' takes the dative case: 'to you'.
- 'You don't do anything without personal gain.' After the preposition 'without', 'personal gain' takes the genitive ending. For once, Arkady does not even deny his reputation.
- 'That's true, but this time it turned out that way.' Right behind him, old Klavdia Viktorovna pokes her head into the room.
- 'That's right, Marinochka, don't believe him!' She uses an affectionate diminutive of Marina's name.
- 'He made a deal with those gangsters.' After the preposition 'with', 'these gangsters' is in the instrumental plural.
- 'They'll split that money!' Having just been terrorised by the two collectors in his own room, Arkady is furious.
- 'What on earth are you saying, Klavdia Viktorovna?' He turns on the old neighbour in outrage.
- 'You hid in your own room altogether!' The reflexive verb means to hide oneself away.
- 'They were frightening the boy then!' Klavdia draws herself up proudly.
- 'I wasn't hiding anywhere!' Again, Russian uses a double negative for 'not hiding nowhere'.
- 'I was controlling the situation.' Fed up with both of them, Arkady waves his hand and walks out. Klavdia immediately turns back to Marina.
- 'Run, Marina, before it's too late!' Literally 'while it is not late'.
- 'And you can leave me the keys.' And then Klavdia reveals her own little angle.
- 'I'll look after the little room.' The verb takes the preposition 'behind' or 'after' with the instrumental case.
- 'Maybe I'll rent it to someone, and we'll split the money!' The final word literally means 'in half' or 'fifty-fifty'.
