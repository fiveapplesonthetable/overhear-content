## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'And what do you want to do?' Golovkin uses the informal 'you' form of 'to want' followed by the infinitive 'to do'.
- 'Nothing. I'll tell everything as it was, let them know.' The first-person future 'I will tell' needs no subject pronoun, and the particle 'let' with a third-person plural verb means 'let them know'.
- 'They will put you in prison then!' Literally, Russian says 'they will seat you into prison', using a third-person plural verb without 'they'.
- 'Well, if they lock me up, they lock me up. Means it's deserved.' Repeating a verb with 'means' in the middle is a classic Russian way to accept your fate.
- 'Wonderful! And did you think about your wife and children?' After the preposition 'about', both 'wife' and 'children' stand in the prepositional case. Suddenly Yulia calls from the other room to look at the television.
- 'Quiet! Look what they are saying on television!' In Russian you watch something 'by television', using the preposition 'by, along' with the dative case.
- 'They write that criminals were hiding bad herbs at the blind society!' Ilona's trick has worked: the news channels are reporting that criminals were using the blind workers to pack illegal herbs as sage.
- 'And they say that our candidate himself destroyed the herbs!' According to the new story, Samson overheard the criminals in the restaurant and bravely rammed the building to destroy the stash himself!
- 'The police say: five kilograms of herbs is a big success!' After 'five', both 'kilograms' and 'of herbs' stand in the genitive case.
- 'And the blind people say: we were deceived!' On screen, low-vision Andryusha explains that the workers had no idea what was in the boxes.
- 'We are only grateful to Mr. Samsonov!' The plural short adjective 'grateful' takes the dative case 'to Mr. Samsonov'. Samson's gloomy face instantly brightens.
- 'Did you hear? They even said thank you to me!' He is back to feeling like a hero.
- 'And your seams, Danya, are still uneven! Got it?' Now that he is no longer guilty, Samson goes right back to teasing Golovkin about the crooked wooden planks on his dacha walls.
