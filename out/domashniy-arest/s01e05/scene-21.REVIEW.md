## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I already explained to you that he's busy.' Notice the emphatic little word right after 'I': 'I already told you'. Ilona steps right past him.
- 'I have important information about your building.' Literally, 'at me there is important information about your building'.
- 'It wasn't Anikeev who held up its construction, it was Osipenko!' Notice the contrast construction 'not X, but Y'.
- 'And here is the construction order for your new building.' After the word for 'order', the little word 'about' takes the location form.
- 'Anikeev signed it a year and a half ago. — So what?' Russian has a single word for 'one and a half', which here takes the 'of'-form singular of 'year'.
- 'And that means the money was allocated.' Because 'money' is plural in Russian, the passive verbal describing word 'allocated' is plural too.
- 'But unfortunately, it never reached you.' Literally, 'they did not walk as far as you'.
- 'And here is another document. Please take a look.' In her haste, Ilona holds the sheet out toward the blind chairman.
- 'I'm blind. — Yes, of course, forgive me! I'll read it aloud.' The prefix on the action word 'to read' means 'to read out to an audience'.
- 'This is a contract for the purchase of construction equipment.' The contract is for the exact same amount of money that City Hall allocated.
- 'And that is Osipenko's cousin's company!' The money went straight from City Hall into Osipenko's family firm.
- 'Do you understand that Osipenko simply took your money?' At this point, Gennady Andreyevich pauses and calls his assistant over.
- 'Andryusha, take a look, please. — Here you go.' Meanwhile, Andryusha peers closely through his thick glasses and nods that the signatures and stamps are genuine.
- 'Wait, he's not blind? — He has low vision.' The compound describing word literally means 'weak-seeing'. Yet even after seeing the proof, the chairman shakes his head.
- 'You will never win! Your candidate is weak.' Notice the double negative in Russian: 'you never will not win'.
- 'And I'll ruin my relationship with the future mayor.' He knows Osipenko is a thief, but expects Osipenko to win the election anyway.
- 'You're all the same! The conversation is over, leave!' He points his white cane toward the door.
