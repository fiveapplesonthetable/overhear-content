## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- Finding young Lyosha alone in Marina's room, Uncle Grisha tells the boy: 'Boy, your mum owes us money!' When Lyosha replies that his mother called them extortionists, Grisha turns to timid Kostik.
- 'Come on, Kostik, let's leave her a message!' Notice the first-person plural future form used as 'let's leave'.
- Kostik is shocked: 'Are you kidding me?' Earlier on the stairs Grisha complained that Kostik did not know how to illustrate a threat, so now Grisha demonstrates.
- 'Watch how you have to illustrate!' Uncle Grisha tells his young sidekick Kostik to watch how a real debt collector explains things.
- 'Smash! Break!' Two blunt commands—'smash!' and 'break!'—as Uncle Grisha starts trashing the room.
- 'Go on, this one, yes!' Hearing the noise from the corridor, Arkady walks in.
- 'Listen, guys, can I have you for a minute?' Literally 'is it possible to have you for a minute?', an everyday polite way to ask for a word with someone.
- 'What for?' One blunt question word.
- 'There's an interesting conversation.' Arkady strolls in and remarks dryly that they seem to be having an interesting conversation.
- He reassures young Lyosha — 'Everything is fine, kid' — and leads the two collectors out to his own room.
- 'Guys, I understand, that's the job you have.' Literally 'at you is such a job' — Arkady says he understands they are just doing their job.
- 'But hurting children isn't right.' Suddenly Kostik stares at Arkady's glasses and recognises him.
- 'Uncle Grisha, I know him!' Kostik suddenly recognises the disgraced mayor from television and nudges Uncle Grisha.
- 'This is the mayor whom they threw out of the window!' Kostik remembers the viral news clip: this is the mayor they threw out of the window!
- 'Quite right, I am the mayor of the town.' Arkady confirms with dignity that he is indeed the mayor of this town.
- 'And my offer to you is this.' A short, punchy nominal phrase built around the Russian word for 'offer'.
- 'You both leave here right now.' Using the present to issue a cold order, Arkady tells both of them to walk out right now.
- 'And you never come back here again. Clear?' Contrast the two direction words: 'from here' when leaving and 'to here' when coming back. Uncle Grisha is unimpressed.
- 'How curious. And why must we obey you?' Uncle Grisha mockingly asks why on earth they should take orders from him.
- 'I have many dangerous friends.' Arkady warns them calmly that he still has plenty of dangerous friends.
- 'Otherwise I'll call the guys.' He adds a final warning: leave now, or else he will call those boys in.
- 'They'll come and hurt you so badly!' Nervous Kostik immediately wants to leave.
- 'Uncle Grisha, maybe let's go from here?' There is no question word here — in Russian, rising intonation on 'from here' is enough to make it a question.
