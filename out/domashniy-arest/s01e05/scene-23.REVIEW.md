## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Thank you very much! You won't regret it.' Ilona uses polite plural 'you' and the future tense of 'to regret'.
- 'If Samsonov wins, we will build your building!' In Russian, 'if' conditions about the future use the future tense in both halves of the sentence: 'if he will win, we will build'.
- 'I haven't changed my opinion. The staff forced me into this.' Gennady Andreyevich insists he still dislikes Samson, blaming his collective of workers for making him cooperate.
- 'Suppose we agree to help you. And what can we do?' The word for 'agree' is a short plural adjective, and 'to help' takes the 'to/for' form 'to you'.
- 'Say that we are not angry?' Even if they forgive Samson on television, everyone has already seen the wrecked wall.
- 'No, that won't help.' Ilona knows a polite apology won't save Samson's campaign. Then she looks down at the green leaves scattered across the floor.
- 'And what is this spilled here?' The final word is a short neuter passive verbal adjective meaning 'spilled, scattered'.
- 'It was sage. We were putting it into boxes.' Dried sage is a harmless medicinal herb that the blind workers were packing to earn money for their society.
- 'And the herbs in the sacks burned. Because of you!' The preposition 'because of' takes the 'of' form, blaming Samson's car crash for burning their sacks of dried herbs. Suddenly Ilona's eyes light up.
- 'Herbs, you say? Excellent! Let's try it, we have no other options.' With 'we have no', 'other options' stands in the plural 'of' form. Opening her handbag, Ilona pulls out her own small packet of illegal herbs and scatters it over the burned sacks.
- 'Andryusha, what is she doing?' Because Gennady Andreyevich is blind, he asks his low-vision assistant what is happening.
- 'I don't know. Oh, the police are here with a dog!' In this moment, Ilona has anonymously tipped off the police, who walk in with a sniffer dog on a lead.
- 'They say the dog found the smell of bad herbs! Five kilograms burned here!' Because the five kilos of sage burned together with Ilona's packet, the police count the whole pile as five kilograms of illegal herbs!
- 'In the sage boxes they also found herbs!' The compound preposition 'from under' is how Russian says 'boxes that used to hold sage'.
- 'What a nightmare! What have I gotten myself into?' The self-action verb form at the end means 'to get tangled up in', and poor Gennady Andreyevich clutches his head as the police write up their report.
