## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Ilona, is our Samsonov really not going to do anything?' The adverb at the start means 'for sure' or 'definitely'.
- 'You convinced him? Are you sure about this?' Notice the feminine past-tense verb and feminine short adjective, because he is speaking to Ilona. Ilona tells him calmly that she convinced Samson and even peeked through the door crack to be sure.
- 'You're a real professional! You know psychology.' In the show Ilona boasts that she is a professional who knows human psychology — and complains she is exhausted after giving up her own stash at the Blind Society.
- 'Alright, go to the hotel, you're tired. Thank you!' Meanwhile, out in the courtyard, Danila Golovkin has brought Samson and Nina home from the dacha.
- 'Danya, sorry once again for yesterday.' Literally, 'for yesterday's thing'. Samson feels bad about how he behaved at the dacha.
- 'We ruined your mood and broke your radio.' Remember that Nina and Samson knocked the radio off the shelf when the news about Samson's crash came on. And Samson takes back his insult about Golovkin's welding.
- 'And your joints are even! Bye, Danya!' Before driving away, Golovkin quietly tells Nina how glad he was to meet her again after all these years, and says she can always count on him. Once they are alone, Samson turns to Nina.
- 'Nina, do you know what I thought?' Literally, 'what I thought of'.
- 'It's time for me to get smart. Seriously.' Literally, 'time for me to take hold of my mind' — a classic Russian idiom for getting your act together and settling down.
- 'I need to drink less. I'm going to read books.' He uses the warm diminutive word for 'books'. And he has bigger plans.
- 'In general, I need to grow in every sense.' After 'in', both 'all' and 'senses' take the location plural ending. Nina smiles fondly at her husband.
- 'Long overdue, Vanya. Forty years old, after all.' Literally, 'it has been time for a long while'. Samson protests.
- 'Oh, come on! I'm telling you seriously!' The little phrase 'well what are you' is how Russians say 'oh, come on, don't tease me!'.
- 'Just wait and see, you'll be surprised yet!' Literally, 'you will look, you will still be surprised'.
- 'I don't doubt it.' Her dry, affectionate tone makes Samson throw up his hands.
- 'There you go! No support at all!' In his own clumsy way, though, Samson really means every word.
