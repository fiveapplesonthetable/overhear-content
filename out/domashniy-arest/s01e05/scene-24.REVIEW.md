## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Marina Sergeyevna, we came as soon as we could.' Both verbs are in the plural past tense ending in '-li'.
- 'You are under our protection. You have nothing to worry about.' The preposition 'under' takes the 'with/as' form, and 'nothing to worry about' literally says 'to you there is not about what to worry'.
- 'I call you, and you don't answer!' Marina is still upset that Zavarzin kept hanging up when the collectors broke into her room.
- 'Understand, there was a small mistake. Please forgive us.' Lavrov uses two polite plural imperatives to smooth things over.
- 'We talked with those people. They won't come to you anymore.' He doesn't mention that he and Zavarzin posed as her relatives from Armavir and beat both collectors up.
- 'Marina Sergeyevna, there is no need to go anywhere. Have you calmed down?' He needs Marina to stay in the communal flat so she can keep watching Arkady for them.
- 'Yes. Wait, what is that on your arm?' As Zavarzin steps closer, Marina catches sight of a gleaming gold watch on his wrist.
- 'That is Anikeev's watch!' In Russian, the word for 'watch' or 'clock' is always plural, literally 'hours', and she recognises the mayor's ten-thousand-dollar Swiss watch that Arkady gave the collectors.
- 'No. This is Vsevolod Alexeyevich's watch.' Without blinking, Lavrov uses Zavarzin's formal first name and patronymic to sound official.
- 'Yes, my commendation watch! I just picked it up from repair.' A commendation watch is an engraved watch awarded to officers for service, and 'just now' goes before the past-tense verb.
- 'Let me help you with your bag.' Zavarzin reaches for her heavy bag, using the preposition 'with' and the 'with/as' form.
- 'No need, I'll do it myself. Goodbye.' The feminine pronoun 'myself' ends in '-a', and Marina walks off down the corridor, leaving the two officers alone.
- 'Seva, have you lost your mind?' The idiom 'went off one's mind' is how Russian says 'went crazy', and Lavrov glares at his partner for wearing stolen evidence in front of their witness.
- 'Sorry, I didn't have time to put it away.' The verb for 'succeeded in time' is followed by the basic verb form 'to put away'.
