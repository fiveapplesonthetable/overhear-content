## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Well, with him it's obvious!' The particle attached with a hyphen adds emphasis and contrast: 'as for him, that's understandable'.
- 'I didn't expect anything more from him.' Notice the double negative with 'nothing' and 'did not wait'.
- 'But you, Nina! I asked you to keep everything under control!' Even when scolding her, Arkady addresses Nina with the polite plural pronoun.
- 'I tried!' Nina uses the feminine past tense of the self-action verb form 'to try'. Arkady isn't buying it.
- 'You tried? With a bottle of wine in your hand?' Look at the three cases in a row: 'with'-form 'with a bottle', 'of'-form 'of wine', and location 'in hand'. When lawyer Danila Golovkin mutters that the car is insured, Arkady snaps at him.
- 'We have an election, do you understand?' Literally 'by us are elections' — the word for an election is always plural in Russian.
- 'We need to think up some other version of events.' The suffix with a hyphen on 'some kind of' means 'some or other, we don't know which yet'. Ilona throws up her hands.
- 'And what is there to think up?' An action verb question meaning 'what could anyone possibly invent here?'
- 'That's it, we've arrived, this is the finish!' Saying 'we've arrived' is a classic Russian idiom for 'that's the end of the road, we're done for'.
- 'Drunk at the wheel, he crashed into the Society for the Blind! Into disabled people!' Literally 'behind the steering wheel', using the 'with/as' form.
- 'No, there is nowhere to retreat! Ahead is prison!' If Samson loses the election to Pasha, Arkady knows he will go straight back behind bars.
- 'Tell the journalists. The press conference will be this evening.' Literally 'today in the evening'. Nina shakes her head at both of them.
- 'Listen, just forget about your election already!' The particle before the command form softens or hurries the command along: 'come on, just forget about it'.
- 'And why is that?' Ilona asks why Nina thinks the campaign is over.
- 'Because I know Samsonov.' Nina refers to her husband by his surname in the direct-object form.
- 'Once he remembers everything, he'll die of shame!' Literally 'how he will remember everything, from shame he will die'.
