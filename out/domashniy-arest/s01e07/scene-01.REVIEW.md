## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'We have a celebration today!' Literally 'by us today is a holiday'.
- 'They officially registered me as a candidate for mayor.' Notice the 'with/as' form on 'candidate' after the registration verb.
- 'I don't care at all!' When Nina's polite lawyer friend Danya excuses himself and leaves, jealous Samson pretends not to care.
- 'No, I am a candidate, I'm having tea!' Samson refuses champagne while Klavdia Viktorovna mutters about a bad dream and Ilona announces that the campaign posters will be printed tomorrow.
- 'Friends, I propose we drink to success!' Arkady rises to offer a toast.
- 'All together, like one big family.' Meanwhile, Arkady keeps handing Marina clean forks and glasses until she confronts him.
- 'What are you doing? You know perfectly well what you are doing!' Marina addresses him with the polite plural pronoun.
- 'You are behaving like a teenager!' Literally 'you lead yourself like a teenager'.
- 'You are flirting with me.' This old-fashioned colloquial verb takes the preposition for 'behind' plus the 'with/as' form.
- 'What kind of silly word is that — flirting?' Arkady is amused by her old-fashioned phrasing.
- 'Yes, I am showing you signs of attention.' Literally 'I render to you signs of attention'.
- 'Because I like you!' In Russian, the person you like is the grammatical subject and 'to me' is in the 'to/for' form.
- 'How interesting! But nothing can happen between us.' Literally 'between us nothing to be cannot'.
- 'Yes, of course, I understand. But why? Because of Eduard Valentinovich?' Arkady brings up her former university dean.
- 'What does Eduard Valentinovich have to do with it?' A useful idiom for asking how someone is relevant.
- 'I feel nothing toward you.' Notice the double negative with 'nothing' and 'not'.
- 'Well, that is wonderful! Before, you felt disgust toward me.' Arkady treats her answer as fantastic news.
- 'And now you feel nothing! That means you will fall in love soon.' The self-action verb form verb means 'you will fall in love'.
- 'The trend is positive!' Literally 'positive dynamics', like a rising chart in an economics report.
- 'Oh, the conversation is over! Shame on you!' The short passive form marks the discussion as finished.
- 'I will win you over anyway, Marina!' This phrase means 'I will achieve my goal' or 'win you over'.
