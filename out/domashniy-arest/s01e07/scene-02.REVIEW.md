## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Watch where you are reaching, grandson!' As Arkady reaches for an old brooch, his late grandmother's voice scolds him from her portrait.
- 'Grandma, quiet! Don't bother me while I look for a present!' Notice the negative command with the 'to me/you' pronoun 'to me'.
- 'What, did you fall in love?' The self-action verb form verb is masculine singular.
- 'And who needs you like this?' Literally 'and to whom are you needed like this?'
- 'What? In your day there weren't even words like that!' In the original she even calls him a 'loser', and Arkady points out that word didn't exist in her time.
- 'Nobody will like this old brooch! Not even Klavdia Viktorovna!' Both 'nobody' and the neighbour's name are in the 'to/for' form.
- 'But Grandpa gave it to you! So there is something special about it, right?' Literally 'something in her there is'.
- 'There is nothing special about it!' Literally 'nothing in her there is not'.
- 'Grandpa worked at the factory where they made these brooches.' Notice 'at the factory' uses the preposition for 'on'.
- 'He took them from the factory and gave them away!' Ongoing-action form verbs show a repeated habit.
- 'I never wore this brooch!' Literally 'in life I did not wear this brooch'.
- 'Right here is where I will put it! It will be an excellent gift!' Unbothered, Arkady places the brooch into a velvet gift box just as someone knocks.
- 'Yes, what do you want?' Literally 'what to you', a blunt colloquial way to answer the door.
- 'Arkady Borisovich, it is time for our consultation now.' It is Nina's polite lawyer friend Danya.
- 'Let's do it later, I'm busy! Wait in the kitchen.' Arkady is too focused on his romantic plan to talk about his court case.
- 'Wonderful, I will wait.' Danya is only too happy to wait in the kitchen, where Nina is making tea.
- 'Nina, hi! I stopped by to see Arkady Borisovich, but he is busy.' Notice the prefixed past verb for 'stopped by on the way'.
- 'I brought pastries! Maybe we could have some tea in the meantime?' Notice the partitive 'of'-form form of 'tea', and Nina gladly accepts.
