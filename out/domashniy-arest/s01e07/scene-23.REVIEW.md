## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I feel terribly awkward. I don't like being a burden.' Literally, 'to be in heaviness' means to be a burden on someone.
- 'And you are a burden. A burden!' Eduard refuses to be polite about having the ex-mayor in their way.
- 'Edik! — What, Edik?' Marina scolds him gently, using the affectionate short form of Eduard.
- 'Well, he's right. I was very rude to you yesterday.' Notice the short-form adjectives for 'right' and 'rude'.
- 'I took your flowers. — You hear that? My flowers!' Yesterday Arkady stole Eduard's bouquet and pretended it was from him.
- 'But I've understood a lot since that moment.' Arkady claims his fall down the stairs changed him.
- 'When you can't even wipe your own nose yourself.' Both of his arms are locked in plaster casts up to the elbows.
- 'Then you look at the world completely differently.' He plays on Marina's sympathy while Eduard rolls his eyes.
- 'I could have died yesterday. Broken my neck.' Literally, 'to twist the neck'.
- 'I don't believe you! People like that live a hundred years.' Eduard knows corrupt officials always land on their feet.
- 'Edik, why would you say that? — What do you mean, why would I say that?' Marina is shocked that Eduard would talk that way about an injured man.
- 'Not why is he, but why am I? No, that's it, I can't do this.' Eduard is furious that Marina is taking Arkady's side.
- 'I think I'll go. — Wait, Edik!' The word for 'perhaps' or 'I think' softens a decision.
- 'Eduard Valentinovich, please don't leave.' Arkady deliberately gets Eduard's patronymic wrong again—he is Vladimirovich, not Valentinovich.
- 'Your porridge. Enjoy your meal.' Marina sets the hot bowl down on the table in front of Arkady.
- 'Try to manage without our help. — Thank you so much!' She wants to go back to Eduard in her room.
- 'Are you mocking me? — Well?' As Arkady clatters the spoon helplessly against his two plaster casts, Marina realizes she will have to spoon-feed him.
