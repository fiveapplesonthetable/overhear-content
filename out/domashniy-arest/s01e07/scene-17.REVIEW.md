## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- In the mayor's office, Pasha addresses his two bodyguards: 'I have a serious assignment for you.'
- 'I can trust you, right?' Notice the 'to me/you' pronoun after the verb for trusting.
- 'As you know, Vitaly, I am taking part in the election,' Pasha continues.
- 'And I have one competitor.' Pasha centres the line on the verb for 'there is' alongside the noun for 'competitor'.
- 'I am talking about Samsonov, of course.' Pasha quickly clarifies that he is talking about their rival Samsonov, of course.
- 'And life is a complicated thing,' Pasha hints darkly.
- 'Do you understand what I am talking about?' There is no question word here — in Russian, rising intonation on 'I' is enough to make it a question.
- 'Say a man is running for mayor,' Pasha explains.
- 'That same Samsonov, for example.' Looking for a scapegoat, he points to that very same Samsonov as an example.
- 'And some hooligans suddenly attacked him.' The verb meaning 'attacked' works together with 'hooligans' to carry the point.
- 'Not badly, of course, but the man feels unwell.' He tries to soften the blow: not badly, of course, just enough that the man feels unwell.
- 'And he cannot run for office anymore.' The self-action verb form on the verb for 'to run for office' turns the action back onto the subject.
- 'So, is that clear?' Pasha asks pointedly.
- To Pasha's surprise, the bodyguard objects: 'Pavel Yuryevich, we are against this!'
- 'We are bodyguards, not body-breakers!' Vitalik says, coining a funny opposite word from the roots for body, guard, and break.
- 'And what were you doing for Anikeyev, I wonder?' Pasha asks sarcastically.
- 'Breaking an arm at karaoke is one thing,' Vitalik explains.
- 'Hurting a person for life is another.' Vitalik puts 'another' up front and finishes with 'life' to make the point unmistakable.
- 'Believe us, you do not need this,' the bodyguard adds.
- 'You ruin your karma that way.' Vitalik uses the adverb for 'that way' right alongside the verb meaning 'ruin'.
- Pasha loses his temper: 'Listen to me carefully, you philosophers!'
- 'I gave you an order!' Losing his patience, Pasha barks that he gave them a direct order!
- 'If you cannot do the job, leave!' he snaps.
- 'Find yourselves another place with a good salary!' He threatens to fire them on the spot: they can go find another job with a good salary!
- Faced with losing their perks, the bodyguards fold immediately: 'Fine, we will do everything.'
- 'I hope so,' Pasha replies.
- 'Go on, act quickly!' he orders them out of his office.
