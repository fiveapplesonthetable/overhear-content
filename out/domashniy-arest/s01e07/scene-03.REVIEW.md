## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'We are broadcasting our report from Krasnoarmeyskaya Street.' Literally 'we lead our report from the street', with the street name in the 'of' form.
- 'Candidate Ivan Samsonov came out into the courtyard.' Notice the direct-object form after 'into' for motion.
- 'Today he paved over an old pothole himself!' The emphatic pronoun means 'himself' or 'with his own hands'.
- 'The road service promised to pave it over ten years ago.' With numbers from five upward, 'years' takes the special plural 'of' form form.
- 'Ivan, your rivals hold meetings and make promises.' Both plural verbs are in the present tense.
- 'And today you paved a pothole in a courtyard.' Here 'in the courtyard' is stationary, so it takes the location form.
- 'Aren't you wasting your time in vain?' The short adverb means 'in vain' or 'for nothing'.
- 'What is the point of chattering?' An action verb question dismissing empty talk.
- 'Everyone talks, and I act!' Samson delivers the punchy campaign slogan Ilona wrote for him.
- 'Stop, that's a wrap! Good job, Samson!' Literally 'stop, filmed!' — the standard director's call when a take is finished.
- 'Hey, I almost broke my car right here!' A passing driver stops by the fresh patch of asphalt; 'almost' is expressed with 'barely not' plus a past verb.
- 'You laid it unevenly over there!' The adverb comes from 'not-even'.
- 'Now there will be a little puddle there!' He uses the diminutive of 'puddle'.
- 'A little puddle?! I will level out that puddle with you right now! Understood?' Samson forgets the cameras and threatens to use the driver as a roller, putting 'you' in the 'with/as' form.
- 'I am not going to vote for you! I am leaving here!' The compound ongoing-action form future is formed with 'I will not' plus the basic verb form 'to vote'.
- 'Well, I will! So, Ilona, what is next?' Samson shouts back like a schoolboy and asks Ilona for his next assignment.
- 'All right, the workers can take their roller back.' The real road crew had lent Samson their hand roller for the camera shot.
- 'Everything is great, get some rest! It is the weekend today, everything is cool!' The plural noun for 'days off' means 'the weekend'.
