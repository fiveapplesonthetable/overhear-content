## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'We are broadcasting our report from Krasnoarmeyskaya Street.' Literally 'we lead our report from Krasnoarmeyskaya Street', where the campaign stunt is underway.
- 'Candidate Ivan Samsonov came out into the courtyard.' Notice the direct-object form after 'into' for motion.
- 'Today he paved over an old pothole himself!' The extra word at the end means 'himself'—he paved over that pothole with his own two hands.
- 'The road service promised to pave it over ten years ago.' With numbers from five upward, 'years' takes the special plural 'of' form form.
- 'Ivan, your rivals hold meetings and make promises.' The reporter contrasts Samsonov's hands-on work with his rivals, who just hold meetings and make promises.
- 'And today you paved a pothole in a courtyard.' Here 'in the courtyard' is stationary, so it takes the location form.
- 'Aren't you wasting your time in vain?' She asks pointedly whether he isn't just wasting his time and effort for nothing.
- 'What is the point of chattering?' Samson dismisses campaign speeches with a blunt question: what is the point of empty chatter?
- 'Everyone talks, and I act!' Samson delivers the punchy campaign slogan Ilona wrote for him.
- 'Stop, that's a wrap! Good job, Samson!' Literally 'stop, filmed!' — the standard director's call when a take is finished.
- 'Hey, I almost broke my car right here!' A passing driver slams on the brakes by the fresh asphalt; Russian expresses 'almost broke' as 'barely didn't break'.
- 'You laid it unevenly over there!' The angry driver points at the lumpy patch of road and yells that they laid it unevenly.
- 'Now there will be a little puddle there!' He uses the diminutive of 'puddle'.
- 'A little puddle?! I will level out that puddle with you right now! Understood?' Samson forgets the cameras and threatens to use the driver as a roller, putting 'you' in the 'with/as' form.
- 'I am not going to vote for you! I am leaving here!' Furious about his suspension, the driver shouts that he won't vote for Samsonov and speeds off.
- 'Well, I will! So, Ilona, what is next?' Samson shouts back like a schoolboy and asks Ilona for his next assignment.
- 'All right, the workers can take their roller back.' The real road crew had lent Samson their hand roller for the camera shot.
- 'Everything is great, get some rest! It is the weekend today, everything is cool!' The Russian word for 'the weekend' is literally 'exit days'—days off from work.
