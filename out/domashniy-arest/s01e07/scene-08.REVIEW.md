## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'One hundred and twenty over eighty, like a cosmonaut's! Now we'll measure it once more.' In Russian, ideal blood pressure is always compared to a cosmonaut's.
- 'Well, what have you got there?' Pasha sees the folder in Byelinkov's hands.
- 'Pavel Yuryevich, you must not get nervous right now.' Meanwhile, Pasha snatches the poll sheet.
- 'I'm not nervous! Samsonov's rating has caught up with ours?' He explodes.
- 'Why did he suddenly catch up? We were ahead by ten percent!' At this point, Byelinkov reminds him of yesterday's news broadcast.
- 'He saved that child! You saw it, after all.' When Samsonov jumped off the bridge to save disguised Lieutenant Lyova, the whole town saw him as a hero.
- 'It's the snowball effect.' Just then, Pasha's special phone rings — it is Governor Viktor Stepanovich.
- 'I'm listening to you, Viktor Stepanovich.' The Governor has seen the same poll numbers.
- 'Pasha, do you even know why I'm calling you?' Meanwhile, Pasha tries to repeat Byelinkov's excuse.
- 'Viktor Stepanovich, don't worry! It's just the snowball effect.' The Governor cuts him off coldly.
- 'And now listen to me! If you don't settle the issue with Samsonov today.' And he delivers his ultimatum.
- 'Then tomorrow I will support him! Did you understand me?' As soon as the Governor hangs up, the blood-pressure monitor beeps again.
- 'Two hundred and ten over one hundred and thirty-nine!' Here, Pasha's blood pressure has skyrocketed. Desperate to match Samsonov's macho image, Pasha goes to a gym to film a campaign video under a heavy barbell.
- 'Twenty-nine! Thirty! Write down the text.' Pinned helplessly under the barbell on his chest, Pasha gasps out a campaign slogan.
- 'We lifted the barbell, we'll lift the region! And now take this barbell away!' Notice the past plural 'we lifted' paired with the future plural 'we will lift'.
