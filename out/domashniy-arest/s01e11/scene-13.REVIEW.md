## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'And you call me simply Vika.' Now Vika returns the gesture, inviting him to drop "Viktoria" for her short name.
- 'Alright, Vika, with great pleasure.' "S bolshim udovolstviyem" is the warm, polite way to say "gladly".
- 'I have not felt so peaceful in a long time.' After hiding out from political schemers, a quiet morning at the kitchen table feels like a sanctuary.
- 'You need to have a proper rest at home.' The word "khoroshenko" means "properly" or "good and well", often used when urging someone to rest or eat.
- 'All the men around only demand money.' Between Arkady's bribes and Pavel's schemes, cash is all she has heard about for months.
- 'I am here solely for your safety.' Lev reminds her that he wants nothing from her except to keep her safe.
- 'You are a very dependable and kind person.' "Nadyozhny"—reliable, someone you can count on—is high praise in Russian.
- 'I always keep my word as a man.' Literally "keep my man's word", a phrase of old-school honor.
- 'Thank you for being here beside me.' Simply having someone trustworthy in the room means the world to her.
- 'I will not leave you in trouble.' "Ne ostavlyu v bede" is a classic Russian promise to stand by someone when things go wrong.
- 'Let us drink some more hot tea.' Over a Russian breakfast, pouring another cup of tea means neither person wants the conversation to end.
- 'With pleasure, I will pour fresh tea.' Lev happily reaches for the teapot to keep the peaceful morning going.
