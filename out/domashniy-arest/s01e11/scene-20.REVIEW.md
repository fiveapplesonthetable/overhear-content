## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Tolya, you said everything absolutely right then.' While packing up their gear, Lyova tells his partner he is proud of how he stood up to the general.
- 'I will also submit an official resignation report.' Out of loyalty to Lavrov, the younger operative is ready to quit on the spot too.
- 'I am tired of everything, better I go into private security.' "Mne vsyo nadoyelo" means "I'm fed up with everything", and private security is the classic fallback job for ex-officers.
- 'Sokolov, do not do such foolish deeds right now.' Lavrov switches to Lyova's surname to make the fatherly warning sink in.
- 'My long-awaited pension is right around the corner.' Literally "my pension is on my nose"—a vivid Russian idiom for something that is right in front of you.
- 'While you still have a long and honest time to work.' In the original Russian, he says "work and work" twice to show how many years Lyova still has ahead of him.
- 'But why does leadership not value us at all?' Lyova cannot stomach watching honest investigators get punished while crooks walk free.
- 'Calm down, the leadership simply does not know the whole truth.' Even after being told to resign, Lavrov still believes the facts will win out.
- 'Look what lies in this old box.' Rummaging through the office boxes, Lyova spots something that does not belong there.
- 'This is the mayor's visitor log from police evidence.' That notebook lists everyone who walked into Arkady's office right before his arrest.
- 'Why did the investigators take this strange document?' Someone buried the log in a box of old files so nobody would connect the dots.
- 'The names of all guests before the arrest are recorded here.' Flipping the pages open, Lavrov realizes they are holding the missing link.
- 'This could help us solve the real crime!' Resignation forgotten, the two partners are right back on the case.
