## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'There is always such a big line here.' Nina looks at the crowd waiting for tables. In Russian a line does not just form, it literally "stands" in place.
- 'They make the most delicious pastries in town here!' Samson is proud of his spot: if the pastries are the best in Sineozersk, the wait is worth it.
- 'Do you know that people often propose here?' Here comes his not-so-subtle hint. Literally Russians say "make an offer" when they talk about proposing marriage.
- 'This is the first time I hear about this.' She has no idea where he is going with this. "First time I hear" is the natural Russian way to say "that's news to me".
- 'The town registry office is right nearby.' For Samson, romance is all about logistics: eat your cake, walk next door to the registry office, and sign the papers.
- 'And what do you want to say by that?' Now Nina hears the trap closing. Literally she asks "what do you want to say by this?", which is how Russians ask "what are you getting at?"
- 'Samson, what is this lying in my pastry?' Her fork hits metal inside the cream. Calling him by his surname shows she is more alarmed than charmed.
- 'This is a beautiful gold ring for you!' He beams as if hiding jewelry inside a sticky pastry were the height of sophistication.
- 'Samson, have you completely lost your mind?' Not the romantic reaction he pictured. Literally the Russian idiom means "stepped down from your mind".
- 'You and I have been together a whole month.' A whole thirty days! Notice how Russian says "we with you" where English says "you and I".
- 'I have a separate room in a communal flat.' In the nineteen-nineties, having your own room in a shared apartment is his big selling point as a groom.
- 'Marry me right now!' He does not ask so much as issue a marching order. The Russian phrase for a woman marrying literally means "step out behind me as a husband".
- 'No, Samson, I do not agree.' Plain, firm, and final: she shuts the whole idea down in five words.
- 'Take this ring back for yourself.' She hands the pastry-covered ring straight back across the table.
