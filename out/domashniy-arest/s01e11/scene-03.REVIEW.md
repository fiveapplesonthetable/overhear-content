## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Nina, did you come back to me?' When the door opens again, Samson can hardly believe his sit-in actually worked.
- 'You are still standing in the cold.' She shakes her head at the sight of him shivering on the street.
- 'I promised that I would wait.' For Ivan, keeping his word is everything: he said he would stay, so he stayed.
- 'You are truly a completely incorrigible person.' Calling someone "incorrigible" in this tone is really a Russian way of saying "you're impossible, and I give up".
- 'So maybe after all it is yes?' Sensing the opening, he tries his luck one more time. "Vsyo-taki" means "after all" or "despite everything".
- 'Well alright, maybe it is yes!' She echoes his own "maybe yes" right back at him, and the smile gives her away.
- 'Are you truly willing to become my wife?' After being turned down inside the cafe, he needs to hear it stated plainly.
- 'Yes, I agree to marry you.' No more "maybe" this time: she gives him the clear yes he waited in the cold for.
- 'Then put on this ring quickly!' He wants that ring on her finger before she has a chance to think twice.
- 'It really is very beautiful and shines.' Once it is out of the pastry and on her hand, the ring actually looks lovely.
- 'Now we will go to the registry office together.' Remember why he picked this cafe in the first place: the registry office is right next door.
- 'And submit our first joint application.' Filing the marriage application together is the official first step for any Russian couple.
- 'I promise to be the very best husband!' Young Ivan means every word of it, which makes their bitter breakup years later all the more poignant.
