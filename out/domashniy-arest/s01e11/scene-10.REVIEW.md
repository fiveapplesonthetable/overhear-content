## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Who are you hinting at right now, Major?' Kruglov narrows his eyes, warning Lavrov that he is stepping onto thin ice.
- 'And I am not hinting at anyone.' Lavrov is done with polite hints and bureaucratic tiptoeing.
- 'I am telling you straight to your face.' Literally "straight into your eyes", which is how Russians say "straight to your face".
- 'Will the president really punish you for the truth?' In the original line he asks even more bluntly: "what, is he going to eat you?"
- 'We simply want to return the money to the state.' For Lavrov, the mission is still simple: find the stolen billions and put them back in the budget.
- 'You are constantly afraid of any risk.' Saying that to a general in uniform takes real nerve.
- 'That is why nothing ever happens with us!' Everyone is so busy protecting their chair that the real crooks walk free.
- 'You have really overstepped now, Major Lavrov!' The word "perebral" literally means "took too much", used both for drinking too much and for crossing the line.
- 'Write your resignation report immediately!' Cornered by the truth, Kruglov reaches for the only weapon a boss has left.
- 'For goodness' sake, I will write it right now!' "Radi boga" means "for God's sake" or "be my guest"—Lavrov does not hesitate for a second.
- 'Your behavior is very brave, but foolish.' Even while firing him, the general cannot help respecting the major's guts.
- 'You have just destroyed your own career.' One honest outburst, and years of service are on the line.
- 'At least my conscience will remain completely clean.' Here is that useful little word "zato" again: "on the plus side", he can look himself in the mirror.
- 'Brave, but very foolish, Major.' Kruglov repeats his verdict as Lavrov turns to go.
