## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'The patient in the ward is making a lot of noise and shouting.' Down the hall, Ivan's roaring about the president can be heard through the walls.
- 'We must immediately give him a calming injection.' In Russian you literally "make an injection" rather than "give" one.
- 'What exact medicine do we need to prepare right now?' The orderly checks which sedative the doctor wants drawn up.
- 'Take the standard ampoule with Rolazine from the cabinet.' Rolazine is a heavy-duty tranquilizer meant to knock an unruly patient flat.
- 'Here is this needed ampoule, hold it.' The orderly pulls the glass vial from the shelf and hands it over.
- 'Hand it over here quickly, draw the exact dose.' "Davay yeyo syuda"—"hand it over here"—is brisk, everyday clinic talk.
- 'The medicine is ready, I am carrying the syringe to the treatment room.' With the syringe filled, Samson's presidential summit is seconds away from disaster.
- 'Act strictly according to the rules of medical instructions.' Everything is done by the book, which will be the doctor's excuse later.
- 'Will he fall asleep quickly after this strong medication?' The orderly wants to know how long they will have to wrestle with him.
- 'Yes, in a couple of minutes he will become completely quiet.' Just two minutes for the shot to turn a roaring candidate into dead weight.
- 'The main thing is that he does not break the medical bed.' Given Ivan's size and temper, the hospital furniture is in real danger.
- 'Go into the ward, I will come right behind now.' "Sledom" means "right behind" or "following in your footsteps".
- 'Good, we will now carefully give this injection.' Armed with the sedative, they head in to quiet the future mayor down.
