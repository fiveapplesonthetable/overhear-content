## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Why are you so irritated today?' Daniil can tell Nina's mind is miles away from their romantic getaway.
- 'It is very strange to me that he does not call.' After posting those provocative vacation photos, she expected her phone to be ringing off the hook.
- 'Who exactly are you talking about now?' Daniil plays dumb, though he knows perfectly well who is on her mind.
- 'I am talking about my husband Ivan.' Calling Samson "my husband" right in front of her new lover tells you where her heart still is.
- 'And what is so strange about that?' From Daniil's point of view, a quiet ex is a blessing, not a problem.
- 'I know him very well after all.' After decades of marriage, Nina knows every move in Ivan's playbook.
- 'Usually he should either shout loudly.' Option one in a Samsonov crisis is an explosion at top volume.
- 'Or he should ask for forgiveness.' Option two is coming back on his knees to beg her to return.
- 'And now for some reason he is simply silent.' Dead silence is the one reaction she has never seen from him, and it scares her.
- 'Well it is good that he is silent.' Daniil just wants peace and quiet, completely missing her worry.
- 'What if something happened to him?' "A vdrug" means "and what if suddenly"—the phrase Russians reach for when their imagination jumps to the worst.
- 'Stop talking foolish nonsense.' Brushing off her fear is the worst move Daniil could make right now.
- 'What a heartless person you are after all!' Suddenly the smooth, cultured boyfriend looks cold next to her hot-headed husband.
- 'I will go home and check on him!' And just like that, the vacation is over: Nina is heading back to the communal flat.
