## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'And I knew that you would return!' Klavdiya greets the returning prisoner in the kitchen with triumphant satisfaction.
- 'Real evil always comes back.' Calling him "real evil" to his face is her idea of a welcome-home speech.
- 'Why are you standing and keeping silent?' Usually Arkady fires back an insult right away, so his silence throws her off.
- 'It is simply very hard for me right now.' Instead of bragging or blustering, he speaks in a quiet, broken voice.
- 'What is happening to you?' Seeing tears in the former mayor's eyes stops Klavdiya in her tracks.
- 'Can these really be your crocodile tears?' Just as in English, Russians say "crocodile tears" when they suspect someone is faking it for sympathy.
- 'Viktor, bring the Corvalol here quickly!' The moment she realizes the breakdown is real, Russian neighborly instinct kicks in: fetch the heart drops!
- 'Don't you see: the man is unwell?' One minute ago he was "real evil"; now he is a suffering neighbor who needs medicine.
- 'Thank you, Klavdiya, I don't need anything.' Even in tears, he declines the fuss with quiet dignity.
- 'Uncle Arkasha, please do not cry!' Little Slavik tugs at his sleeve, calling him "Uncle Arkasha" the way Russian children address adult neighbors.
- 'Let's go to your room quickly.' The boy gently leads the grown man out of the crowded kitchen.
- 'Thank you, Slavik, you are a kind boy.' A simple word of kindness from a kid reaches Arkady when nothing else could.
- 'Go rest, Arkady, calm your nerves.' Even sharp-tongued Klavdiya softens her tone and sends him off to rest.
- 'I will go and lie down in bed.' Humbled at last, he shuffles off to his shared room.
