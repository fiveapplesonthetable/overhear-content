## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Samson, you are late for football!' Walking out of the cafe, Nina tries a practical angle: surely soccer matters enough to make him leave.
- 'Nothing terrible, it will do without football.' For a guy like Samson to skip a match, this really is serious. "Nothing terrible" is the everyday Russian shrug for "no big deal".
- 'I will not leave here anywhere.' He plants himself on the pavement outside. Russian stacks "nowhere" and "not" together to make the refusal sound rock-solid.
- 'And how long do you plan to sit here?' She stops and looks down at him, half exasperated and half curious how far this stunt will go.
- 'I will wait as long as is necessary.' Simple, stubborn, and impossible to argue with: "so much as is needed".
- 'It is getting very cold outside.' Even while rejecting him, she cannot help worrying that he is going to freeze out there.
- 'I am not cold at all in my jacket.' He puffs out his chest and pretends the chill is nothing. In Russian you say "to me it is not cold".
- 'You are behaving like a stubborn child!' She scolds him, though you can already hear her frustration softening into fondness.
- 'I simply love you very much.' No clever comeback, just honest feeling. Russians say "strongly love" where we say "love very much".
- 'We have known each other only one month.' That is still her sensible objection: four weeks is no time at all to rush to the registry office.
- 'For true love this is quite enough.' Ivan answers logic with pure nineties-style romantic conviction.
- 'Please stop standing under the windows.' People are watching, and she does not want the whole street staring at her suitor.
- 'I will wait for your answer anyway!' The words "vsyo ravno" mean "all the same" or "no matter what", and they sum up Samson's whole personality.
