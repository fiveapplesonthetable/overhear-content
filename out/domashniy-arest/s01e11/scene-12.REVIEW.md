## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'You know, Lev, I gain weight very quickly.' Eyeing the stack of pancakes, Vika hesitates over her figure.
- 'Do not say that, you look wonderful!' Lev brushes her worry aside with effortless warmth.
- 'And who will love me if I get fat?' Used to men who treat her as a trophy, she assumes her looks are all she has.
- 'People love a person not for their figure.' In Russian, saying "they love a person" without a subject is how you state a general truth about life.
- 'They love you simply for your kind heart.' Nobody in Arkady's circle has ever talked to Vika about her heart.
- 'Do you truly think so sincerely?' She checks his face to see if he is just flattering her.
- 'Yes, I am speaking the pure truth.' "Chistuyu pravdu"—literally "the clean truth"—is the classic Russian way to say "the honest truth".
- 'Then serve me a couple more pancakes!' Convinced at last, she abandons her diet on the spot. "Parochku" is an affectionate little word for "a couple".
- 'There, that is much better, eat up!' Literally "another matter entirely"—Lev beams as he puts more pancakes on her plate.
- 'Thank you very much for the delicious pancakes.' She is happier over homemade blini than she ever was in a fancy restaurant.
- 'Call me simply Lev from now on.' Dropping the patronymic is a big step toward warmth in Russian conversation.
- 'Agreed, simply Lev, without a patronymic.' Saying "dogovorilis"—"we have a deal"—seals their new friendly footing.
