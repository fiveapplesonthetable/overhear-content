## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Why are you all standing here?' Since Pavel cannot yell at Moscow, he takes his panic out on the nearest campaign staffers.
- 'Quickly go work at your places!' He shoos them down the corridor like schoolchildren caught loitering.
- 'I will fire every lazy employee!' Threatening to sack the whole office shows how badly he is losing his cool.
- 'Pavel Yuryevich, please listen to me.' His assistant uses Pavel's full name and patronymic, trying to talk him off the ledge.
- 'What has happened over there with you again?' Pavel snaps back, bracing himself for even worse news.
- 'Overall our situation is still normal.' "V tselom" means "on the whole", the classic phrase of an aide trying to spin bad news.
- 'You still have a good lead.' On paper the poll numbers still look fine, at least until the president lands.
- 'What lead are you talking about now?' Pavel knows poll numbers mean nothing once voters see his rival on television with the president.
- 'If the president arrives, Samsonov will immediately win!' That is the brutal math of provincial politics in a single sentence.
- 'Do you understand that this is direct support?' Even without a formal speech, a personal visit signals who Moscow backs.
- 'But that is completely unfair!' Hearing a crooked campaign staffer complain that politics isn't fair is pure comedy.
- 'That's it, be quiet and get out!' Pavel has heard enough whining and clears the room.
- 'Leave here right now, I said!' Adding "I said" at the end of an order is how a furious boss makes sure nobody argues.
- 'And this applies to you too!' His faithful assistant thought he was exempt, only to get thrown out with the rest.
