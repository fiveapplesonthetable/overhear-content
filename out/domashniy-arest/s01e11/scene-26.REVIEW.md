## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello, look who came to our hospital today!' "Kakiye lyudi!"—literally "what people!"—is the classic Russian exclamation when an important or unexpected guest walks in.
- 'What did you do to him, he is completely out of it!' Pasha stares in horror at the drooling candidate. "On zhe nikakoy"—literally "he is no kind at all"—means he is completely out cold.
- 'We injected him with Rolazine strictly according to instructions.' As promised, the doctor hides behind hospital protocol.
- 'He cannot be shown to the President in this state!' Delivering a tranquilized zombie to the airfield would end Pasha's career on the spot.
- 'We could show him, but should we do that?' The doctor's dry medical humor is the last thing Pasha wants to hear right now.
- 'Of course we must, this meeting will decide the whole fate!' Now that Arkady has blackmailed him into helping, Pasha's own neck is on the line if Samson misses the meeting.
- 'Let's calmly think about this situation together.' Doctors are never in a hurry, even when politicians are having a meltdown.
- 'Are there normal medications for our candidate's alertness?' Pasha is begging for some kind of medical upper to undo the tranquilizer.
- 'Well, we have not had strong stimulants for a long time.' Naturally, the provincial clinic's medicine cabinet is almost bare.
- 'He must urgently be brought into a presentable state!' Literally "brought into a human look"—made to look like a functioning human being.
- 'I can quickly inject two cubic centimeters of caffeine.' In Russian medical slang, "kubiki"—little cubes—means cubic centimeters in a syringe.
- 'Great, will this definitely help return his strength?' In the original subtitle the doctor admits caffeine won't do a thing, which leads to a much wilder experiment.
- 'Wait ten minutes in the hallway, I will try.' Sending Pasha out into the corridor, the doctor gets ready to mix up a miracle cure.
