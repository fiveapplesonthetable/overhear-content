## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'How much money does it take to be happy?' The question sets up the satirical premise using an infinitive purpose clause.
- 'How much money do you need?' The dative pronoun combines with a short predicative adjective of necessity.
- 'A little bit there, a little bit here.' The rhyming spatial adverbs describe small bribes quietly accumulating.
- 'And just like that, you've got six million!' The genitive plural follows the numeral to denote vast embezzled wealth.
- 'A villa in Monaco, an account in Switzerland.' The prepositional phrases list the quintessential offshore assets.
- 'But you need, need, need even more!' Greed ensures that no amount of money ever feels like enough.
- 'A supermodel wife and a mistress too.' The song mocks the predictable cliches of corrupt officials.
- 'Only she's just a little bit younger!' The comparative adverb is modified by the colloquial phrase for a tiny bit.
- 'A white yacht sails across the sea.' The prepositional phrase indicates movement along the surface of the sea.
- 'Happiness is getting closer, almost here!' The particle expresses something that is right about to happen.
- 'Paintings by Dalí and Van Gogh in the kitchen.' The names of famous artists appear as genitive modifiers.
- 'A church, a mosque, and a synagogue are built.' The short passive participle agrees with the first feminine noun in the list.
- 'And for insurance, a Buddhist temple too!' The official tries to cover all spiritual bases just in case.
- 'If it doesn't pan out here, it will pan out there!' The colloquial slang verb refers to whether an endeavor succeeds.
