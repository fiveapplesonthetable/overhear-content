## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Nina, just don't be angry, okay?' He uses the short, loving form of her name, and the verb is a command with 'not' in front: don't get angry. Then he makes a promise on somebody else's behalf.
- 'He'll wash everything.' He means Arkady, of course. The verb is in the future: the washing hasn't started yet. Nina answers bitterly.
- 'Yes, he said it right. Here's my kingdom.' A few minutes ago Samson shouted that this bathroom was her kingdom. Now she agrees with him, and it hurts. Samson doesn't understand.
- 'Nina, what's wrong?' Literally, 'Nina, you what?' A very common, very casual way to ask what's going on. And Nina says the hardest line of the scene.
- 'I should have said no to you back then.' The little word near the start makes a wish about the past: 'it would have been better if I had said'. She means their wedding day. Notice the verb ends in 'a', because Nina is a woman. Samson turns to Arkady instead.
- 'Arkasha, let's clean up!' He calls the mayor by his childhood name, and 'let's' is one handy word followed by the verb. Arkady looks at the mop in his hands.
- 'What am I doing?' The mayor of the town, on his knees in a flooded toilet. Samson shows him how.
- 'Wipe the floor. Like that.' A plain command, then two little words that mean 'just so', 'like this'. Next, the old neighbour needs the toilet, and the door has no latch. Arkady explains, very politely.
- 'Sit down and hold the door. Nobody will come in.' He uses the polite form with the old lady: both commands end in the same polite ending. And he adds a promise.
- 'Tomorrow we'll put on a latch.' The old lady thanks the dear mayor for his care for pensioners. She doesn't mean it kindly. Meanwhile Samson goes to report to Nina.
- 'Nina, I washed everything. It's all clean.' Here the verb is in the past, with no ending for a man. Arkady, who did the actual washing, can't quite believe it.
- 'You washed it?' Just two words and a question in his voice. Samson keeps trying with Nina.
- 'Honestly, Nina, everything's fine. Say something, please.' The middle word in the second sentence means 'anything at all'. But she answers like a politician.
- 'No comment.' The same two words a mayor might say to a reporter. Arkady is puzzled.
- 'What's the matter with her?' Literally, 'what is with her?' Samson knows his wife well.
- 'She's offended. She's always like that.' The first word is a whole sentence: a past verb with a feminine ending, so we know it's about her. Arkady wants to know what happens next.
- 'And then?' The small word in front means 'and', with a touch of 'but'. And the husband has the answer.
- 'Then it passes.' He sounds very sure. Let's hope he is right.
