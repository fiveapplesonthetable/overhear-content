## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Arkasha, where's our car?' The word for 'our' is feminine, to match the car. Arkady has to admit the worst.
- 'Vanya, it's much worse than that.' In this moment, Vanya is Samson's real first name, the short form of Ivan. The word before 'worse' means 'much'.
- 'I've been cheated.' Literally, 'me they cheated'. Russians often say it this way, with 'they' and no subject at all. Samson doesn't get it.
- 'What? Who cheated you?' The first word is a rough, everyday way of saying 'what'. Arkady spells it out.
- 'I have nothing left.' Literally, 'at me no more nothing is'. Two negatives together are normal in Russian. Samson remembers the promises.
- 'And the list? A flat, a car, a spa!' That's the wish list they drew up together earlier, when Arkady moved in. Nina tries to calm him down.
- 'Vanechka!' An even softer form of Vanya, the way a wife says it when things are going wrong.
- 'I'm telling you: there's no money.' After 'no', the word for money changes its ending. Then a question.
- 'Where am I supposed to get you a flat?' Literally, 'where will I take a flat for you?'. Samson hands him something.
- 'Here, take this. You're going to fix the pipe.' He hands Arkady a tube of sealant. The second sentence is the simple future: 'you will' plus the verb 'to fix'. Arkady refuses.
- 'What for? I'm not fixing anything!' Again two negatives side by side, 'not' and 'nothing'. Now Samson turns on his wife.
- 'What are you staring at, Nina? Your Majesty!' He calls her 'queen', and he doesn't mean it as a compliment. Then he grabs the leaking pipe.
- 'You wanted a spa? There it is, your kingdom!' He tears the old pipe off, and water bursts into the bathroom. 'Wanted' ends in 'a' because he's talking to a woman.
- 'Vanya, the water! There's water everywhere!' The second sentence starts with 'everywhere', and again there's no verb at all. Nina begs her husband.
- 'Vanya, I'm begging you!' The verb is the same one that gives the polite word for 'please'. And she reminds him whose bathroom it is.
- 'This is a shared bathroom, Vanya!' The little word after 'this' adds emphasis, like 'after all'. Everyone in the flat uses this one bathroom.
