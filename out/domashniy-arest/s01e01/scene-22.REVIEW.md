## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Arkasha, I understand, it's hard for you right now. But...' Notice there's no 'it is' in the middle: Russians just say 'to you now hard'. And then the news.
- 'Pasha and I have been together for a month now.' Literally, 'we with Pasha'. That's how Russians say 'Pasha and I'. And the verb stays in the present: they still are together. Arkady is shocked.
- 'What? How could you, Vika?' Literally, 'how can you'. Vika has an answer ready.
- 'Look who's talking.' Word for word, 'who would talk'. She knows about his own affairs. So Arkady reminds her who she is.
- 'You're my wife, Vika!' Here 'my' comes after 'wife', which makes it louder, more emotional. Vika is not moved.
- 'Now you remember?' One word: 'remembered', in the masculine past, because she is talking to a man.
- 'You haven't been interested in me for a long time.' Literally, 'I haven't interested you for long'. The first word in it means 'I', not 'you'. Then she explains.
- 'And with Pasha I feel like a woman again.' 'Like a woman' is just one word here, with an ending that means 'as'. Arkady tries to warn her.
- 'Vika, look at him.' A command in the casual form, the one you use with family.
- 'All he wants is your money.' Literally, 'to him only your money is needed'. A very useful pattern for saying what someone needs. Vika hears it differently.
- 'So you mean I can only be loved for my money?' The first two words mean 'that is', or 'so you mean'. Arkady backs off.
- 'No, Vika, that's not what I mean at all.' The word for 'at all' sits right before 'not'. Too late.
- 'What a horrible person you are!' The first word works like 'what a' in English. And she walks out.
- 'Vikulya, wait!' Vikulya is a tender form of Vika, the name he uses when he wants her back. Now Pasha is the one who speaks.
- 'All right, Arkashenka. We'll be off, it's time.' He uses the sweetest form of Arkady's name, and he doesn't mean it kindly. 'We' means him and Vika. Then he lists his plans.
- 'I've got a lot to do. The mayoral election...' To say 'I have', it's 'at me there is', like last time. Pasha is going to run for mayor himself.
- 'Then I have to give your money from the basement to the Moscow people.' Arkady's cash, hidden in the basement, is going to his Moscow partners. The people come first, with an ending that means 'to'.
- 'And the cluster, of course. Bye!' The project Arkady blocked, and then a cheerful goodbye. Pasha has taken everything.
