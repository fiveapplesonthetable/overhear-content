## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Vitalik, why are we going so slowly?' The action word means 'we are going' by car or any vehicle. On foot it would be a different action word. Then one impatient word.
- 'Faster!' It's the word for 'fast' with an ending that means 'more'. Just then, his phone rings.
- 'Yes, I'm listening.' This is a very common way to answer the phone, and it sounds a little official, just right for a mayor. The caller is polite.
- 'Arkady Borisovich, hello.' The long form of 'hello', the one you use with people you don't call by a nickname. Then he says who he is and why he's calling.
- 'It's Arakelyan.' On the phone, you introduce yourself with 'this is', and your name.
- 'I'm calling about the shopping centre.' The phrase in the middle means 'about' or 'concerning', and the shopping centre takes the ending for 'of'. Now the important news.
- 'Everything is ready on my side.' Literally, 'at me, everything is ready'. He means the money is ready. The mayor gave him three days to pay, in an earlier scene. Arkady is pleased.
- 'It's good that it's ready. We were starting to worry.' Notice Arkady says 'we', as if he were speaking for the whole town hall. Then he proposes a time.
- 'Call me this evening. We'll arrange something for tomorrow.' The first word is a command, 'call', and the last word means 'for tomorrow'. But that doesn't suit Arakelyan.
- 'Sorry, I can't this evening. I'm flying to Yerevan.' The word for flying has a beginning that means 'away': flying away from here. He gives a reason.
- 'My mother is ill.' Literally, 'Mum is ill.' Russians often say just 'Mum' where English says 'my mother'. And he suggests something else.
- 'Maybe we could meet now?' The first word means 'maybe', and the action word means 'we will meet'. A very natural way to suggest something. Arkady grumbles, but agrees.
- 'Your timing couldn't be worse!' Literally, 'how untimely you are!'. Notice that Arkady says the casual 'you' to Arakelyan, while Arakelyan uses the polite one. That tells you who is the boss here.
- 'Where are you now?' Russian omits the present word 'to be', connecting 'where' directly with 'now'.
- 'I'm at the Santa Fe.' It's the name of a café in town. Arkady makes up his mind.
- 'All right, stay there.' Literally 'be there'. It's the command form of the action word 'to be'.
- 'I'll be there in ten minutes.' The first word means 'in' when you count time forward. The last word is 'I will be'. In the present, Russian has no 'am', but in the future you do need this action word.
- 'Fine. I'll be waiting for you.' Literally 'I'm waiting for you', and again, the polite form. And now new orders for the driver.
- 'Vitalik, we're going to the Santa Fe.' The present form, 'we are going', is used for a plan that starts right now. The same action word we heard at the very beginning.
