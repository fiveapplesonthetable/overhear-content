## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Thank you, thank you!' Then he reminds everyone where he comes from.
- 'As you all know, I was born and grew up in Sineozersk.' Two verbs in the past: 'was born' and 'grew up'. They end in a plain consonant because Arkady is a man. Now the sad part of the story.
- 'My childhood was hard.' Literally, 'childhood at me was hard'. It's the same 'at me' you use to say what you have. Then a detail we saw for ourselves.
- 'I grew up in a communal flat.' A communal flat is one flat shared by several families, with one kitchen and one toilet, just like the one in the last scene. So he makes a claim.
- 'I know the problems of ordinary people.' A politician's favourite line. Then he talks about his job.
- 'I've been working as mayor for four years now.' Russian uses the present tense here, 'I work', where English says 'I've been working'. And after the verb 'to work as', the job takes a special ending. Then a classic promise.
- 'We've done a lot, but we need to do even more.' The word for 'need' is the same one little Vanya used about the toilet. Then the big news.
- 'I love our town very much.' The word for 'very' comes before the verb, as in 'I very love'. And so he makes his announcement.
- 'That's why I'm running for a second term.' Literally, 'I'm going for a second term', the everyday way to say a politician stands again. He finishes politely.
- 'Thank you for your trust!' The word for 'for' here is the one you use when you thank someone for something. Then the questions start.
- 'Arkady Borisovich, who are your rivals?' Notice the reporter uses his first name and his father's name, the respectful way to address someone in Russian. Arkady brushes it off.
- 'I have no time to think about that.' Literally, 'to me there's no time to think about it'. He gives a reason.
- 'There's too much to do.' Literally, 'too many things to do'. Another question.
- 'Tell us, when will your programme be out?' The first word is the polite command for 'tell'. His programme means his plans for the town.
- 'Any day now, on the website.' The first two words mean 'in the next few days'. Then the awkward question.
- 'And does the governor support you?' The governor runs the whole region and is Arkady's political boss. Watch how he answers.
- 'The most important thing is the people.' A nice answer, and it doesn't say yes or no. The reporter gives up.
- 'Thank you, Arkady Borisovich!' Again the polite name and patronymic.
