## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Arkasha, so what do you say?' He uses a short, friendly form of Arkady's name, as old neighbours do.
- 'When do I start?' Literally, 'when to start?' Just the word for 'when' and a action word in its basic form. Arkady is all smiles.
- 'Any time you like.' Literally, 'at any moment'. Samson doesn't need to be asked twice.
- 'Right, I'm ready!' The first word means 'all', but here it just means 'that's settled'. Then Arkady slips in one more thing.
- 'There's just one small formality.' The long word sounds just like its English cousin. Samson is puzzled.
- 'What formality?' The question word changes its ending to match the feminine word. Arkady's answer is short.
- 'Kiss me.' This is the command form of the action word 'to kiss'. In the show he names a crude, humiliating kind of kiss; we keep it simple. Samson can't believe his ears.
- 'What?' A casual, rather rough way to say it. So Arkady reminds him of their childhood.
- 'Do you remember the toilet in the communal flat?' When they were boys, Samson used to push little Arkasha's head into that toilet. Notice that the endings change after the little word for 'in'. And the boy made a promise.
- 'I told you back then: you'll kiss me one day.' The word for 'told' is past form and masculine, because Arkady is a man. Samson is amazed.
- 'What, you remember all that?' In this moment, Arkady's answer is the line he has waited thirty years to say.
- 'Oh, I remember everything.' The little word at the start, meaning 'and' or 'but', gives it a quiet, calm sting. Samson hopes it's a joke.
- 'Are you joking?' But Arkady keeps a straight face.
- 'No. I'm serious.' A phrase Arkady likes; you'll hear it from him again. Samson explodes.
- 'No! Never!' He is furious and comes at Arkady, who doesn't even get up.
- 'Don't, Samson. Security will be here any second.' Two very useful words open this line: they mean 'don't' or 'no need'. And the guards do come in.
- 'Please see him out.' To the guards Arkady is polite, so the action word has the formal, plural ending. And as Samson leaves, one last line.
- 'If you change your mind, come back.' There is no word for 'if': the first action word, in the future, does that job.
- 'The job is waiting.' The word here means a post, a position, rather than simple work.
