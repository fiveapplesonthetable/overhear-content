## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Nikolai Filippovich, please forgive me.' He uses the boss's first name and father's name, and the polite form of 'forgive'.
- 'I don't know myself how it happened.' The word for 'myself' is masculine here, because Samson is a man. Then he invents an excuse.
- 'Yesterday I ate pickled mushrooms. Maybe one mushroom was bad.' So it was the mushrooms talking, not him. Nina wants him to keep his voice down.
- 'Quiet!' Literally, 'more quietly'. But the boss is not friendly, and Samson starts to lose his temper.
- 'Why are you talking so rudely?' He still uses the polite 'you' with his boss, even when he's angry. Nina is in despair.
- 'Samsonov, I asked you not to!' She calls her husband by his surname, a sign she is really cross. Notice the feminine ending on 'asked', because Nina is a woman. The call ends badly.
- 'Well, happy now?' Nina was the one who sent him to make peace with his childhood friend, Arkady.
- 'Now I have to look for a new job.' Literally, 'now a new job to look for'. Nina has a different idea.
- 'But you were offered a job. What, is it a bad job?' Literally, 'to you they offered work'. She means Arkady's offer.
- 'Wait, Nina, you don't get it. It's shameful!' He means the humiliating kiss Arkady asked for. Nina is not impressed.
- 'So what?' Short and very common. And she starts to dream.
- 'We could have bought Yulia a sheepskin coat, and Slavik a laptop.' The little word that means 'would' turns this into a wish about what could have been. The names have the 'to/for' ending: 'for Yulia', 'for Slavik'. Samson can't believe his wife.
- 'Nina, what's got into you?' And now Nina says what she really feels.
- 'I'm sick of this communal flat!' Literally, 'this communal flat makes me feel sick'.
- 'Our pipe has been leaking for a week now.' Russian uses the present tense here, where English says 'has been leaking'. Samson has a plan.
- 'I've bought sealant. Everything will be fine.' Nina throws his word back at him.
- 'Fine? It'll be leaking again in a week!' The word for 'it' is feminine, because the word for 'pipe' is feminine.
- 'I can't take the cooking, washing and cleaning any more.' Three verbs in a row, all in the basic form after 'I can't'.
- 'I want to go to the sea!' There's no verb for 'go': 'I want to the sea' is enough.
- 'I want to live like a normal person! I want a bidet, do you understand?' A bidet, the little bathroom basin: the dream of a proper flat. Their daughter has a question.
- 'Mum, what's a bidet?' A handy pattern for asking what any word means. And Nina's answer is very modern.
- 'Look it up on the internet.' The verb is a command, 'look', and it comes at the end for emphasis. What every parent says to a teenager these days.
