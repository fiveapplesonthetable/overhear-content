## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Excuse me, who are you?' Two words do the asking: the polite 'you' and 'who'. He is polite because she is a stranger. She introduces herself.
- 'I'm Marina Sergeyevna.' There is no word for 'am' here: you just say 'I' and your name. Her second name comes from her father's name, and that's the respectful way to address her. Then she points at the young people.
- 'And these are my students.' 'This' works for one person or many, so it covers the whole group. Arkady stays charming.
- 'Pleased to meet you. So what's the matter?' The first two words mean 'very pleasant'. That's the standard reply to an introduction. Marina can't believe he's asking.
- 'Don't you know?' Just 'and you don't know', with a rising voice. Arkady keeps a straight face.
- 'No, I don't.' Literally, 'No, I don't know.' Marina isn't buying it.
- 'Don't lie.' The first two words mean 'there's no need', followed by the action word 'to lie'. It's a firm, everyday way to tell someone to stop doing something. Then the accusation.
- 'They're pulling down the monastery wall!' There's no 'they' in the sentence: the word ending alone means 'people are doing it'. And the wall comes first, because it's what matters to her. And the reason?
- 'They want to build a shopping centre there.' 'Want', then 'to build', just like English. Now Arkady acts shocked.
- 'That can't be. It's our history.' The monastery is eight hundred years old, and he talks about it like a proud local. Then he plays innocent.
- 'How strange, I've heard nothing about it.' Here's a language tip. In Russian, 'nothing' comes with an extra 'not' on the action word: literally 'I nothing didn't hear'. And the past form ends in a plain consonant because Arkady is a man. So he turns on his deputy, in front of everyone.
- 'Pavel Yuryevich, what is this?' He uses Pasha's full, formal name, the way a boss scolds someone in public. Pasha plays along.
- 'We just didn't want to bother you. We'll cancel it all!' The second action word is in the future: 'we will cancel'. Arkady makes Marina a promise.
- 'Marina, I promise you: nothing will happen to the wall.' Again the double negative: 'nothing will not happen'. And 'with the wall' has a new ending, because the word for 'with' changes the word after it. Marina isn't naive.
- 'All right. My students and I will keep an eye on it.' Literally, 'we with the students', which is how Russian says 'my students and I'. Arkady has a clever answer.
- 'That's right. I'm inviting you onto the public council.' A seat on an official council: it sounds like an honour, and it also keeps her close. And then he leaves.
- 'Thank you, Marina. Sorry, I'm in a hurry.' The last word is a whole sentence: 'I'm in a hurry', with the 'I' inside the word ending. But once the crowd is behind him, his tone changes.
- 'Pasha, I don't want to hear about her again. Understood?' Now it's the casual 'you' and a short first name. The last word, 'understood', is a past form, and it's how Russians ask 'got it?'. Pasha knows what he means.
- 'I'll take care of it.' One word: 'I will do'. It's the future of the action word 'to do'. And one last order.
- 'And don't drag your feet with the shopping centre!' So the wall was never safe: Arkady wants that shopping centre built.
