## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I know our town like nobody else.' The verb 'know' comes right after 'I', and the last two words mean 'like no one'. Then comes the news.
- 'That's why I've decided to become mayor.' He has just helped to put Arkady under arrest, and now he wants his job. Samson is impressed by the style.
- 'Look how he talks!' The first word is a casual command, 'look'. Arkady is not impressed at all.
- 'He learned that from me.' The phrase in the middle, literally 'at me', is how you say who you learned something from. Pasha moves on to his big project.
- 'We will build the cluster. That means thousands of jobs.' The cluster is the industrial project Arkady blocked, and the reason Pasha set him up. Arkady talks back to the television.
- 'Pasha, you won't win this election.' The verb here has a future meaning, even though it looks like a present form. That is how completed actions work in Russian. Samson just laughs.
- 'Oh, come on!' Two short words for disbelief. Arkady takes a sip and makes a face.
- 'Very bad vodka.' Spoken like an expert. Samson is not bothered.
- 'Never mind, never mind.' The word literally means 'nothing', and it's how you say 'it's fine, never mind'. Arkady tries to sound hopeful.
- 'As our governor says, there's always a way out.' He tells Samson the governor once burned a pile of money before a police search, and stayed free. Samson is not convinced.
- 'Yeah, sure.' Said slowly, it means 'I don't believe a word of it'. Then he gets serious.
- 'Do you want me to tell you honestly?' The first word is the verb 'want', and the last one, 'I'll tell', is in the future. Arkady lets him go on.
- 'Tell me.' One word, a command from the same verb. And Samson is very direct.
- 'It's all the same to me. You or him.' The first sentence starts with 'to me': in Russian the person who feels something often comes first, in this form. The second literally says 'what you, what him'. Arkady asks why.
- 'Why?' And the answer is short.
- 'You're all thieves.' He uses the plural 'you', for all the officials together. Arkady takes it calmly.
- 'Well, thanks.' Not real thanks, of course. Samson finishes his thought.
- 'And you know the main thing? Nobody cares.' The last sentence literally says 'to everyone, it's all the same', the same phrase he used a moment ago. On that cheerful note, Arkady goes very quiet.
