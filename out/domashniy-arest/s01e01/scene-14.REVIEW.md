## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'So, where are you?' Arkady uses the informal singular 'you' here with 'where', as friends and close colleagues do.
- 'I'm here.' One of the first things you learn: 'I', and 'here'. No verb at all. Arkady is puzzled.
- 'Why are you sitting in the corner?' The last word, 'in the corner', has a special ending: a few masculine words end in a stressed 'oo' sound after 'in'. Arakelyan's answer is one mysterious word.
- 'Secrecy.' A rather grand word for keeping things hidden, the way spies do. Then he greets the mayor properly.
- 'Hello, Arkady Borisovich.' The polite 'hello' and the first name with the father's name. Arkady sees he looks unhappy.
- 'So why so gloomy?' In everyday speech the word 'what' can mean 'why'. And the adjective is masculine, to match Arakelyan. He's handing over a bag of money, and he doesn't like it.
- 'It's a bit much. There's a crisis, you know.' The first word is 'a lot', softened with an ending that means 'a bit'. The small word at the end means 'after all' or 'you know'. Arkady is not sympathetic.
- 'You'll get this money back in six months.' The word for money is always plural in Russian, so 'this' is plural too. And the verb is in the future: 'you will return'. Arakelyan glances around nervously.
- 'Quiet, please.' The first word is 'quieter', which is how Russians say 'shh'. Arkady just laughs at him.
- 'What are you, a spy?' A teasing question. Then he says the line that will come back to haunt him.
- 'This is our town.' Again, no word for 'is'. The word for 'our' matches the town, which is masculine.
- 'We have nobody to fear here.' Literally, 'to us here to fear nobody'. The last word means 'there is nobody', and it goes with a verb in its dictionary form. A second later, someone shouts.
- 'Freeze!' It's the verb 'to stand' in its dictionary form, used as a sharp order. You'll hear it from police in every Russian film.
- 'Captain Gnezdo, Investigative Committee.' That's the agency that investigates serious crimes. His surname means 'nest'. He is here to arrest the mayor.
- 'Citizen Anikeev, you are under arrest.' The officer says 'citizen', the official way to address someone, and the polite 'you'. Arkady can hardly believe it.
- 'What? What for?' The second question literally means 'for what?'. The captain tells him.
- 'For a bribe. For a very large bribe.' The same little word, 'for', again, and the bribe takes the ending of the object. So the meeting was a trap, and only a minute after he said he had nobody to fear.
