## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Why didn't I think of it right away!' Literally, 'how did I not guess at once'. The verb is past and masculine: no extra ending, because Arkady is a man. Then he starts asking strange questions.
- 'Samson, are you Russian?' A question in Russian is often just a statement with a rising voice. Samson answers with a joke.
- 'No, Ethiopian!' A big, blond, very Russian man, being sarcastic. Arkady ignores it and goes on down his list.
- 'Never been in prison?' Literally, 'didn't sit?' In everyday Russian, 'to sit' can mean 'to do time'. Samson is glad to say no.
- 'No, thank God.' Literally, 'glory to God'. Next question.
- 'Orthodox?' He means the Orthodox church, the main church in Russia. Samson finds the question silly.
- 'Well, what else?' Literally, 'and which one?', as if there could be no other answer. Arkady sums it up.
- 'Russian. Orthodox. Never been in prison.' Three ticks on an invisible form. Samson adds his family.
- 'Well, yes. A wife, two kids.' After the word for 'two', the word for 'children' takes a special form, one you hear a lot. Arkady continues.
- 'Worked on a building site. Did you serve in the army?' Again past verbs with no subject: the ending tells you it's a man. Samson is proud of this one.
- 'Of course I served.' He repeats the verb as his answer, which is a very Russian way to say yes. And Arkady has his candidate.
- 'That's it! Samson, you're going to be the new mayor.' After the verb 'to be', the job takes a special ending for roles: you'll be 'as mayor'. Samson thinks it's just drunken talk.
- 'Agreed.' Said like a man accepting another glass. Arkady insists.
- 'You don't understand me. I'm going to make you the new mayor.' Same special ending on the job: 'make you as the new mayor'. Now Samson listens.
- 'Me? Mayor?' Two short questions, and he can hardly believe it. Arkady explains the plan.
- 'The election is in two months. You wanted a new level, didn't you?' The little word in the middle of the second sentence works like 'after all', reminding Samson of his own words. Samson admits it.
- 'Well, I did.' Just the verb again, in the past. And Arkady offers the deal.
- 'I'll help you, and you'll help me.' Both verbs are about the future. Two forms of the same verb, one for 'I', one for 'you', a neat pair to remember.
