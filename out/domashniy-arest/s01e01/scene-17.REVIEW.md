## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Samson, listen.' The casual command, because these two have known each other since they were boys. Then a short confession.
- 'It was revenge.' The word for 'was' ends in 'a' to match 'revenge', which is feminine. Samson isn't sure what he means.
- 'Revenge?' Just one word, and not a friendly one. Arkady explains with a neat little formula.
- 'You got me, I got you.' Word for word, just 'you me, I you'; the verbs are missing, and you don't need them. Then the score.
- 'Now it's one-all.' Like a football score: the toilet when they were boys, and the kiss today. Samson isn't convinced.
- 'Yeah, right.' A tiny word said twice, slowly, and it means 'I don't believe a word of it'. Arkady keeps going.
- 'All by the rules, like a knights' tournament.' He makes it sound like a fair duel between gentlemen. Then he tries charm.
- 'Samson, come on, smile.' The little word in front of the command softens it, like 'come on'. And then the big word of the scene.
- 'We're at peace.' Literally, 'at us, peace'. The same word means both 'peace' and 'the world'. Samson agrees, a little too happily.
- 'Peace!' He takes Arkady's hand, shakes it, and squeezes hard.
- 'Ow! My finger!' The finger is broken. Samson's wife Nina comes in and sees what he has done.
- 'Samson, don't break anything else of his.' After 'not', the word for 'nothing' stays negative too, so Russian happily uses a double negative. Samson promises.
- 'Don't worry, I won't.' The second part is short for 'I won't break anything'. Nina is a nurse, and she gets to work with a bandage.
- 'Give me your hand. That's it.' The first word literally means 'give', and it's a very common way to start a request. Arkady takes his chance to flatter her.
- 'Your wife is a very wise woman.' The word for 'wise' has a feminine ending, to match 'woman'. Then some advice for Samson.
- 'You listen to her.' But Samson has his own plans, and he tells Nina about them.
- 'That's it, Ninok. Now we have our own personal servant.' Ninok is a pet name for Nina. To say 'we have', Russians say 'at us there is'. Arkady can't believe his ears.
- 'I beg your pardon?' Literally, 'what, forgive me?' Very polite, and here very shocked. Samson explains, using a phrase politicians love.
- 'You're a servant of the people. And we are the people.' Officials like to call themselves servants of the people, and Samson takes it literally. Then the conclusion.
- 'So you'll serve us.' The future again: 'will' plus the verb 'to serve'. The tables have turned on the mayor.
