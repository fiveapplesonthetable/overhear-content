## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Well, four-eyes?' The word is a schoolyard name for a child in glasses, and it's what Vanya always calls him. Then he shows his empty hands.
- 'See, there's nothing left.' Literally, 'nothing didn't remain': in Russian, 'nothing' comes with a 'not' as well. Arkady knows that isn't true.
- 'But you've still got some.' Literally, 'at you there is more'. It's the same pattern as 'I have', just with 'you'. Then he asks.
- 'Give me just one.' The first word is a command: give. The answer is short.
- 'No. That's mine.' Remember, his father told him to share with everyone. Arkady has one word for that.
- 'Greedy!' It's what children shout at someone who won't share. Vanya doesn't care, and shows off his markers.
- 'See the markers? They're from Japan!' Literally, 'this is Japan', which is how a boy boasts. But then something goes missing.
- 'Hey, where's the toy car? Where's my toy car?' The word for 'my' ends in an 'a' to match the car, which is feminine. And then Vanya spots Arkady.
- 'Four-eyes! Grab him!' The action word is the one Dad used when he handed the gum over, 'hold', but here it means 'catch him'. Arkady struggles.
- 'Let go!' A command again, a short one. Vanya grins.
- 'Well, well, caught you, didn't we?' One action word in the past says it all. Arkady tries to explain.
- 'I was only looking!' He just wanted to see the car. The action word ends in a plain consonant because Arkady is a boy; a girl would add an 'a'. Vanya has a nasty idea.
- 'Look, how dirty our toilet is!' The word for 'look' ends in the plural, because he's talking to all the children around him. Then he adds something.
- 'It needs a wash!' The first word means 'it is necessary', and you'll hear it everywhere. What happens next is cruel: the boys push Arkady's head into the shared toilet bowl. Afterwards, wet and angry, he makes a threat.
- 'You'll be sorry!' Right then, Vanya just laughs at him.
- 'Oh yeah? When?' The first two words together mean 'oh, really?', said with scorn. And Arkady answers.
- 'When I grow up, you'll be begging me to forgive you!' Both action words point to the future: 'when I grow up', and 'you will be asking'. Remember this line, because the whole series is about this promise.
- 'What did you say?' The action word ends in a plain consonant again, because the 'you' he's asking is Arkady, a boy.
