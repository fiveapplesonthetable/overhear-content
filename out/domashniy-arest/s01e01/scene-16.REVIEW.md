## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Open the door.' A command, but in the polite form, the one you use with an adult you don't know. Then he checks the number.
- 'Is this flat nine?' Just the word for 'flat' and the number, with a question in the voice. The old neighbour opens up, and she knows this face.
- 'Yes. Good Lord, Arkasha!' She uses the name she called him when he was a little boy, a fond but slightly cheeky form of Arkady. He greets her politely.
- 'Hello.' The long, polite greeting, the one for older people and strangers. She wants to know what's going on.
- 'What happened?' A very useful question. The verb is in the past tense, with the ending for 'it', because 'what' counts as 'it'. The officer answers in official language.
- 'Citizen Anikeev will live here.' 'Citizen' plus a surname is how officials talk. The future is easy here: 'will' plus the plain verb, just like in English. Then he turns to Arkady.
- 'Where is your room?' He says the polite 'your', as an official should. It's a harder question than it sounds.
- 'It used to be somewhere here, and now it's gone.' 'Was' has the feminine ending, to match the room. Arkady hasn't lived here for years. The neighbour helps.
- 'The door is behind the wardrobe.' After the word for 'behind', the wardrobe takes the same ending it would take after 'with'. Somebody pushed a wardrobe in front of his old door, so it has to be moved.
- 'Come in.' Another polite command. Inside, the officer gets down to business.
- 'Please put your right foot on the bed.' One word covers both 'leg' and 'foot'. The tag goes on his right ankle. Arkady does as he's told.
- 'There you go.' The word for 'please' again, this time meaning 'here you are'. The officer shows him two things.
- 'This is the tag. And this is the base unit.' The tag is called a bracelet in Russian, and the small box stays in the room. Then comes the rule.
- 'Fifty metres, no more. Otherwise there'll be a signal.' If Arkady walks more than fifty metres from the box, the prison service will know at once. Arkady is a quick learner.
- 'All clear.' Literally, 'everything is understood'. Handy whenever someone explains something to you. One last thing.
- 'Here are the call buttons: ambulance, police, and us.' The word for ambulance literally means 'fast', short for a fast medical car. Arkady was hoping for more.
- 'And that's it?' Two little words and a rising voice. The officer is in a hurry.
- 'That's it. Goodbye.' The standard polite goodbye; literally, 'until we see each other'. And the neighbour has the last word.
- 'Well, Arkasha, welcome back.' Literally, 'with the return'. You say it to someone coming home after a long time away, and here it comes with a little irony.
