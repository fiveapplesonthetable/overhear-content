## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hi, Dad!' This is the casual hello, for family and friends. Dad has something to show him.
- 'Look what I've got.' Literally, 'look, what is at me'. That's how you say you have something: at me there is. The first thing out of the suitcase is a big one.
- 'Wow, a tape recorder! A Japanese one!' In the Soviet nineteen eighties that was a real treasure. Little Arkady is watching from the doorway, and he greets the father politely.
- 'Hello!' This is the long, polite hello, the one a child uses with a grown-up. Then he asks a question a neighbour asked.
- 'How was it there in Hungary?' Literally, 'how is it there, in Hungary'. After the little word for 'in', the country's name changes its ending. The father keeps it short.
- 'Good! Everything's fine over there.' The word for 'fine' looks a lot like 'normal'. Vanya only cares about one thing.
- 'Dad, did you bring any gum?' There's no word for 'did' here: the question is in his voice. And the verb ends in a plain consonant because the person who brought it, Dad, is a man. Dad likes to tease.
- 'Gum? I didn't have enough money.' The word for money is plural in Russian, like 'funds'. Vanya is not having it.
- 'Oh, come on, Dad!' The first little word does the work of 'oh, come on'. So Dad gives in.
- 'All right, here you go.' The second word literally means 'hold', and you hear it whenever someone hands something over. Then he points.
- 'There, look.' And Vanya can't believe his luck.
- 'So much gum! Cool!' The second word really means 'class', but kids use it for 'cool' or 'great'. There's one more present.
- 'And this is for you. A toy car!' The word for the car ends in a little suffix that makes things small, so it's a little car, a toy. Vanya remembers his manners.
- 'Thanks, Dad!' Then Dad gives an instruction.
- 'Share it all equally with everyone, understand?' The verb is a command. And the last word is the past tense of 'understand', but it means 'got it?', right now. Vanya promises.
- 'Got it.' One word, same verb, now as an answer. Keep an eye on that promise. Meanwhile Arkady has a question too.
- 'Can I go and have a look?' Literally, 'can I go, I'll look'. Two verbs side by side, a very natural way to talk. An adult in his room says no.
- 'Not allowed? All right, I'll go and do my homework.' The first word means 'it's not allowed', the opposite of the 'can I' he just asked with. And homework is literally 'to learn lessons'. That's all he gets this time.
