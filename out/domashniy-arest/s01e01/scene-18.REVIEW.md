## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Recognise it?' One word does the whole job: the verb ends in the 'you' form, so no pronoun is needed. Then Samson looks around.
- 'Nothing has changed here.' Notice the double negative: literally 'nothing not changed'. In Russian that's normal, and it doesn't cancel out. Arkady struggles.
- 'Let me go, Samson!' A short command, in the casual form he uses with his old neighbour. Samson gives him his orders.
- 'Now you're going to fix the pipe.' The word for 'you will be', plus a verb, is the simplest way to talk about the future. The pipe is wrapped in old rags, and it leaks.
- 'And don't forget to wash the toilet.' The order is upside down compared with English: toilet first, then 'don't forget', then 'wash'. Arkady refuses.
- 'Listen, Samson, I'm not going to do this.' Again the word for 'I will be', now with 'not'. In the show he says it a lot less politely. Then he changes tactics.
- 'I'll be out of here in two weeks.' The verb is a completed action, so in the present form it points to the future. That's the second way to talk about the future, and you'll hear it everywhere.
- 'I'll solve all your problems.' Same thing: a completed-action verb, so it means 'I will solve'. Spoken like a true politician.
- 'You'll have everything you want.' Literally 'at you will be everything'. That's how Russians say someone has something. Nina is interested.
- 'Everything we want, you say?' She repeats his words, but changes 'you want' to 'we want'. Arkady insists.
- 'Everything you want. I swear!' Right then, Samson isn't convinced at all.
- 'Nina, don't listen. It's another trick.' The word for 'again' makes it clear: he has tricked them before. Arkady has an answer for that.
- 'No, it's not a trick. This time everything will be official.' The last word sounds like the English, so you can guess it. Nina knows what she wants.
- 'You're not leaving here until you sign everything.' Literally 'while you don't sign everything, you won't leave'. A useful pattern for setting conditions. Arkady likes her style.
- 'Now there's a businesswoman, you can see it at once.' The middle phrase means 'it's immediately visible'. Nina calls her daughter.
- 'Yulia, bring a pen and a piece of paper.' The verb is a command, and both things are in the object form, because Yulia is to bring them. Arkady has one more request.
- 'Listen, could we go somewhere else?' This time he uses the polite or plural form of 'listen', because he's speaking to the whole family. The word in the middle is very handy: it means 'is it possible' or 'may we'.
- 'It smells bad in here.' Literally 'here badly smells'. No 'it' is needed. And Nina agrees, for once.
- 'All right, let's go to our room.' The second word means 'let's go'. And so the negotiations move next door.
