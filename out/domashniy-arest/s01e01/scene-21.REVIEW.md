## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Pasha, listen. They weren't local, they were from Moscow.' He means the police who arrested him. The little word in the middle means 'but rather': not this, but that. Then the favour.
- 'Talk to Arakelyan.' He's the businessman who handed Arkady the bag of money. After the word for 'with', his name takes a new ending.
- 'Get him to withdraw his statement.' The first word means 'let': let him take it back. Pasha answers calmly.
- 'He won't withdraw the statement, Arkasha.' The verb comes before 'he' here, which makes it sound flat and final. Arkady doesn't accept that.
- 'Then make sure he does.' Literally, 'then do it so that he takes it back'. And now Pasha drops his bombshell.
- 'And why would I?' Two short words, and they already sound cold.
- 'I'm the one who made him write it.' The word after 'I' means 'myself'. So the arrest was Pasha's plan from the start. Arkady can hardly believe it.
- 'What do you mean, Pasha?' Literally, 'how so?'. Pasha reminds him of their talk that morning.
- 'I told you this morning: don't rush.' The word for 'this morning' is simply 'in the morning'. He means the deal with the people from Moscow.
- 'It's a very important question.' Then he spells it out.
- 'Everyone in town wants this cluster. Only you are against it.' The cluster is the big industrial project Arkady turned down. And Pasha gives him a lesson in leadership.
- 'You've become too sentimental. And a leader has to be tough.' The word for 'has to' changes with the person: here it is masculine, for the leader. Arkady turns to his wife.
- 'Did you get that, Vika? He has cheated us.' He speaks to her as 'you', and the 'a' at the end of 'got' shows she is a woman. Arkady goes for Pasha, and Pasha holds him back.
- 'Easy, easy.' The word really means 'quiet'. Arkady pulls away.
- 'Don't touch me!' A command with the word for 'not' in front. Then he tries to frighten Pasha.
- 'Do you know how much money I have?' To say 'I have', Russians say 'at me there is'. You'll hear that pattern again in a moment.
- 'You'll be sorry!' A classic threat. But Pasha is not impressed.
- 'But you don't have any money.' The same pattern: 'at you there is no money'. And here is why.
- 'All the money is your wife's.' Literally, 'all the money is at your wife's'. Notice that 'your' comes after 'wife' here; in speech that puts weight on it. Every account is in Vika's name. Arkady still trusts her.
- 'So what? What difference does it make? Vika is my wife.' The dash stands for the missing 'is'; Russian simply leaves it out in the present. But Vika has news of her own.
- 'Arkasha, there's something I have to tell you.' Arkasha is the warm, short form of his name. The word for 'have to' ends in 'a' this time, because Vika is a woman.
