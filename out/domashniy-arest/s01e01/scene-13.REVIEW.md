## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Eduard Vladimirovich, I'll only be a minute.' She uses his first name and his father's name, which is how you address a boss or a teacher. The last word is a tiny, friendly form of 'minute'. Then her big news.
- 'I saved the monastery!' The action word is in the past, and it ends in the feminine form, because Marina is a woman. But the dean is not excited at all.
- 'Marina Sergeyevna, I already know. The town hall called me.' Literally, 'to me they called from the town hall'. Russian often says 'they' when nobody in particular is named. Then he warns her.
- 'You know what scoundrels they are.' He calls the officials scoundrels, but he still won't stand up to them. Notice that he uses the casual 'you' with her. Marina asks him straight out.
- 'So what do you suggest?' She answers with the polite 'you', because he is her boss. Here is his advice.
- 'You teach.' A command, made friendlier by the 'you' in front: in other words, just do your job.
- 'And let them build their shopping centres.' The little word in the middle means 'let'. That's how you say 'let them do it' about other people. He adds a grand phrase to make it sound wise.
- 'History will punish them.' The order is 'them will punish history', so the one who punishes comes last. Russian word order is free, because the endings show who does what. Marina can't accept it.
- 'So we just stand and watch?' Two action words in their dictionary form, 'to stand' and 'to watch'. The dean shrugs it off.
- 'Marina, we have no choice.' Literally, 'at us there is no choice'. It's the same pattern Russians use to say what they have, or don't have. Marina reminds him of who he used to be.
- 'Eduard Vladimirovich, that's not what you taught us.' He was once her teacher, so it really stings. Now the dean is worried about what she'll do next.
- 'Marina, what are you doing?' A simple question you'll hear all the time. She tells him.
- 'Well, I'm not going to close my eyes to this.' This is the future with the helping word for 'will', plus the dictionary form, 'to close'. She means to tell everyone, on the internet and among her students.
- 'I'll rally people!' The action word literally means 'I will lift'. Marina knows what this may cost her.
- 'Are you going to fire me?' Short and direct. The action word is in the future. He doesn't say yes, but he doesn't say no either.
- 'I don't want that. But remember, your son is ill.' The second sentence is the threat: she has a sick son and needs her job. Then he asks something that sounds very old-fashioned.
- 'I'm begging you, take it back, Marina. Galileo took it back too.' The action word means to renounce your words, the way Galileo did before his judges. Marina has the last word.
- 'Then go and hire Galileo.' Literally, 'then take Galileo to work'. That's how Russians say 'to hire someone'. And with that, she walks out.
