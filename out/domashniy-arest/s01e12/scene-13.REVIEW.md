## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Nina, in fact nobody robbed me.' It turns out Samson originally made up a robbery story to cover the casino loss, and now he is finally coming clean.
- 'And why did you say nothing to me right away?' Notice she isn't screaming about the money—what hurts her first is that he kept a secret from her.
- 'I was very much afraid that you would leave.' Big, tough Samson admits what terrified him all those years: losing Nina.
- 'Why did you hide the truth from your wife?' Calling herself "your wife" instead of "me" gently reminds him what marriage is supposed to mean.
- 'I felt very ashamed in front of you, Nina.' In Russian, shame is something you feel "in front of" ("pered") the person you let down.
- 'I would never have walked away from you anywhere!' And here is why Nina is the heart of this show: for her, money comes and goes, but family is permanent.
- 'Will you truly stay with me after this?' After carrying the lie for so long, he can barely believe forgiveness is that simple.
- 'We have lived together in the communal flat for so many years.' If shared hallways and a cramped kitchen didn't break their marriage, a lost briefcase of cash won't either.
- 'I promise to return all the lost money back.' With that weight off his chest, Samson vows to earn back every ruble he threw away.
- 'The main thing is that you always tell me the truth.' She sets only one house rule going forward: no more cover stories between husband and wife.
- 'Thank you very much for your forgiveness and love.' Simple, unadorned words from a man who knows he got a second chance he didn't deserve.
- 'Let's go drink tea, we will handle everything.' We come full circle back to the kitchen kettle: in Nina and Samson's home, every storm ends over a cup of tea.
