## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Whisky, please. Thank you.' The businessman Sizov orders a drink, and then Pasha turns to Yulia.
- 'Miss, bring me a cappuccino without cinnamon.' In Russian restaurants, 'young woman' is the polite way to call a waitress. Notice that 'without' takes the 'of' form.
- 'All right, I'll bring it right now.' Yulia turns to leave, and Pasha immediately changes his mind.
- 'Or rather, make it with cinnamon!' Now 'with' takes the 'with/as' form of 'cinnamon'. Pasha then stands up to address the businessmen.
- 'Many of you have begun to have doubts.' Literally 'by many out of you doubts have appeared'.
- 'I want to tell you.' He puts on his most confident smile.
- 'The process is moving along, everything is fine.' Literally 'the process goes'.
- 'The cluster will be built!' He uses the future tense of 'to be' with the short passive form 'built'.
- 'I still expect participation from you. That is all.' The verb 'to wait for' or 'to expect' takes the 'of' form of 'participation'.
- 'Pasha, tell us instead. Is the governor with us?' Sizov cuts straight to the point.
- 'Of course he is with us!' Pasha doesn't hesitate, but Sizov knows better.
- 'But I know that he put the question off.' This phrase means 'laid aside' or 'postponed'.
- 'He is waiting for the election results.' After 'waits for', 'results' is in the plural 'of' form.
- 'That's funny! What election results could there possibly be?' Pasha scoffs at the idea that he could lose.
- 'I don't know. Maybe Samsonov will win.' After the television scandal over the Society for the Blind, everyone in town is talking about Samsonov.
- 'Stay calm! The chairwoman of the election commission is sitting here.' The compound word for 'election commission' is a classic Russian abbreviation.
- 'Right now we will find out everything from her.' Just then, Yulia sets Pasha's cup down on the table.
- 'Here is your cappuccino.' Notice that 'cappuccino' is an indeclinable masculine noun in Russian, so it takes the masculine possessive 'your'.
- 'My dear, I asked for it with cinnamon!' Pasha looks at the cup in annoyance.
- 'Oh, forgive me! I'll do it right now.' Yulia apologizes and hurries back toward the bar.
