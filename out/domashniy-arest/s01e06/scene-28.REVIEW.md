## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Daughter, you take after me completely!' Literally, 'you are all into me' — the same Russian idiom for taking after a parent that we just heard in Marina's room.
- 'I mean, you're just as stubborn.' The three-word phrase at the start literally means 'I have in view', the standard Russian way to say 'I mean'.
- 'Dad, that was all Ilona! Thank her for that.' Notice the 'to me/you' pronoun 'to her' before 'thank you'.
- 'If not for her, I would have got scared again.' By making Galina furious right before she went on stage, Ilona made her forget her stage fright and dance brilliantly. Then Galina asks her father directly.
- 'Will you help her, Dad?' Ignatyev still has a bruised face from Samson's punch earlier today, when Samson thought Ignatyev was preying on Yulia. Yet that punch actually impressed him.
- 'Well, alright. Maybe Samsonov will become a thief too.' After 'will become', 'thief' takes the 'with/as' form.
- 'But at least he loves his daughter!' The three-word expression at the start means 'at least', literally 'by the extreme measure'. Ignatyev respects a father who fights for his child.
- 'Thank you, Veniamin Igorevich! You won't regret it.' Then Ignatyev can't resist grumbling to Galina about his beloved meatpacking plant.
- 'Galya, if only you had continued my business!' He still dreams of handing his sausage empire to his daughter.
- 'You and I would have moved mountains! And you chose ballet!' Literally, 'we would have rolled mountains over'.
- 'Dad, come on, don't start again!' Before father and daughter can quarrel all over again, Ilona steps in.
- 'Right, Ignatyev, first transfer the money to me.' Business first, family arguments second!
- 'And then sort out your relationship!' Literally, 'clarify relations' — the everyday Russian phrase for hashing out a personal argument.
