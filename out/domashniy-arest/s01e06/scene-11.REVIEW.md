## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Because Uncle Edik is coming to see Mum.' She means Dean Eduard Vladimirovich.
- 'And we need to do some work.' Overhearing this, Arkady leans out of his room with a grin.
- 'Why do you need to go anywhere?' The question opens with the question word for 'why' and builds toward 'to go'.
- 'Stay at home.' When Lyosha says Arkady doesn't have an academic degree like Uncle Edik, Arkady boasts.
- 'Why, I'm a professor in this business!' Listen for the word meaning 'professor', which leads straight into 'business'.
- 'Not like your Uncle Edik.' Once Lyosha steps aside, Arkady tries to charm Marina.
- 'Marina, I can be very gentle and affectionate.' Both adjectives are in the 'with/as' form after 'to be'.
- 'Listen, just give me one more chance!' In this moment, Marina cuts him off in the corridor and tells him to stop.
- 'Right, don't ruin my wonderful mood.' Seeing he has no chance with Marina, Arkady switches to plan B.
- 'Fine, then lend me at least some money.' In colloquial Russian, the verb 'borrow' is often used to mean 'lend'. He names his price.
- 'Five thousand roubles.' Listen for the word meaning 'five', which leads straight into 'roubles'.
- 'I'll invite a female friend over.' Right then, Marina ignores him and takes her son by the hand.
- 'Let's go, Lyosha.' As she walks to the door, Arkady calls after her indignantly.
- 'And I saved you from the debt collectors, by the way!' That parenthetical phrase literally means 'between other things'. Marina looks back from the door.
- 'And I said thank you to you!' As the door shuts, old neighbour Klavdia Viktorovna shuffles up behind Arkady.
- 'Arkasha, I can give you five thousand, after all.' In response, Arkady blinks in surprise, and she adds her condition.
- 'For that girlfriend of yours!' Russian omits the present-tense verb 'to be', connecting 'for' directly with 'your'.
- 'Only you'll have to work them off.' Literally 'to you it will fall to work them off'. Arkady beams.
- 'Now that is unexpected!' The line opens with 'now' and lands on 'unexpected' at the end for emphasis.
- 'You little berry of mine!' He bows gallantly to the old pensioner.
- 'Why, I'm ready for anything!' Listen for the word meaning 'ready', which leads straight into 'ready'.
- 'Give the orders.' In response, Klavdia Viktorovna points down the corridor toward a mountain of washing.
- 'We'll start with the laundry.' Notice how 'we'll start' sets up the clause and leads naturally into 'laundry'.
- 'Let's go.' Notice the word order: 'let's go' comes first, and 'let's go' finishes the sentence.
