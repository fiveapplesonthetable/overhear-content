## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Oh, Marinka, I feel as if I've been born again!' Because Klavdia is a woman, the past-tense verb ends in '-las'.
- 'Heels like a baby's.' She uses a tender diminutive for 'heels'.
- 'He even went over them with a pumice stone.' The 'with'-form diminutive means 'with a little pumice stone'. Rubbing his aching back, Arkady holds out his hand for the promised five thousand roubles.
- 'Right, hand over the money, landlady.' Meanwhile, Klavdia points out the kitchen window at a bare tree branch.
- 'Take the bag off the branch and I'll give it to you.' Both verbs are in the future-tense form: 'you will take it off, and I will give it'.
- 'What?' Arkady stares at her in disbelief.
- 'What bag?' He walks over to the window.
- 'It's right here, the upstairs neighbours threw it.' Klavdia Viktorovna puts 'right here' up front and finishes with 'threw' to make the point unmistakable.
- 'It's been hanging outside for a month already!' Russian uses the present tense for an action that started in the past and is still going on.
- 'I walk up to the stove.' The key word here means 'the stove', anchored at the close by 'walk up'.
- 'And it dangles right before my eyes!' After 'in front of', 'eyes' takes the 'with'-form plural.
- 'It's not in the flat, it's outside!' Russian leaves out 'am', 'is', or 'are' in the present tense, linking 'it' straight to 'outside'.
- 'Well, you can see the Moon too.' Arkady points out the flaw in her logic, but Klavdia will not budge.
- 'But I can see it!' The key word here means 'see', anchored at the close by 'see'.
- 'Well, if you don't want to, suit yourself.' Literally 'you don't want, as you want'. Meanwhile, in the Samsonovs' room, soaking-wet Samson and Nina are scolding Slavik over the collage he made at school.
- 'Our daughter hasn't been going to school for two weeks!' The line opens with 'daughter' and lands on 'goes' at the end for emphasis.
- 'Is that normal too?' Nina turns on young Slavik.
- 'Slavik, why didn't you tell us about your sister?' At this point, Slavik mutters proudly that he is not a snitch, and Samson agrees that a real boy never rats anyone out.
- 'We don't know what is happening with our daughter!' After 'with', both 'our' and 'daughter' take the 'with/as' form.
- 'If you don't care about our daughter, I do!' Russian omits the present-tense verb 'to be', connecting 'if' directly with 'not!'.
- 'You're driving.' Nina marches Samson out the door to look for Yulia. Back in the kitchen, Arkady ties a short broom to the end of a wooden mop and makes up a little rhyme.
- 'When there is no money in your pocket.' Listen for the word meaning 'pocket', which leads straight into 'money'.
- 'Tie a broom to a mop!' Leaning out the open window, he pokes at the tree branch with his contraption.
- 'It's not working.' The branch is still just out of reach.
- 'Well, where there's nothing, there's no money either.' He plays on a famous Russian proverb that means 'where there is nothing, there is no trial either'.
