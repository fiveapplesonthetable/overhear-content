## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Girls, don't sleep, do the exercise!' Teachers in Russian often use the first-person plural—literally 'we don't sleep, we perform!'—as a brisk group command.
- 'One, and two, and three.' When counting beats or steps, Russian uses the word for 'once' instead of the number 'one'. Ilona steps up to the teacher.
- 'Hello!' She greets the choreographer politely.
- 'And which one of them is Ignatyeva?' Literally 'who out of them is Ignatyeva?'.
- 'Hello.' The teacher nods toward the end of the wooden barre.
- 'Over there, you see, she's warming up on the side.' The reflexive verb means 'to warm up' or 'limber up'.
- 'And are you from Moscow?' The teacher assumes this stylish visitor is one of the Moscow scouts.
- 'Yes.' Ilona smoothly plays along.
- 'So how is she?' Literally 'well and how is she?'. The teacher sighs and shakes her head.
- 'Nothing special.' Literally 'in no way at all'.
- 'Nothing will come of her.' Literally 'nothing out of her will come out'.
- 'She works a lot.' In rehearsal, Galina is diligent.
- 'But when she goes out on stage, she turns into a tree.' The verb for 'turns into' takes the preposition 'into' with the accusative case.
- 'Stage fright.' Literally 'fear in front of the public'.
- 'You'd do better to take a closer look at Tarabasova over there.' The teacher points to Galina's rival.
- 'Got it.' Literally 'clear'. Now Ilona knows exactly what holds Galina back.
- 'Thank you.' She leaves the teacher to her class.
- 'To the centre, girls!' The dancers move from the barre to the centre of the floor.
- 'The audition is in two hours.' Meanwhile, across town, meatpacking boss Ignatyev pulls his luxury car up to the curb after giving Yulia a ride home through the snow.
- 'Alright, we have arrived.' Yulia thanks him and says she could have taken the bus on her own.
- 'How could you go on your own?' Literally 'where you yourself?'.
- 'Look what a blizzard is out there!' Ignatyev still believes Yulia's tall tale that she is a poor orphan caring for a sick grandmother.
- 'Alright, go on, run along.'
- 'Say hello to your grandmother!' Literally 'to grandmother a greeting!' in the dative case.
