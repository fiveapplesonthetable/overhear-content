## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'You are stupid, and your whole family is like that!' Lyova hurls insults right in Samson's face.
- 'Understand?' That is the masculine past tense of 'to understand', used as a sharp 'got it?'.
- 'Got it, got it.' Samson stays surprisingly calm after his reading lessons.
- 'What's your problem?' Literally 'you what?'. From the bushes, Lavrov whispers that Lyova is doing great, though so far there is no effect.
- 'Well, come on, hit me!' This phrase means 'strike me!' or 'hit me!'. Instead of swinging, Samson looks around suspiciously.
- 'What, do you think Samsonov is a fool?' The verb meaning 'do think' works together with 'fool' to carry the point.
- 'Sonny, are you a provocateur from Osipenko?' He uses an affectionate diminutive for 'son' to mock the baby-faced lieutenant, and then spots the camera lens in the bushes.
- 'Who is filming here?' The verb for 'to take off' also means 'to film' or 'to shoot video'.
- 'Who is the cameraman?' Samson looks straight into the bushes.
- 'Is it recording?' Literally 'is the recording going?'.
- 'It's rolling, it's rolling.' From behind the bushes, the camera operator confirms the video is running.
- 'Give back the little book!' Desperate to provoke a fight, Lyova grabs at Samson's library book.
- 'Hand the book over here!' Notice how the noun for 'book' takes the feminine object ending.
- 'Easy, easy!' It literally means 'quiet, quiet!'.
- 'Hand the book over here.' Samson effortlessly takes his library book back from the tiny lieutenant.
- 'Listen here, you dwarf!' Samson mutters the one word Lyova cannot stand.
- 'Go away from here.' It literally means 'walk away from here'.
- 'Who's a dwarf? I'm a dwarf?' Lyova completely loses his head over being called short.
- 'Right then, you're finished!' Literally 'end to you!'. Lyova takes a running leap at Samson, misses him completely, and flies straight over the bridge railing into the icy river.
- 'I'm drowning! Help me!' Flailing in the freezing water, Lyova cries out for help.
- 'What do you mean drowning?' Samson peers over the railing at him.
- 'It's shallow there!' This phrase means 'it is shallow'.
- 'Stay put!' Seeing that Lyova really cannot swim, Samson throws off his jacket, dives into the river, and pulls the soaking-wet lieutenant onto the bank while passers-by film the rescue.
- 'That's it, he ran away!' Shivering and humiliated, Lyova bolts from the park.
- 'Nothing can be done here now.' The agents realise their trap has only made Samson look like a hero.
