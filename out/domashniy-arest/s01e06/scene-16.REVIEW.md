## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- At the library desk, the stern librarian hands Samsonov his book: 'Samsonov, here, please, is your book. This is Sun Tzu, as you asked.'
- 'The book is new, we just received it.' The two-word phrase before the verb means 'just now'.
- 'Please treat it carefully.' The self-action verb form for 'treat' or 'relate to' takes the preposition 'to' plus the 'to/for' form.
- Samson grins reassuringly: 'Don't you worry, everything will be great!' That casual tone sets the librarian off.
- 'I don't have to give you anything at all!' The librarian uses the adverb for 'at all' right alongside the verb meaning 'can'.
- 'Because you are a chronic debtor.' Literally a malicious or habitual debtor.
- 'When did you borrow Nosov's book?' At this point, Nikolai Nosov is the famous Soviet author of the children's stories about Neznaika, or Know-Nothing.
- 'In the year eighty-four!' Notice the location ending '-u' on the word for 'year'.
- 'Thirty-four years have passed already!' The librarian pairs the verb for 'have passed' with 'years' in the 'of' form.
- 'Aren't you ashamed?' Literally 'to you is it not shameful?'.
- Samson defends himself: 'But I did return Machiavelli!'
- 'And where that Neznaika is, I don't know.' He borrowed it when he was ten years old!
- 'Look for it at home! And treat the new book carefully.' The adjective for 'new' agrees in gender, number, and case with 'book'.
- Samson takes the book politely: 'Thank you. Goodbye!' Outside the library, Major Lavrov and Captain Ovechkin inspect young Lieutenant Lyova in his oversized child's clothes.
- 'Well, does Lyova look like a child?' The short adjective for 'similar to' or 'looks like' takes the preposition 'onto' plus the object-form.
- 'Stop pulling the hat down onto your forehead!' The negative particle sits directly before the verb for 'pull' to negate the action.
- Spotting Samsonov coming out of the library doors, Lavrov gives the signal: 'There goes Samsonov! Right, come on, lieutenant, go!' Suddenly Lavrov notices a bulge under Lyova's jacket.
- 'Stop, why did you take a pistol?' Major Lavrov pairs the verb for 'stop' with 'pistol' in the direct-object form.
- 'Are you out of your mind? Give it here!' Lavrov confiscates the service pistol before sending Lyova to pick a fight.
- 'When you come back, I'll give it back.' Notice how two future-tense single-action form side by side mean 'when you return, I will give it back'. Meanwhile, Samson answers a call from Nina.
- 'Hello, Nina! I was at the library. What happened? Right, I'll be there soon.' The verb for 'was' is in the masculine past tense, marked by the '-l' ending.
- 'I'm walking already!' The key word here means 'I am walking', anchored at the close by 'already'.
- 'Right now through the park and the bridge, and I'm home.' That footbridge is where Lyova is waiting for him.
