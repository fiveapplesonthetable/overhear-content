## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Who will thread the needle for me?' Holding up the tiny needle, Nina asks the room for a hand.
- 'Dad will help us right now.' Before Ivan can even volunteer, she drafts him into the sewing project.
- 'Give it here, I will thread it quickly.' Eager to show he is still useful around the house, Samsonov reaches for the needle.
- 'Today is precisely your turn to help.' In Russian, "kak raz" means "just at the right moment" or "precisely", a little nudge about sharing the chores.
- 'Well, the thread is already in place.' Proud of his steady hands, he hands the threaded needle back in seconds.
- 'Thanks. And how do you like the costume?' Literally "how to you is the costume", the everyday Russian way to ask what someone thinks.
- 'On top the costume looks simply super!' Looking over the upper half of the outfit, Ivan gives it an enthusiastic thumbs-up.
- 'And on the bottom I would change a bit.' Adding the little word "by" softens her critique into "I would change".
- 'Why change anything there?' To Ivan's eye, the costume is fine as it is and doesn't need extra sewing.
- 'Just a tiny bit, the bare minimum.' Pairing two expressions for "a tiny bit" reassures him it won't take long.
- 'Are the pants too long for Slavik?' Looking down at the legs, he guesses why she wants to pin them up.
- 'Yes, need to hem it a time or two.' "Razok-drugoy" literally means "one little time or another", a casual phrase for folding the cuff once or twice.
- 'The main thing is that the children perform well.' For a brief moment, both parents set their quarrel aside for the kids.
- 'We will make everything beautiful, don't worry.' Quietly stitching the hem, Nina promises the family won't embarrass themselves on stage.
