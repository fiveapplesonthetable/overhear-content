## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Vika, let us talk like civilized people.' Built from the word for "human", "po-chelovecheski" is a plea to talk reasonably, person to person.
- 'Take your hands off! When will you return the money?' She has no interest in a heart-to-heart; she wants a date.
- 'I will definitely return everything, I promise you.' Dropping the denial at last, Pasha switches to promising he will pay her back.
- 'But this is a very large sum of money.' Having already blown the cash on his campaign, he starts making excuses.
- 'I need time, approximately six months.' Russian packs "half a year" into one compound word, and Pasha asks for all six months of it.
- 'Three days. That is my maximum!' Six months becomes seventy-two hours flat.
- 'I do not have the whole sum right now!' Panic sets in as he admits his pockets are empty.
- 'Then I will file a report with the police.' Her first threat is a formal criminal complaint for theft.
- 'And I will give an interview to journalists!' Even worse for a candidate running for mayor is a scandal in the press.
- 'Your election will end right now.' In Russian, "elections" is plural, and his campaign will be dead on arrival.
- 'Vika, you cannot treat me like this!' After stealing her savings, he still has the nerve to play the victim.
- 'The clock is ticking, you have three days.' Literally "time has walked", the classic phrase for starting a countdown.
