## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Is the car in place? Did you check?' "Tachka" literally means "wheelbarrow", the go-to Russian slang word for a car.
- 'Yes, Uncle Grisha, the car is parked in the yard.' Note that in Russian a parked car doesn't "sit" — it "stands" in the yard.
- 'Excellent. We will take her in it.' Their plan is to kidnap the homeowner using her own vehicle.
- 'Come on, let us go inside quicker.' Impatient to get the job done, Grisha waves his nervous partner toward the door.
- 'And what if the neighbors see us?' As usual, Kostik is finding new reasons to panic at the last second.
- 'What are you constantly afraid of, Kostik?' Whispering in the dark, the older crook loses patience with his whiny sidekick.
- 'What if someone calls the police?' Starting a question with "vdrug" — "suddenly" — is the natural Russian way to ask "what if?".
- 'What police? The whole village is asleep.' Out in this quiet suburban settlement, every window is dark.
- 'What if some emergency happens?' Borrowing "force majeure" from French, Russians use it in everyday speech for any sudden disaster.
- 'Stop chatting nonsense in the cold!' Freezing outside in the snow, Grisha has zero interest in fancy words.
- 'I just want to do everything neatly.' Kostik tries to pass off his cowardice as careful planning.
- 'That is it, in short, shut up and walk.' "Koroche" — literally "shorter" — is how Russians cut a conversation short: "long story short".
- 'Understood. I am walking behind you.' Naturally, the scaredy-cat makes sure Uncle Grisha goes through the door first.
