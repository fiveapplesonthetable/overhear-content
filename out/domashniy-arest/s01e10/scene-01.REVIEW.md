## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Just don't worry, everything will be fine.' Nina starts with a warm pep talk to calm Yulia's stage fright before the show.
- 'You are a real beauty!' Literally she says "you at me are a real beauty", a tender Russian way for a mother to beam with pride.
- 'Thanks, Mom! Well how is it, Slavik?' Yulia spins around in her costume and looks to her brother for approval.
- 'Of course it's good for you, you're the troubadour!' Slavik is jealous right away: his sister got the dashing lead role from the cartoon.
- 'Try putting on the donkey costume yourself.' Stuck playing the donkey, he dares her to trade outfits and see how she likes it.
- 'I offered to let you be the rooster.' She reminds him that he had another option when they were picking parts.
- 'And I actually wanted to be the cat!' Neither the donkey nor the rooster suited him; he had his heart set on the cat.
- 'And who will loudly crow then?' Someone in the band has to do the rooster's big cock-a-doodle-doo, as Yulia points out.
- 'Why must I scream from the stage?' Standing in front of the whole town and yelling animal noises is the last thing he wants to do.
- 'Slavik, you are always dissatisfied with everything!' His mother has heard enough grumbling for one morning and calls him out on it.
- 'You never like anything.' Russian piles on the negatives here, saying "always nothing not pleases you" to drive the point home.
- 'Soon our amateur talent evening will begin.' The clock is ticking on their community variety show, so there is no time left to argue.
- 'We will sing the Bremen musicians' Song.' That classic Soviet cartoon tune is supposed to make the Samsonovs look like one happy family.
