## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'How much money does it take to be happy?' Slepakov opens the theme song with the one question that drives every crooked official in the show.
- 'How much money do you need?' Repeating the word for "needed" turns the general question right onto the listener.
- 'A little bit there, a little bit here.' A kickback here, a skim off the budget there: that is how a fortune quietly piles up.
- 'And just like that, you've got six million!' Swapping the usual word order to "millions six" gives the Russian line a casual, approximate shrug.
- 'A villa in Monaco, an account in Switzerland.' The standard starter pack for a corrupt bureaucrat dreaming of life abroad.
- 'But you need, need, need even more!' Six million and a villa are never enough once the appetite for stealing kicks in.
- 'A supermodel wife and a mistress too.' The checklist of clichés keeps growing, delivered with a deadpan grin.
- 'Only she's just a little bit younger!' Adding "chut-chut" — just a tiny bit — makes the dig at the official's vanity even funnier.
- 'A white yacht sails across the sea.' In the cartoon credits, the mayor is already picturing himself cruising the Mediterranean.
- 'Happiness is getting closer, almost here!' That little expression "vot-vot" means "any second now", though true satisfaction never actually arrives.
- 'Paintings by Dali and Van Gogh in the kitchen.' Hanging priceless masterpieces in the kitchen shows he has more cash than taste.
- 'A church, a mosque, and a synagogue are built.' Trying to buy forgiveness from heaven, the thief funds every major religion at once.
- 'And for insurance, a Buddhist temple too!' Just in case the first three don't cover his sins, he throws in a fourth as an insurance policy.
- 'If it doesn't pan out here, it will pan out there!' Literally about a fire catching, this everyday slang phrase means a scheme paying off.
