## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello, Galina Valeryevna.' Using her first name and patronymic gives the phone call a polite, official opening.
- 'I have a business proposal for you.' Calling a shakedown a "business proposal" is classic bureaucratic menace.
- 'I am listening. Very interesting.' Having no idea who is on the line yet, the kindergarten director answers cautiously.
- 'A boy named Alexei attends your kindergarten.' Without wasting time, the caller names the exact child he is calling about.
- 'He must feel comfortable.' Behind the polite wording is an order: make sure little Lyosha is treated like royalty.
- 'Otherwise big troubles await you!' Literally "big troubles wait for you", dropping the polite mask altogether.
- 'Are you blackmailing me right now?' Stunned, Galina can hardly believe someone is threatening her over a daycare group.
- 'Yes, finally you get it!' Literally "it finally reached to you", a blunt Russian idiom for when the penny drops.
- 'But this is simply revolting!' Outraged, she tells him straight out how disgusting this intimidation is.
- 'I agree, but life can be cruel.' Rather than arguing, the agent cheerfully agrees and shrugs it off as the way of the world.
- 'What exactly must I do?' Realizing she cannot fight the security services, the director gives in and asks for her orders.
- 'Think about that yourself.' Leaving the details to her imagination makes her work even harder to please them.
- 'In the evening the boy will tell all at home.' Little Alexei's bedtime report to his mother will be the test of whether she obeyed.
- 'I hope he will like everything.' Said in this tone, a polite wish sounds more like a final warning.
