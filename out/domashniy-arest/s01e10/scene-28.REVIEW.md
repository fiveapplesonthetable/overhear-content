## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Put on your hat!' Every Russian morning starts with this battle over a warm winter hat.
- 'We are late!' Looking at the clock, Marina tries to hurry her reluctant son out the door.
- 'I do not want to put on the hat.' Digging his heels in, Lyosha starts his morning protest.
- 'You will catch a cold outside without it.' Russian packs "you will catch a cold" into a single word built from the root for cold.
- 'And I do not want to go to kindergarten at all!' Now the real problem comes out: it isn't about the hat at all.
- 'Why such a bad mood, Alexei?' Switching from "Lyosha" to his full name "Alexei" shows she means business.
- 'Because there is a craft contest there today.' "Podelki" are handmade kids' crafts, and today is judging day.
- 'And I do not know how to make crafts at all.' Embarrassed to show up empty-handed, he would rather stay home.
- 'Everything will be fine with you, do you believe me?' Stepping in smoothly, Arkady gives the boy a knowing wink.
- 'Everyone will bring beautiful things, except me.' He pictures all the other kids showing off fancy projects.
- 'There is nothing terrible about that.' "Nichego strashnogo" — "nothing scary" — is the go-to Russian phrase for "no big deal".
- 'The main thing is, do not worry.' Already scheming to rig the daycare contest too, Arkady tells him to relax.
- 'We will make an excellent craft together!' Even a slapdash piece of clay will win first prize once the mayor's people call.
- 'We really will?' One word, "truth?", is all it takes in Russian to ask "really?".
- 'Thank you, Uncle Arkasha!' Calling an adult family friend "Uncle" plus a nickname is how Russian kids show affection.
