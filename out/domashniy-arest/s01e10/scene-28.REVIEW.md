## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Put on your hat!' This phrase urges an immediate dressing action.
- 'We are late!' The verb of being late indicates running out of scheduled time.
- 'I do not want to put on the hat.' An action verb expressing refusal.
- 'You will catch a cold outside without it.' This phrase warns of future sickness.
- 'And I do not want to go to kindergarten at all!' The emphatic particle reinforces defiance.
- 'Why such a bad mood, Alexei?' Using the full official name highlights motherly seriousness.
- 'Because there is a craft contest there today.' The plural 'of' form follows the contest noun.
- 'And I do not know how to make crafts at all.' The modal verb indicates lack of learned skill.
- 'Everything will be fine with you, do you believe me?' Arkady offers warm adult comfort.
- 'Everyone will bring beautiful things, except me.' The preposition requires the 'of' form.
- 'There is nothing terrible about that.' A common comforting Russian idiom of reassurance.
- 'The main thing is, do not worry.' The parenthetical word focuses on emotional calm.
- 'We will make an excellent craft together!' The future-tense form conveys a firm pledge.
- 'We really will?' The adverb is used interrogatively to verify sincerity.
- 'Thank you, Uncle Arkasha!' The familiar affectionate diminutive shows growing warmth.
