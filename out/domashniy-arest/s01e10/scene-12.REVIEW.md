## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'And who are you?' Starting a question with "and" makes it sound direct and surprised when a stranger speaks up.
- 'I am the new editor for the culture desk.' Russian leaves out "am" here, linking "I" straight to "new editor" as he introduces himself.
- 'Bravo! A bonus for our HR department!' Literally "a bonus to our personnel department", joking about what a prize hire they made.
- 'Excuse me, can I have my phone back?' Asking "is it possible my phone back" is the easiest polite way to retrieve what is yours.
- 'Yes, of course! Someone is suffering in there.' Tatyana teases him about the dramatic text messages lighting up his screen.
- 'If you need help, just ask us right away.' Still keeping a straight face, she offers the whole newsroom as his personal advice column.
- 'Reading other people's messages is bad manners!' The Russian word for "bad manners" literally means "uncultured", which fits the new culture editor.
- 'Look, the new editor is already editing everyone!' She plays on the words for "editor" and "is editing" to mock his lecture.
- 'For that, you get one month of coffee detention!' Combining "coffee" with the word for a prison solitary cell, she invents an office punishment on the spot.
- 'You will bring tea and coffee to everyone for a month.' Here is what "coffee detention" actually means: playing office waiter for thirty days.
- 'A cappuccino for me! Who else wants coffee?' Without missing a beat, she places the very first order by saying "to me a cappuccino".
- 'And green tea for me, two bags.' Accepting his fate, the new guy puts in his own order for a double-bagged green tea.
