## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Dear parents and children!' Standing before the crowded hall, the announcer opens the kindergarten ceremony.
- 'Our craft contest has come to an end.' Literally "walked up to its end", a natural Russian phrase for wrapping up an event.
- 'All our young creators made wonderful crafts!' Calling the kindergarteners "young creators" gives the little contest grand ceremony.
- 'Every work was very interesting.' She praises the whole table of clay figures before the fixed results are read out.
- 'It was very difficult to choose!' In truth, the choice was made for them by an intimidating phone call.
- 'All the children tried very hard.' "Rebyata" is the warm, everyday word teachers use for a group of kids.
- 'Let's give a round of applause to all our participants!' Leading the parents in a round of clapping, she warms up the room.
- 'They deserved the loudest applause.' In Russian, "applause" is always plural, just like "glasses" or "scissors".
- 'And now the long-awaited moment has arrived.' "Dolgozhdanny" welds "long" and "awaited" into one dramatic word.
- 'Before I announce the winner, your attention!' Holding up the envelope, she stretches out the suspense.
- 'Who took first place after all?' Both teachers pretend to wonder, even though the fix is already in.
- 'Let's find out the name of our grand winner!' Everything is set for little Lyosha's unexpected triumph.
