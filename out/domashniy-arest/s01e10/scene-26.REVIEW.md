## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I saw how he was going to the bottom.' The idiom means sinking below the surface.
- 'And what did he say to you?' This phrase indicates the indirect recipient.
- 'He said that he loves me.' In Russian, subordinate clauses preserve direct speech tense.
- 'This is a very sad story.' The neuter demonstrative acts as subject.
- 'For a long time I could not recover.' The Russian idiom means to regain one's composure.
- 'But now you have returned to life.' The location phrase marks a restoration.
- 'Yes, a whole five years passed.' Notice the plural 'of' form after the numeral.
- 'Well, shall we drive away from here?' The single-action form implies departing by vehicle.
- 'Oh come on, wait a little.' This phrase asks for a brief pause.
- 'Time does not wait for us.' The third person present verb expresses impatience.
- 'Let's just sit together.' The 'let's' expression command suggests relaxing quietly.
- 'We are like comrades in misfortune now.' A common Russian phrase for bonded sufferers.
- 'Thank you for the understanding, Grisha.' The preposition with object-form marks gratitude.
