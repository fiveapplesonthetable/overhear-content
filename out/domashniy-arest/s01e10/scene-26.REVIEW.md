## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I saw how he was going to the bottom.' Literally "how he goes to the bottom", using the present as if the memory is still playing in front of her eyes.
- 'And what did he say to you?' Grisha listens in hushed silence, hanging on her every word.
- 'He said that he loves me.' His last words from the freezing water were a declaration of love.
- 'This is a very sad story.' Plain and honest, Grisha offers quiet sympathy.
- 'For a long time I could not recover.' Literally "could not come into myself", the everyday Russian idiom for coming back to your senses after a shock.
- 'But now you have returned to life.' Looking at her warmly, he reminds her that she survived and carried on.
- 'Yes, a whole five years passed.' It took five long years of grief before she felt whole again.
- 'Well, shall we drive away from here?' Feeling a sudden spark between them, Grisha impulsively suggests running away together.
- 'Oh come on, wait a little.' "Da ladno" is a warm, conversational way to say "oh, come on" and slow things down.
- 'Time does not wait for us.' With his dangerous job outside, Grisha knows they don't have forever.
- 'Let's just sit together.' Adding the prefix "po-" to "sit" means sitting for a quiet little while.
- 'We are like comrades in misfortune now.' "Comrades in misfortune" is the Russian equivalent of being in the same boat after hard times.
- 'Thank you for the understanding, Grisha.' After a lonely past, she is grateful just to be heard.
