## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Eduard Valentinovich, what is going on here?' Addressing by first name and patronymic opens the formal confrontation.
- 'Why are you mistreating our staff?' The verb governs the accusative plural of the collective staff noun.
- 'Their salaries went toward your project.' The directional preposition governs the destination of municipal funds.
- 'Then there will be no project!' The negative impersonal construction with future tense governs the genitive case.
- 'I completely drop it.' The reflexive verb meaning 'to refuse' governs the preposition with the genitive pronoun.
- 'Wait! What do you mean you drop it?' The plural imperative calls for an immediate halt to the walkout.
- 'You must do this project!' The modal short adjective expresses strict obligation with an infinitive.
- 'I owe nothing to other people!' Double negation reinforces the speaker's moral rejection of unfair funding.
- 'Wait! Otherwise they will simply kill me!' The conditional adverb introduces a dire personal consequence.
- 'Who will kill you? Are you joking?' Interrogative questions express incredulity at the dean's dramatic claim.
- 'They beat me literally every day!' The third-person plural present verb functions as an impersonal active description.
- 'Two huge and scary men!' The numeral governs the genitive plural case of adjectives and human noun.
- 'These are your problems, not mine.' Demonstrative pronoun agreement contrasts personal responsibilities.
- 'I will not work for someone else's money.' The preposition meaning 'for' governs accusative plural possessions.
