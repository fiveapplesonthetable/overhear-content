## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Tanya, and where did your former husband disappear to?' Over tea, Grisha asks what ever became of her first husband.
- 'He tragically died very many years ago.' Russian uses a special word, "pogib", for dying in an accident, disaster, or war rather than of old age.
- 'What exactly happened with him then, tell me?' Leaning in across the table, Grisha gently prompts her to tell the story.
- 'Remember, a large barge sank on Sineye Lake?' Everyone in town remembers the famous disaster out on the lake.
- 'Of course I remember, that was fifteen years ago.' Even fifteen years later, a wreck like that sticks in local memory.
- 'In that terrible accident many people died.' "Mnogo narodu" is the everyday Russian way to say "a lot of folks".
- 'Lyonya and I were sailing on it then.' Literally "we with Lyonya", placing the two of them right on board that doomed boat.
- 'The barge suddenly hit something and began to sink.' Without warning, the hull struck an obstacle in the water and started going down.
- 'Among frightened passengers a terrible panic began.' Chaos broke out on deck as everyone scrambled for safety.
- 'There were not enough life buoys for all people.' Literally "life rings were not enough", a common Russian way to say a supply ran short.
- 'Lyonya quickly threw a wooden pallet into cold water.' Thinking fast, her husband grabbed a heavy wooden shipping pallet and tossed it overboard.
- 'He placed me onto this raft alone.' That makeshift raft could only hold one person, and he gave his spot to her.
- 'He is a true hero for having saved your life.' Even a hardened crook like Grisha is moved by that kind of sacrifice.
- 'Yes, but he himself drowned in the water.' Tanya finishes her story quietly: she lived because he went under.
