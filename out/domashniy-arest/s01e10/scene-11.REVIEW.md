## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Well now, have all the journalists assembled already?' Marching into the studio, Ilona checks whether the whole newsroom is present.
- 'All the new stories are already in your email.' Eager to please, Zhuchkina reports that the day's pitches are waiting in her inbox.
- 'Yes, I saw them. This is a total nightmare!' So much for a good morning: Ilona read every pitch and hated the lot of them.
- 'Why a nightmare? We tried to do it well.' Defending the team, the reporter insists they put real effort into those segments.
- 'All of you need to work more and better.' Effort doesn't count for much with a boss who only cares about ratings.
- 'You need to walk around town with a camera!' Instead of sitting at their desks, she wants reporters out on the pavement hunting for real news.
- 'I am pulling your story about bitcoins from air.' Literally "I am taking down your story", TV studio talk for killing a segment.
- 'But we already advertised it in advance!' The promos for the bitcoin piece have already aired, making a last-minute cut embarrassing.
- 'This material is far too old and boring.' In the original Russian she calls it "mothballs" — stale news that will put viewers to sleep.
- 'Is it really so difficult to find a good story?' Opening with "neuzheli" expresses disbelief: can it really be that hard?
- 'Almost nothing happens in our small town.' That is the eternal excuse of every quiet provincial news desk.
- 'Search for interesting news, I expect them in an hour!' Giving them a sixty-minute deadline, she leaves the room scrambling.
