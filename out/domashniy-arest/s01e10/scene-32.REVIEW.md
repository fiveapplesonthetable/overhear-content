## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Marina, are you pleased with your project?' Cornering her in the university hallway, a fellow lecturer opens with a loaded question.
- 'Yes, I finished important work.' Still in a great mood after her son's contest, Marina doesn't sense the trap.
- 'Fine, go work on your project further.' Saying "nu i" like this is a bitter, dismissive "well then, go right ahead".
- 'Our salaries were cut because of you!' Literally "they trimmed the salary for us because of you", dropping the ugly truth.
- 'They cut it exactly in half.' Where English says "cut in half", Russian says "cut by two times".
- 'What? Why because of me?' Stunned, Marina has no idea how her historical research could hurt anyone's paycheck.
- 'All the money went to your project.' To fund her monastery film without a budget, Eduard simply raided the faculty payroll.
- 'Lena's husband is actually disabled.' Pointing to a coworker, he shows who is really paying the price for Eduard's favor.
- 'They pay him very tiny pennies.' In Russian, "kopeks" — the tiny coins that make up a ruble — means a pittance.
- 'I knew absolutely nothing!' Horrified, she swears she had no part in the dean's scheme.
- 'Now all employees will suffer.' The whole department is furious and blames her for their lost income.
- 'I will go to the dean right now.' Refusing to profit off her coworkers' backs, she marches straight toward Eduard's office.
- 'I will sort out this situation.' "Razberus" is a firm everyday word meaning "I'll get to the bottom of this and fix it".
- 'Try, if they believe you.' Still bitter, the colleague doubts she will actually give up her funding.
