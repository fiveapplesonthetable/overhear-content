## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Mr Samsonov, good afternoon.' It is the polite title for a gentleman, and nobody has ever called Samson that in his life.
- 'My name is Ilona.' Literally 'they call me Ilona'. Then she states her job.
- 'I head the campaign team of Pavel Yuryevich Osipenko.' Osipenko is Pasha, the acting mayor. The word for 'headquarters' or 'campaign team' is followed by Pasha's full name in the 'of' form.
- 'May I come in?' Literally 'permit to enter?'. Samson is immediately on his guard.
- 'What for? We're leaving right now anyway.' He and Nina are on their way out to film campaign videos in town. Ilona tries to tempt them.
- 'We have a very interesting offer for you.' Notice the chain at the start: 'by us, for you, there is'. Samson remembers Arkady's strict instructions.
- 'All offers go to Anikeev.' The preposition means 'to' or 'towards', so Anikeev takes the 'to/for' ending.
- 'We don't need provocations.' Literally 'to us provocations are not needed'. Ilona smiles reassuringly.
- 'Not at all, no provocations!' The first two words literally mean 'what are you saying!'—a polite way to say 'heaven forbid!'.
- 'Just five minutes of your time.' Just then, Arkady walks down the corridor and stops dead when he sees who is at the door.
- 'Whoa! Arkasha, look who it is!' Literally 'what people!'—what Russians say when they bump into someone unexpected. Ilona is actually from Sineozersk, and she and Arkady have known each other for years.
- 'Well I never!' A great everyday exclamation of surprise, literally 'nothing to oneself!'.
- 'Remember this woman.' Arkady turns to Samson and Nina with a very serious face.
- 'You mustn't talk to her at all.' After 'with', the pronoun 'she' becomes 'her' with an 'n-' in front.
- 'She is our number-one enemy.' Literally 'our most main enemy'. Even though Ilona is a woman, the word for 'enemy' is masculine, so its adjectives stay masculine too. Samson nods.
- 'I gathered that.' Literally 'I understood so'. Arkady adds two more adjectives.
- 'A smart and cunning enemy.' And right after calling her a cunning enemy, his tone changes completely.
- 'Arkasha, what, are you afraid of me?' Ilona laughs at his performance.
- 'Of course I am!' He grins back.
- 'Come here, let me give you a kiss.' Literally 'give, I will kiss'. Samson and Nina stare at them in confusion, and Arkady waves them away.
- 'And why are you standing there?' Then he sends his candidate back to work.
- 'Go on, do good deeds.' Both verbs are plural commands. Once Samson and Nina head out the door, Arkady turns back to Ilona.
- 'Let's go and talk, Ilona. After you.' He leads his 'number-one enemy' straight into his room.
