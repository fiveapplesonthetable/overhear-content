## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Wait, I recognise you! You used to dance at our club!' Both action words are in the past: 'I recognised' has no ending because Arturik is a man, and 'you danced' ends in '-a' because Ilona is a woman.
- 'And don't you remember me? I'm Arturik, I used to come see you often.' He uses the repeated-action word for something he did repeatedly.
- 'You moved to Moscow, right? Let's go to a sauna now and sit for a while!' And with that, he clicks the door locks shut. Ilona stays ice-cold and uses the formal 'you'.
- 'Arturik, I haven't danced in a long time. And I don't go to saunas.' Russian uses the present form with 'for a long time not' to mean 'I haven't done this in a long time'.
- 'I have come to see a very important man.' Literally 'a very serious person'. After the little word 'to', both both words take the masculine the 'to/for' ending.
- 'He is waiting for me.' That important man, as we will soon learn, is Pasha. Arturik just smirks.
- 'Then why didn't this important man meet you at the station?' Meanwhile, Ilona drops the polite tone.
- 'Stop the car!' She puts 'the car' first, in the direct-object form, followed by a sharp singular command.
- 'Did you move to Moscow and get all important? Don't you want to be with your own people anymore?' The '-a' ending on the past form for 'became' shows that the subject is feminine.
- 'No, I don't.' Just two words: 'not I-want'.
- 'Well, you'll have to! Don't be afraid, you'll like it.' The first word is an impersonal future action word that means 'it will be necessary, you'll have no choice'. Seeing that the doors are locked, Ilona changes tactics and pretends to play along.
- 'All right, Arturik. Listen, I really am running late.' Notice that she has switched to the informal 'listen' to sound friendly.
- 'Let's stop somewhere around here instead, eh? Why should we drive to a sauna?' Literally 'why to us to drive to a sauna?'.
- 'What, seriously?' Arturik can hardly believe his luck, and he thinks that she has changed her mind.
- 'Seriously. Let's pull under Devil's Bridge over there.' After 'under' for direction, 'Devil's Bridge' stays in the direct-object form.
- 'Now we're talking! Afterwards I'll drive you back for free.' The first sentence is a handy idiom, literally 'here this is business!'. We'll soon see what Ilona really has planned for him under that bridge.
