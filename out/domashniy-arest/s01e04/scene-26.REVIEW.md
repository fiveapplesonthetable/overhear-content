## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Well then, not bad. Good job.' Pasha is pleased with himself: Ilona got the video of drunk Samson from old Klavdia's phone. He gives the order.
- 'Now we'll upload this video to the internet.' The verb literally means 'we will lay it out', the everyday Russian word for posting a video online. But Ilona doesn't leave.
- 'Pavel Yuryevich, I have one more question for you.' She uses his first name and patronymic, and 'for you' is literally 'towards you'. Pasha looks up.
- 'What question?' The question word takes the masculine singular form to match 'question'.
- 'About Anikeev.' The two-word phrase at the start means 'regarding' or 'about', and Anikeev takes the 'of' ending. Pasha frowns.
- 'And what's the matter with him?' Literally, 'what is such a thing with him?'. Ilona remembers what she saw in Arkady's tiny room behind the wardrobe.
- 'He's living in terrible conditions.' After 'in', both the adjective and the plural noun take the location plural ending. She adds a detail.
- 'He doesn't even have bed linen.' After 'there isn't', 'bed linen' goes into the 'of' case. So she makes a polite suggestion.
- 'Maybe you will move him to another place?' In the show she notes that Arkady owns a dozen flats around town. Pasha's answer is ice-cold.
- 'No. I'm not going to do anything like that.' Another Russian double negative: 'nothing like that I will not do'. Ilona reminds him of the past.
- 'But you studied together with him!' In Russian, 'you with him' means 'you and he', so the verb takes the plural past ending. And she touches Pasha's sorest spot.
- 'Without him you would be hauling souvenirs from China.' Before Arkady made him deputy mayor, Pasha ran a small souvenir business. The particle after 'you' makes the sentence 'would be'. Pasha explodes.
- 'I don't quite understand. Who do you think you are?' The adverb meaning 'somehow' modifies the verb for 'understood' to sharpen the tone.
- 'I danced in a club.' Years ago, Arkady helped her leave that club and paid for her university studies in Moscow. Pasha shouts at her.
- 'Go on, get to work!' Literally, 'go work, come on': two commands side by side. And he adds a threat.
- 'Bring him a pillow and I'll fire you at once!' The line opens with 'you bring' and lands on 'I'll fire' at the end for emphasis.
- 'Clear? Go!' He points at the door. Ilona's face is calm, and her voice is dangerously polite.
- 'All the best. Goodbye.' The first two words are a formal farewell, literally 'of everything good'. Pasha has just made a very dangerous enemy.
