## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Viktor Stepanovich, greetings.' A formal, slightly stiff greeting to his boss.
- 'I'm not happy at all.' The governor has just asked why Pasha looks so cheerful when Samsonov is on television all day. The describing word has the masculine ending '-iy'. The governor gets straight to the point.
- 'Are you doing anything about it?' Literally 'are you undertaking anything?'. Notice the informal 'you': the governor talks down to Pasha.
- 'Of course I am. How else?' Pasha repeats the key word in the 'I' form to sound keen. The governor does not believe him.
- 'Your voice doesn't sound very confident.' Literally 'somehow the voice is kind of not very confident'.
- 'What specifically?' Two sharp words, and Pasha stammers.
- 'Well, we've been discussing everything here.' Pasha claims they have been sitting here discussing the problem. That sets the governor off.
- 'Don't discuss, act!' Two commands side by side: first a negative command, 'don't discuss', and then a positive one, 'take action!'.
- 'Samsonov must be eliminated!' The surname takes the object-form '-a' ending. Pasha takes the action word way too literally.
- 'What do you mean, Viktor Stepanovich? Just like that?' The opening two-word phrase literally means 'in the sense?' and is the everyday Russian way to ask 'what do you mean?'. The governor yells into the camera.
- 'Politically!' Just one sharp word ending in '-ski': he means eliminate him politically, not literally! Pasha wipes his brow.
- 'Ah, well, that's what I thought. Politically, of course.' Out in the snowy park, the governor finishes staging a photo of himself in a tracksuit claiming he just ran ten kilometres, then demands cognac because he is freezing. Meanwhile, in Pasha's office, Ilona steps forward.
- 'There was a mix-up.' The word means an overlap or a hitch in plans, and the action word has the feminine past ending '-a'.
- 'Nobody met me at the station.' Notice the double negation: 'nobody did not meet me'. Pasha doesn't apologise.
- 'That's not a mix-up.' Short and cold.
- 'Nobody was supposed to meet you.' Literally 'they were not even supposed to meet you'.
- 'How so?' Two tiny words of surprise.
- 'Just like that. Because our budget has been cut.' Remember, Arkady is still holding the forty million roubles Pasha counted on. Then Pasha reminds Ilona who is in charge.
- 'You no longer work for your friend Anikeev.' Working 'for' someone uses the little word 'onto' plus the object-form. He uses a dismissive word for 'little friend'.
- 'You work for me.' Same little word, followed by the object-form of 'I'. Then his final order.
- 'I expect only one thing: results from you.' After 'wait for' or 'expect', both 'one thing' and 'results' take the 'of' form.
- 'Get to work!' A plural formal command. Ilona swallows her anger.
- 'Fine. Fine.' She repeats the word calmly, but she will not forget how Pasha treated her.
