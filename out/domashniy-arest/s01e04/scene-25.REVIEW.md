## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Listen, Nina, this is that very restaurant!' The two words before 'restaurant' mean 'that very, the exact same'.
- 'We have a gift for seven thousand there!' Earlier, Yulia told them the restaurant had given candidate Samsonov a seven-thousand-rouble dinner voucher.
- 'Come on, let's go!' Russians often pair 'went' with 'we'll go and come back' as a lively way to say 'let's pop in'. Nina remembers Arkady's strict campaign rule.
- 'But Anikeev forbade it! We mustn't drink.' Literally, 'to us it is forbidden to drink'. Samson waves her worry aside.
- 'Oh come on, stop it! We'll only eat.' The command literally means 'throw it, drop it'. Inside, a polite waiter recognises the famous candidate at once.
- 'Good evening! We are very glad to see you in our restaurant.' The short plural form 'glad' is followed by the dictionary form 'to see'.
- 'Here is our best pink champagne.' Because 'champagne' is a neuter word in Russian, 'our', 'best', and 'pink' all take neuter endings.
- 'Great! Give us the pink one.' Samson uses the neuter describing word on its own to mean 'the pink one'.
- 'We have a big platter of fish and crabs.' After 'platter', both 'of fish' and 'of crabs' stand in the 'of' form. Then the waiter suggests ravioli, and explains what they are.
- 'And then Italian dumplings. All the guests love them very much.' In the show, when Samson asks what ravioli are, the waiter explains that they are little dumplings, which immediately wins Samson over.
- 'We'll have the dumplings, right, Nina? Bring everything!' Using 'we will' directly with a food word is the everyday Russian way to say 'we'll have the dumplings'.
- 'Very well! Have a pleasant evening.' This wish stands in the 'of' form because 'I wish you' is understood. As the waiter pours the sparkling wine, Nina looks uneasy.
- 'Vanya, maybe we shouldn't have champagne?' Literally, 'maybe not needed champagne?' Samson laughs it off.
- 'Nina, it's just sweet water!' In the show he calls pink champagne fizzy candy-water, not real alcohol at all.
- 'Come on, to our new life! Very tasty!' A Russian toast always uses the little word 'for' with the direct-object form: 'for our new life!'
