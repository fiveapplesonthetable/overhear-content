## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Not five stars, of course.' After five, the word for 'stars' takes the plural 'of' form form, just a bare stem with no ending. Ilona looks around with a smile.
- 'That's nothing, I even like it.' The second word means 'nonsense' or 'a trifle'. Then she asks about Samson.
- 'How do you like your new candidate?' Literally 'how is to you your new candidate?'. Instead of complaining, Arkady makes his pitch.
- 'By the way, if you want, join our team.' Literally 'come to us into the team'.
- 'Our chances are huge.' And he knows what usually persuades consultants.
- 'And I'll pay you money.' He promises he will pay her as soon as he gets out of house arrest.
- 'Ten times more!' Russian says 'in ten times more', using the preposition 'into'. Ilona shakes her head.
- 'Arkasha, you understand, it's not about the money.' Literally 'the matter is not in money'. Arkady asks the obvious question.
- 'Then what is it about?' Literally 'and in what?'. Ilona gives her reason.
- 'I have a reputation, I don't lose.' Every campaign she runs wins.
- 'Your Samsonov has no chance.' Literally 'by your Samsonov of chances there is not'. Just at that moment, the door flies open without a knock, and old Klavdia Viktorovna pokes her head in.
- 'Well then, Arkasha, how is my question going?' She means the big television he promised her in exchange for her phone video of Samson. Arkady loses his temper.
- 'Your question is being sorted out, Klavdia Viktorovna!' The self-action verb form on the verb turns 'is solving' into 'is being solved'.
- 'Can't you see I have a meeting?' Meanwhile, Klavdia is not intimidated by anyone.
- 'Why are you shouting?' Then she reminds him of her deadline.
- 'You have until evening!' She shuts the door, and Ilona raises an eyebrow.
- 'Pensioners?' She thinks Klavdia is just an angry voter. Arkady tries one last way to win Ilona over: reminding her of the past, when she was a young club dancer in Sineozersk and he helped her go to university in Moscow.
- 'Yes, it happens.' Then he leans closer.
- 'Have you forgotten who helped you?' The verb 'helped' takes the 'to/for' form: 'to you'.
- 'Who gave you money for your education? Have you forgotten?' Meanwhile, Ilona doesn't budge.
- 'Don't try to make me feel guilty.' Literally 'do not press on my conscience'.
- 'I paid it back in full at the election.' She already helped him win his previous race for mayor, so they are even.
- 'So it's nothing personal. Sorry.' Across the courtyard, the two agents watching through binoculars see Arkady sit down holding his head, and realise his brain works just fine. Meanwhile, out in the corridor, sharp-eared Ilona spots Klavdia Viktorovna.
- 'Good afternoon!' She stops the old lady politely.
- 'Tell me, what was that question you had with Anikeev?' The split phrase 'what ... for a question' is how Russian asks 'what kind of question?'. And just like that, Ilona is on the trail of Klavdia's video.
