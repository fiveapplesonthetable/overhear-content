## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'What am I, a clown or something?' The two particles at the end add a tone of annoyed disbelief: 'or something?'. Nina corrects him matter-of-factly.
- 'Not a clown, a waffle cone!' Literally 'a waffle little horn', which is what Russian calls an ice-cream cone. Samson tries to pull the costume off.
- 'I won't be a waffle! I'll find another job right now.' Notice the instrumental ending on 'waffle' after the future 'I will not be'. Nina blocks his way.
- 'When you find another job, you'll leave this one.' Two perfective futures side by side, without 'when': 'you will find another, you will leave from this one'.
- 'Nina, don't you understand that this is humiliating?' Nina fires the word right back at him.
- 'Humiliating? And being unemployed — isn't that humiliating?' Again, after 'to be', the adjective 'unemployed' takes the instrumental ending. Just then, a young woman on the pavement stops and stares at Samson's face.
- 'Hello! Excuse me, are you by any chance Samson?' The adverb in the middle literally means 'by chance' or 'accidentally'. Samson blinks in surprise.
- 'What? Yes, I'm Ivan Samsonov.' The young woman turns and waves to her friends down the street.
- 'It's him, guys, Samson! Guys, come over here!' Suddenly a whole group of young people comes running with their phones out.
- 'Hurry up! May I take a photo with you, please?' Literally 'is it possible with you a photograph, please?'. Samson stands frozen inside his giant waffle cone as everyone poses with him.
- 'What is going on? I don't understand.' He has not seen the internet yet today.
- 'Haven't you understood yet? You're famous now!' At that moment Nina's phone rings: it is their daughter Yulia.
- 'Hello, Yulia, I'll call you back!' The prefix on the verb means doing something again or back: 'call back'.
