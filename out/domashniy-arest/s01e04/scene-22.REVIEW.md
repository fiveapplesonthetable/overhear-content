## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Let's go this way, through the showroom.' The past plural 'went' is used in everyday speech to mean 'let's go'. Instantly, the dealership manager hurries after them.
- 'Ivan Vasilyevich, wait! I beg your pardon.' Now that Samson has been on TV as a candidate for mayor, the manager knows his full name and patronymic. Samson turns around gruffly.
- 'What do you want?' Literally, 'of what is it needed?' The manager bows and scrapes.
- 'There was a terrible misunderstanding this morning. I didn't recognise you right away.' Literally, 'in the morning came out a terrible misunderstanding'. Samson enjoys his moment.
- 'You need to look more carefully.' Literally, 'to look it is necessary better'. Then the manager makes his offer.
- 'We are ready to give you this car for a test drive.' The short plural form for 'ready' matches 'we'.
- 'You can take it until ten o'clock tonight.' Because the word for 'car' is feminine in Russian, 'it' is 'her'.
- 'We drive Russian cars. Let's go!' Nina remembers Arkady's strict campaign rule: a man of the people can't be seen in a luxury foreign car. Samson is heartbroken.
- 'Wait, what's wrong with you? What a car, come on!' But Nina stands firm.
- 'Listen, Samson, how old are you? Those are silly childish dreams.' Literally, 'to you how many years?'.
- 'And right now we have a grown-up goal: the carrot.' She means the big prize of winning the election and getting a proper flat. A minute later, Samson claims he is going to buy sparkling water, and instead pulls up at the kerb driving the gleaming SUV.
- 'Samson, have you lost your mind? Is that how you went for a drink of water?' The idiom literally means 'have you stepped off your mind?' Samson grins from the driver's seat.
- 'Just look what a car! Let's go! Jump in!' Nina hesitates for a second.
- 'What about the blind people? I'm supposed to keep an eye on you!' Remember, Arkady told Nina to watch Samson all day so he wouldn't get into trouble.
- 'Keep an eye on me right here! Hold on, we're taking off!' Literally, 'we are flying up!' Once Nina sinks into the soft leather seat, she is won over too.
- 'Better than our sofa!' And off they roar down the street.
