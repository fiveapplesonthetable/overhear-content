## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Klavdia Viktorovna, and here is your document.' The little phrase 'and here is' is how you proudly present something people have been waiting for.
- 'It is a plot at the cemetery.' The word for 'place' or 'plot' is neuter, and 'at the cemetery' uses the preposition 'on' with the location form.
- 'Next to Grigory Soprykin!' After 'next to, beside', the preposition 'with' puts both her late husband's first name and surname into the 'with/as' form.
- 'Here are the seal and signature, as I promised.' The verb 'promised' has the masculine past ending because Arkady is speaking. Klavdia Viktorovna looks at the paper in amazement.
- 'Well, I never! And I asked for ten years!' Her verb ends in '-la', the feminine past tense, and after 'ten' the word for years becomes the plural 'of' form 'of summers'. After Golovkin shyly confirms he wanted to help, Arkady reaches out his hand.
- 'Klavdia Viktorovna, give me your phone.' Just as Klavdia is about to hand it over, the door flies open and Ilona bursts in.
- 'Nobody move! What's going on?' Literally, 'to everyone, to stand!', using the 'to'-form plural and an action verb as a sharp command. Then she turns on her warmest campaign smile.
- 'The city values you and your husband highly.' Because 'husband' is an animate masculine noun, its object-form form matches the 'of'-form.
- 'Such people should lie in a special place!' The plural word for 'must, should' is followed by the basic verb form 'to lie', and 'in a special place' takes the location form. Ilona unrolls an ornate church scroll from the local bishop.
- 'Here is the document. A place in the main cathedral of the city!' She has arranged for both Grigory and Klavdia to rest inside the crypt of the Alexander Nevsky Cathedral itself. Klavdia gasps.
- 'Good Lord! This cannot be!' With negative 'cannot be', the pronoun 'this' stands in the 'of' form singular.
- 'My Grisha could not even dream of this! Thank you, dear!' The verb 'to dream' is followed by the preposition 'about' with the location form, and she calls Ilona 'little daughter', a warm way for an older woman to address a young woman.
- 'I'm so glad! And now may I have your phone?' Ilona uses the feminine short adjective 'glad', and asks 'is it possible your phone?' Arkady panics.
- 'Wait, Klavdia Viktorovna! One second!' He uses the polite plural command 'stand, wait', and 'one second' is in the direct-object form for a span of time.
- 'Don't give her the phone! I'll be right back!' The self-action verb form verb at the end means 'I will return', and Arkady dashes out of the room to play his final card.
