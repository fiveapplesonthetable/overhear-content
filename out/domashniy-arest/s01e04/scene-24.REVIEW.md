## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Klavdia Viktorovna, here you go. Someone wants to speak with you.' Literally, 'with you they want to talk', using the third person plural with no 'they' to mean 'someone wants to speak to you'. Klavdia hears the famous voice on the line.
- 'Lev Valeryanovich! Do you really remember me?' On the phone, the legendary singer warmly tells her that he remembers how she applauded at his concert and wishes her all the best.
- 'Thank you! Lev Valeryanovich, my dear!' Overwhelmed with emotion, she hands the phone back to Arkady.
- 'Thank you, my dear! Bye, I embrace you.' Arkady chats with the singer like an old friend and hangs up, sure that he has won. Instead, Klavdia Viktorovna sits down in tears.
- 'I worked my whole life at the factory.' The phrase 'the whole life' is in the direct-object form to show how long she worked, and 'at the factory' uses the preposition 'on'.
- 'My whole life!' In the show she names the town's chemical plant, where the air ruined her lungs.
- 'My husband worked there too. And he died young.' Her husband Grisha died at forty-nine from the same hard work.
- 'And nobody ever helped me!' Notice the triple negative: 'nobody', 'never', and 'not' all stand together in a Russian negative sentence.
- 'I filmed a drunk neighbour on my phone.' The verb 'took, filmed' has the feminine past ending, and 'drunk neighbour' is in the animate object-form.
- 'And right away everyone helps me!' After decades of honest work that nobody noticed, one ugly video brought her two televisions, a cathedral crypt, and a phone call from her idol. She cannot bear it.
- 'Here it is, the phone! Decide for yourselves what to do. Without me!' She puts her phone down on the table and walks out of the room. For a quiet moment, even the two cynical politicians look ashamed.
- 'Even I feel a bit ashamed right now.' Again, feelings use the 'to/for' form: 'even to me now somehow it is shameful'.
- 'Me too. I don't even understand how we live like this.' Literally, 'and to me'. Then, in the same instant, both of them snap out of their guilt and lunge for the phone on the table.
- 'Right, the phone! Quickly! Where is the phone?' At this point, Ilona grabs it first, darts into the bathroom, and locks the door while she forwards the video to Pasha's headquarters. When she comes out, Arkady is furious.
- 'You managed to send the video?! Why did you do that?' The verb 'managed, had time to' is followed by the basic verb form 'to send'. Ilona calmly holds out the phone.
- 'It's my job. Take your phone.' She switches right back to the polite plural command.
