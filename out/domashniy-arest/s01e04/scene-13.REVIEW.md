## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'We conducted polls. Verses work best of all!' 'Best of all' is literally 'better of everything'. Ksyusha explains why.
- 'It is easy, accessible, and easy to remember.' Literally 'is remembered well'. Then she reads the slogan on the poster.
- ''Pensioner, does your knee hurt?' There is no question word here — in Russian, rising intonation on 'knee' is enough to make it a question.
- 'Vote for Pavel Osipenko!' In Russian, the word for 'knee' rhymes with the surname 'Osipenko'. Notice that voting 'for' someone uses the preposition 'behind, for' plus the direct-object form. Ksyusha beams proudly.
- 'Pavel Yuryevich likes this very much!' Literally 'to Pavel Yuryevich this is very pleasing'. From the back of the room, Ilona speaks up coolly.
- 'I'm glad.' The short adjective has the feminine ending because Ilona is a woman.
- 'Only all of this needs to be redone.' The prefix on the verb means 're-, over again'. Ksyusha bristles, thinking Ilona is just a focus-group volunteer.
- 'And we think that nothing needs to be changed.' So Ilona steps up to the board and introduces herself.
- 'I am the new head of Pavel Yuryevich's campaign headquarters.' Just then, Pasha himself hurries into the room.
- 'I apologise for being late! Sit, sit. Have you met already?' And he confirms who Ilona is.
- 'Ilona Leonidovna from Moscow will lead our headquarters.' The verb 'to lead, to head' takes the 'with/as' form: 'our headquarters'. Then Pasha sits down to business.
- 'Well, let's begin. What's new with us?' In this moment, Ksyusha looks at her tablet with bad news.
- 'We have a big problem!' Literally 'by us is a big problem'.
- 'A new rival — Ivan Samsonov.' In response, Pasha laughs it off.
- 'So what?' Three small words to brush off the news.
- 'He has neither brains, nor money, nor education!' Repeated before each noun, that little negative particle means 'neither... nor...', and every noun after it goes into the 'of' form. Ksyusha asks a pointed question.
- 'Pavel Yuryevich, do they show you a lot on television?' In response, Pasha mumbles.
- 'Well, not yet so far.' And Ksyusha delivers the punchline.
- 'And they show him all day long!' The key word here means 'day', anchored at the close by 'show'.
- 'He is the most famous man in Sineozersk.' The exact same words Arkady just used! Pasha loses his temper.
- 'He is the most famous clown in Sineozersk!' The adjective for 'famous' agrees in gender, number, and case with 'clown'.
- 'I don't want to hear about this.' Pasha puts 'want' up front and finishes with 'to hear' to make the point unmistakable.
