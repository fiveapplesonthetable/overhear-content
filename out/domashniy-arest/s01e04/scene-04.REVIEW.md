## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Please, have a seat.' The first word literally means 'I ask', a polite way to say 'please, go ahead'. The verb is a soft, extra-polite command for sitting down.
- 'Allow me, I'll make it more comfortable.' The last word is the comparative form of 'comfortable'.
- 'Is that better?' Just two words: 'so better?'. Samson is in heaven.
- 'Yes, I think it's great!' The hyphenated word in the middle means 'in my opinion'.
- 'How much power does this car have?' Literally, 'how many strengths in this car?'. Russian measures engine power in 'horse strengths', and after 'how many' the noun takes the plural 'of' form. Just then, Nina walks into the showroom, and the salesman tries to stop her.
- 'Excuse me, please, I'm working with clients right now.' After the preposition 'with', 'clients' takes the 'with'-form plural ending. Nina ignores the salesman completely.
- 'Samsonov, is this how you stepped out for a smoke?' She calls her husband by his surname, which is a sure sign that she is annoyed. 'Stepped out' is masculine past tense, followed by the basic verb form 'to have a smoke'.
- 'Let's go, our turn in the queue is coming up.' Literally, 'at us the queue approaches'. Samson doesn't want to budge.
- 'Listen, look what a car!' Both commands are informal singular, spoken to Nina.
- 'We can do a test drive.' Russian has borrowed the English phrase 'test drive' straight into the language. Samson turns back to the salesman.
- 'Young man, how do I sign up for a test drive?' 'Young man' is the standard polite way to address a younger stranger in a shop. The self-action verb form at the end means 'to sign oneself up, to book an appointment'. Now the salesman realises Samson isn't buying anything.
- 'Our test drive is only for regular clients.' After 'for', both 'regular' and 'clients' take the plural 'of' ending. Samson is offended.
- 'I can be a regular client too!' After 'to be', the phrase 'regular client' goes into the 'with/as' form. Nina brings him back down to earth.
- 'Regular client of the grocery shop! Let's go.' She mocks his grand ambitions and pulls him towards the door as her phone rings.
- 'Yulia, I'll call you back.' The prefix on the verb means 'to call again, to call back'.
