## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Mum, I'm filming! Dad, go on!' The verb for 'I'm filming' is the everyday verb for taking a photo or shooting a video. Samson looks into the camera and delivers his line.
- 'Everyone talks, while I get things done!' Literally 'all speak, and I do'. Then he climbs the tree and brings down a fat ginger cat for an old lady, only to notice a small problem.
- 'Here you go. Only this isn't Matilda, it's a boy cat!' In Russian, the word for a general cat is feminine, while a male cat has its own short masculine noun. The real Matilda is still sitting higher up in the tree. Next, they film Samson beating rugs in the snow.
- 'Yulia, are you filming?' Nina is taking her role as campaign director very seriously.
- 'Vanya, go on, harder, further right!' Both words end in the comparative suffix '-eye': 'stronger' and 'more to the right'. Then they spot two men struggling with a heavy log.
- 'Lads, why are you just standing there? Let's help!' When the men say they can manage on their own, Samson simply picks up the whole log himself.
- 'No need, I've got it myself.' Literally 'I myself'.
- 'And that's it: everyone talks, while I get things done!' He repeats his slogan for Yulia's phone, then wipes his forehead and asks what is next on the list.
- 'Yulia, what do we have next?' Yulia checks her notes from the voter calls.
- 'On Moskovskaya Street someone tipped over the rubbish bins.' Notice the plural past verb with no subject: 'they tipped over', meaning 'someone tipped over'. Samson draws the line there.
- 'No, I'm not going to clean up rubbish!' Even a man-of-the-people candidate has his limits. Nina agrees to skip that one.
- 'Fine, we'll do that tomorrow.' Then Yulia remembers a much nicer call.
- 'By the way, they called from the Italian restaurant.' It's the fancy restaurant Francesco.
- 'They're giving Dad a romantic dinner for two.' Literally 'for two people'. And it's already paid for.
- 'There's a deposit for seven thousand roubles!' Samson's eyes light up.
- 'Seven thousand, let's go!' The verb means to go somewhere and come back: 'let's pop over'.
- 'After all, you love seafood.' Literally 'sea products'. Remember, Arkady warned Nina not to let Samson near any alcohol. In the show, Nina says she wants the big prize carrot—winning the election—not a distraction.
- 'No, I want to win! Yulia, what's next?' Yulia reads out one more invitation.
- 'They also called from the Society of the Blind.' The word for 'blind' is an adjective used as a noun here, in the plural 'of' form.
- 'They can't watch the video.' The viral clip of Samson throwing the mayor out of the window has four million views, so they want to hear it in person.
- 'They're asking you, the people's hero, to tell them how it all happened.' Notice how 'people's hero' matches 'you' in the object form animate case. Nina makes the decision at once.
- 'Right, we're going to the blind people. And you go home and do your homework.' Yulia heads home with the phone videos, and Samson and Nina set off across town.
