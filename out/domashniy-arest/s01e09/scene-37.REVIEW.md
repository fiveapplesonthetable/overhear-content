## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Why are you just sitting here?' The interrogative adverb opens an impatient question about inaction.
- 'We need to go in there and stop everything.' The impersonal predicative modal expresses immediate necessity.
- 'Calm down, the special operation continues.' The adverb urges patience as the imperfective verb describes an ongoing action.
- 'Uncuff me, my hand has gone numb.' The imperative verb commands the officer to release the handcuffs.
- 'You will wait while the operation goes on.' The future tense verb indicates required waiting until the task finishes.
- 'Quiet! My phone is ringing.' The short imperative calls for silence while the verb indicates an incoming call.
- 'Hello, yes, Alla Yevgenyevna.' The standard phone greeting is followed by the formal name and patronymic.
- 'No, nobody is groaning here.' The negative pronoun pairs with a negated present tense verb.
- 'I will be home soon, darling.' The future compound construction indicates planned arrival with an affectionate term.
- 'Vegetable stew for dinner?' The neuter adjective agrees with the noun denoting a domestic meal.
- 'Yes, of course, that sounds wonderful.' The modal particle and short neuter adjective convey enthusiastic agreement.
- 'And can I go home?' The modal predicative combines with a motion verb in a polite request.
- 'My wife is also very worried.' The reflexive verb describes emotional anxiety on the part of the spouse.
- 'Fine, go away from here quickly.' The imperative plural gives permission to depart immediately.
