## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'So, Arkasha?' At this point, Arkasha is the childhood form of Arkady, and from Pasha it sounds mocking. Then comes the joke.
- 'Night has fallen, the mafia wakes up.' These are the words of the host in a popular party game called Mafia, where the 'mafia' players secretly wake up at night. Arkady is not amused.
- 'What kind of jokes are these?' The two little words at the start together mean 'what kind of', and they often sound annoyed. Pasha brushes it off and gets to the point.
- 'Normal jokes. Where's the money?' The word for money only exists in the plural, so it behaves like 'they'. Arkady answers with a famous phrase.
- 'There's no money, but hang in there.' This is a well-known phrase from Russian public life that people quote all the time as a joke. After 'there's no', money takes the ending for 'of'. Pasha turns it back on him.
- 'You're the one who should hang in there.' Here, Arkady used the polite plural form of the command; Pasha uses the short, familiar one. Then he calls in the muscle.
- 'Vitalik, have a word with him.' The command ends the plural way, because Pasha means all the guards, not just Vitalik. And 'a word' here is not a friendly chat. But Vitalik has news.
- 'Pavel Yuryevich, we're not going to have a word with Arkady Borisovich.' First name plus father's name is the respectful way to address someone. 'We won't' is literally 'we will not be', followed by the verb. Pasha can't believe it, so Vitalik explains.
- 'He gave our sister a flat.' The person who receives something takes the ending meaning 'to': 'to our sister'. And that's not all.
- 'And he sent our grandmother to Israel for treatment.' The verb comes last here, which is very common in speech. Pasha finds a nasty answer.
- 'But she died anyway.' The middle two words mean 'all the same', 'anyway'. Vitalik doesn't back down.
- 'But he did send her.' The little word after 'he' adds emphasis, like 'he did'. Arkady is delighted.
- 'Good deeds come back to the sender.' Like 'what goes around comes around', with the good, and the last word is the one used for the sender of a letter. Feeling bold, Arkady gives an order of his own.
- 'And now break Pavel Yuryevich's arm.' Again Pasha gets the ending for 'to': literally, 'break to Pavel Yuryevich the arm'. Russian often shows whose body part it is this way. The guards refuse him too.
- 'We can't do that either.' Just 'also we can't'. Then the real reason.
- 'We don't work for you any more.' To work for someone is literally to work 'on' them. They nod at Pasha.
- 'We work for him.' Meanwhile, Pasha is the acting mayor now. So he threatens them.
- 'Then I'll fire you.' This verb is in the future tense, a single finished action. The guards are not worried.
- 'Fire us if you like.' Literally, 'you want, fire'. The command is in the polite plural form. And they add one more thing.
- 'You won't find better anyway.' The same two words for 'anyway' we heard a moment ago. Pasha gives up.
- 'What a circus.' Literally, 'some kind of circus'. A good word for this whole evening.
