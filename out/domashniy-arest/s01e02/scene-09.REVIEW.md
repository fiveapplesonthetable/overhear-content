## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Pasha, what does all this mean?' Word for word: 'what all this means'. He still talks to Pasha as his junior, with the informal 'you'. Pasha explains, very sweetly.
- 'We're all chipping in for the cluster.' The cluster is the big industrial project with partners from Moscow. The verb means 'to chip in', when everyone puts money together. Then the sum.
- 'Today I have to hand over a hundred and fifty.' He means a hundred and fifty million roubles. 'I have to' is literally 'to me it's necessary'. Arkady doesn't care.
- 'So what?' Three small words, and a very handy shrug. Pasha does the maths.
- 'Without your forty I have a hundred and ten.' 'Without' is followed by the form that means 'of': your forty. So that's where Arkady's missing forty million come in.
- 'I was counting on your money.' A masculine past form, because Pasha is a man. Arkady gives him some advice.
- 'Next time don't count on my money.' The same verb, now as a command with 'not': 'don't count'. Negative commands are almost always made from this kind of verb, the ongoing-action one.
- 'And everything will be fine.' A friendly phrase used as a weapon. Then he laughs at Pasha's tough-guy act.
- 'What are you, the mafia? You're a supply manager, third generation.' The little word after 'you' means 'after all'. A supply manager runs a storeroom: Arkady is saying Pasha is no gangster, just a petty official from a family of them. Pasha gets down to business.
- 'So, here's the offer.' Literally 'so, the offer is such'.
- 'You tell me where your forty million is.' He uses the present tense for a plan, just as English can: 'you tell me, I do this'. Arkady wants to hear his part.
- 'And what do I get from you?' Literally 'and you to me what?'. Russian is happy to drop the verb altogether.
- 'And I get you a suspended sentence.' That means no prison, as long as you behave.
- 'Plus you take all of Vika's money.' 'Vika's' is just her name with the 'of' ending. Remember, the cash at home is in Vika's hands, and Pasha is the one who is with her now. Arkady pretends to think about it.
- 'It's a good offer, of course.' The word in the middle means 'of course'. But you can hear a 'but' coming.
- 'Do you know what I'll tell you?' 'I'll tell' is a single-action verb, so it points to the future. Pasha takes the bait.
- 'What?' Just one word.
- 'I'll get out of here, they'll acquit me.' Both verbs are single-action verbs futures. The second has no subject: 'they'll acquit me', meaning the court.
- 'Arakelyan will withdraw his statement.' The verb comes last here, for emphasis. Arakelyan is the businessman who handed him the bribe. Arkady thinks he can make him change his story. At that, a guard twists his hand.
- 'Ow! It hurts, it hurts!' A word you'll hopefully never need. Pasha gives him a deadline.
- 'Right then, you've got until tonight, understood?' 'Until' is followed by the 'of' form of 'evening'. And Arkady asks the obvious question.
- 'And if not?' Short and defiant.
- 'Then the boys will have a little word with you.' Literally 'will work with you': a polite way to threaten a beating. And then, suddenly very formal.
- 'Goodbye, Arkady Borisovich.' First name plus father's name: the respectful way. Coming from Pasha now, it's pure mockery. Arkady answers the only way he can.
- 'Goodbye.' The standard polite goodbye. Literally 'until we see each other again'.
