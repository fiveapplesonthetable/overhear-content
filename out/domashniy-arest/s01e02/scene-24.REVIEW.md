## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Kids, give me some money, eh?' After 'give', the money takes the form for 'some': literally, 'give of money'. Yulia answers first.
- 'I haven't got any.' Literally, 'at me there isn't'. The same pattern as 'I have', with the word for 'there isn't'. So Arkady looks around the room.
- 'So who has?' Literally, 'and at whom is there?' Then he turns to the quiet boy on crutches.
- 'Right, Shoelace, you've got a piggy bank, haven't you?' Shoelace is Arkady's cheeky nickname for skinny Lyosha. The little word in the middle turns the question into 'surely you have'.
- 'Mum and I are saving up for a games console.' Russian says 'we with mum', which means 'Mum and I'. Arkady gets interested.
- 'Really? And how much have you saved so far?' The verb for saving has a little prefix here, which means the result: the money already in the bank.
- 'Seven hundred and fifty roubles. We've been saving for half a year.' Russian uses the present tense for something that started in the past and is still going on. And the price?
- 'And how much does the console cost?' The word for 'costs' is the third person form of 'to cost'.
- 'Five thousand roubles.' After five and above, the noun takes the plural form for 'of': so 'five of thousands', 'five thousand of roubles'. Now Arkady has an offer.
- 'Listen, kid, I've got a business proposal.' The word for 'kid' literally means 'little one'. And here is the deal.
- 'You give five hundred roubles, and I buy you the best console in the world.' He promises in the present tense, as if it were already happening. Lyosha isn't a fool.
- 'And what if you don't get out?' He uses the polite 'you' with the grown-up. Arkady shows him his wrist.
- 'See this watch? It's Swiss gold.' In Russian, a watch is always plural, like 'scissors' in English. That's why the next line says 'them'.
- 'I'll sell it and buy you a console. Well, deal?' Both verbs are single-action verbs, so they're in the future: one complete action each. The last word, literally 'agreed?', is masculine, because Lyosha is a boy. But Lyosha remembers what his mum said.
- 'Mum said you're all crooks.' She means all the officials. Arkady turns it around.
- 'Let's say I'm a crook. But you're greedy, and you don't help people in trouble.' The word for 'greedy' here is a childish one, 'a greedy-guts'. 'People' takes the form for 'to', because you help to someone in Russian. Lyosha is hurt and walks out.
- 'What, are you offended? Go on, off you go.' Repeating the command makes it sound dismissive. But a moment later Lyosha is back, holding out his money.
- 'Here. I'm not greedy.' The same childish word, now with 'not' in front. He has proved his point.
- 'I see, I see, you're a hero. Well done.' The last word is the everyday praise for children, and for adults too.
