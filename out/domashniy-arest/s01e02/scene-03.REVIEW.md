## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Vodka is liquid bread.' An old drinking joke: it's made from grain, so it must be food. In Russian there is no word for 'is' in a sentence like this; the dash does the job.
- 'You'll make a great mayor, Samson.' Literally, 'a great mayor will come out of you'. The first word is a casual 'great, cool'. Then Arkady looks his candidate up and down.
- 'You've got a belly like a member of parliament.' The phrase in the middle, literally 'at you', is how Russians say 'you have'. In Arkady's world, that's a compliment. Samson just nods.
- 'Uh-huh.' Not a word, just a sound for 'yes'. Now Arkady shows him how a candidate should look.
- 'Watch. I'm taking off my ring.' The first word is a casual command, 'look'. An expensive ring doesn't look good on a man of the people. Samson is puzzled.
- 'What are you taking off? Why?' Two short questions. Notice the word ending changes: Arkady said it with 'I', Samson says it with 'you'. And the answer comes straight away.
- 'Because I'm not a thief.' The first two words together mean 'because'. Samson still doesn't get it.
- 'I didn't understand a thing, Arkash.' Russian likes a double negative: literally, 'I nothing not understood'. The action word is past form with no ending, because Samson is a man. So Arkady spells it out.
- 'I'm an honest man.' Literally, 'I am a clean man', with the describing word after the word for a bit of weight.
- 'I'm not a thief at all.' The word in the middle means 'at all', and it makes the denial stronger.
- 'That's how you talk at rallies, remember that.' Two commands: 'say' and 'remember'. The phrase about rallies uses the word for 'at, on', and the word changes its ending after it. Samson repeats it, slowly.
- 'Ah, at rallies, right.' Then Arkady gets philosophical.
- 'Thief, thief. Where does that word come from?' The question word means 'where from'. Arkady has his own answer.
- 'It's business, Samson. Business.' The same word as in English. Samson tries it out.
- 'Business?' He doesn't sound convinced. So Arkady explains how roads get built.
- 'What does it matter who builds the roads?' The first two words mean 'what difference'. The action word is in the future, made with the action word 'to be' plus a basic form: 'will build'.
- 'My firm, or some guys from Moscow.' Literally, 'uncles from Moscow', a casual way to say 'some people'. So the mayor's own company was building the town's roads. Samson raises his glass.
- 'Here we go!' Literally, 'we've set off'. It's what the first man in space said at take-off, and it's a favourite toast.
