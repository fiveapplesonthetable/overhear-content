## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Stick with me, and everything will be fine for you.' The first word is a polite or plural command, 'hold on to'. Before this, Yulia told him she wants to move to Moscow, and he promised to help. Then he asks a question, expecting praise.
- 'Because who am I?' Russian can leave the question word at the end, for effect. But the answer is not what he hoped.
- 'A bribe-taker!' One word: someone who takes bribes. It's what everybody in the flat calls him. Arkady corrects the boy like a lawyer.
- 'First of all, there hasn't been a trial yet.' After 'no' in the past, the trial takes the ending meaning 'of', and the verb stays in the neuter form. Lyosha waits for the rest.
- 'And second?' The word means 'secondly', and it pairs with 'firstly' from the line before. Here comes the real point.
- 'And second, I can buy you a toy, and your mum can't.' The verb 'can' appears only once: at the end, 'your mum, no' is enough. The boy wants to know why.
- 'Why?' One of the first question words you learn. Arkady answers with the matching word, 'because'.
- 'Because she's naive.' The word normally means 'simple', and about a person it means trusting, easy to fool. In the original he uses much ruder words.
- 'Everybody cheats her.' 'Her' comes first and 'everybody' second: the object can lead the sentence when it's the topic. Lyosha stands up for his mother.
- 'My mum is good!' No verb again: just 'mum good'. Arkady backs off, and then he notices the boy can't breathe.
- 'She is good, nobody's saying she isn't.' The little word in the middle adds 'after all'.
- 'Hey, what's wrong?' Literally, 'hey, you what?' Lyosha is having an allergic attack. Arkady shouts for an ambulance; the children call out the American number, and he snaps that they need the Russian one. Then Marina rushes in.
- 'Lyosha, what happened?' A question you'll hear in any emergency. Then she helps him breathe.
- 'Breathe, son, breathe!' The command comes from the verb 'to breathe', and the middle word is a loving form of 'son'. Arkady tries to explain.
- 'We were only eating caviar!' 'Ate' is in the plural past, because 'we' ate. Of course, that is the problem.
- 'The child told you he has an allergy.' She says 'you' politely, even now. Literally, 'at him there is an allergy'. Arkady's excuse is honest, at least.
- 'I thought you were lying to him because caviar is expensive.' 'Thought' has a man's past ending. After 'I thought', the verb 'lying' stays in the present, where English shifts it to the past. Marina's answer is sharp.
- 'I never lie to my child.' Russian likes a double negative: 'never' and 'not' together. Then Lyosha confesses.
- 'Mum, I gave him my piggy bank for a games console.' The word for 'gave away' is masculine, because Lyosha is a boy. Arkady took the money for the phone app. Marina says that, even for him, this is too much. He shrugs.
- 'What's the problem? I'll buy him the console.' The verb comes first, which sounds offhand, as if it were no big deal. Marina doesn't want his gifts.
- 'We don't need your console!' Literally, 'to us your console is not needed'. The word for 'needed' takes a feminine ending to match the console. Then she warns him.
- 'Come near my child again and you'll be sorry!' Both verbs are in the future. In the original her threat is far more violent. Arkady only remarks on her perfume, then bows.
- 'I'm leaving. Your son will be poor and honest.' After 'will be', the two describing words take a special ending, the one used for what someone becomes. And he walks out, as if he had won.
