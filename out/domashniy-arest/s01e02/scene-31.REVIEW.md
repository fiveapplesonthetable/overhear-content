## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Well, hello, performer.' The second word is the casual 'hi', which shows the governor isn't being polite. And 'performer' is a dig: he knows all about the trick call. Arkady answers carefully.
- 'Hello.' This is the long, polite greeting, the one you use with a boss. The governor waves him into a chair.
- 'Sit down, don't be afraid.' Two informal commands: the governor says 'you' to Arkady as to an inferior. Then he makes a confession.
- 'I very nearly let you go myself.' The word for 'nearly' needs a 'not' with it, which looks odd but is how Russian says it: 'nearly not let you go' means he almost did. Arkady wants to know what went wrong.
- 'So what happened?' The verb is in the neuter past, because 'what' counts as neuter. Bad luck for Arkady.
- 'A lucky break: Tretyakov called back.' The first word means 'it was lucky', with no subject at all. The real Tretyakov phoned, without knowing anything about the fake call. Arkady asks the obvious.
- 'And what did you tell him?' Arkady uses the polite 'you' with the governor. The answer comes as a little scene.
- 'I say: everything's fine, we're releasing Anikeev.' He tells the story in the present tense, as people often do when retelling. Anikeev is Arkady's surname, and it takes the object ending.
- 'How he yelled!' The little word after 'how' just adds force. Arkady offers a weak apology.
- 'Well, sorry.' The polite form of 'forgive'. The governor, oddly, is impressed.
- 'Well, good for you, it sounded just like him.' The last two words mean 'very similar'. And then a request.
- 'Come on, do it once more!' The little ending on the first word makes it friendlier, and the last word is a cosy form of 'once'. So Arkady does the voice again.
- 'Well, why are you so quiet? You're fat, you're no athlete!' In the original he says much ruder things about the governor's weight. A judo-loving governor does not enjoy this.
- 'Enough, enough!' The word comes from a verb that means 'to be enough'. Arkady says sorry.
- 'I'm sorry.' Another verb for 'forgive', in its polite form. And the governor gives his verdict.
- 'Spot on.' Literally, 'one in one': a perfect copy. Then the real reason for the lunch.
- 'And now, give back the money.' The command means 'give it away', 'hand it over'. Money is always plural in Russian. He wants Arkady's hidden millions for the Moscow cluster project: he says he really needs it. Arkady tests him.
- 'And if I don't?' Literally, 'and if I won't hand it over?' The future is made from a completed-action verb, with no extra word for 'will'. The governor's answer is a threat.
- 'Right now you're alone against everyone.' The word for 'against' is followed by the ending meaning 'of'.
- 'There won't be a happy ending.' After 'won't be', the happy ending takes the 'of' ending too. He tells Arkady to go home and think it over. Arkady has already thought.
- 'Well, I'm not giving you anything.' Again a double negative: 'nothing' plus 'not'. He even gives back the focaccia he was offered, and walks out. The governor has one last word for him.
- 'Clown.' The same word as in English, with the stress at the start.
