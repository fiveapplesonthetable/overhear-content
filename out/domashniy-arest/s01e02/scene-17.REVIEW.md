## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'What's this menu here?' The first two words together mean 'what kind of', a very common way to ask about something strange. Then the question that matters.
- 'How do I make a call?' Just 'how' and the verb 'to call', with no 'I' at all. Slavik shows him.
- 'Like this, and there's the little phone.' He means the small green receiver key on the keypad. The ending of that word makes it small and cute, like adding 'little'. Now Arkady needs his old iPhone back.
- 'Right, give me the iPhone.' He uses the short command for one person he knows well. Slavik is not having it.
- 'What for? We swapped, it's mine.' The phone is masculine in Russian, so it's 'he' and not 'it'. Arkady explains.
- 'I need to look up the governor's number.' Literally, 'to me it's necessary to look'. And the word for phone also means a phone number. The governor comes after it with the ending for 'of'.
- 'Give it here.' The second word means 'to here', with movement. The boy sets a condition.
- 'Only if I hold it.' Word for word, 'only from my hands'. The new owner keeps a firm grip on his prize. Arkady is not impressed.
- 'You're such a greedy little thing!' The little word after 'you' adds 'what a', so it's an exclamation, not a statement. And then he finds the contact.
- 'The governor... Here he is. Pig.' Yes: Arkady saved his own boss in his phone under the name 'Pig'. Slavik thinks it's very funny.
- 'Pig? That's Peppa Pig!' The cartoon pig is a little girl, so the boy uses the feminine word for a small pig. Arkady wants the number, not jokes.
- 'Don't laugh. Read me the number.' Two short commands. The second verb means to dictate, to read something out for someone to write down or dial.
- 'Eight, nine, six, six...' Russian mobile numbers start with eight. Arkady dials digit by digit on the old phone. Then he wants some privacy.
- 'Right, kids, go out of the room.' This time the command is for several people, so it ends in a different sound. After 'out of', the room gets the ending for 'of'.
- 'I need to talk to the governor.' The governor now has the ending for 'with', because in Russian you talk with someone. The children don't move.
- 'This is our room.' A fair point: it's the Samsonovs' room, and the mayor is a guest. Arkady tries his old authority.
- 'Doesn't the mayor's word mean anything any more?' Two negative words side by side, 'nothing' and 'not': in Russian they don't cancel out, they make the 'no' stronger. Yulia spots her chance.
- 'Will you give me an iPhone too?' The verb is the one for giving a present, in the future tense. She is polite to the grown-up. And Arkady will promise anything.
- 'I will, I'll give you everything!' The first word alone means 'I'll give it as a present'. A promise from a man who has no money left at all.
