## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'We need surveillance on this mayor.' Literally, 'to us, watching over this mayor is needed'. After the word for 'over', the mayor takes the ending for 'with'. His partner, Captain Zavarzin, prefers rougher methods.
- 'Why bother? Just put pressure on him, and that's that.' The last three words are an everyday way to say 'job done'. Then the chief notices that someone is still in the room.
- 'Lyova! Are you still here?' Lyova is his nephew, a young lieutenant who wants real work. He says he's taking notes.
- 'Off to the archive, now!' The first word means 'quickly', and there's no verb at all: the direction is enough. Zavarzin enjoys the show.
- 'You've got nothing but professionals out here in the provinces.' It's sarcasm. The word for 'nothing but' is the plural of 'one': 'only ones'. Lavrov steers them back.
- 'Let's get to the point.' The first word, 'let's', is the polite form, for a group. A useful phrase in any meeting.
- 'In that flat we need ears.' Ears means an informer: someone who listens and reports.
- 'We need information on all the residents.' 'Needed' changes its ending: plural for the ears, feminine for information. The chief has the files ready.
- 'Right, the Samsonov family. He's a builder, she's a nurse.' There is no word for 'is' in the present tense. Just 'he builder, she nurse'.
- 'She's not very reliable. I wouldn't recommend her.' The little word after 'I' makes the verb mean 'would'. The verb is in the past tense form, the one for a man. Lavrov wants the next name.
- 'All right. Next.' The second word means 'further' or 'next'. Here comes their neighbour, Marina.
- 'Marina, a lecturer. Divorced, raising a sick child.' Her son is Lyosha, the boy Arkady already met.
- 'The boy walks on crutches. And she's an activist, too.' She protested against the mayor's plans with her students. One agent is curious.
- 'Interesting...' Just one word, but remember who said it. The chief goes on to the last resident.
- 'That leaves Klavdia Soprykina. A communist, a believer.' The old neighbour who has lived in the flat forever. The verb at the start means 'remains'.
- 'She knows everything about everyone.' Two little words side by side, 'everything' and 'about everyone'. A perfect informer. But Zavarzin has his own idea.
- 'I like the mum with the sick boy better.' Literally, 'to me, the mum pleases more'. That's how you say you like something. The chief is not convinced.
- 'Well, take her, if you want to ruin everything.' The polite command, for 'you' as a group. And then he spots his nephew again.
- 'Lyova, where did I tell you to be? In the archive!' Poor Lyova has heard that word all day.
