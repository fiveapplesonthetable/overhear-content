## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Let me have a go!' Literally, 'give to play'. The command 'give' plus another verb is a handy way to say 'let me'. Slavik is in the middle of a game.
- 'I'll finish the level and then I'll give it to you.' Both verbs are single-action verbs, so in this form they point to the future: first one thing, then the next. Then Arkady comes in.
- 'Hi, neighbours!' He's all smiles. Then he gets to the point.
- 'Listen, buddy, lend me your phone for a minute.' The word for buddy is a friendly little form of 'friend', the way adults talk to kids, and 'a minute' is also a little form. He's being extra sweet. Slavik isn't fooled.
- 'What for?' A short, suspicious question.
- 'I need to make a call.' Literally, 'to call is needed'. There's no 'I' at all: Russian often says 'it's needed' instead of 'I need'. Slavik points out the obvious.
- 'You've got your own.' Notice the polite 'you': the boy speaks politely to a grown-up neighbour, while Arkady speaks to the kids in the friendly form. Arkady explains.
- 'The court had it blocked.' Word for word, 'it in the court they blocked'. The verb has no subject, which means 'somebody did it'. Remember, Arkady is under house arrest. But Slavik still says no.
- 'I won't give it to you.' Just 'not' and 'I'll give'. Short and final. Arkady tries Slavik's sister Yulia, but she has no money on her phone, so he turns to Lyosha.
- 'Oh, you greedy thing!' A mild telling-off, the kind you'd say to a child who won't share. Then the question for Lyosha.
- 'And have you got a phone?' Literally, 'at you is there a phone?'. This is how Russian says 'to have': 'at me', 'at you', plus the word for 'there is'. Lyosha, Marina's son, has an answer ready.
- 'No. You take bribes.' Literally, 'you are a bribe-taker'. One word in Russian for the person who takes bribes. Everybody in town has seen the news about the mayor. Arkady is not amused.
- 'Then don't talk.' A negative command in the friendly form. Arkady goes back to Slavik with a new idea.
- 'Slavik, look here.' The word at the end means 'to here', with movement, as in 'look over this way'. And then the offer.
- 'I'll swap my new iPhone for your old phone.' The present tense can make an offer: 'I swap' means 'I'm offering to swap, right now'. And the word for 'for' in a swap is the little word that also means 'on'. Slavik has heard this kind of thing before.
- 'Yeah, right, like with the Cayenne?' In the last episode Arkady gave the family his wife's car, a Porsche Cayenne, and then took it back. Arkady swears it's different now.
- 'This time it's a fair deal.' Literally, 'this time, honestly'. And then he adds a sweetener.
- 'In a crocodile-skin case!' After the word for 'in', both words get the ending for place. Slavik says nothing, so Arkady plays his last card.
- 'Well, suit yourself.' Literally, 'as you know'. It's what you say when you pretend not to care.
- 'I'll sell it to Klavdia Viktorovna.' She's the old neighbour down the corridor. 'To' someone is shown only by the endings of her name, the case for giving something to somebody. That does it.
- 'Wait!' A polite command to a grown-up. The deal is done: the boy gets the shiny new phone, and Arkady gets a phone that works.
