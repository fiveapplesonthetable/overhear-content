## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- His wife is Vika, and this is a soft, affectionate form of her name. Russians love these little endings on names.
- 'Sorry, darling, I'm late.' The first word is a casual 'sorry' between people who say 'you' informally. And notice the last word: it's past tense, literally 'I came late', and because Arkady is a man it has no ending. Vika is not impressed.
- 'Hi.' Just one short word, and rather cool. Arkady explains himself.
- 'Work, work. It's all for us.' The first word means business, things to do, and he says it twice, as if he had no choice. Vika reminds him of a promise.
- 'But you promised: New Year just the two of us.' The little word after 'you' adds 'but, after all'. The last word means 'the two of us together'. The problem is the guest standing behind Arkady.
- 'Vika, come on, he's all alone.' The small word in the middle is a soft 'come on', 'well'. And who is so lonely?
- 'I can't just leave him on his own.' The verb looks like a present but means the future: 'I won't leave'. The guest, of course, is Pasha, his deputy, and he greets Vika politely.
- 'Good evening, Vika. Happy New Year!' The greeting literally says 'with the new year'. You'll hear it everywhere on the last night of December. Arkady waves him inside.
- 'Pashulya, come in, come in!' Another pet name, this time for Pasha, with the same kind of affectionate ending. And then the two men head somewhere else in the house.
- 'Wait, where are you two going?' The word for 'you' is plural here, because she is speaking to both of them. The last word means 'where to', for movement.
- 'We'll be right back. Come on, Pasha.' The first sentence is literally 'we, now', short and casual. Vika points at the television.
- 'But the president is on TV!' Literally, 'the president is speaking there'. Every New Year's Eve, just before midnight, the president gives a speech on television.
- 'We have to open the champagne.' The first word means 'it's necessary', and the verb after it stays in its basic form. Arkady is not worried.
- 'We'll make it! The president always talks for a long time.' The first word is one verb, 'we'll be in time'. Then he tells her what this night means to him.
- 'It's a family holiday.' The first word is the handy 'this is', 'it's'.
- 'Like when we were kids, with presents!' Literally, 'as in childhood'. The little word for 'with' makes the word after it change its ending. A family holiday, says the man who brought his deputy instead of staying home with his wife. He calls Pasha along.
- 'Pasha, come on, keep up!' The middle word is a friendly 'come on', and the command means 'don't fall behind'. Pasha hurries after him.
- 'Coming, Arkasha, coming!' The verb means 'I'm going', and here it's how you say 'I'm coming'. Arkasha is a friendly form of Arkady's name.
