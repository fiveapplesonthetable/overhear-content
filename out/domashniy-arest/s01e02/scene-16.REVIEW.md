## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'So, did you arrest them?' Literally, 'well, what, did you take them?'. 'Take' is the everyday word for 'arrest' here. The major answers in military style.
- 'Negative, Comrade General.' The first two words, literally 'in no way not', are the army's way of saying a firm no. 'Comrade General' is how officers still address a general. The general asks one word.
- 'Why not?' Right then, Lavrov explains.
- 'The mayor has hidden the money somewhere.' The mayor is Arkady, of course. The word for money is always plural in Russian, like 'funds'. And that causes a problem.
- 'The local officials can't put together the sum they need.' 'Can't' is 'not' plus 'they can'. These are the people who were supposed to pay for the cluster deal. The general takes it in.
- 'I see. And what now?' The first word, 'it's clear', is a very common way to say 'I see' or 'got it'.
- 'We gave the target three days.' The target is Pasha, the acting mayor. 'To the target' takes the ending for giving something to someone. The chief has a simpler plan.
- 'They should be arrested, and we'll find the money later.' Literally, 'to arrest them is necessary'. Then 'we'll find' is a single-action verb, so it's future. Lavrov has to explain the whole point.
- 'Comrade General, the operation is called Golden Yard.' 'Yard' here is slang for a billion, a short way of saying the full word. So golden yard means 'a golden billion'. Then he spells it out.
- 'Our task is to take a billion roubles from the bribe-takers.' The short dash in the middle stands for 'is'. 'From' someone uses the same little word as 'at', with the ending for 'of'. That's why arresting them now would spoil everything.
- 'The cover story with the cluster is excellent.' The word for legend is also the spy word for a cover story. The cluster, Pasha's big industrial project, is just bait. And it's working.
- 'Everything works.' Now the general is convinced.
- 'Yes! We have to take all the money.' The verb 'take' comes before 'we have to', for emphasis. Then the general notices someone new at the table.
- 'Right, and who's this?' The first word, 'so' or 'right', is how Russians start a new point. The chief answers.
- 'Lieutenant Sokolov, Comrade General.' Rank, surname, and the title, as the army likes it.
- 'He works in our archive.' 'At us' means 'in our office'. The general isn't happy.
- 'And why is he at a secret meeting?' After the word for 'at', meeting takes the ending for place. The chief has an answer, of sorts.
- 'And at the secret meeting, he is my nephew.' He simply repeats the general's words and adds 'my nephew'. That's his whole explanation. Luckily, the general has bigger things on his mind.
- 'Right, the operation goes on.' Literally, 'the operation we continue'. The object comes first here, because it's the thing everyone is talking about. Then an order for the chief.
- 'Mikhail Mikhailovich, give Lavrov your help.' In this moment, Lavrov gets the ending for 'to', because in Russian you help to somebody. And the chief answers like a soldier.
- 'Yes, sir!' It's the ordinary word for 'there is', but in the army it means 'yes, sir, will do'.
