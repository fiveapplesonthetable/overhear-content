## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Marina Sergeyevna, Major Lavrov.' He uses her first name and her father's name, the formal way, and then his own rank and surname. Then he points to his colleague.
- 'This is Captain Zavarzin.' The little word in front means 'this is', the easiest way to introduce someone. Marina is not surprised at all.
- 'I know why you've come.' She is sure someone has reported her for her opinions. The verb for 'came' is in the plural here, because she is using the polite form of 'you'. The major is puzzled.
- 'I don't follow.' Major Lavrov puts 'understood' up front and finishes with 'understood' to make the point unmistakable.
- 'I did say it, about Crimea.' She once said in public what she thinks about Crimea, and she believes this visit is the price. Her verb ends in the feminine form, because she is a woman. And she isn't backing down.
- 'You can arrest me, I'm ready.' The arrest word is easy to recognise. The agents step aside for a moment, and Zavarzin whispers to his partner.
- 'Tolya, she's really strange.' Tolya is a friendly short form of the major's first name. Lavrov has his answer ready.
- 'Yes, but better than the old lady.' The last word normally means 'grandmother', but it is also how people talk about any old woman. They seem to have had another neighbour in mind as an informer. Then they explain what they want, and Marina's answer is quick.
- 'You're wasting your time.' The second word means 'for nothing', 'in vain'. Then she says exactly what she won't do.
- 'I'm not going to spy for you.' The verb is the spy word you can probably guess. Putting it first makes it sound stronger, like 'spying for you, that I won't do'. The major tries to explain.
- 'You must understand, this man takes bribes.' The man is Arkady, of course. The verb is the ordinary verb 'to take', and the last word means 'bribes'. Marina is not impressed.
- 'Well, then they'll put him in prison.' The verb literally means 'they'll sit him down', which is the everyday way of saying 'they'll lock him up'. Then she asks what else they want.
- 'What more do you want from him?' The small word in the middle means 'more', or 'else'. Zavarzin answers.
- 'For him to give back the money he stole.' The first word means 'so that', and the verb after it, 'give back', is in the past form, as it always is after that word. Marina has her doubts about where the money would go.
- 'And who will get it? The people who sit higher up?' She means the bosses, the people with higher posts. Notice the same idea of sitting: you sit high up in power, and you get sat down in prison. Then the agents change their tune.
- 'But we can get your job back for you.' Literally 'your job, it's possible to return'. Marina has just been fired from the university, so this is a real temptation. And there's more.
- 'And we pay well.' A short, clear offer of money. Then Zavarzin adds something very personal.
- 'Buy your child a games console.' The verb is the polite command, 'buy'. Marina and her son are saving for exactly this, so it gives her a shock.
- 'What, are you watching us?' The verb means 'to follow', 'to keep watch on', and it goes with a little word meaning 'behind', literally 'you follow behind us'. Zavarzin brushes it off.
- 'Come on, all children want a games console.' The word for 'all' comes before 'children', just as in English. Marina doesn't take the bait.
- 'No. I'm not for sale.' The verb means 'to sell oneself': the little ending on it means 'oneself'. The major holds out his card anyway.
- 'Please take this. In case you change your mind.' Again the polite command form, and the polite 'please'. Marina has the last word.
- 'I won't change my mind.' The verb is in the future here, 'I will not change my mind'. She sounds very sure, but the card stays behind. Meanwhile, on the building site, Samson has been showing off by carrying heavy radiators, until his boss makes the whole crew help.
