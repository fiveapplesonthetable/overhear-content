## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'How come you got here early?' The word for early comes before arrive. The ending marks the arrival as something that has happened; it does not change the word for you.
- 'I'm not as highly valued as you are.' The comparison literally says I am not like you in being so valued. The word for you stays the same whether the other person is a woman or a man.
- 'They haven't given me much work to do.' The negative here goes with something that has not happened. Much comes before the word for work, while do follows it to say what the work is for.
- 'I stayed half a day and came back, that was all.' Half a day tells us how long she stayed. The small final word gives her explanation a casual sense of that is simply how it was.
- 'Then why didn't you go in first?' First comes before go in. The question is asking why she did not enter earlier, rather than asking her to explain how a door works.
- 'Professor Fang asked me to wait for you so we could go together.' The family name comes before the respectful word for teacher. Wait for you puts the person directly after wait, and together completes the shared plan.
- 'So we wouldn't waste his time.' The opening word means to avoid an unwanted result. His time comes after waste, so the sentence explains why she waited instead of entering alone.
- 'Just look at her. How come she isn't the slightest bit mean?' The words for a little combine with all and not to mean not the slightest bit. Xiaogu answers the earlier jab with an exaggerated claim that her friend is not mean at all; this is an ironic tease.
- 'Let's go.' The word for go is followed by a small ending that turns it into a suggestion. It is a handy way to invite someone to leave with you. They go inside to meet the professor.
- 'Xiaogu, Qin Xiao.' The professor uses the shortened given name for one student and the full name for the other. Names keep their normal spoken form when used to call someone.
- 'Come on in.' Repeating come makes the invitation welcoming and immediate. Chinese can repeat an ordinary action word like this without adding a separate word for please.
- 'Both of your initial thesis drafts have been approved.' The word for you has a plural marker, and two makes the number explicit. Both tells us that the approval applies to each student's draft.
- 'Next, make good use of the time.' The expression for make good use literally includes grasp tightly. Time is what they should grasp, meaning they should get on with the work promptly.
- 'Hand in the final draft.' The final draft is placed before hand in. The little opening word sets up that object first, directing attention to what needs to be submitted.
- 'Get ready for your thesis defence.' The word for prepare follows the word for thesis defence. The action at the beginning asks them to do the preparation well, rather than merely to begin it.
- 'Yes, Professor.' The short affirmative accepts the instructions. Adding the respectful title makes it a direct reply to the professor without repeating the whole task.
