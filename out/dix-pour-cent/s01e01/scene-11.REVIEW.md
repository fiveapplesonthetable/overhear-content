## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Are you Mathias Barneville's assistant?' Notice how it's built: you are, the assistant, of Mathias. French has no apostrophe-s for owners; it uses a little word meaning 'of'. Noémie answers at once.
- 'He's very nice.' The last word is a short, friendly way of saying 'nice' or 'likeable'. Hervé doesn't agree at all.
- 'Everyone thinks he's horrible.' Word for word: all the world finds him horrible.
- 'This is Accounts. They're lovely.' Hervé points at the accountants, and Camille, being Camille, says hello to them.
- 'Hello, I'm Camille!' The verb 'to be', first person: I am. Short and warm.
- 'Now I'll show you the Legal Department.' Here Hervé still uses the formal 'you' with the new girl. Then come the scripts.
- 'Here are the scripts.' This little word is how you present something: here is, here are.
- 'No script, no film!' Literally 'without a script, not of film'. In quick speech the French often keep only the second half of the 'not' and drop the verb completely. Camille spots a name on a shelf.
- 'And here, who's Arlette?' A casual question: it is who, Arlette? Hervé explains with some pride.
- 'Arlette is the most senior agent in Paris.' The word he uses means the oldest member of a group, the one who has been there longest, and it ends in a feminine way because Arlette is a woman.
- 'She says impresario, not agent.' The old-fashioned word, very Arlette. Then Camille notices that one shelf is almost empty.
- 'And Andréa doesn't read?' Here is the full negative: one little word before the verb and one after it, wrapped around 'reads' like two slices of bread. Noémie switches to the casual 'you'.
- 'Are you kidding?' The casual 'you', between assistants of the same age. Compare it with the polite form at the start.
- 'Andréa reads everything.' The same verb as before, now without the negative around it.
- 'She's scared of missing something.' Literally 'she has fear': in French you have fear, you don't 'are' scared. Next stop, the press room.
- 'And here's the press room.' This is the cousin of the word we heard with the scripts, and it often means 'there you go'.
- 'We call it the shop.' Free gifts for the stars end up there. The first little word means 'we' or 'people' in everyday speech. And then the gossip turns to the star of the day.
- 'Cécile de France? Turned down, because of her age.' Here, Camille remembers this. It will matter very soon. At that moment Noémie looks out of the window.
- 'Look! Cécile's here!' The first word is a polite command, 'look', and the second sentence uses a word meaning 'there' that really means 'here, now'. The whole office starts shouting.
- 'Cécile de France? Here?' Camille can hardly believe it: on her very first morning, the star walks in. Good. Now let's hear the tour once more, from the top.
