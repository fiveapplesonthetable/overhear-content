## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Excuse me, dear colleagues.' The opening is a polite instruction with 'me' attached after the action. The description 'dear' is plural because Gabriel addresses several people.
- 'Gabriel, you look pleased with yourself.' The French expression for 'look' or 'seem' is built with 'have' and a word meaning 'air'. The final 'yourself' points back to Gabriel.
- 'We lost a big contract.' Mathias uses the everyday form of 'we'. A helping form of 'have' followed by 'lost' puts the action in the past.
- 'But we kept a great actress.' Gabriel answers with the same past pattern, now using 'kept'. The description of the actress has a feminine form.
- 'But we lost a big contract.' Mathias repeats the contrast word 'but', turning the argument back to the money. Then a call arrives from Rio, and Andrea puts it through, assuming it is Samuel.
- 'Hello. I'm Jean-Louis d'Entremont.' A greeting and 'I am' are enough to give a full name. This voice belongs to someone at the consulate, rather than Samuel.
- 'I'm calling you from the French consulate in Rio.' The polite object meaning 'you' comes before 'call'. The short word before 'consulate' combines 'from' with the masculine 'the'.
- 'I have bad news.' The French noun for pieces of news is feminine and plural. The description 'bad' has matching written endings.
- 'Mr Samuel Kerr has passed away.' This formal action expresses death gently. Its completed past uses 'be' as the helping verb.
- 'He died in hospital.' The past form of 'die' also uses 'be'. French keeps the little word meaning 'the' before 'hospital', where English simply says 'in hospital'.
- 'He swallowed a wasp.' Here the helping action changes back to 'have'. The small word before 'wasp' has its feminine form to match it.
- 'I'm sorry.' The description has a masculine spelling for the male caller. The first episode ends with the agents still trying to understand the news.
