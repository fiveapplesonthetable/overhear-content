## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Thanks, Mathias!' Pure sarcasm. Then he says what he really thinks.
- 'You stole Cécile!' This is the everyday past: the action word 'to have' plus the past form of 'steal'. It's the language point of this scene, and you'll hear it again and again.
- 'And don't say it was for the good of the agency.' Gabriel gives a negative command where the negative wraps around the casual 'say'. Gabriel knows exactly what Mathias will answer.
- 'Of course it was, Gabriel.' Literally 'of course yes'. Mathias doesn't apologise for anything.
- 'Two other agencies wanted Cécile.' The verb is 'to want' in the past, describing how things stood: a different past tense, used for background.
- 'Twenty-four more hours, and she comes back!' Gabriel speaks in the present to make it vivid: one more day, and she would have come home.
- 'We didn't have twenty-four hours.' The word at the start means 'we' in everyday speech.
- 'She's not a file. She's Cécile.' The word for 'file' is what agents call a case or a deal. For Gabriel she's a person, not a deal.
- 'Yes, it's Cécile.' Mathias repeats it calmly, and Gabriel storms off. Now Andréa speaks up.
- 'Congratulations, Mathias.' The word looks long but it's simply 'congratulations'. She says it drily.
- 'You stole Gabriel's star with a lot of class.' The past tense again, with 'have'. To say 'Gabriel's star', French says 'the star of Gabriel'.
- 'Are you proud of yourself?' Andréa asks a question made just by raising her voice at the end. Mathias isn't ashamed one bit.
- 'I saved one of the agency's clients, that's all.' Past tense with 'have' once more: 'I have saved'. The word for 'client' has an extra letter at the end because Cécile is a woman.
- 'Now, let me tell you something.' The short command meaning 'let' has 'me' attached with a hyphen. The final informal noun means 'a thing' or 'something'.
- 'Camille? But why?' Andrea identifies the assistant with a surprised question. The last word asks for a reason.
- 'Your new assistant told Cecile that she is too old.' The description 'new' has a feminine form to match the female assistant. The final description of age is feminine as well.
- 'Gabriel knows nothing. But fix this problem very quickly!' The first sentence combines the small negative before 'knows' with 'nothing' after it. The second gives Andrea a direct instruction.
- 'Where did you find her? She's dangerous.' This question word for 'where' comes at the very end, which is common in spoken French.
- 'Gabriel knows nothing.' The negative pair leaves out the ordinary word for 'not': 'nothing' supplies the second part. Mathias says he has kept the assistant's mistake from Gabriel.
- 'But fix this problem very quickly!' This instruction uses the familiar singular form without a separate 'you'. The final two words add urgency.
- 'Have a nice day.' Literally 'good day'. A sweet goodbye that leaves Andréa with a big problem. Here is the whole scene one more time.
