## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Are you coming or not?' Literally 'you're coming, yes or no?'. Here is our grammar point for today: the verb 'to come'. In this short form it means 'are you coming?'. Out on the street, Andréa uses the very same verb again.
- 'Where are you from?' The same verb plus the little word for 'from' at the end: 'you come from where?'. That's how French asks where someone is from. Camille answers with the same verb.
- 'I'm from the Côte d'Azur. Near Cannes.' The Côte d'Azur is the sunny south coast of France, and Cannes is the town with the famous film festival. Andréa jumps on that.
- 'Cannes? Did you work at the festival?' This is the everyday past tense: a form of 'to have' plus 'worked'. Camille isn't going to let a good chance go.
- 'Yes, exactly. The Cannes Film Festival.' The word for exactly looks almost like English, so it's an easy one. Andréa wants details.
- 'Doing what?' Literally 'to do what?'. Short and very blunt, which is how Andréa talks to everyone.
- 'It depends.' Camille buys herself a second to think. And then she makes up a wonderful job.
- 'At first, I was in the stars' underwear.' She means she handled underwear emergencies for the stars: a strap that snaps, a line that shows. Then she acts out what she used to tell them.
- 'Don't cry, we're going to fix this!' Notice she uses the polite 'you' to the stars. The second half is the near future: a form of 'to go' plus a verb, exactly like 'going to' in English. Andréa takes a phone call, then carries on with her list.
- 'Have you got a boyfriend?' Literally 'a little friend'. That's how French says boyfriend. Camille doesn't even blink.
- 'No.' That's the first of many short answers. Andréa moves on.
- 'Friends in Paris?' No verb at all, just the words that matter. Camille's answer brings back our verb.
- 'No, I've just arrived.' The verb 'to come', then the word for 'from', then another verb: that means 'I have just done it'. So the same verb gave us 'coming', 'from' and 'just'. Then she adds something more personal.
- 'My dad, but it's complicated.' We know why: her dad is Mathias, the agent upstairs, and nobody here knows. Andréa asks one more question about him.
- 'Do you look after him?' The verb means to take care of someone, or to deal with something. Camille's answer is firm.
- 'No way!' Literally 'out of the question'. Andréa likes that. Then she gets to the real test.
- 'Can I call you at four in the morning? Do you work on Sundays?' The first question uses 'can' followed by a verb, the way English does. It's the job description of an agent's assistant. Camille doesn't hesitate.
- 'Yes to everything.' Andréa has found someone as tireless as she is.
- 'Very good. Can you start now?' 'Can' plus a verb again, the same pattern as before. And Camille, who came to Paris looking for a job in films, gets one in five minutes.
- 'Yes, right now!' Just the two words she needs.
