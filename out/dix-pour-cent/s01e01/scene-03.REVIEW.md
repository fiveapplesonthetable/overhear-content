## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Either Thursday at two, or Friday at eleven.' The same little word, said twice, gives you 'either, or'. And two in the afternoon is fourteen hours again, office style.
- 'Hello.' Camille waits politely for her turn.
- 'Can I help you?' Sofia uses the formal 'you', the right choice with a stranger at a front desk.
- 'I'm here to see Mathias Barneville.' A short word meaning 'in order to' links 'here' to the action 'see'. Camille introduces her visit with his full name.
- 'Is he expecting you?' Literally 'he waits for you'. The formal 'you' sits before the verb.
- 'No, but it's important.' The short expression after 'but' joins 'it' and 'is'. You can use the same beginning before other descriptions, such as 'possible' or 'difficult'.
- 'Leave your photos and your CV here.' Sofia gives a polite command with the formal ending. She is sure Camille is one more young actress hoping to be discovered.
- 'This agency only represents stars.' Here's a neat trick: the negative pair is used with a different second word, and the meaning becomes 'only'. Nothing but stars.
- 'I'm not an actress.' The ordinary negative this time, wrapped around the verb 'to be'. And with jobs, French drops the word for 'a'.
- 'I just want to see Mathias.' The small word meaning 'just' limits what Camille is asking for. It comes between 'want' and the action she wants to do.
- 'Your name?' The polite possessive goes with one name. When Sofia asked for photos earlier, its plural form went with the things she wanted.
- 'He knows me.' The object meaning 'me' comes before 'knows'. Sofia is not convinced.
- 'Without an appointment? I'm not promising anything.' The first word means 'without'. The next sentence pairs the small negative before 'promise' with 'nothing' after it.
- 'Have a seat.' Literally 'sit yourself down', a polite command you'll hear in any waiting room.
- 'Thank you.' This short expression can stand on its own. Here it acknowledges Sofia's invitation to sit down.
- 'Mathias, Camille Valentini is here.' The name is followed by the same short pair meaning 'is here' that you can use to announce any visitor.
- 'Sorry to bother you.' The apology is followed by a little linking word and the action 'to disturb'. Sofia uses the polite 'you' with her boss as well.
