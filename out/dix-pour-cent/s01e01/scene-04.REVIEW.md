## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello, Camille.' Polite and cool, the way you greet a visitor.
- 'Come in.' This command is in the formal form, as if she were a stranger. Remember that, because it won't last.
- 'A coffee, please.' The last part literally means 'if it pleases you', with the formal 'you'; it's the standard way to say please to anyone you don't know.
- 'Very well. And you? Tea?' Noémie turns to her boss; she knows how he takes his drinks.
- 'Fine. Thank you.' The first word literally means 'in agreement', the everyday way to say okay. Noémie leaves, the door closes, and the tone changes at once.
- 'Why didn't you call?' Now he uses the casual 'you'.
- 'I wanted to surprise you.' Camille answers with the casual 'you' too; the small word for 'you' sits right before the verb.
- 'It's nice here. Free lollipops!' She has spotted a bowl of sticks on the reception desk. The word for 'nice' here is a friendly, informal one.
- 'It's sugar for the coffee.' They are sugar sticks, not sweets. Mathias is not amused.
- 'What are you doing in Paris? Are you on holiday?' The first part is the most common way to ask 'what' in a question. The word for holiday is always plural in French.
- 'I've been here for three weeks.' French uses the present tense for this: literally 'it makes three weeks that I am here'. She has moved to Paris without telling him.
- 'Didn't you like Mandelieu? It's a lovely place.' Mandelieu is a seaside town on the Riviera, near Cannes, where Camille used to live. He knows exactly where she comes from.
- 'I've got a little loft. And I'm looking for work.' The word for work here is a casual one, like 'a job'.
- 'You could help me.' This is the gentle, polite 'could'. She hopes he'll use his contacts in the film business.
- 'Another whim?' Mathias asks a cutting question. He seems to think she changes her plans all the time.
- 'I'm serious.' The word for 'serious' takes a feminine ending, because Camille is a woman. The man's version ends differently.
- 'I've always loved films.' She even offers to fetch coffee and hold actors' umbrellas, anything at all.
- 'Listen, it's an industry, and it's full of sharks.' He means that in this world you eat or you get eaten. Then he jokes that if she loves films so much, she should buy a cinema pass.
- 'Sorry, that's all I can do.' The answer is no. Camille has come all the way from the coast, and Mathias won't help her. Now listen to the whole conversation one more time.
