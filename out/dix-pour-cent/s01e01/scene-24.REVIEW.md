## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello, Mathias. Come in.' That command uses the polite form of 'you'. Keep an eye on that: by the end of the scene they will both slip into the casual one. Mathias holds out his flowers.
- 'Hello, Cécile. These flowers are for you.' The word for 'flowers' is feminine and plural. He is still using the polite 'you', the careful voice of an agent.
- 'Thanks. Put them here.' Another polite command. The little word stuck on with a hyphen means 'them', the flowers. She doesn't make a fuss over the bouquet.
- 'I'm gardening. Are you coming?' French has no separate 'I am doing' form: 'I garden' also means 'I'm gardening'. Out in the garden, Mathias starts with an apology.
- 'You want to win me back, but there is no chance.' Literally 'in the name of the agency, I present you my excuses'. It's a very formal, very official way to say sorry.
- 'I know you're here to get me back.' The action word at the end means 'to get back' or 'to recover', like getting back something you've lost.
- 'But there's no chance.' The expression for 'there is' becomes negative, and the word for 'no' here means 'not a single'. So Mathias changes the subject.
- 'Are you going to sign with StarMédia?' Here he switches to the casual 'you' for the first time. And it's the near future again: 'you go' plus 'to sign'.
- 'I don't know.' The negative wraps around the action word in two parts. In everyday speech the French often drop the first part, and Cécile does in the show, but the full form is the one to learn.
- 'Everyone lies in this business.' Literally 'all the world lies in this circle'. That's a dig at Gabriel, and at agents in general.
- 'Can you give me a hand?' Now Cécile uses the casual 'you' as well. Asking for help with the garden is her way of letting him in a little.
- 'Yes, of course.' A smooth agent always says yes.
- 'Hand me the bucket.' A casual command this time: for verbs like this one, the casual command simply drops the final 's' of the casual 'you' form. Compare it with the polite command at the start of the scene.
- 'The bucket? Oh, yes.' Mathias isn't sure he heard right; he is not a man who usually carries buckets.
- 'It's compost. It feeds the earth.' The first little word before compost means 'some', because you can't count compost. The man in the elegant suit is carrying a bucket of it.
- 'The earth is honest.' Literally 'the earth, it is true'. French often names the thing first and then repeats it with a short stand-in word.
- 'It doesn't know how to lie.' After the verb for 'to know how' comes an infinitive: 'to lie'. Unlike people, she means. Mathias puts on his gentle voice.
- 'Listen, Cécile. I understand your disappointment.' The first word is a casual command, 'listen'. Then he reveals why he came.
- 'I talked to Miranda.' This is the past tense with 'have' plus the past form of 'talk'. Miranda is the producer who said Cécile was too old.
- 'They're ready to talk about it.' The tiny word in front of the verb means 'about it'. And that changes everything for Cécile.
- 'Really? What did she say?' The first two words show surprise, a bit like 'oh really?'. Suddenly she is listening very closely. Let's listen to the whole scene again, in one go.
