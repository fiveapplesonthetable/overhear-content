## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'What are you doing?' The familiar 'you' comes before the action and the question word comes after it. French uses the same action word for 'do' and 'make'.
- 'I'm working.' One action word in the present covers both 'I work' and 'I'm working'. From the sofa, that's a bold claim.
- 'Do you know her?' The short object meaning 'her' comes before 'know'. English places it after the action instead.
- 'She's really good.' The short description at the end can express approval of a performer. The word before it means 'really' and strengthens Gabriel's praise.
- 'Yes. She catches the light well.' The short word for 'well' follows 'catches'. In this film conversation, the expression praises how the actress appears on camera.
- 'She's done a bit of theatre.' That's the past with 'to have' plus the past form of 'to do'. Gabriel keeps selling.
- 'With her, everything is still to be done.' French literally says 'everything is to do'. Gabriel sees a new career to build, rather than an established star to represent.
- 'So Cécile changes agent, and you look for a replacement?' The last word is the feminine form: a woman who replaces another. Arlette is teasing him for rushing to forget Cécile.
- 'Stop it, Arlette!' Gabriel gives a familiar instruction without a separate 'you'. He is tired of her teasing.
- 'Samuel forgot to teach you tenacity.' After the completed past action 'forgot', a linking word introduces 'teach'. The object meaning 'you' sits before that second action.
- 'So what do I do?' The question word comes after the action in this spoken question. The final word then adds 'so', as Gabriel asks what Arlette expects.
- 'She doesn't want to talk to me anymore.' That's 'not anymore' again: two small words around the verb, the second meaning 'more'.
- 'She left with Mathias. It's her choice.' The action 'leave' uses 'be' to build its completed past. The final written letter agrees with the woman who left.
- 'You think so?' The short form of 'think' works in a brief question with a rising voice. Gabriel uses familiar 'you', as Arlette does with him.
- 'I know Cecile. She hates men like Mathias.' Knowing a person uses a different French action word from knowing a fact. Gabriel is beginning to question why Cecile chose Mathias.
- 'Come on, Gabriel! Don't just lie there doing nothing!' Arlette uses two negatives here: first 'don't stay', then 'nothing', both built around the action word. And it works: in the next scene, Gabriel goes off to find Cécile. Now, the whole scene again, a little faster.

## Source review

Original video and unprompted French audio confirm familiar address throughout. The coarse opening question is cleaned; the uncertain actress name is omitted. The original says that everything remains to be done with this new actress, rather than that she is ready for the agency. Arlette suggests that Mathias may suit Cecile; Gabriel doubts that and says that he knows Cecile.
