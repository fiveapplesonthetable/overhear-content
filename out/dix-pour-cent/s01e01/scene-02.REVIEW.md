## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Morning! Hi, Mathias.' This first word is the all-purpose greeting; the second is the casual hello you use with people you know well. Colleagues here are all on first-name terms.
- 'Yes, three hundred thousand.' Mathias is negotiating a fee for an actor, and he doesn't waste words.
- 'I'm talking about his twenty years of career.' In other words: you're not paying for twenty days of filming, you're paying for twenty years of being a star.
- 'Magali, I have to see the Koltès play this week.' The verb for 'have to' comes first, then the plain verb 'to see'. Koltès is a famous French playwright.
- 'Two tickets for Tuesday?' The word for ticket here really means 'seat', as in a seat at the theatre.
- 'On Tuesday you've got the Kechiche film.' Magali uses the casual 'you' with her boss, and that's normal at ASK. Days of the week need no little word in front of them.
- 'Thursday, then.' Andréa decides in a second.
- 'And tomorrow you're having lunch with Audiard.' French has a single verb that means 'to have lunch'. Audiard is a big film director, so Andréa grabs the chance.
- 'I'll take that. Thanks.' The present tense is enough to say what you're about to do.
- 'You're also having lunch with Dany Boon tomorrow.' The trouble is she already has a lunch with a famous comedian tomorrow.
- 'You're a pain, Magali.' Said with a sigh, to the one person keeping her life in order.
- 'One at noon, the other at two.' French offices often use the twenty-four-hour clock, so two in the afternoon is fourteen hours.
- 'I'm going to have lunch twice.' Here's a handy pattern: the verb 'to go' plus a plain verb gives you the near future, exactly like 'I'm going to' in English.
- 'Call Clémentine.' Now a string of orders in the casual form. Clémentine is a woman Andréa is seeing, and she wants to cancel tonight's date.
- 'Tell her I can't come tonight.' This command comes first, joined to the little word for 'to her'.
- 'Clémentine? The one from last week?' Magali can't keep up with Andréa's love life.
- 'Is there another Clémentine?' Andréa teases her right back.
- 'What do I tell her?' Magali starts with a useful question phrase that simply means 'what'.
- 'I don't know. Think of something.' Another casual command: think up, come up with something.
- 'Say I've got a meeting. Improvise!' That last word is the same as in English, and it's Andréa's motto. Meanwhile, across the room, Mathias stops at Gabriel's desk.
- 'Gabriel. Aren't you calling Cécile de France back?' Here, Cécile has been calling, and Gabriel hasn't picked up. He mumbles that he'll call her. Only we know why he's hiding. Let's hear the morning rush once more, all the way through.
