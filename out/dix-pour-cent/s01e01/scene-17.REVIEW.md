## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Cécile, great! How are you?' Those two little words at the end are the everyday way to ask how someone is. Cécile plays along.
- 'Fine, and you?' She uses the casual 'you' with Gabriel; they've worked together for years.
- 'I tried to call you.' The past action is made with 'have' and 'tried'. A short linking word then connects it to the action 'call', with 'you' attached just before that action.
- 'It's non-stop at work today.' The French borrow the English 'non-stop', and use their casual word for 'work'.
- 'Still no news?' The middle word means 'no' or 'not a single', before a noun.
- 'Oh, yes! I had Miranda Jones on the phone.' That first little word is a special 'yes', used to contradict a negative question. Miranda Jones is the American producer.
- 'Tell me everything.' In this positive instruction, the object meaning 'me' attaches after 'tell' with a hyphen. The last word means everything here.
- 'I never lie to you, Cécile.' The negative wraps around the verb again, but this time the second word means 'never'. The audience knows it's a lie. Then he invents a long story about the Wild West, where women died young.
- 'They thought it would be better with a man.' Gabriel reports a past decision using 'have' and 'thought'. Inside the reported idea, 'would be' presents the proposed change as hypothetical.
- 'A man?' She repeats it, staring at him.
- 'I know, it's crazy!' He pretends to be as shocked as she is.
- 'It has nothing to do with me?' Literally 'that has nothing to see with me'. She's giving him one last chance.
- 'Not at all!' Gabriel uses a strong, useful way to say no.
- 'She said you were fantastic.' The second verb is in the past form French uses for descriptions, 'you were'.
- 'I know you're disappointed, but we'll bounce back.' The description 'disappointed' has a feminine spelling for Cecile. After 'but', a present form of 'go' introduces their supposed next step.
- 'Do you know how much I wanted this part?' The question word measures the strength of her wish. The past form of 'want' presents a feeling that continued over time.
- 'What are you doing?' The question asks about an action in progress. The same French present form can also describe a regular habit in a different context.
- 'I'm changing agents.' The verb is in the present: Cecile announces her decision now. French says 'change of agent', without an article before the job.
- 'You're a liar!' The noun ends in the masculine form because Cecile is addressing Gabriel. Its feminine counterpart has a different ending.
- 'It's because of my age, isn't it?' The expression meaning 'because of' is followed by a noun. The small 'no' at the end presses Gabriel to acknowledge what she knows.
- 'Why give you ten percent if you don't stand up for me?' After 'why', the action 'give' is in its plain form. The percentage is an agent's commission, and gives the series its title.
