## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Miranda, you look radiant.' French literally says 'you are radiant'. Listen for the final consonant in the feminine description; the masculine version ends sooner.
- 'Paris suits you wonderfully.' Mathias uses a set phrase: the city 'goes to you' marvellously. Flattery, in the smoothest possible French.
- 'Thank you, Mathias. I love Paris!' The action word here is 'to adore', and the French use it for things they like a lot, cities included.
- 'I lived here when I was a student.' The word for 'student' takes the feminine ending too, because Miranda is talking about herself. She jokes that she won't say how long ago that was.
- 'We understand Tarantino's hesitation.' Mathias uses the word for 'we' to speak for the agency. The ending of 'understand' matches that subject.
- 'Yes, Quentin has a lot of talent.' A short word meaning 'of' links 'a lot' to 'talent'. The French noun can stay singular when talking about someone's ability.
- 'It's a real shame about Cecile.' The short expression beginning 'it's' introduces disappointment. Here the word for 'for' naturally becomes English 'about'.
- 'But he wants someone under thirty.' Literally 'someone of less than thirty years'. So now we know: it really is about age.
- 'Yes, Cecile is a little older.' French builds 'older' by putting 'more' before the description of age. The extra written letter for Cecile's gender is silent here.
- 'A little? She's forty-one.' Listen to how age works: in French you don't 'are' an age, you 'have' it. She has forty-one years.
- 'Forty. She's just turned forty.' A present form of 'come', a short word meaning 'from', and another action together mean 'has just'. The action 'have' belongs to the French way of saying someone's age.
- 'She'll be forty-one for the film.' French again uses 'have' for age, this time with an ending pointing to the future. Miranda is thinking about Cecile's age during filming.
- 'Yes, but she looks thirty.' French uses the action 'do' before an age to say how old someone looks. It describes her appearance, rather than her actual age.
- 'I'm sorry, Mathias, it's decided.' The word for 'sorry' is spelled with the feminine ending, because a woman says it, but it sounds exactly the same as a man's. The door is closed.
- 'Try a macaron! It's their speciality.' Mathias changes the subject with a polite invitation. The possessive before 'speciality' refers to the tea room and has a singular spelling for one speciality.
- 'Thank you.' Miranda gives a short polite response to his offer. Her decision about the film part still stands.
