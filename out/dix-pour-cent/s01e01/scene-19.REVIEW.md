## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Isn't Hervé here?' The verb sits between two small words that together make it negative. That two-part negative is the grammar of this whole scene, so keep an ear out for it. With Hervé gone, Camille turns to Noémie.
- 'Noémie, I have a problem.' Simple and direct: I have, then the thing. She tries to sound casual.
- 'I think you can help me.' Notice that Camille uses the polite, formal 'you' with Noémie. She is new, and she wants a favour, so she is careful.
- 'I don't think so.' Short and cold, the same two-part negative around the verb. Noémie is not in a helpful mood, because earlier she saw Camille arguing with Mathias, and she is quietly in love with him.
- 'Please.' Literally 'if it pleases you', again with the formal 'you'. Then Camille tries to clear the air.
- 'There's nothing between Mathias and me, okay?' Here the negative pairs with the word for 'nothing' instead of the usual 'not'. Camille thinks Noémie is jealous. Of course she can't say that Mathias is really her father.
- 'Excuse me? That doesn't worry me at all.' The words at the end mean 'at all', and they make the denial stronger. She says it a little too fast to be believed.
- 'So, what's your problem?' Very informal: Noémie uses the casual 'you' with the newcomer, while Camille keeps the polite one. That uneven pairing tells you who feels senior here.
- 'I don't know anything about contracts.' Negative plus 'nothing' again. Camille plays the innocent beginner, which is partly true.
- 'Can I look at one of Mathias's contracts?' To say one thing belongs to someone, French puts the owner after a small word meaning 'of': a contract of Mathias. Then she slips in the one she really wants.
- 'Canet's, for example?' The first word means 'the one', so it's 'the one of Canet'. That's the contract Andréa asked about, and Noémie sees straight through it.
- 'No, no, no! That's impossible.' The triple no says everything.
- 'He doesn't let anyone see his contracts.' This time the negative pairs with the word for 'nobody'. So you have now heard 'not', 'nothing' and 'nobody', all built the same way. Just then, Gabriel bursts in.
- 'Where's the traitor? Where's the mole?' A mole is the spy inside a company, in French just as in English. Notice the first noun is masculine and the second feminine, although both mean a person.
- 'What's going on?' A fixed phrase you'll hear constantly, worth learning as one block.
- 'Someone told Cécile she was too old for the part.' The verb for 'told' is in the past, and 'she was' uses the past tense for describing. Camille says nothing. She is the someone, though Gabriel doesn't know it.
- 'She's gone! She's left me.' Two past forms: the first is built with the verb 'to be', because it's about leaving a place, and the second with 'to have'. Gabriel is heartbroken, and paranoid too: he grabs a colleague and accuses them of working for a rival agency.
- 'Yes, Mathias? Of course.' Noémie's phone rings and her voice turns soft. Her boss is calling.
- 'He wants the number of Miranda Jones, Tarantino's producer.' Again the owner comes after the word for 'of'. Mathias wants to meet the woman who turned Cécile down, and Noémie gets to work at once.
