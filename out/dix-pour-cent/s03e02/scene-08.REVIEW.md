## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Here we are, we have arrived in front of your place.' Gabriel signals the end of the walk home. The particle 'voila' marks arrival at a destination.
- 'Thank you for this very lovely evening.' Chantal expresses polite appreciation. The feminine noun phrase 'cette tres belle soiree' praises the shared time.
- 'Well, it is late now.' Gabriel notices the late hour. The impersonal expression 'il est tard' states the late time of night.
- 'Time passes very quickly with you.' Gabriel pays a gentle compliment. The adverbial phrase 'tres vite' emphasizes how fast time flew by.
- 'Yes, I had an excellent time.' Chantal confirms her enjoyment. The compound past 'ai passe' describes a completed positive experience.
- 'I must get going now.' Gabriel prepares to leave. The modal verb 'dois' followed by the adverbial pronoun 'y' expresses impending departure.
- 'Do you have a long day tomorrow?' Chantal inquires about his schedule. The adjective 'longue' qualifies the upcoming workday.
- 'Yes, I have a lot of work at the agency.' Gabriel explains his professional obligations. The noun 'agence' refers to ASK.
- 'Then you must rest well.' Chantal offers considerate advice. The reflexive structure 'te reposer' expresses getting rest.
- 'You too, rest well.' Gabriel returns the affectionate wish. The imperative 'repose-toi' directly bids someone to rest.
- 'Good night, Chantal.' Gabriel offers a formal bedtime farewell. The standard polite formula 'bonne nuit' bids someone good night.
- 'Good night, Gabriel.' Chantal replies in kind. The reciprocal greeting concludes their conversation outside her door.
- 'Shall we call each other soon?' Gabriel suggests a future contact. The pronominal verb 's'appeler' expresses mutual phoning.
- 'Yes, with great pleasure.' Chantal accepts with enthusiasm. The polite idiom 'avec grand plaisir' confirms keen agreement.
- 'I will call you tomorrow afternoon.' Gabriel sets a specific time frame. The time expression 'demain apres-midi' denotes the next day.
- 'Okay, see you tomorrow, Gabriel.' Chantal brings the parting to a close. The parting phrase 'a demain' anticipates their next conversation.
