## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Noemie, would you be free tonight?' Herve asks casually at the office. The conditional 'serais libre' frames a polite invitation.
- 'I have a ticket for a sold-out theater show.' Herve pitches an exclusive cultural outing. The adjective phrase 'complet' denotes a sold-out venue.
- 'You could be my talent scout.' Herve proposes that Noemie scout new actors. The idiom 'tete chercheuse' designates a talent scout.
- 'No, sorry, tonight I cannot make it at all.' Noemie turns down the offer. The emphatic negative 'pas du tout' stresses absolute unavailability.
- 'You are not seeing Philippe again, at least?' Herve suspects an illicit rendezvous. The interrogative 'au moins' seeks reassurance against backsliding.
- 'Of course not, not at all!' Noemie quickly defends herself. The phrase 'mais non' expresses indignant denial.
- 'You are not falling back into it, are you?' Herve warns against repeating past romantic mistakes. The verb 'replonger' metaphorically describes a relapse.
- 'Friends serve to remind us of truths.' Herve defines genuine friendship. The verb phrase 'servir a' expresses utility and duty.
- 'We shared very beautiful things with him.' Noemie fondly defends her past affair. The compound past 'avons vecu' recalls shared moments.
- 'Together, we really freed ourselves.' Noemie describes an exhilarating emotional escape. The reflexive verb 'se liberer' conveys personal liberation.
- 'We became different from normal life.' Noemie contrasts romance with daily routine. The imperfect 'devenait' depicts a transformative mental state.
- 'It was really incredible between us.' Noemie highlights the extraordinary passion. The adjective 'incroyable' underscores intense romance.
- 'I had the impression of being a magician.' Noemie remembers feeling uniquely powerful. The noun phrase 'une magicienne' evokes enchanting romantic influence.
- 'Noemie, this man is toxic for you.' Herve provides sobering objective advice. The adjective 'toxique' describes harmful emotional dynamics.
- 'It is hard to forget memories like that.' Noemie acknowledges lingering attachment. The infinitive 'oublier' is governed by the adjective 'difficile'.
- 'Come to the theater with me to think about something else.' Herve suggests a healthy distraction. The imperative 'viens' encourages her to join him.
