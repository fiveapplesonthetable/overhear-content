## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'So, what do you think of these photos?' Monica asks for Gabriel's honest professional feedback. The interrogative phrase opens the review.
- 'Advertising is not cinema.' Gabriel distinguishes commercial promo aesthetics from films. The negative copula defines separate media.
- 'These are very different codes.' He notes that advertising operates by artificial standards. The plural demonstrative clause describes marketing conventions.
- 'You know very well what pleases you.' Monica challenges him to judge purely as an observer. The relative clause addresses his personal perception.
- 'Here, this is not exciting at all.' She expresses dissatisfaction with the clinical studio shots. The negative adverbial phrase stresses complete indifference.
- 'Well, it is still a little bit exciting!' Gabriel gently disagrees with her harsh self-critique. The contradictory affirmative particle qualifies her statement.
- 'Honestly, who is this woman?' Monica feels alienated by the synthetic marketing image. The rhetorical question highlights identity disconnection.
- 'This image is so heavily retouched!' She decries the aggressive digital post-production. The intensifying adverb emphasizes synthetic alteration.
- 'I no longer have any natural expression.' Monica laments the erasure of authentic human facial traits. The negative adverb denotes complete loss.
- 'Look, I do not even have elbows anymore!' She points out ridiculous anatomical airbrushing. The imperative verb draws attention to the flaw.
- 'People rarely look at your elbows.' Gabriel offers humor to lighten her frustration. The impersonal construction brings amusing perspective.
- 'Personally, I really like how you look at me.' Monica pivots to an intimate personal compliment. The emphatic pronoun phrase signals emotional warmth.
- 'Your gaze is always kind and gentle.' She appreciates his sincere, non-predatory attention. The coordinated adjectives describe comforting affection.
- 'With you, I feel confident and safe.' Monica expresses profound trust in her dedicated agent. The prepositional phrase reinforces emotional security.
- 'I am extremely flattered, but it is taboo.' Gabriel acknowledges her affection while upholding professional ethics. The contrasting conjunction sets a firm moral limit.
