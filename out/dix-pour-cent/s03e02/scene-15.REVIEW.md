## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I was like a curled-up hedgehog!' Gabriel uses a self-deprecating animal metaphor. The preposition comme introduces the vivid comparison.
- 'You always exaggerate your emotions, Gabriel.' Andrea gently teases his dramatic nature. The adverb toujours modifies the verb exagères.
- 'She is so beautiful and sensual.' Gabriel recalls Monica's mesmerizing presence. The adverb tellement intensifies the adjectives.
- 'She is a magnificent and kind woman.' Andrea reinforces her admiration. The feminine adjective magnifique describes high elegance.
- 'I ruined everything with my fears!' Gabriel laments his missed chance. The compound past ai gâché comes from gâcher.
- 'You panic over a simple meeting.' Andrea offers a grounded perspective. The verb paniquer means to panic.
- 'I could have been her ideal man!' Gabriel mourns the lost possibility. The past conditional aurais pu expresses an unfulfilled potential.
- 'Where are you going to sleep tonight then?' Andrea inquires about his immediate shelter. The adverb alors concludes the inquiry.
- 'I cannot go home alone.' Gabriel reveals his vulnerability. The adjective seul expresses solitude.
- 'Do you want to come to my place tonight?' Andrea offers her hospitality. The preposition chez indicates someone's home.
- 'Prepare your sofa for me, Andrea!' Gabriel eagerly accepts the invitation. The imperative prépare addresses a close friend.
- 'My sofa is ready for you.' Andrea confirms her warm welcome. The adjective prêt agrees with the masculine noun canapé.
- 'Thank you, my taxi is arriving there soon.' Gabriel watches the street approach. The adverb bientôt indicates near future arrival.
- 'Rest well during the trip, Gabriel.' Andrea closes the late-night call kindly. The reflexive imperative repose-toi urges relaxation.
