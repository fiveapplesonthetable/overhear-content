## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I would like this girl to know her history.' Hicham explains his paternal perspective. The conditional verb expresses personal yearning.
- 'Yes, of course, we agree.' Andrea politely acknowledges biological reality. The idiomatic phrase indicates shared baseline understanding.
- 'The story is that I am her father.' Hicham firmly asserts his biological parenthood. The copular clause identifies his central role.
- 'That is a small part of the story.' Andrea downplays his role in daily parenting. The noun phrase qualifies the scope of his involvement.
- 'Without me, there is no story.' Hicham claims ultimate credit for the conception. The prepositional phrase stresses his indispensability.
- 'So, I would like to officially recognize her.' He declares his intent to claim paternity. The adverb highlights formal civil registration.
- 'That is not what we had decided!' Andrea reacts with shock at his broken promise. The relative clause recalls their original verbal pact.
- 'Maybe, but it is my legal right.' Hicham stands firm on statutory paternity entitlements. The possessive noun phrase emphasizes legal standing.
- 'If you officially become the baby's father,' Colette begins her emotional counterargument. The conditional clause sets up the legal consequence.
- 'I will never really be her mother.' Colette fears losing all maternal recognition. The negative future verb underscores total exclusion.
- 'Full adoption will no longer be possible.' She points out the strict legal obstacle. The legal terminology defines irrevocable parentage.
- 'You can still do a simple adoption.' Hicham suggests a lesser legal compromise. The modal future verb offers an alternative procedure.
- 'But that would be revocable with your consent!' Colette rejects second-rate maternal status. The conditional clause exposes precarious dependency.
- 'Without Colette, I would never have kept the child.' Andrea defends her partner unconditionally. The past conditional construction expresses profound loyalty.
- 'Because I cannot have children myself.' Colette reveals her painful biological infertility. The negative modal verb silences the table in grief.
