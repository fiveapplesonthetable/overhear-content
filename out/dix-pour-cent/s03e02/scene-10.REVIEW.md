## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello, is Gabriel there?' Monica calls the talent agency looking for her agent. The question 'est la' inquires about his presence.
- 'No, I am his boss, Hicham Janowski.' Hicham introduces himself with executive authority. The noun 'patron' denotes the head of the company.
- 'Hello Hicham. I would like to speak to Gabriel.' Monica maintains polite but focused intent. The conditional 'voudrais parler' expresses a courteous wish.
- 'Hello, Monica! It is me, it is Gabriel.' Gabriel snatches the telephone receiver. The emphatic phrase 'c'est moi' identifies the speaker warmly.
- 'Hello Gabriel, how are you doing?' Monica greets her agent warmly. The standard inquiry 'comment ca va' opens their exchange.
- 'Very well! I thought about the advertising contracts.' Gabriel shares his positive reflection. The past participle 'reflechi' denotes considered thought.
- 'It could really interest us.' Gabriel confirms the commercial opportunity. The conditional verb 'pourrait' frames a prospective business deal.
- 'Great, I am very delighted about it.' Monica welcomes his agreement. The feminine adjective 'ravie' expresses pleasure.
- 'Do you want to come by the office to discuss it?' Gabriel proposes a standard agency meeting. The infinitive 'passer' means to drop by.
- 'Couldn't you come by my place instead?' Monica shifts the venue to her private residence. The adverb 'plutot' suggests an intimate alternative.
- 'I would like to show you photos for an ad.' Monica provides a professional pretext. The noun 'pub' is common French shorthand for an advertisement.
- 'Yes, that is an excellent idea.' Gabriel enthusiastically agrees. The adjective 'excellente' modifies the feminine noun 'idee'.
- 'Tonight, can you come to my place?' Monica proposes dinner that very evening. The modal verb 'peux venir' asks about availability.
- 'Yes okay, that is perfect for me.' Gabriel confirms the evening plan without hesitation. The adjective 'parfait' conveys complete satisfaction.
- 'I will make you my secret risotto recipe.' Monica entices Gabriel with homemade cooking. The future tense 'ferai' promises a culinary treat.
- 'See you at your place at eight thirty!' Gabriel confirms the dinner appointment. The noun phrase 'rendez-vous' sets the meeting time.
