## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Yes Catherine, what is this appointment?' Mathias asks his wife about their planned meeting. Inverting the verb and pronoun around 'appointment' creates a crisp, polished question.
- 'I am waiting for you on the street corner.' Catherine tells him where to find her. Notice how 'am waiting for' leads directly into the noun meaning 'corner'.
- 'All right, I arrive in five minutes.' Mathias promises to meet her shortly. Notice how the short vowel drops before a vowel sound around 'arrive', making the phrase flow smoothly as one unit.
- 'Watch out, this car is driving too fast!' Catherine panics as a speeding vehicle approaches. Dropping the subject pronoun turns the verb for 'drives' into a direct, punchy command.
- 'Oh my God, I fell down!' Catherine cries out after slipping onto the sidewalk. The verb meaning 'fallen' pairs naturally with the noun for 'God' in everyday conversation.
- 'Catherine, answer me, what is happening?' Mathias grows terrified hearing distress over the phone. The clause hinges on the everyday verb meaning 'answer me'.
- 'Help me please, I am hurt!' Catherine pleads for assistance from passersby. Notice how the short vowel drops before a vowel sound around 'help me', making the phrase flow smoothly as one unit.
- 'Stay calm, above all do not move!' Mathias instructs her to remain still after the fall. The descriptive adjective for 'calm' agrees in gender and number with its noun.
- 'Quickly call the fire department immediately!' Mathias urges bystanders to dial emergency services. Notice how 'call' leads directly into the noun meaning 'firefighters'.
- 'My leg hurts me very much.' Catherine complains of severe leg pain. The short 'self' word sits right in front of the verb for 'makes', turning the action back onto the subject.
- 'I run to join you, hold on!' Mathias dashes frantically across town toward her. The clause hinges on the everyday verb meaning 'run'.
- 'In how much time do they arrive?' Catherine asks how long the ambulance will take. Notice how 'arrive' leads directly into the noun meaning 'time'.
- 'The rescue arrives in a few minutes.' Mathias reassures her that help is on the way. The verb meaning 'arrive' pairs naturally with the noun for 'emergency services' in everyday conversation.
- 'I am here with you, Catherine.' Mathias comforts his injured wife by her side. The informal 'you' form around 'this line' marks a direct, personal relationship.
