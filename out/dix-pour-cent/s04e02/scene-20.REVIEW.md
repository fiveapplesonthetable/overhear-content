## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'The day was particularly exhausting.' Andrea pours herself a drink at her desk. The compound past emphasizes the long day.
- 'Will you pour me a glass too?' Elise asks from the office doorway. The adverbial pronoun en refers back to the drink.
- 'Elise? What are you still doing here?' Andrea looks up in utter surprise. The inverted question marks her shock.
- 'The entrance door had remained open.' Elise explains her quiet arrival. The pluperfect passive tense justifies her sudden presence.
- 'I was not expecting you.' Andrea sets down her bottle with caution. The reflexive imperfect conveys her complete surprise.
- 'I need to drink a glass.' Elise steps into the dim room. The common phrase avoir besoin de expresses urgent need.
- 'Sit down, here is a glass for you.' Andrea offers hospitality despite their rivalry. The imperative sets a cooperative tone.
- 'Thank you Andrea, I really appreciate this gesture.' Elise takes the tumbler. The polite verb acknowledges the small kindness.
- 'You look really very troubled.' Andrea studies the rival agent closely. The idiom avoir l'air describes her tense appearance.
- 'I had an absolutely terrible evening.' Elise takes a deep sip of alcohol. The strong descriptive adjective signals bad news.
- 'A serious problem with your clients?' Andrea tries to guess the crisis. The noun phrase investigates potential agency drama.
- 'No, it is much worse than that.' Elise shakes her head grimly. The comparative adverb highlights the gravity of the shock.
- 'So tell me what is going on.' Andrea leans across the desk attentively. The imperative verb invites an honest explanation.
- 'Prepare yourself for quite a surprise.' Elise leans forward with a weary smile. The expressive adjective foreshadows big news.
