## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Mathias, wait a second please.' Andrea stops Mathias before he reaches the lift. The imperative 'attends' urges someone to pause briefly.
- 'Yes Andrea? What is the matter?' Mathias wonders what further request remains. The phrase 'qu'est-ce qu'il y a' inquires about an unexpected issue.
- 'You must give back your agency keys.' Andrea requests the surrender of his keys. The verb 'rendre' means to return or give back an item.
- 'Are you treating me like a thief now?' Mathias feels insulted by the demand. The noun 'voleur' denotes a thief or burglar.
- 'Noemie already gave back hers this morning.' Andrea mentions Noemie's compliance. The verb meaning 'has' pairs naturally with the noun for 'morning' in everyday conversation.
- 'I ran this agency for thirty years!' Mathias recalls his long-standing authority. The passe compose 'ai dirige' marks his executive career.
- 'We simply want to protect our new contracts.' Andrea defends client confidentiality. The verb 'proteger' expresses preservation and defense.
- 'Are you afraid of my future competition?' Mathias suspects fear of his new firm. The idiom 'avoir peur de' expresses fear or apprehension.
- 'We want to avoid changing the locks.' Andrea provides a pragmatic financial reason. The noun 'serrure' refers to a door lock.
- 'Take these keys, I do not want them anymore.' Mathias gives in with irritation. The pronoun 'en' replaces the prepositional phrase de ces cles.
- 'Thank you Mathias, good luck with your projects.' Andrea offers civil farewell wishes. The phrase 'bonne chance' wishes someone good fortune.
- 'Goodbye. Seriously, I cannot believe it.' Mathias shakes his head in disbelief. The colloquial expression 'ne pas en revenir' means to be unable to believe something.
- 'A new story begins for all of us.' Andrea looks forward to leading the agency. The verb 'commencer' signifies the start of a new chapter.
- 'Good luck with your new daily problems!' Mathias delivers a sardonic parting remark. The adjective 'quotidien' describes routine or daily occurrences.
