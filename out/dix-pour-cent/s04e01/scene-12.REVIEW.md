## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Ah, I am sorry, Colette!' Andrea expresses her immediate remorse. The feminine past participle 'desolee' agrees with the speaker.
- 'You scared me in the hallway.' Colette explains her sudden shock. The idiom 'faire peur a quelqu'un' means to frighten someone.
- 'I had not seen you here.' Andrea explains why she made a noise. The plus-que-parfait 'avais pas vue' describes an earlier state.
- 'Flora woke up with a start.' Colette points to the startled baby. The reflexive verb 'se reveiller' indicates waking up.
- 'Forgive me, I return too late.' Andrea acknowledges her late arrival. The adverbial locution 'trop tard' signals an excessive delay.
- 'She is crying for a whole hour.' Colette describes the prolonged distress. The preposition 'depuis' indicates an ongoing action started in the past.
- 'Give me the baby a moment.' Andrea offers to take the child. The imperative 'donne-moi' makes a direct caring request.
- 'Come here, my darling little girl.' Colette speaks tenderly to her daughter. The affectionate term 'cherie' expresses motherly love.
- 'I can rock her gently.' Andrea offers a soothing gesture. The verb 'bercer' means to rock or cradle an infant.
- 'There, there, mommy is here.' Colette reassures baby Flora softly. The repeated interjection 'la, la' is common when comforting children.
- 'She begins to calm down at last.' Andrea notices the baby relaxing. The pronominal verb 'se calmer' means to quiet down.
- 'This day was very difficult.' Colette sighs under the weight of fatigue. The adjective 'difficile' describes an arduous daily experience.
- 'Rest, I take over now.' Andrea encourages Colette to take a break. The idiom 'prendre le relais' means to take over duties.
- 'Thank you Andrea, I really needed it.' Colette accepts the welcome relief. The expression 'avoir grand besoin' highlights an urgent necessity.
