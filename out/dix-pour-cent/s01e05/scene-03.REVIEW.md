## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello? Thank you, Mum.' French uses a special greeting for answering the telephone. The word for Mum is the familiar family form, rather than a formal title.
- 'Stop it! I have work here.' The first word addresses someone close to her. Camille is resisting her mother's suggestion that she should return home.
- 'I'll try to come down for a weekend soon.' The first action points to the future. Travelling down suits a journey south from Paris, rather than a literal descent down stairs.
- 'Mum, that's enough!' The short expression says that the situation has reached its limit. It is a useful way to ask someone to stop a topic.
- 'I have a scene with JoeyStarr, I really have to focus.' The word meaning have to comes before the action she needs to perform. The little reflexive word makes focusing something she does herself.
- 'Thank you, Gran. That's really a lovely thought.' A kind gesture can be called an attention in French. Here it means consideration, rather than concentrating on something.
- 'Camille, do you have a moment?' Asking whether someone has a moment is an ordinary way to interrupt politely. He is asking for her time, not for a physical thing.
- 'I'll call you back, Gran, okay? Big kisses!' The action is written in the present, but it promises a call shortly afterwards. The affectionate ending fits the family conversation.
- 'What is this?' The question word can come after the identifying expression in casual speech. Camille wants to know what the envelope contains.
- 'The ministry report on cinema funding. For Andrea.' The words connecting report and ministry combine of with the article the. They do not mean some in this phrase.
- 'What is the matter?' The question asks what there is, in an everyday expression for noticing a problem. Mathias has seen her disappointment.
- 'No, nothing. It's for Andrea.' The short answer closes the conversation. Camille repeats the recipient's name instead of explaining why she is hurt.
