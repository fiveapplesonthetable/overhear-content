## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I'm glad to be going back to Paris.' The description pleased is masculine for Gabriel. The basic action return follows it to explain what he is pleased about, and to introduces the city he is returning to.
- 'Do you have any news of Brancillon?' News is plural in this question, unlike a single piece of news. The surname identifies whose news Gabriel is asking for, rather than naming a new person in the car.
- 'Her name is Colette. Her ribs hurt.' The reflexive action is called gives Colette's first name. Her pain uses have with bad, while the plural ribs identifies the injured part of her body.
- 'She must hate me!' Must here expresses what he assumes, not an order she has to obey. The little word for me stands before hate, naming the person he thinks she dislikes.
- 'She doesn't understand why you eat so much with your clients.' The question word why introduces the behaviour Colette cannot understand. So much refers to the amount of eating with clients, not to the number of clients alone.
- 'You can explain to her, can't you?' Can is followed by the basic action explain. The small word meaning to her comes before it, identifying Colette as the person who needs his explanation.
- 'Actors are fragile and sensitive.' The two descriptions are plural because they refer to the actors. Sensitive describes their feelings, rather than a French word meaning sensible or reasonable.
- 'When things go badly, they eat. And I eat with them!' When introduces what happens whenever the actors feel bad. With them uses a strong plural pronoun after the connecting word with.
- 'When I saw Colette in the street, she was coming out of your place!' The completed action saw marks one occasion in the past. Was coming out describes what was happening then, and your place uses the familiar expression for someone's home.
- 'Are you really a couple?' The word for you is plural here for Andrea and Colette together. It does not switch Gabriel into polite speech with Andrea; he is asking about the two of them as a couple.
- 'I think so, yes.' The action believe or think can stand alone as a cautious answer. Yes confirms that she thinks the relationship has begun, without claiming certainty about its future.
- 'And how is it going?' The question asks how it is going. French uses a reflexive action for how events are unfolding, rather than a word about physically walking somewhere.
- 'It's going really well.' Really strengthens well. The same reflexive expression answers how the relationship is going, not how either woman is travelling.
- 'It's strange, I know, but there it is.' The first description acknowledges that the situation feels strange. The final short expression closes her explanation with the sense that this is simply how things stand.
- 'Don't say anything to anyone, Gabriel.' The familiar negative instruction addresses Gabriel alone. Nothing and nobody work together in the French warning, where English normally says anything to anyone.
- 'That would be bad for all of us, okay?' Would be makes the possible consequences hypothetical. The strong word for us is followed by all, including the whole agency rather than just the two people in the car.
