## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I have a scene with JoeyStarr!' The word for with connects her small role to the actor she will work alongside. That connection is what makes her so excited.
- 'I have two lines, but they're quite long.' The French word for an actor's lines is feminine. The words meaning they and long follow that choice in this sentence.
- 'Julie is waiting. We're already late.' The word meaning already makes the delay more urgent. It says the problem exists now, not merely that it might happen later.
- 'She can wait.' The short word meaning can comes before the action wait. JoeyStarr uses the possibility as a reason to ignore the assistant.
- 'Hi! What are you doing?' The question word comes last in this familiar spoken question. He keeps the same easy word order as an ordinary statement.
- 'Hi! I'm playing a servant, like you.' French has a feminine word for the servant Sofia plays. The final comparison connects her character with his.
- 'Don't I have a scene with you?' The question is negative, even though he suspects the answer is yes. Listen to the particular affirmative reply she chooses.
- 'Yes, that's right!' French has a special yes for contradicting a negative question. Sofia confirms that they do have a scene together.
- 'Have you got stage fright?' The French expression builds stage fright with have. It names the nervousness that comes before a performance.
- 'No. Well, yes, a lot!' The hesitation word lets her revise her first answer. The last word strengthens the admission of nerves.
- 'Joey, Julie is getting angry. She's going to walk off the set!' A form of go introduces the next action. The assistant warns about what she is about to do.
- 'Tell me your lines.' In this instruction, the little word for me attaches after the action. In a normal statement it would usually stand before it.
- 'Really? Okay.' The first word checks that his offer is sincere. She has not expected the star to spend time on her rehearsal.
- 'Lower your eyes before the duchess!' The word for eyes has a different shape from the singular eye. The grand instruction belongs to her character's period dialogue.
- 'Beware! She could cost you your life!' The first expression literally says take care. The last part gives the person affected before the action about costing a life.
- 'That's very good! Come on, we're on our way.' The final action is in the present, but here it announces that they are coming immediately. At last the assistant gets an answer.
