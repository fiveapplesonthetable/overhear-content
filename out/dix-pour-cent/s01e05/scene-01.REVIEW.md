## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Madam.' The polite form of address used by a servant speaking to a noblewoman in the film.
- 'No! I don't believe it!' Julie breaks character and pulls away as JoeyStarr shoves her into the hedge. The little pronoun before the verb means 'in it'.
- 'Cut! What is going on?' Director Samir rushes over from his monitor. Literally 'what is it that passes itself?'.
- 'He pushes her into the bushes.' Julie complains that the script calls for a passionate embrace, not a rough shove.
- 'Are you made of glass?' Literally 'are you in glass?'. Notice how the two stars use the formal 'you' even while arguing.
- 'No, I am an aristocrat, he is a servant.' In French, professions and social roles after the verb 'to be' often drop the word for 'a'.
- 'He has to show respect.' Literally 'make proof of respect', a fixed expression for showing or demonstrating a quality.
- 'I'm working, okay?' JoeyStarr insists he is simply playing the passion of the scene.
- 'Could we have a little delicacy and elegance in this scene?' The conditional of 'can' softens her demand into a pointed question.
- 'Is that aimed at me? I'm fed up.' Literally 'is that for me?' followed by a very common casual idiom meaning 'I have had enough of it'.
- 'Me too! Go to your high-society dinners!' As they talk over each other, JoeyStarr mocks Julie's posh Parisian world.
- 'Articulate, please. I don't understand a word.' Julie fires back at his gravelly delivery before storming off to her trailer.
- 'How far behind schedule are we?' Literally 'we have how much of delay?'.
- 'Five days. I don't believe it!' Samir repeats the assistant director's answer in horror.
- 'Call their agents right now!' With both stars locked in their trailers, only ASK can save the shoot.
