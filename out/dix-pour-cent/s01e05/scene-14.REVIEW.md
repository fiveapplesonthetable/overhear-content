## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'With all these delays, we have half a day left in the barn.' The expression literally says there remains to us. The little word for us marks whose time is left, while half a day is the remaining amount.
- 'I had to rewrite the schedule.' The completed past of have to is followed by the basic action rewrite. The borrowed word for schedule refers to the filming timetable.
- 'We shoot the love scene tomorrow.' The action meaning turn can mean film when used with a scene. The present tense and tomorrow together announce a scheduled future event.
- 'Maybe it is not the right time.' The expression meaning maybe softens the objection. Good before moment means right or suitable, rather than morally good.
- 'It is tomorrow, we have no choice!' Having no choice uses the ordinary action have with a negative. It presents the timing as something they cannot change.
- 'We are going to manage, Samir.' The expression about arriving there can also mean managing to do something. The small word for there points back to the result they need to reach.
- 'If you say so. Tell the actors, I am too busy.' The first sentence cautiously accepts someone else's claim. In the next instruction, the word for it attaches to tell and to the actors identifies the recipients.
- 'Why are there always love scenes?' The familiar expression for there are stays singular in French even before plural scenes. Why asks for the reason these scenes keep appearing.
- 'It would be so much stronger to suggest it symbolically.' Would be presents a possible alternative. More strengthens the comparison with the planned scene, and the little word for it refers to the intimacy she wants implied.
- 'Meaning what?' The whole expression literally means that is to say. Said as a question, it asks the speaker to explain what she means.
- 'I don't know.' The negative wraps around know. This present-tense expression admits she has no ready answer, rather than saying she forgot one.
- 'A flowing river? Animals frolicking in the woods?' The first description uses a singular action for the river, then a plural one for the animals. The last word means woods, rather than the material used to make furniture.
- 'Yes! The audience would prefer to see rabbits in the woods.' Would prefer presents a hypothetical audience preference. Seeing remains in its basic form after prefer, with rabbits as what they would see.
- 'Rather than you and JoeyStarr together on screen!' Rather than introduces the alternative being rejected in the comparison. On screen refers to their appearance in the film, rather than their physical location now.
