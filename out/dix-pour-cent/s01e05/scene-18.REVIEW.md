## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'We're together all day, every day.' All follows two different French words for day. The first is feminine and singular for the whole day; the second is masculine and plural for every day.
- 'But we don't really enjoy each other's company.' The final expression pairs one person with the other. Enjoying each other's company is something shared, rather than each person enjoying the job alone.
- 'We shouldn't let the job define us.' The form meaning should softens an obligation into advice. The little word for us stands before define, naming the people whose lives are being shaped.
- 'Arlette, for example. What do we know about her? Or about Mathias?' The word for about introduces whose life he means. The short final question leaves out the repeated words about what they know, because the comparison is clear.
- 'That's what the job is, Gabriel.' French can name something after first saying that is it. Putting work at the end makes it sound like a blunt explanation of the whole situation.
- 'And us. Look at us. What do I know about you?' In the instruction look at us, the word for us attaches after the action. In the following question, the strong word for you comes after the connecting word of.
- 'We coddle our actors too much while forgetting to live for ourselves.' The action about coddling can also describe a bird brooding over eggs. The expression while forgetting links their care for the actors to what they neglect in their own lives.
- 'Yes, you're right. I'm exhausted.' Being right literally uses have with reason. The next description is a familiar way to say she is completely worn out, with the feminine ending written for Andrea.
- 'I think I'm going to go to bed. Good night.' The plan to go to bed uses go before the next action. Putting oneself to bed is reflexive in French, even though English usually just says go to bed.
- 'No, it isn't that.' The short reply rejects an explanation already offered. It does not yet explain what the real reason for her sadness is.
- 'Someone important to me forgot my birthday.' Someone important is connected with the little word often meaning of. The completed action forgot identifies the event that has hurt her.
- 'No, it's past midnight.' French uses an impersonal expression about the time having passed. Midnight is the point after which she considers the birthday over.
- 'He forgot. It's too late now.' The first short sentence finally names him as the person who forgot. Too strengthens late, saying that an ordinary delay has become unacceptable.
