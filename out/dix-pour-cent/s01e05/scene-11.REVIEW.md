## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello?' This is the special greeting used when answering a telephone. It does not tell us yet whether the call is genuine.
- 'Yes, yes.' Repeating yes makes his answer sound like an ongoing conversation with the caller. It helps the pretence before he gives a reason to leave.
- 'Okay, I'm on my way.' The present action literally says I arrive. French uses it when promising to come immediately, even before reaching a destination.
- 'Yes, right away.' The final three words mean immediately. Together they work as a fixed expression about time, rather than a sequence to translate word by word.
- 'A mate in trouble.' Mate is a familiar word for a friend. The expression for trouble describes being in a difficult situation; he uses it as an excuse.
- 'I have to go.' The action meaning must or have to comes before go. The small word for there refers to the place he claims he needs to reach.
- 'Yes, of course.' The final two words mean of course. Julie appears to accept the excuse before asking a more awkward question.
- 'But can I get my phone back before you leave?' Recover means get back because it is her own phone. Your departure is a noun phrase after before, where English can instead use before you leave.
- 'Didier?' Gabriel calls the actor by his first name. A person's name alone can ask where they are or draw their attention.
- 'Julie!' Now the actor calls his co-star by name. The brief shout marks a sudden change from the attempted departure to alarm.
- 'Julie!' The repeated name makes the call more urgent. Short warnings often repeat the person addressed rather than explain the danger in a full sentence.
- 'What's going on?' The question asks what is happening. The expression uses a reflexive form for an event, rather than for physically passing another person.
