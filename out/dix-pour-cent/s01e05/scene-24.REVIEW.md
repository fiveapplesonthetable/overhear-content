## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Julie, you come in this way and sit down next to him.' The familiar form of you addresses Julie alone. The reflexive action sit down follows where she should come from, and beside him gives her final position.
- 'And then you play the scene, okay?' The word for you now addresses both actors together. Then places playing the scene after the movement he has just described.
- 'Are there insects in the hay?' The impersonal expression there are introduces the insects. In tells us where he fears finding them; hay is the material covering the floor.
- 'I'm allergic!' The description allergic follows be. It gives a reason for his worry, rather than saying that the hay itself has an allergy.
- 'Can we start? Silence, please!' Can is followed by the basic action start. The short request for silence addresses the group politely as the crew prepares to record.
- 'Why are you looking at me like that?' The question starts with why. The little word for me stands before look at, identifying the person who feels he is being stared at.
- 'If you're already angry, it's impossible!' If introduces a condition that could prevent the take. Already says that the anger has begun before they have even played the scene.
- 'Rolling! Action!' The film-set expression literally says we turn. In this setting it means the camera is rolling, followed by the director's signal to begin acting.
- 'Cut! No, that's not working.' The instruction cut stops the take. The next negative uses go to judge how the performance is working, rather than describing someone travelling.
- 'They were waiting for this moment for months!' The past form describes a period of waiting. The time expression for months gives how long the fictional characters have wanted this moment.
- 'We're too cold, is that it?' Too strengthens cold, here describing a performance that lacks warmth. The final question asks the director to confirm her understanding of the problem.
- 'Yes, a bit. Right, let's start again. Action!' A little qualifies his agreement. The basic meaning of begin again becomes let's start again because he includes the whole group in the next attempt.
- 'An insect! There was an insect!' There was uses the past version of the impersonal expression there is. Repeating insect first makes the alarm sound immediate before he explains it.
- 'Fire! Fire!' The short warning for fire uses a contracted expression with the word for fire. Repetition functions as an alarm rather than as two separate descriptions.
- 'It's okay, Julie. Come here!' The familiar instruction come addresses one person. Here adds the place he wants her to move to, as he reassures Julie and brings her closer.
- 'There! That's perfect!' The opening word can mean there or that's it. The description perfect approves the result, even though the actors reached it through an unexpected mishap.
