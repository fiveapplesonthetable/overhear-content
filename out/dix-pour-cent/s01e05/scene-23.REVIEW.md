## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Thank you, Loïc!' The short thanks is followed directly by a name. It acknowledges the person who has brought Andrea back, without adding a separate action meaning to thank.
- 'Your attention, please!' The possessive for your addresses the whole group. The polite expression please follows the request for their attention, as the assistant brings the set together.
- 'Everyone in place for this afternoon.' Everyone literally combines all with the world. The time expression this afternoon sets when they need to be ready, rather than naming the next day.
- 'Scene sixty is cut.' The description cut is feminine because scene is feminine. The number after scene identifies which scene, much as an English scene number does.
- 'Cut?' A single repeated description can work as a surprised question. Sofia is checking the word she has just heard, without repeating the entire announcement.
- 'We want the slaves behind the chateau, okay?' Behind places the background actors on the far side of the chateau. The plural noun slaves names the roles in this period film, not the actors' real status.
- 'Excuse me! My scene is cut?' The polite request to excuse her gets the assistant's attention. My scene then identifies why the change matters personally to Sofia.
- 'Yes, it isn't needed.' The feminine pronoun refers back to the scene. The negative surrounds is, rejecting its usefulness without changing the gender of the noun it describes.
- 'Why? You can't cut a scene like that!' The opening asks why before she objects. Can not is followed by the basic action cut, and like that criticises doing it in this abrupt way.
- 'We already have enough problems today.' Already tells us the problems exist before Sofia's complaint. Enough limits the amount of difficulty the assistant thinks they can manage today.
- 'Is it because of Joey?' The fixed expression because of introduces a possible cause. Here a person's name fills that place: she wonders whether the established actor is responsible.
- 'He didn't want to act with me?' The past form wanted describes an intention. Act with me follows it as a basic action, identifying what she fears he refused to do.
- 'Listen, I have fifteen horses to get ready.' The polite instruction listen addresses Sofia. French has an irregular plural for horse; the number fifteen makes clear why the assistant is busy.
- 'Go with the others, please. Thank you.' The polite or plural instruction to go is followed by with the others. The final please softens the command even though she is dismissing the discussion.
- 'It's not fair!' Calling something fair uses the description just. The negative turns it into an objection to the decision, rather than a statement that the scene is inaccurate.
