## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'What's that?' A short, natural question for everyday speech.
- 'Stay there, I'll go and see.' An informal command to his wife, followed by the near future. Down in the dark kitchen, Mathias surprises Hippolyte.
- 'You scared me!' Literally 'you made fear to me'. French uses 'to make' with 'fear' for 'to scare'.
- 'What are you doing?' Here, Hippolyte is holding bottles from his father's wine cellar.
- 'Nothing, I was a bit thirsty.' Just like hunger or shame, thirst uses 'to have': literally 'I had a little thirst'.
- 'My friends came round, we had nothing to drink.' The first verb is in the past with 'are', and the second uses the negative pair for 'nothing'.
- 'Are you with Camille?' Mathias guesses who the wine is really for.
- 'No. Why do you ask?' Hippolyte tries to hide it, as Camille asked him to.
- 'For nothing.' A two-word answer meaning 'for no reason'.
- 'Yes you are! Did she tell you something?' Notice the special word for 'yes' at the start: French uses it to contradict a negative statement.
- 'No. But she's not right for you.' Literally 'she is not made for you', with the feminine ending on 'made'.
- 'Explain that to me.' An informal command with 'to me' joined by a hyphen.
- 'Be careful with her. She did not tell you everything.'t trust her. She hasn't told you everything.' Literally 'one cannot make trust to her'.
- 'You're scaring me, Dad. What?' There's that expression 'you make fear to me' again, this time in the present.
- 'She has a boyfriend. He comes to the agency.' Rather than admit that Camille is his daughter, Mathias invents a rival in a suit and tie.
- 'You saw her with him? Okay.' Notice the feminine ending on 'seen', agreeing with the object pronoun 'her' before the verb. Hippolyte leaves hurt and convinced.
