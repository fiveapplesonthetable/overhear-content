## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Noémie, send flowers to Marie-Sophie Garnier, please.' Notice that Mathias uses the formal command here in the open office.
- 'Sign the note: Dear Marie-Sophie, I am sorry.' The adjective for 'dear' is in its feminine form with a grave accent. Of course Jonathan Joubert has no idea he is apologising.
- 'A new start. Signed Jonathan Joubert.' Mathias is Dictating a fake apology on his actor's behalf.
- 'Mathias, Zinedine Soualem is here.' Zinedine Soualem is another actor on Mathias's list, and he has just walked in.
- 'Not now!' Mathias ducks back into his office and shuts the door.
- 'No, Mathias isn't here.' Loyal Noémie lies without blinking. Zinedine asks her if Mathias knows he speaks Spanish for Almodóvar's next film.
- 'He can call you whenever he wants. Goodbye!' Literally 'when he wants'. Noémie politely sends the actor on his way.
- 'It's fine, he's gone.' The verb 'to leave' takes the verb 'to be' as its helper in the past. Mathias pokes his head back out.
- 'Don't forget the flowers for Marie-Sophie Garnier.' Now that they are alone, Mathias switches back to the informal command with Noémie.
- 'Of course. Interflora, please.' As Noémie orders the flowers, Camille and Hervé talk about the news.
- 'Is it true that they're going to kill off Ryan?' Near future with the verb 'to go' plus 'kill'.
- 'That's not possible!' Doctor Ryan is the star of the daytime soap.
- 'My mother is going to be very sad.' Camille's mother in the south of France is a huge fan.
- 'She watches the series every day!' Literally 'all the days', the standard French phrase for 'every day'.
- 'If I tell her that, she's going to organise protests!' The indirect object word before 'say' means 'to her'.
- 'That's going to make a stir in my mother's hairdressing salon!' Literally 'make noise in the hairdressing salon of my mother'. And that gives Camille an idea.
