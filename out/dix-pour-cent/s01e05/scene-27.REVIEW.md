## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Good morning, everyone. Thank you all for coming.' The greeting addresses everyone with a plural word for all. The completed form having come uses be as its helper, giving the reason he thanks the people in the room.
- 'This morning, I have good news for you.' The expression this morning names when he is speaking. The word for news is feminine and singular, so good takes its feminine form too.
- 'ASK will not be sold.' Will be places the announcement in the future. The feminine description sold agrees with the agency, while the negative says that sale will not happen.
- 'Is Hélène Kerr keeping Samuel's shares?' Keeping takes the shares directly as its object. The connecting word of links those shares to their previous owner, Samuel.
- 'No. I'm buying them.' The little plural word for them comes before buy out. It refers back to the shares, avoiding a second full mention of what he is buying.
- 'ASK's future is secure. You can be relieved.' The description secured goes with the masculine word future. The plural description relieved addresses the colleagues together, rather than just one listener politely.
- 'How long have you known?' Since when asks about the starting point of his knowledge. The little word for it stands before know, referring to his plan to buy the shares.
- 'I found a financial partner yesterday.' The completed action found describes what he achieved yesterday. Financial describes the kind of partner he has found, rather than saying that the partner is another agent.
- 'But you didn't want to introduce us to him?' The question includes both to us and him before introduce. It challenges his failure to bring the financial partner into the discussion.
- 'I didn't intend to, because it's my wife.' The past form had describes his intention before the announcement. The word of it points back to the introduction he says he had not intended to arrange.
- 'What? And we're supposed to be relieved?' Supposed to is a description followed by a basic action. The plural ending agrees with the colleagues included in we; Andrea questions whether relief is an appropriate reaction.
- 'Well done!' This short expression normally congratulates someone. In the surrounding objection it becomes sarcastic praise, so the literal words alone do not make it sincere approval.
- 'Would you have preferred the Germans?' Would have preferred imagines a different choice in the past. The plural word for Germans refers to the earlier potential buyers whom he now uses as a comparison.
- 'With him, even good news sounds very sad!' Even makes the judgement apply to a good announcement too. The feminine description good follows the feminine word for news, while sad describes how the news seems.
- 'Yes? Julie Gayet and JoeyStarr have disappeared.' The completed plural action have disappeared describes the two missing actors together. Their full names identify exactly whose absence has interrupted the agency's argument.
- 'Julie, it's me again. Call me back, please.' Again tells Julie that this is another attempt to reach her. The familiar instruction call me back attaches me after the action, followed by a familiar polite request.
- 'Didier, call me back. Production is furious!' The familiar instruction again attaches me after call back. The shortened word for production refers to the film production team, whose anger creates urgency.
