## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'You're with the tax inspector! You're crazy!' The word for inspector is feminine because the inspector is a woman. The description crazy is feminine too, matching the person Gabriel is addressing.
- 'You're behaving like a kid!' Behaving uses a reflexive action in French. The informal word for a little girl makes his comparison deliberately patronising, rather than a neutral comment about age.
- 'Why didn't you tell me?' The question puts to me and it before the past helper. The small word for it refers to Andrea's relationship, not to the inspector herself.
- 'Because of this! Why would it go badly?' The short opening gives his reaction as her reason for keeping quiet. Would go makes her question about a possible bad outcome, rather than something that has already happened.
- 'It always goes badly, Andréa! You make bad choices!' Going badly literally uses turn with bad. The plural description bad agrees with choices, expressing his judgement about more than this one decision.
- 'Because you're the ideal lover, is that it?' The strong word for you is placed first to contrast his own love life with hers. The question at the end challenges him rather than asking for information about a different man.
- 'Stop the car! Gabriel, stop the car!' The familiar instruction addresses the driver. Stop takes the car directly as its object; she is asking him to bring the vehicle to a halt.
- 'No. Fine, there!' The first refusal is followed by a short word meaning there or that's it. In the learner exchange it marks the point when the argument ends and she leaves the car.
- 'I took photos of him, as if it were real life.' Taking photos uses take as in English. The little word for him follows of, while as if introduces the made-up appearance of ordinary life in her montage.
- 'But look: more than ten thousand signatures in one night!' The word for more is positive here: more than ten thousand. It is not the negative expression meaning no longer. The final time phrase gives the short period in which the signatures arrived.
- 'I think it's good.' The strong word for me emphasises her own opinion before I think. The following that introduces what she thinks about the result, without making it a question.
- 'Should I delete it?' French can use an ordinary present-tense question to offer an action. The little word for it stands before delete, referring to the online petition.
- 'Hello? I don't have a car, I'm on foot!' Not having a car contrasts with being on foot. French uses have for possessing the car and be for describing how she is travelling.
- 'There's no signal. Can you send someone?' The opening is an impersonal expression for there is, made negative around have. The next question politely asks an unknown person to arrange help.
- 'It's an emergency! I'm all alone in the middle of the countryside!' The word for alone has a feminine ending for Andrea. All strengthens it, while the final phrase places her in open countryside rather than in a town.
