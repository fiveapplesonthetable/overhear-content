## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I had lunch with my father today. He is worried about you.' The completed past says the lunch took place earlier today. Worrying is expressed with a reflexive action, while for identifies whose future concerns him.
- 'Really? What's wrong with my professional future?' The question about what is wrong literally asks what does not go. The description professional is masculine because it follows the masculine word for future.
- 'You just turned down a golden opportunity at StarMédia.' Coming from an action can mean having just done it. The golden opportunity uses the word for gold as an image of something especially valuable.
- 'To stay at ASK, where the situation is uncertain.' The opening to introduces the purpose of refusing the other job. Where then connects the uncertain situation to the agency she chose to mention.
- 'But he wants to stick his nose in everywhere and tell me what to do!' Putting one's nose everywhere is the same intrusive image as in English. The word for me before tell names the person he resents receiving instructions.
- 'That's a shame. It was good news. Good night.' The French word for news is singular here, so good is feminine and singular too. The last farewell closes the subject just as he becomes curious.
- 'What good news? What is it?' The question word for what or which agrees with the feminine word for news. Repeating the question makes his sudden interest plain.
- 'Especially since he wants to lend us money.' Lend has both a recipient and a thing lent. The small word for us comes before lend, while money is the thing he might provide.
- 'Sorry, what?' The word used for an apology can also ask someone to repeat surprising news. Here it means that he wants to check he heard correctly.
- 'To buy out ASK.' The action means buy out or buy back. The opening for or to gives the purpose of the loan, rather than naming its recipient.
- 'Was it his idea?' The possessive his is used before a feminine word beginning with a vowel. French chooses that sound pattern even though the thing possessed, idea, is feminine.
- 'I brought it up. And I convinced him.' The first expression strongly identifies who raised the subject. The short word meaning him then stands before the past helper in the completed action convinced him.
- 'He is lending the money to me. I do what I want with it.' The first sentence emphasises to me as the loan's recipient. In the next one, the little word for of it or with it points back to the money she expects to control.
- 'If we buy back the agency, you will be the new boss of ASK.' The if part describes the possible purchase, and the following future form says what would follow. Boss is masculine here for Mathias in the learner dialogue.
- 'Do you know what I'd like? A writers-directors department.' Would like makes the wish hypothetical. The compound description combines writers and directors, imagining a department that can represent both.
- 'And an image-management department for the actors.' Management of image means handling how the actors appear publicly. The connecting word for marks whose interests the proposed department would serve.
- 'And production!' A short addition beginning with and can continue the speaker's own list. Production adds making films to the other activities he has imagined.
- 'Why not?' The brief question means she sees no reason to rule the idea out. It answers his suggestion without committing them to a finished plan.
