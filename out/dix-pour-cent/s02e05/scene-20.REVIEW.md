## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'The apartment viewing has just finished.' Mathias speaks with weary relief. The construction vient de se terminer expresses recent completion.
- 'How did the visit go with the buyers?' Noémie inquires with caring interest. The masculine plural noun acheteurs refers to property buyers.
- 'They loved the place and want to make an offer.' Mathias reports their positive reaction. The noun phrase faire une offre means to make an offer.
- 'It is good news to settle your problems.' Noémie tries to highlight the positive aspect. The feminine noun locution bonne nouvelle means good news.
- 'Yet, I feel completely overwhelmed.' Mathias shares his internal emotional turmoil. The past participle bouleversé means shaken or overwhelmed.
- 'What is hurting you, Mathias?' Noémie gently probes the cause of his sadness. The idiom faire de la peine means to cause grief or sorrow.
- 'This young couple reminds me of Catherine and me in the past.' Mathias is haunted by nostalgia. The verb rappelle means reminds or recalls.
- 'We were so happy in this apartment.' Mathias looks back on happier years. The imperfect étions expresses a past ongoing state.
- 'I understand your sadness about this change.' Noémie shows deep empathy for his pain. The prepositional locution face à means in the face of.
- 'I cannot take it anymore, everything is becoming too difficult.' Mathias admits his exhaustion. The verbal idiom n'en pouvoir plus means to be at one's limit.
- 'You are brave, you will overcome this trial.' Noémie offers steadfast reassurance. The infinitive surmonter means to overcome.
- 'My father used to say that life is a long trial.' Mathias recalls a somber family proverb. The imperfect disait introduces past speech.
- 'Your father was wrong to be so pessimistic.' Noémie rejects the cynical worldview. The idiom avoir tort de means to be wrong to do something.
- 'Maybe, but I have trouble keeping hope.' Mathias struggles against despair. The expression avoir du mal à means to find it difficult to.
- 'Look at me, Mathias. You are not alone.' Noémie reminds him of her loyal presence. The adjective seul means alone.
- 'I am here with you, we will succeed together.' Noémie promises her loving support. The simple future réussirons conveys determination.
