## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Here it is, and he that was called Judas!' Herve summons Camille with ominous biblical solemnity. The presentative 'voici' introduces a dramatic quotation from the scripture.
- 'Judas Barneville, daughter of Mathias!' Herve delivers his biting personal accusation. He substitutes Mathias's family name to brand Camille as a betrayer.
- 'Herve, stop... I am sorry.' Camille tries to apologize for the unexpected promotion. The adjective 'desolee' expresses genuine regret for the fallout.
- 'Let me finish! You betrayed your friends.' Herve cuts off her defense angrily. The imperative 'laisse-moi finir' demands uninterrupted expression of his anger.
- 'It was Hicham. I never asked for it.' Camille explains that the decision came entirely from Hicham. The verb 'chercher' here means to seek or actively campaign for something.
- 'Can you swear on your heart and soul that you never wanted this job?' Herve demands a moral confession. The idiom 'jurer en ton ame et conscience' appeals to absolute honesty.
- 'At first, a little. But I never put myself forward as a candidate.' Camille admits her private longing honestly. The reflexive locution 'se porter candidate' means to apply formally.
- 'Not to your father?' Herve suspects family lobbying behind closed doors. The preposition 'aupres de' means with or in the presence of someone influential.
- 'No. In passing, maybe, but that is all.' Camille downplays any casual conversation. The locution 'en passant' means casually or incidentally.
- 'Now it all makes sense! It was all premeditated.' Herve fits the conspiracy pieces together. The passive reflexive 'tout s'explique' indicates that everything is now clear.
- 'Not at all! That is not true.' Camille denies any underhanded calculation. The emphatic phrase 'ce n'est pas vrai' rejects the accusation outright.
- 'Yes! Mathias pulled strings for his daughter!' Herve accuses Mathias of blatant nepotism. The colloquial verb 'pistonner' means to pull strings or recommend through favouritism.
- 'He influenced Hicham to pick you!' Herve asserts Mathias's hidden leverage over the agency boss. The preposition 'pour' takes the infinitive 'te choisir'.
- 'And Miss Judas was telling me that it is bad to trip up others!' Herve mocks Camille's earlier moral advice. The verb 'faire trebucher' means to make someone stumble or sabotage them.
- 'Stop, Herve! I did not want to hurt you.' Camille pleads for understanding. The verb 'blesser' means to hurt someone emotionally or physically.
- 'You took my place, Camille.' Herve states his deep feeling of professional loss. The phrase 'prendre ma place' underscores his sense of stolen opportunity.
