## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hi Mom, things are not going well.' Camille reaches out to her mother in tears late at night. The informal greeting coucou is used with close family and friends.
- 'What is going on, my sweetie?' Annick responds with maternal warmth and concern. The reflexive phrase se passe asks what is happening.
- 'At the agency, it is a real hell!' The young woman pours out her distress over the hostile atmosphere. The masculine noun enfer means hell.
- 'They are all jealous of me.' She explains how her promotion created resentment among colleagues. The plural adjective jaloux agrees with the masculine plural subject ils.
- 'Herve will not even talk to me anymore.' Her former friend and fellow assistant has turned against her. The negative structure ne ... meme plus means not even anymore.
- 'He calls me Judas all day long.' Herve considers her promotion a betrayal of their assistant solidarity. The expression toute la journee means all day long.
- 'Andrea is always yelling at me.' Her boss takes out stress on her through verbal outbursts. The phrasal verb crier dessus means to yell at someone.
- 'I cannot take it anymore, Mom!' Camille reaches her breaking point under the toxic pressure. The fixed expression n'en pouvoir plus means to not be able to stand it any longer.
- 'Do not cry, my little girl.' Annick tries to comfort her weeping daughter over the phone. The negative imperative ne pleure pas urges her to stop crying.
- 'Come home with me.' Her mother urges her to abandon Paris and return to the south of France. The imperative rentre instructs someone to return home.
- 'Work must not be painful.' Annick affirms that no job is worth sacrificing mental health. The modal verb doit expresses an ethical requirement.
- 'I will pay for your ticket.' The mother generously offers to cover her train fare home. The near future vais payer uses aller followed by an infinitive.
- 'We will go together to the beach.' She paints an idyllic picture of rest and recovery by the Mediterranean. The simple future ira comes from the irregular verb aller.
- 'Thank you Mom, I will think about it.' Camille feels soothed by her mother's unconditional love. The adverbial pronoun y refers back to the suggestion of returning home.
