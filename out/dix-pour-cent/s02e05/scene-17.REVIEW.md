## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I really love Julien Doré's new screenplay.' Élisabeth expresses high enthusiasm for the project. The adverb vraiment emphasizes genuine appreciation.
- 'It is a very original and ambitious project.' Gabriel supports his client's artistic vision. The adjective ambitieux describes high artistic aspirations.
- 'I find his writing particularly mature.' She compliments the depth of the text. The feminine noun écriture refers to screenwriting style.
- 'Yes, but this movie pushes the boundaries quite far.' Gabriel hints at daring content. The idiom pousser les limites means to push the boundaries.
- 'Are you not afraid of an age restriction?' Élisabeth wonders about censorship ratings. The verb craignez comes from the irregular verb craindre.
- 'There is nothing too explicit in the text.' Gabriel tries to sound reassuring. The indefinite expression rien de takes a masculine adjective.
- 'Except maybe that scene near the lake.' Gabriel unwittingly opens a dangerous topic. The preposition sauf means except.
- 'Which scene are you talking about exactly?' Élisabeth asks for immediate clarification. The interrogative adjective quelle agrees with the feminine noun scène.
- 'The scene where the woman cuts off the man's thing.' Gabriel blurted out the shocking detail. The colloquial noun machin means thing or thingy.
- 'I never read this sequence in my file!' Élisabeth reacts with utter shock. The compound past n'ai jamais lu indicates past reading.
- 'Sorry, I think that I made a big blunder.' Gabriel realizes his terrible mistake. The feminine noun gaffe designates an embarrassing blunder.
- 'Did you send a toned-down version to the committee?' Élisabeth becomes suspicious of deception. The adjective édulcorée means sanitized or toned down.
- 'No, not at all, it was just a first draft.' Gabriel scrambles to minimize the slip. The masculine noun brouillon denotes a rough draft.
- 'Tell me the end of this scene anyway.' Élisabeth demands full transparency. The imperative racontez-moi requests a detailed explanation.
- 'She cooks the piece on a barbecue.' Gabriel confesses the absurd climax sheepishly. The causative construction fait cuire means to cook.
- 'Gabriel, your client is completely crazy!' Élisabeth concludes in disbelief. The adjective fou describes irrational or eccentric behavior.
