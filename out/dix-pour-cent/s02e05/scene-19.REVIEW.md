## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Sorry for my agitation earlier.' Guy apologizes for his visible stress. The locution tout à l'heure means earlier or a short while ago.
- 'You do not have to apologize, Guy.' Arlette dismisses his concern gently. The expression n'avoir pas à means not to need to.
- 'Your dog Jean Gabin is really nice.' Guy compliments her companion. The adjective gentil means kind or nice.
- 'He has a good nose, like his mistress.' Arlette makes a humorous comparison. The masculine noun flair refers to intuition or keen scent.
- 'It is true, you always guess the problems.' Guy acknowledges her sharp intuition. The verb devines means guess or sense.
- 'I have known my actors for a long time.' Arlette explains her deep experience. The prepositional phrase depuis longtemps means for a long time.
- 'Why does everyone call you Arlette Davidson?' Guy asks about her famous nickname. The compound name Arlette Davidson evokes the motorcycle brand.
- 'Because I always ride a motorcycle!' Arlette proudly reveals the origin. The prepositional phrase en moto means by motorcycle.
- 'You are a free and rebellious woman.' Guy praises her independent spirit. The adjective rebelle describes a nonconformist nature.
- 'I always refused to make concessions.' Arlette speaks of her lifelong principles. The verb construction refuser de means to refuse to do something.
- 'You often saved me on movie shoots.' Guy expresses sincere gratitude. The compound past as sauvé indicates past assistance.
- 'It is the job of a good agent.' Arlette defines her professional duty. The feminine noun agente refers to a talent agent.
- 'Thanks for coming today, Arlette Davidson.' Guy repeats her affectionate nickname. The expression merci d'être venue thanks a woman for coming.
- 'I will always be there for my friends.' Arlette reaffirms her enduring loyalty. The plural noun amis includes her cherished colleagues.
- 'Now, I can return to filming peacefully.' Guy feels calm and focused again. The adverb sereinement means peacefully or calmly.
- 'Go on, Guy, show them your immense talent!' Arlette sends him off with strong encouragement. The imperative vas-y encourages immediate action.
