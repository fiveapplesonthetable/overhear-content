## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I refuse the junior agent job.' Camille shocks Hicham by rejecting the promotion to agent. The verb refuse indicates a deliberate decision to decline an offer.
- 'Say that again, I did not understand.' Hicham asks her to repeat her unexpected declaration. The imperative repete commands someone to say something again.
- 'I refuse this fine promotion.' She reiterates her decision despite the status of the offer. The demonstrative cette agrees with the feminine noun promotion.
- 'I prefer to remain an assistant for now.' Camille values her current apprenticeship under Andrea. The time locution pour l'instant means for the time being or for now.
- 'Do you truly realize what you are saying?' Hicham questions her grasp of agency career stakes. The relative pronoun ce que means what or that which.
- 'I know exactly where my place is.' She asserts her comfort and confidence in her current role. The adverb exactement expresses precision.
- 'No, you know nothing at all.' Hicham dismisses her perspective as naive and shortsighted. The negation ne ... rien du tout means nothing at all.
- 'What do you mean?' Camille asks for clarification on his harsh assessment. The phrase vouloir dire means to mean or signify.
- 'An agency evolves like a river.' The boss uses a philosophical metaphor about corporate change. The comparison comme une riviere illustrates irreversible forward flow.
- 'We never go backward.' He insists that career steps cannot simply be reversed. The idiom en arriere means backward or in reverse.
- 'But I love my current work.' Camille pleads her passion for her daily tasks. The adjective actuel means current or present.
- 'You have the choice, my dear.' Hicham condescendingly frames the situation as an ultimatum. The affectionate term ma cherie sounds patronizing in this context.
- 'Take this job or take the door!' He delivers a ruthless career ultimatum to the young assistant. The idiom prendre la porte means to leave or be fired.
- 'That is unacceptable blackmail, Hicham.' Camille pushes back against the coercive pressure. The adjective inacceptable describes something that cannot be tolerated.
