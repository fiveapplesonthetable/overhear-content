## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'There is a wide selection of cheeses here.' Guy admires the canteen buffet. The noun phrase grand choix means wide selection.
- 'Hello Guy! How is your filming going?' Arlette greets her long-time client warmly. The pronominal verb se passe asks how things are progressing.
- 'I confuse the pepper and the salt today.' Guy admits his momentary confusion. The verb confonds means to mix up or confuse.
- 'That is not serious, that happens to everyone.' Arlette reassures him immediately. The phrase tout le monde means everyone.
- 'People are asking me questions about my memory.' Guy expresses his frustration with inquiries. The feminine noun mémoire refers to memory recall.
- 'They are afraid that I forget my lines.' Guy senses the crew's anxiety. The subjunctive j'oublie follows avoir peur que.
- 'You have fifty years of career behind you.' Arlette reminds him of his vast experience. The numeral cinquante indicates fifty.
- 'Look, Jean Gabin loves the butter!' Guy feeds a treat to the dog. The masculine noun beurre refers to butter.
- 'Jean Gabin, behave yourself at the table!' Arlette scolds her pampered dog gently. The reflexive imperative tiens-toi bien means behave yourself.
- 'Guy, are you still doing your famous butter trick?' Arlette playfully brings up his signature habit. The adjective fameux means famous or well-known.
- 'It is an old habit to charm the dairywoman.' Guy chuckles at the recurring joke. The feminine noun habitude means habit or custom.
- 'Do not get upset over a few memory blanks.' Arlette urges him to stay calm and poised. The negative imperative ne t'énerve pas advises against anger.
- 'I want to succeed in my scene this afternoon.' Guy focuses on his acting performance. The verb réussir expresses successful execution.
- 'You are a wonderful actor, have confidence.' Arlette bolsters his self-assurance. The imperative aie comes from the verb avoir.
- 'Thanks for coming to see me on the set.' Guy appreciates her physical presence. The prepositional infinitive d'être venue expresses gratitude.
- 'I will always be there to watch over you.' Arlette pledges her unwavering loyalty. The simple future serai expresses an enduring commitment.
