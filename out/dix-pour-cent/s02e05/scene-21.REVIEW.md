## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'What are you doing here, Andrea?' Hicham stops his jogging to question her unexpected presence. The interrogative phrase qu'est-ce que asks for information.
- 'Wait just one little minute.' Andrea urgently asks for a moment of his undivided attention. The imperative attends requests someone to pause.
- 'I am doing my last lap.' The executive explains that he is concluding his workout. The adjective dernier precedes the masculine noun tour.
- 'I must ask you something.' She insists that she has an important topic to bring up. The modal verb dois expresses an obligation or need.
- 'Can we have a drink together?' Hicham proposes meeting more casually over drinks. The idiom boire un verre means to have a drink.
- 'Why should we see each other?' Andrea remains skeptical of his motives after their past encounter. The question word pourquoi asks for a reason or purpose.
- 'To talk calmly, that is all.' He promises that he only wishes to converse peacefully. The adverb calmement modifies the infinitive parler.
- 'Do you want to seduce me again?' She confronts him directly about his flirtatious tendencies. The verb seduire means to charm or entice.
- 'No, that is not true!' Hicham firmly denies having romantic designs on her. The negative structure ce n'est pas vrai refutes a claim.
- 'I am pregnant, Hicham.' Andrea drops the life-changing bombshell without preamble. The adjective enceinte means pregnant.
- 'What? Are you really serious?' Stunned by the revelation, he struggles to process the news. The feminine adjective serieuse agrees with the female speaker.
- 'I absolutely had to tell you.' She feels an ethical responsibility to inform the father. The imperfect devais denotes a strong past obligation.
- 'Are four minutes enough for you?' He abruptly pivots to managing the conversation within his schedule. The verb suffit means is sufficient or enough.
- 'Yes, that is more than enough.' Andrea accepts the brief window to outline her stance. The adverb largement means broadly or amply.
