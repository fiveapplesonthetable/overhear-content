## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'And now, what would you say to officially welcoming our new junior?' Hicham opens the floor dramatically. The polite formula 'que diriez-vous de' invites opinions using the conditional form of dire.
- 'You should have consulted us beforehand.' Andrea objects to the unilateral move. The past conditional 'vous auriez du' expresses reproach for an omitted duty.
- 'I consulted myself. And I decided.' Hicham asserts his autocratic authority boldly. The reflexive passe compose 'je me suis consulte' emphasizes his sole decision-making.
- 'Our new junior is a woman.' Hicham begins unveiling his choice. The noun phrase 'notre nouveau junior' is followed by the predicate noun 'une femme'.
- 'And her name is Camille Valentini.' Hicham drops the bombshell announcement. The reflexive verb 's'appeler' is conjugated in the 3rd person singular.
- 'What? Camille?' Andrea gasps in shock. The interrogative pronoun 'quoi' conveys disbelief at the unexpected choice.
- 'There were candidates who knew very well how to sell themselves.' Hicham explains his reasoning. The imperfect 'savaient' introduces the pronominal reflexive infinitive 'se vendre'.
- 'She did not talk, she acted.' Hicham praises Camille's quiet initiative. The contrast between 'n'a pas parle' and 'a agi' uses the passe compose.
- 'Camille, were you a candidate?' Andrea questions Camille directly. The imperfect form 'etiez' asks about an ongoing status during the search.
- 'No, not at all.' Camille answers honestly. The emphatic negative idiom 'pas du tout' denies any prior ambition.
- 'No, she was not, but she came to Paris alone.' Hicham defends her independent spirit. The verb 'venir' takes the auxiliary etre in 'est venue seule'.
- 'She made a place for herself there without revealing who her father was.' Hicham admires her discretion. The preposition 'sans' governs the infinitive 'reveler' followed by an indirect question.
- 'Gritting your teeth, confiding in no one.' Hicham lists the qualities of resilience. The idiomatic infinitive 'serrer les dents' represents quiet perseverance under pressure.
- 'Those are the qualities you need in this business.' Hicham summarizes the agency virtues. The impersonal expression 'il faut' signifies essential requirements.
- 'So welcome, Camille, our new agent.' Hicham concludes his promotion speech. The interjection 'bienvenue' greets the newly elevated colleague warmly.
- 'Thank you, I will do my best.' Camille accepts the daunting challenge. The idiom 'faire de son mieux' promises wholehearted effort.
