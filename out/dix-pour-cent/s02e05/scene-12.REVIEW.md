## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'My Camille. Do not cry.' Andrea comforts the weeping assistant warmly. The affectionate possessive 'ma Camille' softens the negative imperative 'ne pleure pas'.
- 'Everyone hates me now.' Camille expresses her overwhelming feeling of isolation. The indefinite subject 'tout le monde' takes the 3rd person singular verb 'deteste'.
- 'I believe you. I know you did not want it.' Andrea reassures Camille of her unconditional trust. The transitive verb 'croire' takes the direct object pronoun 'te'.
- 'Herve thinks that my father arranged everything.' Camille explains the hurtful accusation. The verb 'arranger' indicates orchestrating or fixing an outcome behind the scenes.
- 'It is destiny knocking at your door.' Andrea frames the sudden opportunity poetically. The metaphor 'frapper a ta porte' symbolizes life-changing opportunities.
- 'And girls like us, we have no right to refuse.' Andrea identifies with Camille's struggle. The idiom 'avoir le droit de' means to have the right or permission to do something.
- 'Because it will not come back knocking. Do you understand?' Andrea stresses the urgency of seizing one's chance. The future 'ne reviendra pas frapper' emphasizes the rarity of such career breaks.
- 'Yes, I understand very well, Andrea.' Camille absorbs her mentor's advice. The adverbial intensifier 'tres bien' indicates complete clarity and comprehension.
- 'You know, your story with Christophe Lambert...' Andrea recalls Camille's earlier romantic cover story. The noun 'histoire' refers to Camille's fabricated excuse about her secret father.
- 'I never believed it.' Andrea confesses her lasting skepticism. The adverbial pronoun 'y' stands for the unbelievable cover story.
- 'Intellectually, yes, but not deep down.' Andrea contrasts head and heart. The adverb 'cerebralement' denotes rational acceptance, contrasted with 'au fond de moi'.
- 'I always knew there was something else.' Andrea explains her instinctive intuition. The compound tense 'j'ai toujours su' expresses continuous inner certainty.
- 'Something deeper, that would bring us closer one day.' Andrea speaks of an enduring bond. The conditional 'rapprocherait' suggests a predestined emotional connection.
- 'And you are not angry with me?' Camille seeks final reassurance. The prepositional phrase 'en colere contre' expresses anger directed at someone.
- 'No, not at all. We are like a family.' Andrea reaffirms their sisterly connection. The comparative preposition 'comme' establishes the warmth of kinship.
- 'Thank you, Andrea. That makes me feel good.' Camille finds comfort in her friendship. The idiom 'faire du bien' means to provide emotional relief or comfort.
