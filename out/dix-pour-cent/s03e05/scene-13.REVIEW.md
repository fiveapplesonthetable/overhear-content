## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'You must leave over there immediately!' Valentin recites his dramatic audition dialogue loudly. The modal expression 'tu dois' expresses strong obligation.
- 'It is a very big opportunity for you.' The character emphasizes what is at stake. Valentin uses the casual singular 'you' with 'opportunity', showing close familiarity.
- 'Do not waste this unique opportunity!' Valentin urges his fictional partner not to squander her future. The verb 'gacher' means to waste or spoil.
- 'Why do you not want to come with me?' Valentin pushes with intense emotion. The preposition 'avec' introduces accompaniment.
- 'Why are you shouting like that?' Camille halts the rehearsal to question his volume. The verb 'crier' means to shout or cry out.
- 'It is a very intimate love scene.' Camille reminds him of the tender context. The adjective 'intime' describes close, private moments.
- 'You do not need to yell.' Camille explains that projection is unnecessary for film. The verb 'hurler' means to scream or yell loudly.
- 'But my character is really angry!' Valentin justifies his intense vocal delivery. The idiom 'etre en colere' expresses the state of anger.
- 'There must be a progression in the scene.' Camille teaches pacing and gradual buildup. The noun 'progression' signifies a step-by-step development.
- 'The camera guesses your secret thoughts.' Camille notes how the camera magnifies subtle thought. The noun 'camera' denotes the movie camera.
- 'Keep your face calm like a mask.' Camille urges a restrained, interior performance. The masculine noun 'masque' refers to a protective or disguising mask.
- 'You know what, forget it, I stop!' Valentin feels hurt and threatens to walk away. The colloquial idiom 'laisser tomber' means to drop it or give up.
- 'Wait, stay, I am sorry Valentin.' Camille rushes to comfort her vulnerable boyfriend. The adjective 'desolee' conveys heartfelt apology.
- 'Sorry, it is entirely my fault.' Camille softens the critique to rebuild his confidence. The noun 'faute' means mistake, error, or fault.
