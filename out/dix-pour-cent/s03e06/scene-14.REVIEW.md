## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Quiet, she begins her line.' Gabriel urges everyone on set to listen. The verb 'commencer' takes a direct object here.
- 'I return from the realm of the dead.' The director recites the theatrical script line. The noun 'royaume' evokes a mythical realm.
- 'To bid you farewell at last.' Gabriel completes the tragic farewell sentence. The phrase 'dire adieu' expresses a definitive parting.
- 'Oh my! Cut right away!' The director suddenly halts the recording. The imperative 'coupez' orders the camera operators to stop.
- 'What is the problem this time?' Gabriel asks what caused the interruption. The interrogative adjective 'quel' agrees with the masculine noun probleme.
- 'I hear a very annoying noise.' The director points out an acoustic defect. The adjective 'genant' describes an unwelcome disturbance.
- 'It is only the night wind.' Gabriel downplays the extraneous sound. The adjective 'nocturne' relates to the night.
- 'The leaves falling on the floor!' The director identifies the rustling debris. The relative clause 'qui tombent' describes the action of the leaves.
- 'The noise gives a dramatic tone.' Gabriel highlights the poetic mood. The adjective 'dramatique' conveys heightened theatrical intensity.
- 'This crunching ruins all the emotion.' The director remains unconvinced by the sound. The verb 'gacher' means to spoil or ruin something.
- 'The sound engineer can clean that.' Gabriel suggests fixing the audio in postproduction. The modal verb 'peut' indicates technical capability.
- 'You always have a solution, Gabriel.' The director admires Gabriel's endless resourcefulness. Notice how 'have' leads directly into the noun meaning 'solution'.
- 'Let's keep this wonderful take now.' Gabriel presses to save the take and move on. The imperative 'gardons' calls for retaining the shot.
- 'Agreed, it is good for me!' The director finally gives his approval. In spoken French, the little word before 'the next word' drops its final vowel and attaches with an apostrophe.
