## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Look at this silhouette in the mist.' Gabriel points toward the mysterious actress. The imperative form 'regarde' is used to draw attention.
- 'That ghostly pallor seems strange to me.' The director studies her unusual appearance. The adjective 'spectrale' evokes an eerie apparition.
- 'It is the effect intended by the story.' Gabriel justifies the eerie visual tone. The past participle 'voulu' acts as an adjective.
- 'Her look reminds me of nobody.' The director feels the face is unfamiliar. The verb 'rappeler' means to remind or call to mind.
- 'She embodies a nameless ghost.' Gabriel explains the mystical role. The noun 'fantome' describes a spirit from beyond.
- 'The audience will recognize the stand-in.' The director worries about viewer scrutiny. The noun 'doublure' refers to an actor substitute.
- 'The lighting conceals all features.' Gabriel explains the visual illusion. The verb 'masquer' means to hide or obscure from view.
- 'She must say the key sentence.' The director insists on the crucial dialogue line. The noun 'cle' figuratively designates something essential.
- 'I changed my face.' Gabriel quotes the mysterious character line. The passe compose 'ai change' describes a completed transformation.
- 'This line works very well.' The director appreciates the poetic phrasing. The verb 'fonctionner' means to work effectively.
- 'Cinema magic always works.' Gabriel praises the power of artistic illusion. The verb 'operer' means to produce an intended effect.
- 'Let's film this take without waiting.' The director decides to start rolling. The imperative 'tournons' invites immediate shooting.
- 'Silence on set, please!' Gabriel calls for absolute quiet around the cameras. The verb meaning 'pleases' pairs naturally with the noun for 'silence' in everyday conversation.
- 'Action! Everyone is ready.' The director begins the crucial camera take. The adjective 'pret' indicates readiness to perform.
