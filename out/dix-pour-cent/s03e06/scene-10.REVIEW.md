## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hi Sophia, are you packing your boxes?' Gabriel asks about the packed apartment. The noun 'carton' commonly refers to a cardboard moving box.
- 'I am returning to live with my parents, Gabriel.' Sophia reveals her forced relocation. The preposition 'chez' precedes the noun designating someone's home.
- 'Why didn't you tell me anything?' Gabriel feels hurt that she kept it secret. The negative adverb 'rien' expresses complete negation of speech.
- 'Because your help never helps me.' Sophia criticizes his well-meaning intervention. The adverb 'jamais' indicates habitual lack of success.
- 'The film's flop is not my fault.' Gabriel disclaims responsibility for the failure. The noun 'faute' denotes personal blame or fault.
- 'You were thinking about yourself, not me.' Sophia reproaches his agency priorities. The imperfect 'pensais' describes an ongoing selfish mindset.
- 'I only wanted to protect your acting career.' Gabriel asserts his honest goal. The adverb 'seulement' restricts his intent to protecting her.
- 'I haven't worked in six months.' Sophia explains her financial desperation. The preposition 'depuis' indicates a duration extending to the present.
- 'Do you think my life is easy?' Gabriel bristles at her sarcastic tone. The adjective 'facile' describes a life without hardship.
- 'You, you still have your great job.' Sophia assumes Gabriel remains secure. The adverb 'toujours' expresses continuity of employment.
- 'I lost the woman of my life.' Gabriel expresses his deep romantic regret. The passe compose 'ai perdu' denotes irreparable loss.
- 'And I will lose my agency tonight.' Gabriel reveals that ASK is on the brink of collapse. The near future 'vais perdre' warns of imminent disaster.
- 'Good evening, sorry to have bothered you.' Gabriel steps away feeling defeated. The polite expression 'excuse-moi' softens his sudden departure.
- 'Wait, Gabriel, what is happening at ASK?' Sophia drops her bitterness out of genuine concern. The imperative 'attends' urges him to stop and explain.
