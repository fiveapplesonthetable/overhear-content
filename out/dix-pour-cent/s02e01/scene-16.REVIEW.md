## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Gabriel, Mathias is in his office.' Camille points Gabriel toward the corner office.
- 'Mathias, can I have a word with you?' Dire un mot is an idiom meaning to have a quick word.
- 'What is going on?' Qu'est-ce qui se passe is a standard question asking what is happening.
- 'Regarding Virginie, I would prefer that you take her back.' Preferer uses the conditional je prefererais.
- 'No, please have mercy!' Pitie is a noun meaning pity or mercy, used here as an exclamation.
- 'She is on the verge of breaking down.' Etre a deux doigts de means to be inches away from or on the verge of.
- 'I just asked you for help.' Demander de l'aide means to ask for assistance.
- 'Do you really want me to help you?' Vraiment is an adverb meaning really or truly.
- 'Of course!' Bien sur is an emphatic expression meaning of course or certainly.
- 'Since you separated from your wife, you are more human.' Se separer de means to separate from a spouse.
- 'It is temporary.' Temporaire is an adjective meaning temporary or short-lived.
- 'The separation or the humanity?' Gabriel teases Mathias with a clever contrast.
- 'Once reconciled, I will have both again.' Une fois followed by a participle means once or as soon as.
- 'When does a real relationship begin, then?' Une vraie relation means a genuine long-term relationship.
- 'A romantic comedy.' Une comedie romantique refers to the lighthearted early stage of love.
- 'A romantic comedy is not bad at all!' Pas mal is a conversational idiom meaning not bad or quite good.
