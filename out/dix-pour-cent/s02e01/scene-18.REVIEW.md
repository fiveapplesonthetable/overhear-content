## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'This guy is the golden goose.' Andréa spots a massive financial opportunity. The French expression literally says the hen with golden eggs.
- 'He sold his website for two hundred million.' She shares the incredible news with Gabriel. The passé composé uses the auxiliary verb to have.
- 'Are you listening to me, Gabriel?' She snaps her colleague's attention back to her plan. The direct object pronoun precedes the verb.
- 'Yes, but why would he invest in us?' Gabriel remains cautious and skeptical. The conditional mood expresses a hypothetical action.
- 'His mother told me that he is getting divorced.' Andréa reveals her insider intelligence. The verb for divorce is used in the present tense.
- 'He is looking for a new project.' She notes that he needs a fresh challenge. The verb to look for takes a direct object without a preposition.
- 'I am going to speak to him, he is ready.' Andréa decides to make her move immediately. The near future combines to go with an infinitive.
- 'Speak to him how?' Gabriel wonders what leverage she could possibly have. Notice the indirect object pronoun placed before the verb.
- 'We were very good friends in high school.' She reminds Gabriel of their past bond. The imperfect tense describes an ongoing past relationship.
- 'Look! Wait!' Seeing Hicham's car about to leave, she shouts urgently. The imperative commands are short and punchy.
- 'Hicham, attends!' She calls after him as he starts his engine. The imperative form of to wait uses the regular second-person ending.
- 'So, Andréa... you ran to say goodbye to me?' Hicham rolls down his window with a teasing smirk. The question uses the conversational past tense.
- 'Where are you going?' Andréa quickly checks his destination. The question places the interrogative word at the end.
- 'To Dole airport.' Hicham explains that he is heading back to his private jet. The preposition specifies the airport destination.
- 'Can you drop me off at the shoot? It's on the way.' She maneuvers her way into his sports car. The phrase déposer someone means to drop someone off.
- 'Léa Seydoux will thank you in person. Promised.' She sweetens the ride with a star encounter. The future simple expresses a confident promise.
