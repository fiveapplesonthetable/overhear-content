## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Ah, the sleepyhead is finally awake!' Gabriel greets Virginie as she wakes up. The feminine noun la dormeuse refers to a sleeping woman.
- 'Good morning Gabriel. Do you have coffee?' She immediately looks for her morning dose of coffee. The partitive article du introduces the beverage.
- 'Yes, take a cup in the kitchen.' Gabriel invites her to make herself comfortable. The imperative prends gives a warm and informal invitation.
- 'If your agency closes, what will you do?' She asks about his plans if the agency goes bankrupt. The conjunction si introduces a conditional clause.
- 'I do not know. My job is my security.' Gabriel worries about losing his steady career. The possessive adjective ma agrees with the feminine noun.
- 'You could become a great actor.' Virginie teasingly proposes a dramatic career shift. The conditional pourrais makes a playful suggestion.
- 'You are completely crazy, Virginie!' Gabriel laughs at her absurd idea. The feminine adjective folle matches the female subject.
- 'Not at all! I can give you lessons.' She eagerly offers to act as his private mentor. The phrase mais non strongly contradicts his objection.
- 'Dance, singing, comedy!' She lists the classic disciplines of stage performance. The preposition de is repeated before each separate discipline.
- 'The sky is purer than my heart!' She recites a dramatic classical verse to demonstrate. The comparative adjective plus pur expresses a poetic comparison.
- 'You just want to make fun of me.' Gabriel suspects she is only trying to tease him. The reflexive verb se moquer de means to mock or poke fun.
- 'You will be magnificent on stage.' She assures him that audiences will be dazzled. The future tense form seras indicates future confidence.
- 'Come on, let's get to work right away.' She enthusiastically pushes him to begin his training. The expression se mettre au travail means to start working.
- 'All right, I am willing to try.' Gabriel gives in playfully to her theatrical coaching. The phrase je veux bien indicates willing consent.
