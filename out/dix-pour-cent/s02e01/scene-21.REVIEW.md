## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I am at my sister's, it is horrible!' Virginie begins by explaining her awful living situation. The preposition chez indicates staying at someone's house or place.
- 'The paparazzi are in front of the building.' She describes the persistent reporters waiting outside. The preposition devant means in front of an entrance or building.
- 'They wait for the kids at school.' Photographers are even stalking young family members. The verb attendre is used here in the third-person plural present tense.
- 'Plus, her boyfriend looks at me strangely.' She feels awkward around her sister's partner as well. The connecting phrase en plus adds another frustrating element to the situation.
- 'How so? Judgmental looks?' Gabriel seeks clarification on what kind of looks she means. The phrase how so is used to ask for a clearer explanation.
- 'No, he stares at me too much. I am getting paranoid.' She admits the intense media pressure is getting to her. The adverb trop expresses an excessive degree.
- 'My sister is completely overwhelmed.' The whole family is suffering under the media intrusion. The past participle describes being deeply shaken or upset.
- 'I cannot stay over there.' Virginie realizes that remaining in the apartment has become impossible. The negative structure ne peux pas expresses an inability to act.
- 'Can I sleep at your place for two nights?' She asks Gabriel for temporary refuge. The modal verb peux asks for permission in an informal tone.
- 'My apartment is under renovation.' Gabriel tries to hesitate by mentioning the ongoing construction. The phrase en travaux refers to construction or remodeling work.
- 'That does not bother me at all.' She is willing to put up with dust and noise to escape the cameras. The phrase pas du tout emphasizes the total lack of objection.
- 'I just need a quiet spot.' Any small corner will suffice for her temporary hideout. The expression avoir besoin de means to need something essential.
- 'What do you mean by my door code?' Gabriel is surprised that she is already asking for access. The phrase qu'est-ce que tu veux dire asks for further explanation.
- 'I just have pajamas and tissues. Thank you!' Virginie accepts without waiting for him to object. The adverb juste means only or simply in this conversational context.
