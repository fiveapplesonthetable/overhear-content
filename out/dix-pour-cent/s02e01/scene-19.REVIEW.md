## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'It is funny to meet again like this.' Andréa breaks the ice as they drive. The infinitive to meet again is reflexive in French.
- 'Maybe it is a sign.' Hicham replies with an amused tone. The adverb for maybe comes right after the verb to be.
- 'Nobody expected much of us in high school.' She looks back on their awkward adolescence. The negative pronoun nobody takes the particle ne.
- 'We still did pretty well.' Hicham proudly reflects on their unexpected success. The idiom s'en sortir means to manage or succeed.
- 'Why did you get into the car?' He cuts through the small talk with a direct question. Because Andréa is a woman, the past participle takes a silent extra e.
- 'When you are rich, you learn to recognize self-interest.' Hicham shows he is no naive fool. The verb to learn is followed by the preposition à and an infinitive.
- 'It's true, I had something in mind.' Andréa admits that she has an ulterior motive. The imperfect tense j'avais describes a continuing state in the past.
- 'But I must make sure that I can trust you.' She sets high terms before unveiling her plan. The phrase faire confiance à someone means to trust someone.
- 'We were in high school together. At that age, one doesn't lie.' Hicham invokes their shared roots. The pronoun on is used here for people in general.
- 'Do you remember it?' Andréa tests his memory of their wild high-school parties. The reflexive verb se souvenir de takes the adverbial pronoun en.
- 'Yes, first heartbreak!' Hicham laughs about their youthful romantic disasters. The compound noun chagrin d'amour literally means grief of love.
- 'I wanted to show that I would never be like them.' He explains his rebellious outsider spirit. The conditional form je serais expresses a future seen from the past.
- 'So we are kind of the same.' Andréa establishes their deep emotional affinity. The adjective pareil means alike or identical.
- 'The truth is that my agency... It's a great company. A jewel, envied by all.' She begins her pitch. Notice the colloquial business noun boîte for firm or agency.
- 'The owner died last winter, since then, the jewel has been for sale.' She explains the agency's crisis. The prepositional phrase à vendre means for sale.
- 'We don't want someone to buy us, but to marry us.' She delivers her brilliant closing metaphor. The passive infinitive être acheté contrasts with être épousé.
- 'We're here.' Hicham pulls the car to a halt right as she finishes her pitch. The short phrase on y est marks arrival at a destination.
