## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'This shoot was a big mistake.' Virginie reflects on the romantic comedy they filmed together. The adjective grande comes before the noun to emphasize its importance.
- 'Why do you say that?' Gabriel asks her to explain her sudden regret. The question phrase pourquoi est-ce que is a standard way to ask why.
- 'Playing the ideal couple makes no sense.' Pretending on screen felt completely false to her. The infinitive jouer acts as the subject of the sentence.
- 'We never talk about our real worries.' Deep communication issues were constantly ignored. The negative adverb jamais follows the verb directly in present tense.
- 'Do you have worries between you two?' Gabriel inquires gently about their personal relationship. The preposition entre means between two people or things.
- 'He acts as though everything were fine.' Ramzy avoided confronting any unpleasant truth. The conjunction phrase comme si takes the imperfect tense to describe a condition.
- 'He always wants to joke with everyone.' Humor became a constant defense mechanism. The adverb toujours sits right after the conjugated modal verb.
- 'Me, I prefer to speak about real things.' Virginie needs emotional honesty and open dialogue. The emphatic pronoun moi highlights her own personal contrast.
- 'So after five months, the tension exploded.' The prolonged pressure on set finally reached a breaking point. The preposition apres indicates time that has elapsed.
- 'Do you have news from him today?' Gabriel wonders if Ramzy has tried to get in touch. The expression avoir des nouvelles de means to hear from someone.
- 'No, he still refuses to speak to me.' Ramzy remains hurt and completely unreachable. The adverb encore expresses the ongoing continuation of an action.
- 'It weighs on me to hurt him.' She carries a heavy emotional burden from their breakup. The impersonal construction expresses a feeling of being weighed down with guilt.
- 'Look through the window, they are still there!' She spots the persistent paparazzi waiting outside on the street. The imperative regarde draws direct attention to the outside scene.
- 'Leave me alone, go away from here!' She vents her anger and frustration into the night air. The polite imperative form laissez-moi asks for immediate personal space.
