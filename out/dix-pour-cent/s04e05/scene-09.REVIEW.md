## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'You are late, Madame Martel!' The director expresses her immediate frustration. The prepositional phrase 'en retard' means late.
- 'I know, I had an emergency.' Andrea justifies her arrival with a serious excuse. The noun 'urgence' refers to an emergency.
- 'You left your daughter alone!' The director reproaches Andrea for leaving Flora unattended. The adjective 'seule' describes being alone.
- 'She was safe here.' Andrea argues that the daycare is a protected place. The feminine noun 'securite' designates safety.
- 'It is a complete lack of respect.' The director condemns the late pickup. The masculine noun 'manque' means lack or shortage.
- 'It was proof of trust!' Andrea reframes her lateness as total confidence in the staff. The feminine noun 'preuve' signifies proof.
- 'No, it is unacceptable for us.' The director rejects Andrea's rationalization. The adjective 'inacceptable' means unacceptable.
- 'Say goodbye to the daycare!' The director delivers the severe consequence. The noun 'creche' designates a daycare center.
- 'Are you expelling Flora for that?' Andrea is shocked by the sudden expulsion. The verb 'renvoyez' comes from 'renvoyer', to dismiss or expel.
- 'Yes, we enforce the rules.' The director insists on strict administrative regulations. The noun 'reglement' means rules or regulations.
- 'Very well, goodbye, madame.' Andrea accepts the verdict coldly without pleading. The phrase 'tres bien' serves as a formal acknowledgment.
- 'You are really crazy!' The director watches Andrea leave in bewilderment. The feminine adjective 'folle' means crazy or insane.
- 'Come, Flora, we are going home.' Andrea speaks gently to her infant daughter. The verb form 'rentre' comes from 'rentrer', to return home.
- 'Goodnight, my little baby.' Andrea tucks Flora safely into her crib for the night. The masculine noun 'bebe' designates a baby.
