## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I like your movie project very much.' Bernard shares his initial artistic interest. The adverb 'beaucoup' modifies the verb 'aimer'.
- 'It is an honour to act with you.' Sigourney pays polite respect to her French colleague. The noun 'honneur' designates honor or privilege.
- 'But I have a very strict condition.' Bernard introduces his personal working constraint. The adjective 'stricte' describes an inflexible rule.
- 'Oh yes? Which one, dear Bernard?' Sigourney reacts with friendly attentiveness. The interrogative pronoun 'laquelle' asks which one.
- 'I count all my steps.' Bernard monitors his physical output closely on set. The masculine noun 'pas' indicates a walking step.
- 'No more than fifteen steps per scene!' Bernard states his firm contractual limitation. The numeral 'quinze' means fifteen.
- 'Really? And for the dance scene?' Sigourney questions how his rule applies to their choreography. The noun phrase 'scene de danse' refers to a dance sequence.
- 'A slow dance, very calm, I hope.' Bernard assumes their performance will be low-energy. The feminine adjective 'lente' means slow.
- 'No, I chose the lindy hop!' Sigourney reveals that she updated the dance to swing jazz. The compound past 'ai choisi' comes from 'choisir'.
- 'The lindy hop? That is very fast!' Bernard worries about the intense physical exertion. The adjective 'rapide' means quick or speedy.
- 'Look, I am going to show you.' Sigourney offers a live demonstration right in the dining room. The infinitive 'montrer' means to show.
- 'But what are you doing?' Bernard gasps as Sigourney hops energetically across the floor. The clause hinges on the everyday verb meaning 'are doing'.
- 'Dance with me, it is wonderful!' Sigourney invites him to share her joy of movement. The imperative 'dansez' addresses Bernard with polite respect.
- 'No, that is too many steps!' Bernard grabs his coat and flees in terror of exceeding his quota. The quantifier 'trop de' expresses an excessive amount.
