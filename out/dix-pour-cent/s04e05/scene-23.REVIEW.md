## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'How could you betray our agency?' Andrea demands an explanation. The compound past with as-tu pu highlights her profound disbelief.
- 'It is only business, Andrea.' Elise offers a callous commercial excuse. The plural demonstrative ce sont les affaires detaches personal morality.
- 'You stole all my actors!' Andrea expresses outrage at the predatory raid. The compound past verb as volé accuses her directly of theft.
- 'Why make this situation so personal?' Elise questions her emotional reaction. The interrogative infinitive phrase rendre si personnelle deflects responsibility.
- 'I spent nights staying awake.' Andrea evokes her endless dedication to clients. The verb phrase passé des nuits emphasizes years of sleepless devotion.
- 'You are far too emotional, my dear.' Elise mocks her vulnerability. The patronizing address ma chère sharpens the insult.
- 'I lost my own family for them.' Andrea confesses her deepest domestic wound. The compound past verb ai perdu lays bare her personal sacrifice.
- 'It is very touching, but it is over.' Elise dismisses the heartbreak with a smirk. The paired phrases c'est très touchant and c'est fini close the door on sympathy.
- 'I am proud of my accomplished work.' Andrea refuses to feel shame for caring. The adjective phrase fière de mon travail asserts moral dignity.
- 'You are going to feel very lonely from now on.' Elise delivers a cruel parting shot. The adverb désormais underlines permanent isolation.
- 'You have no respect for the artists.' Andrea identifies the fundamental difference between them. The negative quantifier aucun respect underscores the lack of honor.
- 'They all prefer to sign with me.' Elise boasts of her commercial triumph. The verb préfèrent tout signer claims total victory.
- 'Repeat that to my face if you dare!' Andrea's temper explodes into fury. The challenge si tu oses dares her opponent to continue.
- 'Let go of my arm immediately, stop!' Elise panics as physical violence erupts. The urgent imperative lâche-moi signals sudden chaos.
