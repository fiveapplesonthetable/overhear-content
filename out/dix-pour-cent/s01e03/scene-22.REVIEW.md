## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'You have lots of calls.' The expression for 'a lot of' is followed by the short word for 'of'. Before the vowel in 'calls', that word loses its vowel.
- 'Isabelle Adjani has a very urgent hair problem.' French links 'problem' and 'hair' with a word meaning 'of'. The description 'very urgent' follows the problem it describes.
- 'Who do we start with?' Literally 'we start by whom?'. The question word comes last, and the everyday word for 'we' takes a singular action form.
- 'Solveig Akerfelt.' A name alone answers the question about whom to call first. Gabriel has heard that the director rejected Nathalie and wants to speak to her.
- 'She isn't on my list.' The two negative words surround 'is'. Herve checks his own list, so the possessive before it means 'my'.
- 'She doesn't want Nathalie Baye.' The negative surrounds 'wants'. French adds a short word meaning 'of' before the person who is being rejected in this expression.
- 'That's mean!' The describing word can mean unkind or nasty. Used after 'that is', it comments on the director's decision.
- 'You made Laura do that for nothing!' French uses 'make' twice: a completed 'made', then 'do' in its basic form. This expresses making someone else perform an action.
- 'Without Nathalie, Laura can play the daughter now.' The first word removes one person from the situation: 'without'. The final word means 'now', because the news has changed Laura's options.
- 'Am I fired?' The past form of 'fire' describes Herve's feared situation. It has a masculine spelling here because Herve is a man.
- 'Yes. I'm sorry.' Gabriel's apology has a masculine written ending. The spoken form sounds the same as the apology with a feminine ending.
- 'Am I really fired?' The word for 'really' sits between 'am' and 'fired'. Herve repeats the question to check that Gabriel means it.
- 'No, it's a joke!' The final word means 'joke' and follows the feminine word for 'a'. Gabriel reveals that he was pretending to dismiss Herve.
