## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Summer twenty eleven. She broke her ankle.' With parts of the body, French uses a reflexive verb and the word for 'the' instead of 'her'.
- 'I couldn't walk. I stayed at my mother's.' The first verb describes how things were, and the second is a single event. The preposition before 'my mother' means 'at the home of'.
- 'Total horror! It was as if she were thirteen.' Again, French expresses age with the verb 'to have': she had thirteen years.
- 'I completely lost it. We said horrible things to each other.' The first verb literally means to crack or snap.
- 'Never again!' Literally 'never more'. Both mother and daughter swear the same thing.
- 'I'm not going to sacrifice our relationship for a film.' Nathalie tells Mathias her decision is final. Yet in Gabriel's office, Laura has no idea her mother is saying no.
- 'But as for me, if I say no, she'll think it's because of her!' The three-word phrase near the end means 'because of'.
- 'That's horrible! It's going to break her heart.' Literally 'break the heart to her', with the indirect object word before the verb 'break'.
- 'You're right, Gabriel. I can't refuse.' Literally 'you have reason', the standard French idiom for 'you are right'.
- 'Yes, you can. You can make sure that she refuses.' Notice the special word for 'yes' at the start, contradicting Laura's negative sentence.
- 'Who?' A single short question word, because Laura thinks that Gabriel still means her mother.
- 'Solveig! Just do a Bardot.' He means the famous French icon Brigitte Bardot, using her name for a whole way of behaving.
- 'What's that?' Literally 'it is what, that?', the casual spoken way to ask what something means.
- 'You become capricious, a difficult star like Brigitte Bardot.' Gabriel wants Laura to act like a spoiled diva at her audition.
- 'Akerfelt hates that!' A strict Scandinavian art-house director won't tolerate diva behaviour for a second.
- 'But that's ridiculous! It's a very bad idea.' The adjective for 'bad' is in its feminine form to match 'idea'.
- 'I'm not actually going to do a Bardot!' The two-word phrase inside the negative adds 'all the same' or 'really'. And yet, in the very next scenes, both mother and daughter will try to sabotage their auditions.
