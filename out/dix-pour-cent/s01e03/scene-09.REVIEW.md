## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Sofia, did you put your photo in this envelope?' The everyday past of 'to put' uses a short past form after 'have'.
- 'Yes! And she wants me for the casting!' The short object word meaning 'me' comes before 'wants'. Sofia makes herself the centre of the sentence: the casting director picked her.
- 'No, Sofia! You're not with our agency.' Literally 'in our agency'. Sofia works on the front desk, not as one of their clients.
- 'And I don't know if you can act.' Notice the same action word, 'to know', used twice: first 'know a fact', then 'know how to act'.
- 'You don't know? Then come and see my show!' This command 'come' is followed straight by 'to see', with no little word in between.
- 'Nobody offers me auditions!' With the word for 'nobody' at the start, French doesn't use the usual second negative word.
- 'But here, you stole this audition!' The action word 'to steal' in the everyday past. Sofia objects to the word.
- 'No, I just added my photo, that's all.' The two words at the end literally say 'it is all', a very common way to finish an excuse.
- 'Does she want to see me? Does she like my look?' Both questions keep the same word order as statements. A rising voice makes them questions, and the object 'me' goes before the action.
- 'Calm down, Sofia!' Literally 'calm yourself'. Then Gabriel's phone rings: it's Laura Smet.
- 'Hello, Laura?' That opening greeting is used only on the telephone. Sofia doesn't care that he's on a call.
- 'Gabriel, we're talking! I invited everyone to my show.' The three-word expression for 'everyone' literally says 'all the world'. It includes all her colleagues, even though none of them came.
- 'And nobody came!' The verb 'to come' makes its past tense with 'is' rather than 'has'.
- 'One second, Laura, please.' He uses the casual 'please' with his client Laura, then turns back to Sofia to get some peace.
- 'That's enough! I'll come and see your show, okay?' The first two words mean 'it suffices', and then he uses the future tense of 'to come'.
- 'You'll come and see me? Thank you!' Sofia lights up at once, using the present tense for the near future.
- 'An agent's promise! Yes, Laura, I'm with you.' Literally 'promise of agent', without an article, and then 'I am to you' as he goes back to his phone call. Let's listen to the whole scene once more.
