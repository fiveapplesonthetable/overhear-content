## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'You don't want to work with me?' Listen to the 'you': it's the polite form, from a mother to her daughter. It's unusual, a family habit, a bit old-fashioned and grand. Laura denies it.
- 'No, that's not true at all.' The two negative words wrap around 'is', and 'at all' makes it stronger. Her mother doesn't believe her.
- 'Tell the truth.' A command in the polite form. Laura turns the question around.
- 'And you? You don't want to work with me?' Exactly her mother's question, sent back. Nathalie dodges.
- 'I'm the one asking the question!' Literally 'it's me who asks the question'. That's how French puts weight on one person. Laura draws her conclusion.
- 'You're not answering. I get it.' The second sentence literally says 'I have understood'. Then she turns to Solveig.
- 'She's my mother. It's monstrous!' She's shocked that the director wants to use their tension. Nathalie hits back.
- 'Are you ashamed of me?' Literally 'do you have shame of me?'. French uses 'to have' for feelings like shame, hunger or thirst.
- 'Like when I took you to school?' The verb is in the past form for things that used to happen again and again. A mother's memory, used as a weapon.
- 'I never said that!' Here the second negative word means 'never' instead of 'not'. The pattern is the same: one little word before the verb, one after.
- 'With you, I'm always a kid.' The last word is a casual word for a little girl. Then she imitates her mother's voice.
- 'Stand up straight!' The thing her mother always said. And notice: in this quote it's the casual 'you', the way a mother talks to a little child. Solveig is delighted and calls them a monstrous mother and an unbearable daughter. That makes Nathalie furious.
- 'My daughter is not unbearable!' Suddenly the mother defends her child. Laura defends their right to fight.
- 'If I can't argue with my mother, then who with?' The verb 'to argue' comes with a little word for 'myself'.
- 'Of course my mother annoys me!' The first three words mean 'of course'. And her mother agrees.
- 'Well, obviously!' The scene ends with mother and daughter on the same side, against the director. Later, in the lift, their agents ask how it went.
- 'Ten out of ten. But we're not doing it.' Solveig gave them full marks, and they still said no to the film. The second sentence is a clean negative with the everyday 'we'.
- 'One day, we'll make a film together.' The verb is in the future: you can hear the ending. A warm end for mother and daughter.
