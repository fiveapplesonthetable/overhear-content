## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Go and get some champagne at the bar.' A casual command: Mathias uses the casual 'you' with Camille. The small word before champagne means 'some'.
- 'Okay. See you in a bit.' The second phrase is a handy goodbye for when you'll meet again the same day.
- 'Camille! Over here.' Literally 'by here'. Mathias waves to her across the room.
- 'This is Dominique Besnehard.' Literally 'I present to you'. It's the standard way to introduce one person to another. Camille answers very politely.
- 'Hello, delighted to meet you.' The word for 'delighted' ends with an extra silent letter, because Camille is a woman.
- 'A producer.' Mathias adds it in a low voice, so that she knows how important this man is.
- 'Hello, Camille. Mathias has told me a lot about you.' The everyday past tense, 'has spoken', with the little word for 'me' tucked in before the helper verb. Notice he uses the polite 'you' with her.
- 'Really? Good things, I hope!' Literally 'in good'. A light, charming answer.
- 'Absolutely. Do you like films?' A question made just by raising the voice at the end.
- 'You bet! It's my dream.' The first two words literally mean 'and how!', a cheerful way to say 'absolutely'.
- 'I can offer you an internship.' Here's our grammar point: 'I can' followed by a verb in its dictionary form, with the polite word for 'you' placed just before that second verb.
- 'It's on Guillaume Gallienne's next film.' To say 'Gallienne's film', French says 'the film of Gallienne'.
- 'Really?' Camille can hardly believe it.
- 'She's perfect for it.' The adjective is in its feminine form, because it's about Camille. Mathias looks very pleased with himself.
- 'My card. Call me at the office.' A polite command, 'call me', with 'me' stuck to the verb by a hyphen.
- 'I'm not going to call Besnehard.' The next morning, in the agency corridor. This is the near future, 'I am going to call', made negative: the two little words of the negative wrap around 'going'.
- 'Why? He's a great producer!' Mathias doesn't understand. The word for 'great' here is the casual 'super'.
- 'I'm happy here.' Literally 'I am well here'. It's what Camille has been saying since she arrived.
- 'I want to learn to be an agent.' Three verbs in a row: 'I want', then 'to learn', then 'to be'. Notice there's no word for 'an' before a job in French.
- 'I have no qualifications, but I can learn.' The negative here uses a word that means 'not a single one'.
- 'Otherwise, I'll look for a job at another agency.' The first word means 'if not'. She's polite, but it's a real ultimatum.
