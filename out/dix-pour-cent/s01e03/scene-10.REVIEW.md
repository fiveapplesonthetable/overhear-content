## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'What?' Short and blunt, the everyday way to react to a surprise.
- 'Don't you like the script?' The negative goes around the action word for 'like'; before a vowel the first little word shrinks. Now listen carefully to the first word of Laura's answer.
- 'Yes, I love it.' That's the special yes again, the one you use to contradict a negative question.
- 'But three months of shooting, on a boat, out at sea!' Literally 'in full sea'. You can hear she isn't thrilled.
- 'I can't stand that.' The action word 'can' plus another action word in its basic form, with the negative wrapped around 'can'.
- 'I know an excellent acupuncturist for seasickness.' Literally 'against the sickness of the sea'. Gabriel, ever helpful, has completely misunderstood.
- 'No. I'm talking about my mother.' In French you 'talk of' something. And the word for 'my' is feminine here, to go with 'mother'.
- 'Three months! Do you know an acupuncturist for that?' She throws his own words back at him.
- 'But you were happy!' The action word 'to be' in the form for describing how things were. And 'happy' has a feminine ending for Laura.
- 'I love her.' The same verb can mean liking something or loving someone. The small object word before it refers to Laura's mother and loses its vowel before the action.
- 'But I cannot stay with her on a boat!' After the preposition, French uses the full pronoun to identify Laura's mother. It happens to look the same as the subject word for 'she'.
- 'Wait.' Gabriel gives a casual command you'll hear all the time.
- 'Talk to Akerfelt first.' This phrase in the middle means 'first of all'. Gabriel thinks the director will win her over.
- 'Afterwards, you'll see things differently.' The verb 'to go' plus 'see' talks about the near future: you are going to see.
- 'If she's a genius, it'll be even harder to say no.' That's the real future tense: 'it will be'. And 'even more hard' is how French says 'even harder'. Laura is afraid of liking it too much.
- 'Laura, you can't say no!' The agent's cry from the heart. A film with mother and daughter would be a big deal for the agency.
- 'Okay. I'll talk to her.' Near future again, and that small word before the verb means 'to her'. For now, Laura gives in. Good. Let's listen to the whole call once more.
