## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I am the seagull.' In the play, a young actress called Nina says this about herself. It's one of the best-known lines in the theatre.
- 'No, that's wrong.' She has got a line in the wrong order and starts again. The word for 'wrong' here also means 'false'.
- 'Chekhov, The Seagull, act four.' Here, Hervé and Sofia, listening at the desk, are impressed.
- 'I did it at drama school.' The little word for 'it' sits before the helper word and loses its vowel. The drama school is the famous Paris acting school. Then someone walks in.
- 'Mum?' The everyday French word for mum. Laura is surprised: she didn't know her mother had an appointment too.
- 'Well, well!' The same word twice, from the action word 'to hold', said with mock surprise.
- 'It's not easy to learn lines with all this noise.' The negative wraps around the verb 'to be'. The word for lines here is simply 'a text'. A mother's advice, given in a busy reception area.
- 'Really work on it at home.' Nathalie gives a casual command, with the little word for 'it' joined on after it. The last two words mean 'at your place'.
- 'Thanks, Mum.' Laura sounds a little stiff. Then Gabriel finally comes down.
- 'Sorry, we were in a crisis meeting.' The verb describes what was going on, in the past tense used for background.
- 'A little hitch.' A hitch is a small problem. Gabriel always makes things sound smaller than they are.
- 'A crisis or a hitch?' Nathalie isn't fooled for a second.
- 'For Gabriel, even a disaster is just a hitch!' The word for 'even' comes before what it stresses. Everybody knows Gabriel.
- 'Laura, listen.' As Laura leaves with Gabriel, her mother calls her back.
- 'Yes?' Laura waits for the next piece of advice.
- 'Emmanuel, she must stand up straight.' The verb for 'must' is followed by a reflexive verb meaning 'to hold oneself'. Nathalie is asking Gabriel to watch Laura's posture, but she calls him by the wrong name.
- 'It's Gabriel, Mum!' Laura is embarrassed for him.
- 'Gabriel, right.' Nathalie repeats it, as if it were a small detail. Let's hear the reception scene once more, all the way through.
