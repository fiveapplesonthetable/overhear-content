## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'What's wrong?' The final action word literally means 'go'. French often asks whether things 'go well' where English asks whether they are all right.
- 'You're lying!' This is the familiar 'you' form of 'to lie'. The final written s is silent, so the accusation is very short.
- 'I saw Mathias put that envelope on your desk.' After 'saw', the next action stays in its basic form. The person who did that action goes between the two verbs.
- 'Stop it, you're annoying, Noemie!' The first word is a direct instruction. The adjective at the end can mean difficult or tiresome; applied to a person, it expresses irritation.
- 'Why you, then?' The form of 'you' here stands on its own. It is the same form you use after a word such as 'with' or 'for'.
- 'Because I'm new. He talked to Andrea.' The describing word for 'new' has a feminine form because Camille is speaking about herself. She offers a quick explanation for the invitation.
- 'You're looking up and to the left! So you're lying to me.' Listen for the small linking word meaning 'so'. Noemie treats the direction of Camille's gaze as proof, an unreliable claim the story plays for comedy.
- 'On the first day, you saw Mathias. So you know him!' The object meaning 'him' appears before 'know'. The same spelling can also mean 'the' when it comes before a noun.
- 'Are you secretly working for Mathias?' French keeps the word order of a statement and lets the rising voice ask the question. The expression in the middle means 'in secret'.
- 'Okay! In fact, he's a friend of my father's.' The words for 'in fact' introduce a correction or explanation. French expresses 'my father's friend' as 'a friend of my father'.
- 'My father is married, with children. It's a secret.' The adjective for 'married' has a masculine ending because it describes her father. This much is true; Camille still keeps his identity hidden.
- 'One of the agency's stars? One of Mathias's clients?' The two openings mean 'one of the', first with a feminine noun and then with a masculine noun. French follows the grammatical gender of the group.
- 'Yes. But I can't tell you.' Two small object words stand together before 'tell': first 'to you', then 'it'. They refer to Noemie and the father's identity.
- 'Who? Tell me, or I'll tell Andrea everything!' In the command, 'me' attaches after the action with a hyphen. In the next sentence, 'everything' goes before the final action word.
- 'You're Christophe Lambert's daughter?' French uses a word meaning 'of' before the father's name. Noemie follows Camille's gaze to a film poster and invents the wrong answer.
- 'But you really look like him! I'm really sorry!' French says 'resemble to someone', so the object before the action means 'to him'. It points back to Christophe Lambert.
- 'Don't talk about it to anyone.' This negative instruction uses the word for 'no one' instead of the usual second negative. The small word before 'talk' refers to the secret: 'about it'.
- 'Of course! It will stay our little secret.' The ending of 'stay' points to the future. The possessive 'our' turns the lie into something Noemie feels she shares with Camille.
- 'Actors have unusual lives. We are just their agents.' The possessive before 'agents' means 'their' and ends in s because the agents are plural. Noemie already counts herself among them.
