## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Why do you get in this elevator?' Gabriel demands to know why Elise followed him into the elevator. The masculine noun 'ascenseur' means elevator or lift.
- 'We must talk about our work.' Elise insists on establishing an open dialogue with her reluctant peer. The modal verb 'devons' expresses duty or necessity.
- 'The atmosphere is already very clear.' Gabriel coldly dismisses her proposal to clear the air. The adverb 'deja' means already.
- 'You hate me since my arrival.' Elise bluntly confronts Gabriel about his lingering resentment. The verb 'detester' means to hate or loathe.
- 'You stole our best clients!' Gabriel accuses her of poaching ASK talent while at StarMedia. The past participle 'vole' comes from the verb 'voler'.
- 'That is the game of our profession.' Elise defends competitive poaching as standard industry reality. The masculine noun 'metier' means profession or line of work.
- 'Press the button to go down.' Gabriel just wants the elevator to reach the lobby so he can leave. The verb 'appuyer' means to press.
- 'Wait, the elevator stops by itself!' Elise notices the machinery shudder and stall. The reflexive verb 's'arreter' means to stop.
- 'No, we are completely blocked!' Gabriel realizes the cabin is trapped between floors. The adjective 'bloques' means stuck or blocked.
- 'Do not panic, it is nothing.' Elise tries to reassure her visibly shaking colleague. The negative imperative 'ne panique pas' means do not panic.
- 'I am extremely claustrophobic!' Gabriel reveals his severe phobia of confined spaces. The adjective 'claustrophobe' describes someone who fears enclosed areas.
- 'Breathe calmly, they will help us.' Elise instructs Gabriel to steady his breathing. The imperative 'respire' comes from the verb 'respirer'.
- 'I send an emergency text to Andrea.' Gabriel frantically pulls out his phone to seek rescue. The feminine noun 'urgence' means emergency.
- 'We are going to spend time together!' Elise ironically remarks that fate has forced them to bond. The near-future phrase 'allons passer' means are going to spend.
