## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Oh my goodness, it is Christophe Lambert!' Noemie is astonished to see the famous film star walk into the lobby. The phrase oh mon Dieu expresses sudden shock or delight.
- 'Hello Noemie! How are you doing?' Christophe warmly greets her by name with relaxed charm. The question comment tu vas is an informal greeting used among acquaintances.
- 'I am doing very well, thank you very much.' She responds politely while trying to hide her excitement. The adverb phrase tres bien indicates feeling great.
- 'Can I take a photo with you?' She gathers her courage to ask for a souvenir snapshot. The modal verb peux asks for polite permission.
- 'Yes, no problem with pleasure!' Christophe is gracious and happy to grant her request. The expression pas de probleme reassures someone completely.
- 'I am a very big fan.' She confesses her longstanding admiration for his filmography. The adjective grande emphasizes her deep enthusiasm.
- 'Me too, I am very friendly.' He jokes playfully to put the nervous assistant at ease. The adverb phrase moi aussi adds a friendly humorous touch.
- 'Get closer. Big smile!' She prepares the smartphone camera for the picture. The imperative mettez-vous instructs him where to stand.
- 'Hi Christophe! Come this way.' Mathias steps out of his office to welcome his high-profile client. The imperative viens invites someone to follow.
- 'Mathias! It is a pleasure to see you.' The actor greets his agent with genuine warmth. The phrase c'est un plaisir expresses polite enthusiasm.
- 'The director wants you for this role.' Mathias pitches an exciting new film offer right away. The direct object pronoun te comes before the conjugated verb.
- 'Read the script tonight.' The agent encourages him to evaluate the material without delay. The imperative form lis gives a direct instruction.
- 'All right, I will read it quickly.' Christophe agrees to look through the project immediately. The near future structure vais lire expresses immediate intention.
- 'See you later Mathias. Goodbye!' He wraps up the visit on an upbeat note before leaving. The phrase a tout a l'heure means see you shortly.
