## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Good evening Andrea! You look very elegant.' Fabrice greets Andrea warmly as they meet at the restaurant table. The adverb tres intensifies the adjective.
- 'Thank you Fabrice. Bravo on your show!' She immediately congratulates him on his stage performance. The interjection bravo expresses enthusiastic praise.
- 'It was truly a magnificent play.' Andrea highlights the beauty and depth of the production. The adverb vraiment adds genuine conviction.
- 'Did you like my performance?' The actor seeks her candid artistic reaction. The compound past tense tu as aime asks about past experience.
- 'I absolutely loved your acting.' She expresses unbounded admiration for his theatrical craft. The verb adorer indicates passionate enjoyment.
- 'And that young blonde woman from yesterday?' Fabrice turns the conversation to her romantic life. The noun phrase femme blonde describes the person in question.
- 'No, she is not my type.' Andrea brushes off the inquiry with a casual denial. The negative phrase ce n'est pas rejects the assumption.
- 'You like her very much. Go for it!' The actor playfully encourages her to take a bold chance on romance. The colloquial imperative fonce means go for it.
- 'Do you really think that would work?' She questions whether taking such an initiative is wise. The verb croire expresses personal belief.
- 'It is completely obvious!' Fabrice insists that the romantic connection is undeniable. The phrase tout a fait means completely or entirely.
- 'I do not know where to start.' Andrea admits feeling completely at a loss with dating. The interrogative phrase par ou asks which route or approach to take.
- 'Trust me when it comes to love.' He poses as an experienced philosopher of romantic relationships. The imperative fais-moi confiance asks for complete reliance.
- 'Shall we eat something now?' He suggests turning their attention to ordering food. The pronoun on is used colloquially for we.
- 'Yes, let us order a good dinner.' Andrea agrees happily to start their meal. The first-person plural imperative commandons proposes action together.
