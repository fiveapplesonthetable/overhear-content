## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Do you have info on Luchini?' Andrea asks Herve for background intelligence on the actor. The informal abbreviation 'infos' is commonly used in everyday speech for information.

- 'He just moved into a building in Montmartre.' Herve shares recent residential details. The recent past construction 'venir de' plus infinitive expresses something that just happened.
- 'And he is allergic to coconut.' Herve adds an obscure dietary detail. The adjective 'allergique a' governs the noun phrase introduced by the feminine article.
- 'We are not planning a homicide!' Andrea points out the absurdity of the trivia. The pronoun 'on' stands for we, negated with 'ne ... pas' around the present tense verb.
- 'He works out at a gym.' Herve reveals the vital operational lead. The pronominal verb 's'entrainer' means to work out or practice athletically.
- 'Good! Which day, at what time?' Andrea demands precise logistical details. The interrogative adjective 'quel' and 'quelle' agree with the masculine and feminine nouns.
- 'No idea for the moment.' Herve admits his intelligence has limits. The negative determiner 'aucune' emphasizes the complete absence of information.
- 'Then find out quickly!' Andrea orders him to dig deeper without delay. The reflexive imperative 'renseigne-toi' commands someone to find out or inquire.
- 'Pull, release! It is a bit slack.' Herve barks orders during the gym workout. The paired imperatives 'tirez' and 'relachez' describe rhythmic physical exercise.
- 'This machine is too heavy for me.' Andrea complains about the physical exertion. The adverb 'trop' intensifies the adjective 'lourde', agreeing with the feminine noun.
- 'I did not put any weight on it!' Herve reveals the machine is completely unloaded. The negative phrase 'ne ... aucun' negates the existence of any added weights.
- 'The machine must be broken.' Andrea excuses her complete lack of muscle strength. The modal verb 'devoir' conveys high probability when combined with the infinitive.
- 'Sweat or it will not look credible.' Herve insists on physical authenticity for the cover story. The imperative 'transpirez' pairs with the future tense 'ne sera pas' in the negative.
- 'You are as stiff as a hammer!' Herve critiques her rigid posture bluntly. The comparative structure 'aussi ... que' expresses an equality comparison with humour.
- 'Now run, every second counts! Come on!' Herve urges her onto the treadmill. The imperative 'courez' and the exclamation 'allez' drive the urgent pace.
