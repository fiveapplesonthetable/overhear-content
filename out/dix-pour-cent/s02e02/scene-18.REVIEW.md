## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'It's open! Would you like tea?' Luchini welcomes Andréa into his chaotic apartment. The partitive article du introduces the beverage.
- 'No thanks, I'm fine.' Andréa politely declines refreshment. The everyday phrase ça va means I'm fine.
- 'How are you? You seem in a state.' Luchini notices her agitated expression. The French idiom être dans tous ses états means to be completely flustered.
- 'Me, I'm pretty depressed. I missed out on a James Bond movie.' The star shares his disappointment. The verb rater means to miss out on or fail.
- 'So I left Bréhier.' Luchini drops the bombshell about firing his agent. The connector alors introduces a logical consequence.
- 'That's good!' Andréa can barely contain her excitement for ASK. The short phrase c'est bien expresses approval.
- 'Don't get carried away. I am going to take an agent break.' Luchini immediately dampens her hopes. The reflexive idiom s'emballer means to get ahead of oneself.
- 'Meaning?' Andréa asks him to clarify his strange decision. The set expression c'est-à-dire asks for an explanation.
- 'I have no films planned.' Luchini explains his schedule. The negative determiner aucun takes the particle ne.
- 'I'm doing theatre, I don't need anyone.' The actor boasts of his theatrical independence. The phrase avoir besoin de means to need someone or something.
- 'I am sorry, I really liked you, Andréa.' Luchini expresses genuine fondness for her. The imperfect tense j'aimais describes a continuing feeling.
- 'It's like getting dumped when you were never even together.' Andréa laments the strange situation. The informal verb se faire larguer means to get dumped.
- 'Yes, that's kind of it.' Luchini chuckles at her candid observation. The phrase un peu ça confirms a playful analogy.
- 'I lost her forever.' Andréa opens up about her heartbreak over Colette. The direct object pronoun l' takes feminine agreement on the past participle perdue.
- 'What if we went to the theatre tonight, then had dinner?' Luchini offers a platonic evening out. The construction et si followed by an imperfect makes a friendly suggestion.
- 'I would like that.' Andréa accepts the warm invitation. The conditional ça me plairait expresses polite enthusiasm.
