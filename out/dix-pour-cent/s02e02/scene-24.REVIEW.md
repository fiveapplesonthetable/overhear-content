## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Do not let yourself be fooled, Fabrice!' Brehier barges into the dinner with an urgent warning. The reflexive structure ne te laisse pas avoir warns against deception.
- 'This woman is very dangerous.' The rival agent attacks Andrea's integrity directly. The feminine adjective dangereuse describes a serious threat.
- 'Excuse me? What are you talking about?' Fabrice is taken aback by the sudden aggressive outburst. The question de quoi est-ce que asks for clarification.
- 'The Bond film story was total nonsense!' Brehier drops the bombshell about the fraudulent audition. The colloquial phrase du bidon means completely fake.
- 'It is Andrea who engineered everything.' He points fingers directly at her manipulative tactics. The past participle manigance describes plotting behind the scenes.
- 'I do not have the power to make that up.' Andrea calmly tries to deflect the damaging accusation. The infinitive inventer means to fabricate or invent.
- 'It was not the real casting director.' Brehier exposes the impostor who conducted the fake meeting. The adjective vraie comes before the noun.
- 'Well then, who was this woman?' Fabrice begins to suspect that he was indeed tricked. The connector mais alors questions the identity of the stranger.
- 'She wanted to get revenge on our agency.' Brehier explains the bitter motive behind the scheme. The reflexive verb se venger expresses retaliating.
- 'It is you who called the tax inspectors!' Andrea hits back with Brehier's own underhanded sabotage. The noun fisc refers to the government tax audit office.
- 'Your agency is completely ruined!' He sneers at ASK's desperate financial state. The past participle ruinee describes total economic ruin.
- 'Believe what you want, Fabrice.' She refuses to grovel or debate further in front of her rival. The imperative crois invites independent judgment.
- 'You agents are really strange!' Fabrice is disgusted by the vicious pettiness of talent agents. The plural adjective bizarres highlights their absurd rivalry.
- 'Leave me alone, Francois!' Andrea finishes the confrontation by dismissing Brehier completely. The colloquial imperative fiche-moi la paix orders him to back off.
