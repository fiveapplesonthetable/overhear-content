## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Cheers to everyone!' Hervé raises his glass to toast the group. The noun 'sante' is the standard French toast, literally meaning health.
- 'How are you doing tonight, Camille?' Sofia checks in with her colleague warmly. The interrogative 'comment tu vas' is a common conversational way to ask how someone is.
- 'I am doing very well, thank you very much.' Camille replies politely. The adverbial intensifier 'tres bien' pairs with 'merci beaucoup' in everyday social exchanges.
- 'Why don't you go out with Hippolyte?' Sofia innocently brings up the handsome young actor. The negative question 'pourquoi tu ne sors pas' uses the verb 'sortir avec' for dating.
- 'Do not say foolish things, Sofia.' Camille deflects the awkward question instantly. The idiom 'dire n'importe quoi' means to talk nonsense or speak recklessly.
- 'We are friends, that is all.' Camille firmly sets the record straight. The informal pronoun 'on' pairs with 'est amis', ended by the conclusive idiom 'c'est tout'.
- 'Are you kidding? You are made for each other!' Hervé teases them enthusiastically. The expression 'etre faits l'un pour l'autre' means to be made for each other.
- 'No, we are too similar.' Camille explains her hesitation. The reflexive verb 'se ressembler' expresses mutual resemblance, modified by the adverb 'trop'.
- 'No, I am just going to the restroom.' Camille excuses herself from the table. The phrase 'aller aux toilettes' is the customary French way to say going to the restroom.
- 'Their fathers know each other very well.' Hervé drops a mysterious family hint. The possessive determiner 'leurs' agrees with the plural noun 'peres'.
- 'Why does she not say it?' Sofia wonders why the family connection is kept quiet. The direct object pronoun 'le' replaces the unspoken fact before the verb 'dit'.
- 'It is a big family secret.' Hervé explains the air of mystery. The noun phrase 'secret de famille' describes a confidential household matter.
- 'Who is her real father?' Sofia asks with intense curiosity. The interrogative pronoun 'qui' asks for an identity, qualified by 'vrai pere'.
- 'Christophe Lambert! That is her father!' Hervé dramatically reveals the star name. The exclamation pairs the famous actor's name with the presentative 'c'est'.
- 'Seriously? That is incredible!' Sofia reacts with genuine astonishment. The adjective 'incroyable' expresses disbelief, following the informal exclamation 'serieux'.
- 'She looks a lot like him, do you think so too?' Hervé seeks agreement on the likeness. The phrase 'ressembler a quelqu'un' takes the indirect pronoun 'lui'.
