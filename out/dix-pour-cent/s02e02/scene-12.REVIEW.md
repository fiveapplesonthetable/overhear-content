## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello, Fabrice! Do you come here often?' Andrea initiates conversation with casual charm. The temporal adverb 'souvent' modifies the present tense question about routine attendance.
- 'I know you, you are an agent, right?' Luchini spots her professional identity immediately. The verb 'connaitre' is used for recognizing or being acquainted with a person.
- 'Andrea Martel, I work at ASK.' Andrea introduces herself with professional pride. The preposition 'chez' denotes working at a particular company or agency.
- 'Should you not be at the office?' Luchini raises an ironic eyebrow at her fitness location. The conditional verb 'devriez' expresses expectation or obligation in a polite register.
- 'In fact, I am at work right now.' Andrea asserts that scouting him is official business. The discourse marker 'en fait' signals an unexpected clarification.
- 'I am here for you. You are a rare talent.' Andrea delivers her direct flatteries. The adjective 'rare' follows the masculine noun 'talent' to convey exceptional distinction.
- 'What are you offering exactly?' Luchini invites her specific commercial proposal. The interrogative phrase 'qu'est-ce que' asks what she proposes, sharpened by 'exactement'.
- 'I offer a new artistic impetus.' Andrea pitches fresh creative energy for his career. The masculine noun phrase 'un nouvel elan' uses the special masculine form 'nouvel' before a vowel.
- 'All of this is a bit strange.' Luchini comments on the theatrical ambush. The demonstrative pronoun 'tout ca' is modified by the adjective 'bizarre'.
- 'Turning up in a gym to speak to me!' Luchini notes the comical unorthodoxy of the setting. The informal infinitive 'debarquer' evokes showing up suddenly and unannounced.
- 'I have been with Brehier for thirty-five years.' Luchini reveals his deep agency history. The temporal preposition 'depuis' indicates an ongoing state that started in the past.
- 'We are an old couple. I like him.' Luchini describes his relationship with his agent. The verb 'aimer bien' expresses warm affection or fondness without romantic love.
- 'Novelty is a concept that does not interest me.' Luchini rejects trendy career shifts. The relative clause 'qui ne m'interesse pas' negates personal appeal emphatically.
- 'Maybe you just want to be bored.' Andrea challenges his resistance with witty sarcasm. The reflexive infinitive 'vous ennuyer' denotes experiencing boredom or dullness.
- 'Do not speak ill of boredom.' Luchini defends existential boredom as a creative state. The negative imperative 'ne dites pas du mal de' forbids criticizing something.
- 'I leave you then to be bored with Brehier.' Andrea concludes her exit with sharp wit. The verb 'laisser' governs the reflexive infinitive 'vous ennuyer' in a graceful departure.
