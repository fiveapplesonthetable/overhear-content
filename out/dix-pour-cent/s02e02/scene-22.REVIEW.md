## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Seriously Hicham, everything is progressing very well.' Andrea reassures the new boss about her negotiation progress. The adverb serieusement introduces a frank statement.
- 'Trust me with this file.' She asks for professional autonomy without constant oversight. The imperative fais-moi confiance asks for complete faith.
- 'All this pressure does not help me.' Heavy management pressure is only hindering her work. The negative structure ne m'aide pas expresses obstruction.
- 'I have a gift for you.' Hicham presents a luxury box with a strategic intent. The preposition pour indicates the recipient of the gift.
- 'Open the box right away.' He insists on seeing her reaction immediately. The temporal phrase tout de suite means right away.
- 'We have not won yet.' Andrea remains cautious about signing the unpredictable actor. The negative structure n'a pas encore indicates an incomplete goal.
- 'Wear this dress tonight.' Hicham wants her to look stunning for the theater dinner. The demonstrative determiner cette specifies the garment.
- 'Try it on now in front of me.' He inappropriately asks her to change right in front of him. The spatial preposition devant indicates in someone's presence.
- 'No, I am not trying it on now!' Andrea forcefully draws a firm professional boundary. The direct object pronoun l' refers back to the dress.
- 'We are old schoolmates.' He tries to use their past history to justify his boundary crossing. The adjective vieux precedes the plural noun.
- 'There is no question of slipping this on.' She shuts down his intrusive demand with absolute authority. The fixed idiom il n'est pas question de expresses a flat refusal.
- 'Very well. Do not let go of Luchini.' Hicham backs down but reminds her of the high stakes. The negative imperative ne lache pas orders persistence.
- 'I do not want to look foolish.' His massive pride cannot tolerate a public professional failure. The idiom avoir l'air means to look or appear.
- 'All right, I am managing the situation.' She concludes the confrontation on her own terms. The present tense verb gere expresses competent control.
