## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Did we have a wild night?' Arlette teases Andréa's disheveled and glowing appearance. The phrase passer une nuit means to spend the night.
- 'Exactly! Guess with whom.' Andréa hypes up the mystery. The imperative form devinez commands them to guess.
- 'With whom?' Mathias asks with dry skepticism. The interrogative prepositional phrase uses qui.
- 'None other than Fabrice Luchini.' Andréa drops the superstar name triumphantly. She pauses for dramatic effect.
- 'No. You shouldn't have gone that far.' Mathias immediately suspects a scandal. The impersonal imperfect il fallait describes what was required.
- 'No... it was wild and platonic.' Andréa sets the record straight. The adjectives fou and platonique qualify the experience.
- 'He left Bréhier.' She delivers the crucial agency intelligence. The passé composé uses the auxiliary to have.
- 'That is excellent news! I love it!' Arlette rejoices at their rival's loss. The verb adorer expresses delight.
- 'Calm down, Arlette.' Mathias tries to maintain composure. The reflexive imperative calme-toi uses the informal pronoun.
- 'What a killjoy!' Arlette mocks Mathias's perpetual pessimism. The exclamatory word quel introduces the compound noun rabat-joie.
- 'We talked all night, I adore this guy.' Andréa describes their deep mutual connection. The phrase toute la nuit indicates uninterrupted duration.
- 'I don't want to brag but I think he likes me too.' She notes their instant chemistry. The reflexive verb se vanter means to brag or boast.
- 'I don't understand. Did he say yes?' Mathias demands practical confirmation. The verb to say is in the conversational past tense.
- 'Not yet, but he invited me to the theatre tonight.' Andréa reveals her follow-up date. Because Andréa is female, the past participle invitée takes an extra e.
- 'Let's not celebrate too quickly, but it looks promising!' She cautions prudence while staying optimistic. The pronominal idiom s'annoncer bien means to look promising.
- 'You see, Mathias? We just had to dare.' She proudly claims credit for taking the risk. The verb oser means to dare.
