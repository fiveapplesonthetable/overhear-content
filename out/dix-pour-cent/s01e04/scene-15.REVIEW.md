## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I'm very pleased too.' Literally 'me too, I am very pleased', in the masculine form.
- 'We're going to shoot the Canadian part first.' The verb for filming a movie is the same as the verb 'to turn'.
- 'What Canadian part?' This question word at the start is in its feminine form to match 'part'.
- 'A month of filming in Canada. Didn't Mathias tell you?' Before a masculine country name like Canada, French uses the combined word for 'to the'.
- 'No, he forgot.' Audrey covers for Mathias, though she is privately furious that he hid a whole month abroad.
- 'I'll explain to you now. It's in the north, near the lakes.' French uses the present tense here where English says 'I will explain'.
- 'The landscape is magnificent!' Augustin is carried away by his artistic vision.
- 'Sorry, but there are incredible places around Paris.' Audrey cannot bear the thought of leaving her babies for a month.
- 'Do you know Rambouillet? There are big forests there too!' Rambouillet is a wooded town just outside Paris.
- 'The film tells the story of my mother's life, and she's Canadian.' That settles the question of filming near Paris.
- 'Oh, I see.' Three simple words when you finally understand.
- 'So I really want to shoot over there.' The hyphenated word at the end means 'over there'.
- 'Yes, of course.' Meanwhile, in the next office, Gabriel is pitching Sofia to a director making an eighteenth-century costume film.
- 'I'm not saying to replace Julie Gayet with a Black actress.' Here, Julie Gayet is a well-known French star.
- 'I can suggest Sofia Leprince to play the countess.' Gabriel tries to get Sofia a noble part.
- 'That's perfect! See you soon.' The director agrees to see her, and Gabriel is thrilled.
