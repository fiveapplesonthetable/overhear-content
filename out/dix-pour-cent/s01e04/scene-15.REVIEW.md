## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'We're going to shoot the Canadian part first.' The verb for filming a movie is the same as the verb 'to turn'.
- 'What Canadian part?' This question word at the start is in its feminine form to match 'part'.
- 'A month of filming in Canada. Didn't Mathias tell you?' For being in a masculine country such as Canada, French uses the combined form of 'at' and 'the'. The short object before 'has told' refers to this news.
- 'No, he forgot.' Two short forms make the past action: 'he has', then 'forgotten'. Audrey simplifies the awkward explanation rather than saying Mathias intentionally hid the trip.
- 'I'll explain everything to you now. It's in the north, near the lakes.' The short object before the action marks Audrey as the listener. The word for everything follows the action; the final place phrase locates the film shoot.
- 'The landscape is magnificent!' The word for landscape is masculine, so it takes the masculine article meaning 'the'. The describing word sounds and looks the same for either gender.
- 'Sorry, but there are incredible places around Paris.' The expression for 'there are' stays the same even with several places. Audrey proposes a location closer to her children.
- 'Do you know Rambouillet? There are big forests there too!' Before a plural describing word and its noun, the article meaning 'some' shortens. Listen for the brief sound before 'big forests'.
- 'The film tells the story of my mother's life, and she's Canadian.' The possessive before 'mother' is feminine because it agrees with that family word. The nationality describing his mother is feminine too.
- 'Oh, I see.' Three simple words when you finally understand.
- 'So I really want to shoot over there.' The hyphenated word at the end means 'over there'.
- 'Yes, of course.' The two-word expression after 'yes' strengthens Audrey's agreement. We then move to Gabriel's office, where he is proposing Sofia for another film.
- 'I'm not saying to replace Julie Gayet with a Black actress.' To replace someone uses a short word meaning 'by' or 'with' before the replacement. Gabriel is proposing a different role for Sofia.
- 'I can suggest Sofia Leprince to play the countess.' The verb meaning 'can' takes a plain action after it: 'suggest'. Later, the short word before 'play' gives the purpose of the suggestion.
- 'That's perfect! See you soon.' The farewell literally points to a future meeting, 'until soon'. You can use the same short phrase after making an arrangement by phone.
