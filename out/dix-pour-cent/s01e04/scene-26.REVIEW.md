## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Mr Audibert will see us in five minutes.' Literally 'receives us in five minutes'. French often uses the present tense for something happening very soon.
- 'What are we going to say to him?' The long question phrase at the start means 'what', followed by 'we are going to say to him'.
- 'He helps us, or we stop sending our clients here.' After the verb for 'to stop', French puts the little word for 'of' before the next plain verb.
- 'As usual.' Literally 'like of habit'. A very common everyday phrase.
- 'Oh no! Look!' Andréa spots someone walking into the hotel lobby.
- 'What? Who is there?' Two short question words: 'what?' and 'who is there?'.
- 'It's Brancillon! She already knows everything about the room!' The tax inspector is right here at the hotel.
- 'Hello, Ms Brancillon.' Gabriel panics and greets her politely as she walks past.
- 'It's not what you think.' Literally 'that which you believe'. Gabriel makes them sound guilty straight away.
- 'Leave it, darling.' Andréa thinks fast: to hide why they are really at the hotel, she takes Gabriel's arm and pretends they are secret lovers.
- 'Hello. It's none of my business.' Literally 'that does not look at me'. Colette stays cool and polite.
- 'Come on!' Andréa pulls a bewildered Gabriel back towards the front desk.
- 'I'd like to see Mr Audibert, please.' Colette uses the polite conditional form 'I would like' at the desk.
- 'Our room, please.' Andréa keeps up the act right beside her.
- 'The same as usual. Thank you.' Literally 'the same as of habit'. Now they have no choice but to go upstairs. Now let's hear the whole scene again from start to finish.
