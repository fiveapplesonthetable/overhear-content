## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Who wants some good news?' In French, 'a piece of news' is a countable feminine word: literally 'a good news'.
- 'Sofia got the part!' Literally 'Sofia has had the role'. The short form meaning 'had' comes after the helping word 'has'. Together they describe a completed success.
- 'Great! In the costume drama?' Literally 'in the film in costume'.
- 'The director is smart.' The description has a masculine form because it refers to the male director. Gabriel assumes the casting decision will challenge the usual stereotypes.
- 'No, Sofia is playing the slave.' The role follows the action 'play' directly. The article before the role is shortened because the next word begins with a vowel.
- 'No! Really?' Gabriel reacts with disbelief. The second question literally asks whether it is true.
- 'Yes, she does the cleaning for the marchioness.' French combines doing with the word for housework to express cleaning. The final feminine title refers to the noblewoman Sofia's character serves.
- 'She's going to be furious with me.' The description has a feminine ending for Sofia. French uses the word meaning 'against' where English says 'furious with' someone.
- 'I'm playing the slave?' The same spoken form of 'play' works for 'I' and 'she'. Sofia turns the sentence into a question by raising her voice at the end.
- 'Yes, but with lines to say as well.' Literally 'with some text in addition'. It's a speaking part, not just an extra.
- 'I got the part?' Literally 'I have had the role?'. Only now does it sink in for Sofia that she actually passed the audition.
- 'In a film with JoeyStarr!' The short word before the actor's name means 'with'. Even without a main action word, the phrase makes clear what excites Sofia about the role.
- 'That's brilliant! Oh, Gabriel!' The describing word after 'it is' has a masculine form. It refers to the good news as a whole, rather than describing Sofia herself.
- 'Thank you! I love you, I love you!' Sofia keeps polite address even in this exuberant expression of gratitude. She repeats the same affection rather than adding a longer explanation.
