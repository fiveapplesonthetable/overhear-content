## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Back already? I invited the kids for dinner!' Literally 'already of return?'. Catherine has no idea why Mathias looks so pale.
- 'I just wanted to come home.' He uses the past tense for how he was feeling on the way back.
- 'I came to get Hippolyte's photos.' The form meaning 'come' has a feminine ending for Camille. It uses 'I am' as the helper for this completed past action.
- 'Andréa asked me to.' Literally 'Andréa asked it to me', with 'to me' and 'it' placed before the helper word.
- 'What did you study at university?' The last word is the everyday short word for university. At the table, Mathias starts grilling Camille.
- 'I did a year of sociology, then I stopped.' Sociology is shortened too in casual speech.
- 'That's a shame. Do you want to be an agent?' The expression for 'that's a shame' has no article before the word for damage or pity. It reacts to Camille giving up her studies.
- 'I'm thinking about it.' The tiny word before the verb means 'about it'.
- 'What do your parents do?' The form of 'do' is plural because it refers to both parents. In this question, doing something means their occupations.
- 'My mother is a hairdresser. She has her own salon.' Notice there is no word for 'a' before a profession.
- 'That's great! And your father?' A question can be reduced to 'and' plus the person you want to hear about. Catherine now moves from Camille's mother to her father.
- 'My father repairs boats.' The word for boats has a written plural ending with x. The action remains singular because the father, rather than the boats, is doing it.
- 'Yes, I sail a lot with my father.' The amount word can stand alone after an action. Here it means that she sails often or a great deal, without an extra word for 'of'.
- 'It was nice having you.' This polite goodbye looks back on the visit with 'it was'. The 'you' here is an object after 'have', rather than the person doing the action.
- 'I'll go! I have to talk about one of Andréa's projects.' The short place expression in the first sentence means setting off. In the next, 'have to' is followed by the unchanged action 'talk'.
- 'Andréa is the one who made me come for the photos!' French combines 'made' directly with 'come' to describe causing someone to do something. Camille points out that the visit was a work assignment.
- 'But you didn't have to stay!' Literally 'you were not obliged to stay'.
- 'Stop seeing him, okay?' Mathias gives a direct command, with 'him' right before the basic form 'to see'.
- 'I can't believe this! Tell him why yourself!' The first phrase literally says 'I'm dreaming', but expresses disbelief here. The emphatic word after 'tell him' puts the responsibility back on Mathias.
- 'We'll talk about it tomorrow.' The little word before 'talk' means 'about it'. The action has an ordinary present form; 'tomorrow' makes the future timing clear.
