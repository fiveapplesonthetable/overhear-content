## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Let go of me!' Mathias uses a casual command with 'me' attached after it. He is talking to the dog biting his trouser leg.
- 'Jean Gabin, stop it! He bites the legs of traitors!' The form meaning 'bites' is singular because it refers to the dog. The short word before traitors means 'of the', rather than 'some'.
- 'You're leaving the agency, is that it?' The little tag at the end, literally 'it's that?', asks for confirmation.
- 'I was going to tell you.' The past form of going introduces something Mathias intended to do. Two short objects before telling identify the people he would tell and the news he would give them.
- 'It's nothing personal. The agency can't go on like this.' Notice how 'nothing' takes an adjective after the little word for 'of'.
- 'I'm within my rights. I'm leaving at the end of the month.' French uses the present tense very naturally for a fixed plan in the near future.
- 'Do you sleep well at night?' The form of 'sleep' addresses one person casually. The word after it describes how he sleeps; Arlette is really questioning his conscience.
- 'One last thing. It concerns Samuel and it's important.' The feminine form of 'last' comes before the feminine word for a thing. Mathias adds a final warning before he leaves.
- 'He had a room all year round at the Hotel Saint-Georges.' The past form of 'have' describes an ongoing arrangement. The expression 'all the year' means throughout the year, not just one night.
- 'He was paying for it with the agency's money.' The word before 'was paying' means 'it', referring to the room. It is feminine because the French word for room is feminine.
- 'Colette Brancillon is going to find out. I just wanted to warn you.' Notice that here Mathias switches to the plural 'you', addressing all three partners together.
- 'Oh, Samuel, what did you do?' The question uses the helping form 'you have', followed by the past form 'done'. Arlette reacts to a past action whose consequences matter now.
