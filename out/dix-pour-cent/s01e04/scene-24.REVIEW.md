## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Let go of me!' An informal command with 'me' attached by a hyphen: you always talk to a dog with the casual 'you'.
- 'Jean Gabin, stop it! He bites traitors' legs!' Arlette calls her dog off, though she's secretly proud of him.
- 'You're leaving the agency, is that it?' The little tag at the end, literally 'it's that?', asks for confirmation.
- 'I was going to tell you.' The verb 'to go' in the past tense plus an infinitive gives 'was going to'. Both pronouns 'to you' and 'it' sit before the verb.
- 'It's nothing personal. The agency can't go on like this.' Notice how 'nothing' takes an adjective after the little word for 'of'.
- 'If you leave with your actors, we're going to court!' Andréa threatens legal action.
- 'I'm within my rights. I'm leaving at the end of the month.' French uses the present tense very naturally for a fixed plan in the near future.
- 'Do you sleep well at night?' Arlette's quiet question cuts deeper than Andréa's shouting. Before he leaves, Mathias drops a bombshell.
- 'One last thing. It concerns Samuel and it's important.' Even as he leaves, he still wants to protect the agency from the tax audit.
- 'He had a room all year round at the Hotel Saint-Georges.' Their late founder Samuel kept a secret hotel room in Paris.
- 'He was paying for it with the agency's money.' Both verbs are in the past tense for ongoing habits. And using company money for a private hotel room is illegal.
- 'Colette Brancillon is going to find out. I just wanted to warn you.' Notice that here Mathias switches to the plural 'you', addressing all three partners together.
- 'Oh, Samuel, what did you do?' Arlette sighs at her old friend's carelessness.
- 'We know the owner of the hotel. We're going to talk to him.' Andréa and Gabriel immediately decide to rush over to the Hotel Saint-Georges before the tax inspector gets there.
