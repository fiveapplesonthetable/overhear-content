## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Make a list of Mathias's actors. We have to be quick.' Andréa starts with a casual command to Camille. The second sentence uses an impersonal expression for needing to act quickly.
- 'The German is pulling out.' Arlette reports the buyer's withdrawal. The self-directed action describes pulling out of the purchase rather than removing a physical object.
- 'What?' This short question stands by itself when something is surprising or unclear. Andréa asks for confirmation of what she has just heard.
- 'He heard that Mathias is joining StarMédia.' The completed action of learning uses a past helping word. The following statement gives the news that reached the buyer.
- 'He says that without him, the agency is going to lose too much value.' After the preposition meaning without, French uses an independent form for him. Mathias's absence would make the agency less valuable to the buyer.
- 'Are you sure about it?' The short object before being sure refers to the news. The feminine ending on sure matches Arlette, whom Andréa is asking.
- 'She's furious with Mathias.' The describing word has a feminine ending for Hélène. The preposition following it identifies the person her anger is directed at.
- 'A generous act from Mathias? What a joke!' The word for act means a deed here. The final question-shaped exclamation uses a feminine form before the word for joke to express disbelief.
- 'Maybe not generous, but he's a strategist.' Arlette concedes that generosity may not explain the result, then offers a different description. The final word means someone who plans moves carefully.
- 'He's pulling out?' The same withdrawal expression becomes a question through the rising voice. Mathias is hearing the news on his phone as he arrives.
- 'No, I didn't tell Mr Hirsch that I was joining StarMédia.' The negative surrounds the past helping word. The final action puts the planned move in the past as something he supposedly told the buyer.
- 'I don't know how he found out. Hélène, calm down.' The two short words before learned combine an object referring to the news and the helping word for the past. Mathias ends with a polite self-directed command to calm down.
- 'I'll call you back.' The polite object refers to Hélène as the person he will call again. A present form can describe the immediate plan to call back in this context.
