## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Augustin had chosen another actress. It was horrible.' Both Audrey and Mathias use the casual 'you' because they have worked together for years.
- 'Actors always take someone else's role.' The verb 'to take' is in its plural form, and 'always' sits right after it.
- 'Did you take a good look at Armelle? She's going to be fantastic!' First the past with 'have', then the near future with 'is going to be'.
- 'So you don't need money, after all.' In French, 'to need' is built with 'to have': literally 'to have need of'. And after a negative, 'of' loses its article.
- 'You have a horrible view of life. She moved me.' The last verb literally means 'touched', and takes a feminine ending because 'me' is Audrey.
- 'It's not because of that! You don't want to leave your children.' Mathias sees the real reason behind her generosity.
- 'Nonsense!' A very common three-word expression that literally means 'no matter what', used whenever someone says something ridiculous.
- 'Your children are going to grow up and leave.' The near future again: 'are going to' followed by two verbs.
- 'I want to work, but they're small!' Now Audrey admits the truth.
- 'They need me. So what do I do?' There's that 'have need of' expression again, this time in the third person plural.
- 'Do I abandon them for six months? You didn't tell me about Canada!' The little word for 'them' goes before the verb. With a masculine country name like Canada, 'about' combines with 'the'.
- 'I didn't know.' Mathias claims he didn't know about the Canadian shoot either.
- 'And it's impossible to work with a baby everywhere.' He complains that bringing a baby to a film set never works.
- 'You owe a lot of money in taxes. But I wish you good luck!' He leaves her on the pavement and heads into the metro.
- 'Good day!' As the train doors close between them, they throw the word for 'hello' at each other sarcastically instead of 'goodbye'.
