## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Are you sure that your parents aren't here?' The word for 'sure' is masculine because she is asking Hippolyte.
- 'No, it's fine. I'll go and get the photos.' Literally 'I am going to look for the photos'.
- 'These are the best ones.' The plural form of 'it is' introduces a plural noun.
- 'The one with the bike is good. Where is that?' The first word means 'the one', feminine to match 'photo'.
- 'At my grandmother's, in Burgundy.' French has a special preposition that means 'at the home of'.
- 'Why do you ask me so many questions about my family?' In French you don't 'ask' a question, you 'put' it.
- 'I like hearing about families.' Literally 'hearing talk of families'. Camille is secretly curious about the family she never had.
- 'And I like you.' Literally 'you please to me'. Then he leans in to kiss her, and Camille panics.
- 'I have a lot of work. I have to go.' She jumps up to leave.
- 'Camille, what is the problem?' Hippolyte doesn't understand why she always pulls away.
- 'It's not you, I swear to you.' She can't tell him that they have the same father.
- 'How do you know? I feel something special between us.' In truth, what they have between them is a family resemblance, and just then his mother Catherine walks in.
- 'It's Camille! Hello, Camille.' Catherine met her briefly at the agency and is delighted to see her again.
- 'Are you staying for dinner?' Literally 'you stay to dine?'
- 'Thank you, but I have to go home.' Camille tries to escape before Mathias gets back.
- 'Excellent idea! Your dad is at a screening.' Hippolyte thinks his father is out for the evening.
- 'Don't leave me alone! Do it for me!' Two warm commands from Catherine.
- 'Do you like chicken, Camille?' And before she knows it, Camille is staying for dinner at her father's house.
