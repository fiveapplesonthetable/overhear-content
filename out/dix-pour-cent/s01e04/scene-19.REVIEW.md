## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Right, I'm leaving.' Literally 'I go away from here': a very common everyday way to say you are heading off.
- 'Enjoy the screening.' Noémie gives a polite command using the formal 'you', which she always uses with Mathias at the office.
- 'I really don't feel like it. See you tomorrow.' Literally 'I really don't have the desire of it'. That is why Mathias is about to go straight home instead.
- 'Yes, Mathias.' Noémie uses a plain affirmative reply to acknowledge him. The name follows it as direct address, so a small pause separates the two.
- 'He forgot his phone. Mathias!' The action word 'to forget' in the everyday past. She runs after him to give it back.
- 'Contract sent by email. A new adventure begins.' A message can omit a full helping verb before 'sent'. The next sentence uses an ordinary present form: an adventure 'begins'.
- 'What? Oh, thanks. See you tomorrow, Noémie.' A short reaction, thanks and a farewell can make a whole exchange. The farewell points to the next day with the expression literally meaning 'until tomorrow'.
- 'Noémie? What's wrong?' The long question expression literally asks 'what is there?' In this context it asks what has happened or what the matter is.
- 'I don't understand. It's Mathias.' The negative wraps around the verb 'understand'.
- 'He didn't even ask me to come with him.' The word for 'even' strengthens the negative. After 'asked me', a short linking word introduces the next action, 'come'.
- 'He didn't even ask me to come with him.' Notice the word for 'even' placed right after the negative word.
- 'He's abandoning me.' The little word for 'me' shortens before the vowel of the verb.
- 'Of course not, Noémie.' Literally 'but no'. This small response can gently reject a person's worried conclusion rather than argue with a fact.
- 'I want to die, Hervé.' The form of 'want' is followed directly by the unchanged action 'to die'. No extra linking word is needed between those two actions.
- 'I want to die, Hervé.' Poor Noémie is always dramatic when it comes to Mathias.
