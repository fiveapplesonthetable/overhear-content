## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Are you looking for something?' In French, the verb 'to look for' has the 'for' built right into it, with no extra word needed.
- 'Yes, some medicine. I'm in pain.' French expresses pain with 'I have', followed by the word for pain. She has not said which part of her body hurts in this short reply.
- 'I did a rock-climbing course this weekend.' The word for a short training course or workshop is the same word used for an internship.
- 'Did you like it?' Hear the little helping word for 'have', followed by the past form of 'like'. Together they ask about her completed weekend activity.
- 'Yes, a lot! But I'm a city girl.' Literally 'a girl of the city'.
- 'And the harness really hurts!' This time the thing causing pain is the subject. French uses 'makes pain' to say that it hurts. The rope passes through the climbing harness.
- 'Hervé, on the Internet, this actress is ten years older!' Literally 'has ten years of more'. Remember that French uses 'to have' for age.
- 'Just correct the age.' Literally 'correct the age, all simply'. Hervé doesn't even look up.
- 'I can take off another five years!' Here the word often translated 'still' adds an extra amount: another five years. Camille jokes about making the actress younger on paper.
- 'Hello? Yes, thank you! Kiss-kiss!' The first greeting belongs to telephone calls. The repeated farewell is familiar and affectionate, which surprises Noémie in a professional conversation.
- 'Do you say kiss-kiss to a big star?' Notice that Noémie still uses the formal 'you' with Camille when she's scolding her.
- 'No!' Camille tries to deny it.
- 'Yes, you did!' There's that special French 'yes' again, used to contradict a negative statement.
- 'It's just a habit.' The short word after 'it is' means 'just' or 'only'. Camille uses it to make the offence sound small.
- 'You can't do whatever you want!' She now switches from polite 'you' to casual 'you' as she scolds Camille. The words after 'do' mean 'everything that', or 'whatever' here.
