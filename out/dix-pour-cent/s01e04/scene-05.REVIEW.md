## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hervé, on the Internet, this actress is ten years older!' French expresses age with 'has' and a number of years. Camille means the age shown online has been increased, not that the woman has suddenly aged.
- 'What do I do?' This everyday question leaves the question word at the end. It is a short way to ask someone what action you should take.
- 'Just correct the age.' A casual command starts directly with the action 'correct'. The two words at the end literally mean 'all simply', a common expression for 'just' or 'simply'.
- 'Okay.' The brief agreement means she accepts Hervé's instruction. It can stand alone without another action or a subject.
- 'I can take off another five years!' The word often translated 'still' adds an extra amount here: another five years. Camille jokes about making the actress even younger online.
- 'Eight might be too much.' The form of 'be' means 'would be', making this a cautious comment. The word meaning 'perhaps' adds another note of uncertainty.
- 'Hello? Is this about the product placement?' Hervé answers a separate phone call. The expression at the end means showing a branded product inside a film or programme.
- 'Kiss-kiss!' Camille ends her own call with a familiar, affectionate farewell. Its repeated form is cheerful, but Noémie thinks it is too casual for business.
- 'Do you say kiss-kiss to a big star?' Noémie uses polite 'you' to address Camille while scolding her. The describing word for 'big' is feminine to match the word for star.
- 'No!' Camille denies it with a one-word answer. Noémie immediately challenges that denial.
- 'Yes, you did!' This kind of 'yes' contradicts a negative statement. French distinguishes it from the ordinary affirmative answer.
- 'It's just a habit.' The word after 'it is' means 'just' or 'only'. Camille makes her behaviour sound harmless and ordinary.
- 'Noémie, phone call.' Hervé uses just her name and the word for telephone. In an office, that short announcement is enough to say there is a caller for someone.
- 'Just a second.' Literally 'one second'. This short time expression asks someone to wait briefly, without a complete sentence.
- 'You can't do whatever you want!' Noémie switches to casual 'you' in her reprimand. The expression after 'do' means 'everything that', or 'whatever' here. She still wrongly believes Camille is Christophe Lambert's daughter.
