## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'And Sofia Leprince? Too exotic?' The word before 'exotic' means 'too' or 'too much'.
- 'Do you mean too black?' Literally 'you want to say too black?'. That's how French says 'you mean'. Notice the feminine ending on the colour word, for Sofia.
- 'And not exotic enough for the part of the nanny?' The two words before 'exotic' mean 'not enough'. Then the casting director says the opposite.
- 'She's too white? But she has talent!' French describes talent with 'she has', just like English. Gabriel contrasts the casting director's complaints with the quality he thinks should count.
- 'And for the part of the student?' He tries one last character in the script, using the feminine word for a university student.
- 'No? Okay, thank you.' The short expression in the middle means 'agreed' or 'okay'. It can politely close a phone call even when the result is disappointing.
- 'Sofia, everyone thinks you're fantastic.' Literally 'everyone finds you fantastic'. The short word for 'you' goes before 'finds'. With the expression for 'everyone', the action stays in the singular.
- 'Fantastic? Then why don't I get a part?' After a negative verb, 'a part' becomes 'any part'.
- 'You're just starting out. Rejections are normal.' Literally 'the refusals, it's normal'.
- 'Three failed auditions!' Gabriel repeats Sofia's disappointing total. The last word is the past form of an action meaning to miss or fail; its feminine plural ending matches auditions.
- 'Do you know how many auditions Cécile de France failed?' The two-word pattern after 'know' asks 'how many'. It comes directly before the thing being counted: auditions.
- 'Three.' Sofia offers the same number as her own unsuccessful auditions. A number can answer a counting question without repeating the action or the thing being counted.
- 'Three?' Gabriel repeats the number as a question. Repeating a short word with a rising voice can express surprise or uncertainty, rather than confirm a fact.

Audio confirms Gabriel addresses Sofia politely, Sofia offers Three, and Gabriel repeats Three as a question. The script does not present a documented count of Cécile’s rejections.
