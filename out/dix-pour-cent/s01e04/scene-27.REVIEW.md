## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Will you deal with it, Mathias?' Andréa asks her colleague to handle the scandal. The self-directed action is followed by a short object referring to the matter that needs attention.
- 'Mathias! He had promised me secrecy.' The helping form 'had' puts the promise before this conversation. The object before it means 'to me', showing who received the promise.
- 'If Kadropov goes out with a beautiful woman, he tells everyone!' The feminine describing word goes before 'woman'. Mathias includes Audrey in that compliment, and she immediately picks up on it.
- 'Do you think I'm beautiful?' Literally 'do you find me beautiful?'. French uses 'to find' for 'to think someone is'.
- 'Yes, you're very beautiful.' The describing word is feminine for Audrey. The word for 'very' strengthens it without changing to match her.
- 'That's so kind.' The word before 'kind' means 'so' or 'so much'. Audrey starts to cry.
- 'I'm tired, Mathias. I can't take it anymore.' This second sentence is a fixed expression: literally 'I cannot of it any more'.
- 'Mummy isn't really crying, sweetheart.' Her little boy walks into the room, and she calls him 'my heart'.
- 'I'm rehearsing a part. Go and play in your room.' The verb for 'to repeat' also means 'to rehearse' for an actor.
- 'I love them so much.' The short object before 'love' is plural and means 'them'. It refers to her children, rather than being an article before the name of a thing.
- 'I am there for them, but it is never enough.' Audrey describes being available for her children. The independent word for them follows the preposition; the negative ending expresses that her effort never feels sufficient.
- 'No, it's never enough.' The second part of the negative is 'never', so it does not need the ordinary word for 'not' as well. Mathias repeats the same pattern Audrey used.
- 'How do you manage to be a good father?' Audrey asks Mathias about his own parenting. The question combines how with doing something in order to reach a goal; his answer repeats the description of a good father negatively.
- 'I'm not a good father.' The negative surrounds 'am', but the article before 'good father' stays. With 'to be', a negative description can still say 'not a good father'.
