## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Clémentine! What are you doing here?' The long question phrase after Clémentine's name is the everyday spoken way to ask 'what'.
- 'I have to do this in order to heal.' The first verb means 'must' or 'have to', and the preposition before 'heal' means 'in order to'.
- 'Okay, but another time!' Andréa desperately wants to get rid of her before Colette hears too much.
- 'You're a monster of seduction.' Clémentine is an actress, and she delivers her speech as if she were on stage.
- 'Clémentine, listen.' A casual command for one person, with no pronoun.
- 'You devoured me like a hyena!' The past participle has an extra feminine letter at the end because the object word before the auxiliary refers to Clémentine.
- 'That's ridiculous. It didn't last a month!' The two-part negative wraps around the auxiliary verb in the past.
- 'I'm talking about feelings! I had feelings for you.' The second verb is in the past form used for how things used to be.
- 'And you asked your assistant to break up with me!' Listeners who remember the first episode know that this is completely true: Andréa made poor Camille do it.
- 'No, that's not true!' Literally 'it is false', with Colette standing right there listening to every word.
- 'You're rotten! But it's over.' Clémentine's grand tragedy reaches its climax.
- 'I forgive you.' The little object word for 'you' sits right in front of the verb. Having delivered her big forgiveness line, Clémentine stalks away.
- 'You need a new doctor, you're crazy!' Literally 'you have need of a new doctor', and the adjective at the end is the feminine form of 'crazy'.
- 'People are crazy, aren't they?' Literally 'people are sick, no?'. Andréa turns to Colette with an awkward smile, but Colette simply bids Ms Martel a polite goodbye and walks off.
- 'Yes? Hello?' Saved by the bell, Andréa answers her ringing phone.
