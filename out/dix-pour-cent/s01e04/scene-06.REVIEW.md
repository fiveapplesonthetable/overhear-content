## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Sweetheart, do it for Mummy.' Notice the hyphenated command at the start: 'do it'. Then she turns back to Mathias.
- 'Tell me about this Augustin. What's his surname?' Literally 'his name of family'.
- 'He's just called Augustin. He's a web star.' The word for 'just' means 'simply' here: he uses only the one name. The last two little sounds join 'of' and 'the' before 'web'.
- 'I don't know anything about all that. I even turned off the Wi-Fi!' Literally 'I have even cut the Wi-Fi'.
- 'It's bad for young brains.' Hear the word meaning 'for' between 'bad' and 'brains'. The same pattern lets you say that something is good or bad for a person or a thing.
- 'Look, this is the best of his videos.' The word for 'the best' has a feminine ending here to match 'video'.
- 'Mathias, he's twelve years old!' Audrey exaggerates how young he looks. Age uses 'he has' in French, followed by the number and the word for years.
- 'He's young, but very talented. Like Xavier Dolan.' The short word before the director's name means 'like'. It makes a comparison, rather than saying that the two men are the same person.
- 'Really? Sweetheart, do you want a fruit compote?' The form meaning 'you want' belongs to a casual conversation with one person. Audrey uses it with her daughter as she offers food.
- 'It's the story of an ecologist stripper. Very funny, very modern.' Notice the short casual word for 'ecologist' or 'green'.
- 'It's the perfect film for your comeback.' The word for 'return' also means an actor's comeback.
- 'I love the subject. But what's my part?' The possessive before 'part' means 'my'. It follows the masculine word for a role, even though Audrey is a woman.
- 'The ecologist stripper.' A short answer can repeat just the role, without a whole sentence. Both words describe a woman here; the second is the informal word for being green or environmentally minded.
- 'What? But does he know I had two children recently?' She uses the same ordinary past construction as before: a helping form of 'have', then the past form meaning 'had'. The last word places it in the recent past.
- 'That doesn't matter at all!' Literally 'that has no importance'. The feminine form of 'no' matches the feminine word for importance. This is a stronger dismissal than simply saying it is not very important.
