## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'ASK, hello. Yes, I'll put you through to her.' Literally 'I pass her to you', with both short words stacked right before the action word.
- 'Gabriel! Here are my new photos.' The word for 'new' is in its feminine plural form to match 'photos', and stands before the noun.
- 'They're in black and white.' The word for 'they' is the feminine plural form because it refers to the photos. The colour words here describe the kind of image, rather than the photos themselves.
- 'Do you think they're good?' The question keeps ordinary statement order. The rising voice and question mark turn 'you think' into 'do you think'.
- 'Yes, very good. Your photos are good.' The feminine plural describing word agrees with the photos. The possessive before photos is the plural form used when addressing someone politely.
- 'Really?' One word, asking if he truly means it.
- 'You're photogenic.' This describing word has the same form for a man or a woman. Gabriel is saying that Sofia looks good on camera.
- 'I like the look in your eyes, and the smile too.' French uses 'the eyes' where English says 'your eyes'.
- 'Well, you're beautiful.' Gabriel keeps polite address as he compliments Sofia. The describing word has a feminine ending because he is speaking about her.
- 'It's simple and natural.' Both adjectives stay in the masculine singular after 'it is'.
- 'You have grace. Yes, that's it, grace.' The two words before 'grace' mean 'some', used before an abstract quality.
- 'Thank you, Gabriel!' Sofia is glowing as the lift doors open.
- 'Right, back to work!' Literally 'to the work'. Gabriel hurries into the lift, where Mathias is waiting.
- 'And after Sofia, are you taking on the girl from the bakery too?' The action word normally means 'take'. For an agent, taking someone means adding them as a client.
- 'She'll be on the cover of a big magazine!' Literally 'she will make the cover', using the future tense of 'to make'.
- 'You like her, don't you?' Adding the word meaning 'well' after 'love' makes the everyday expression for liking someone. The final 'no?' asks him to confirm Mathias's suspicion.
- 'Never with my clients!' The final word has a feminine ending because he means female clients. Its spoken ending differs from the masculine form: listen for the clear final consonant.
