## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'ASK, hello. Yes, I'll put you through to her.' Literally 'I pass her to you', with both pronouns stacked before the verb.
- 'Gabriel! Here are my new photos.' The word for 'new' is in its feminine plural form to match 'photos', and stands before the noun.
- 'They're in black and white.' Literally 'in black and white', the same order as in English.
- 'Do you think they're good?' Sofia is nervous: she worries you can see her gums too much.
- 'Yes, very good. Your photos are good.' Gabriel reassures her right away.
- 'Really?' One word, asking if he truly means it.
- 'You're photogenic.' The camera loves her, just as he saw on stage at the theatre.
- 'I like the look in your eyes, and the smile too.' French uses 'the eyes' where English says 'your eyes'.
- 'Well, you're beautiful.' Notice how 'well' sets up the clause and leads naturally into 'beautiful'.
- 'It's simple and natural.' Both adjectives stay in the masculine singular after 'it is'.
- 'You have grace. Yes, that's it, grace.' The two words before 'grace' mean 'some', used before an abstract quality.
- 'Thank you, Gabriel!' Sofia is glowing as the lift doors open.
- 'Right, back to work!' Literally 'to the work'. Gabriel hurries into the lift, where Andréa is waiting.
- 'And after Sofia, are you taking on the girl from the bakery too?' Andréa mocks him for signing the agency's receptionist.
- 'She'll be on the cover of a big magazine!' Literally 'she will make the cover', using the future tense of 'to make'.
- 'You're in love with her, aren't you?' In the show Andréa puts it much more bluntly.
- 'Never with my clients!' Notice the feminine plural ending on 'clients': Gabriel has a strict rule. Let's listen to the whole scene once more.
