## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'For the dance, I know that you like tango.' Literally 'the dance at the bar'. He uses the polite 'you' with Audrey.
- 'No, I also know how to do a bit of pole dancing.' To say you know how to do something in French, you put 'know' straight before the verb 'to do'.
- 'Really? Do you really know how to pole dance?' Augustin can hardly believe his luck.
- 'Yes. I know the basics.' Notice that French uses a different verb for 'know' when it's followed by a noun instead of a verb.
- 'That's more than enough. Fantastic!' Literally 'widely sufficient'.
- 'I like to work with what's real.' Literally 'with the true'.
- 'In the film, it's a gesture of rebellion.' Augustin launches into his big artistic vision for the character.
- 'She looks like Isabelle Adjani.' The verb for 'resembles' takes the linking word 'to' before the person's name.
- 'Lock the door!' Literally 'close with a key'. Audrey has decided to prove right here and now that she can dance.
- 'There is no key.' The negative 'there is not' is followed by 'of', meaning 'no key at all'.
- 'Hold the door closed and draw the curtains.' Two polite commands, because she doesn't want the whole agency watching through the glass.
- 'Audrey, are you okay?' Mathias has no idea what his client is about to do.
- 'You're not angry? It's just an idea.' Augustin is worried he offended her with his script.
- 'Can you give us five minutes?' Outside, Arlette tries to open the door, and Mathias leans against it to keep her out.
- 'Ten minutes? No problem.' Meanwhile, Audrey uses the office coat stand as a dance pole.
- 'Magnificent!' Augustin is completely won over: the part is hers. Now let's hear the whole scene in Mathias's office again.
