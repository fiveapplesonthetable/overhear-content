## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'As for pole dancing, I know you do tango.' The expression for pole dancing literally says 'dance at the pole'. To describe the dance she practises, he uses 'you do', rather than the word meaning 'you like'.
- 'No, I also know how to do a bit of pole dancing.' To say you know how to do something in French, you put 'know' straight before the verb 'to do'.
- 'Really? Do you really know how to pole dance?' The polite form of 'know' comes before 'do'. The word for 'really' strengthens his surprised question.
- 'Yes. I know the basics.' Notice that French uses a different verb for 'know' when it's followed by a noun instead of a verb.
- 'That's more than enough. Fantastic!' Literally 'widely sufficient'.
- 'I like to work with what's real.' Literally 'with the true'.
- 'In the film, it's a gesture of rebellion.' The short word between 'gesture' and 'rebellion' means 'of'. It links an action to the attitude that action expresses.
- 'She looks like Isabelle Adjani.' The verb for 'resembles' takes the preposition 'to' before the person's name.
- 'Lock the door!' Literally 'close with a key'. Audrey has decided to prove right here and now that she can dance.
- 'There is no key.' The negative 'there is not' is followed by 'of', meaning 'no key at all'.
- 'Hold the door closed and draw the curtains.' Audrey gives two polite commands, because she doesn't want the whole agency watching through the glass.
- 'Audrey, are you okay?' The everyday question literally means 'is it going?' Here the rising voice makes it a concerned check on how Audrey feels.
- 'You're not angry? It's just an idea.' The word meaning 'angry' has a feminine ending for Audrey. The second sentence tries to make the suggestion sound smaller and less serious.
- 'Can you give us five minutes?' Literally 'can you leave us five minutes?' The short word before 'leave' means 'us', so Mathias is asking for time for himself and Audrey.
- 'Ten minutes? No problem.' The reply leaves out a full verb: 'no problem' is enough. The little word before 'problem' is the reduced article used after a negative.
- 'Magnificent!' This describing word can be used by itself as enthusiastic praise. Its form is the same whether the thing being praised is masculine or feminine.
