## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'As for pole dancing, I know you do tango.' The expression for pole dancing literally says 'dance at the pole'. To describe the dance she practises, he uses 'you do', rather than the word meaning 'you like'.
- 'No, I also know how to do a bit of pole dancing.' To say you know how to do something in French, you put 'know' straight before the verb 'to do'.
- 'Really? Do you really know how to pole dance?' The polite form of 'know' comes before 'do'. The word for 'really' strengthens his surprised question.
- 'Yes. I know the basics.' Notice that French uses a different verb for 'know' when it's followed by a noun instead of a verb.
- 'That's more than enough. Fantastic!' Literally 'widely sufficient'.
- 'I like to work with what's real.' Literally 'with the true'.
- 'In the film, it's a gesture of rebellion.' The short word between 'gesture' and 'rebellion' means 'of'. It links an action to the attitude that action expresses.
- 'She looks like Isabelle Adjani.' The verb for 'resembles' takes the preposition 'to' before the person's name.
- 'Lock the door!' Literally 'close with a key'. After leaving the meeting, Audrey asks for privacy in another room so that she can breastfeed her baby.
- 'Augustin mustn't see me!' The word for 'must' is followed directly by the action 'see'. The short object meaning 'me' comes before that second action. Audrey wants privacy to feed her baby.
- 'Hold the door closed and draw the curtains.' These are two commands to the people helping her. The word for 'closed' has a feminine ending to match 'door', while 'curtains' is plural.
- 'You're not angry? It's just an idea.' The word meaning 'angry' has a feminine ending for Audrey. Augustin reduces the force of his suggestion with the small word meaning 'just'.
