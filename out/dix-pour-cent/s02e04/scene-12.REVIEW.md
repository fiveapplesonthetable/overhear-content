## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'This way, miss, please.' Gabriel politely guides Eugenie toward the office. The polite formula 's'il vous plait' concludes the directional invitation 'par ici'.
- 'Thank you very much. Hello.' Eugenie replies courteously. The polite opening greeting is preceded by warm thanks.
- 'What did you say to her, Noemie?' Gabriel questions Noemie's suspicious interference. The interrogative 'qu'est-ce que' introduces a question in the passe compose.
- 'I gave her a little tip to succeed.' Noemie innocently disguises her sabotage. The preposition 'pour' takes the infinitive 'reussir' to state the intended goal.
- 'What kind of tip?' Gabriel presses for clarification. The interrogative adjective 'quel' agrees with the masculine noun 'genre'.
- 'I told her that the real boss here was Arlette Azemar.' Noemie reveals her fabricated hierarchy. The superlative adjective 'vraie' emphasizes the supposed true leader of ASK.
- 'And that she was the one who had to be convinced.' Noemie explains her misleading instruction. The impersonal imperfect 'il fallait' expresses an obligation in the past.
- 'Arlette? But she is not the one hiring!' Gabriel protests the absurd misdirection. The verb 'recruter' means to hire or recruit new personnel.
- 'I told her to go for it, to speak very loudly.' Noemie describes her outrageous delivery advice. The idiom 'y aller' encourages bold action, paired with 'parler tres fort'.
- 'And why speak very loudly?' Gabriel asks with growing disbelief. The interrogative adverb 'pourquoi' questions the reason for speaking loudly.
- 'Because she is deaf.' Noemie delivers the punchline of her prank. The causal conjunction 'parce que' introduces the explanation with the adjective 'sourde'.
- 'But Arlette is not deaf at all!' Gabriel points out the obvious flaw. The negative locution 'pas ... du tout' expresses complete negation.
- 'Hello, ma'am!' Eugenie shouts enthusiastically across the reception area. The title 'madame' is used here to address the senior agent with utmost respect.
- 'So happy to meet you!' Eugenie bellows her eager admiration. The feminine adjective 'heureuse' expresses joy, intensified by 'tellement'.
- 'Working alongside you is a dream for any young agent!' Eugenie yells her flattery loudly. The prepositional phrase 'a vos cotes' means by your side or alongside you.
- 'There, her career at ASK is off to a great start.' Noemie watches the unfolding chaos with satisfaction. The presentative 'voila' marks a humorous summary of the situation.
