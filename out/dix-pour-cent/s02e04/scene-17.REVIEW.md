## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Here, Benoît. Here is your coffee.' Noémie hands him a warm beverage. The interjection tiens is often used when offering something.
- 'Thanks. Do you work here?' Benoît starts polite small talk. The adverb ici indicates the agency premises.
- 'Yes, I am an assistant.' Noémie confirms her administrative role. The feminine noun assistante takes no article after être.
- 'You are here for the assistant job?' She inquires about his presence. The noun phrase le poste d'assistant means the assistant position.
- 'Yes, I am having an interview.' Benoît explains his purpose. The expression passer un entretien means to attend a job interview.
- 'It is to work with Gérard Depardieu.' Noémie drops the intimidating name. The prepositional infinitive pour travailler indicates intended purpose.
- 'With Depardieu? Is he difficult?' Benoît asks nervously. The adjective difficile describes an exacting personality.
- 'Let's say he is quite extreme.' Noémie uses a classic euphemism. The adjective entier means uncompromising or intense.
- 'Do you have good health insurance?' She raises medical concerns immediately. The feminine noun mutuelle refers to complementary healthcare coverage.
- 'Standard insurance. Why?' Benoît becomes alarmed. The question word pourquoi inquires about her motive.
- 'In case of back fractures.' Noémie lists physical hazards casually. The prepositional locution en cas de means in case of.
- 'Gérard is a real heavyweight.' She emphasizes his imposing stature. The compound noun poids lourd literally means heavy vehicle or boxer.
- 'Have you ever been to Russia?' She asks about international travel. The adverb déjà means already or ever in questions.
- 'No, I have never been there.' Benoît admits his lack of experience. The adverbial pronoun y replaces the destination en Russie.
- 'Over there, they play Russian roulette.' She spins a terrifying tale. The noun phrase roulette russe refers to the deadly game.
- 'I think that I will leave.' Benoît decides to flee the agency. The immediate future vais partir combines aller with partir.
