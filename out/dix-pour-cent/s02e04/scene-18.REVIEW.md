## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'What do you mean, Isabelle is unavailable?' Sacha reacts with disbelief and irritation. The phrase comment ça expresses shock or surprise.
- 'She has a fashion show and costume fittings.' Andréa explains the packed schedule. The noun défilé refers to a fashion runway show.
- 'I stayed in Paris specially for her!' Sacha stresses his personal sacrifice. The adverb exprès means deliberately or on purpose.
- 'She charms me, then she snubs me.' Sacha complains about erratic behavior. The adverb puis marks chronological succession.
- 'Sacha, do not be so paranoid.' Andréa tries to calm his growing suspicion. The formal imperative ne soyez pas expresses gentle advice.
- 'I do not need Adjani for my movie.' Sacha defensively claims he can manage without her. The verbal expression avoir besoin de means to need.
- 'There are other great actresses.' Sacha insists that alternatives exist. The plural adjective autres precedes the noun.
- 'Wait, I am going to call her right away.' Andréa takes matters into her own hands. The locution tout de suite means immediately.
- 'Hello Isabelle? It is Andréa Martel.' Andréa greets the star politely on the phone. The interjection allô is standard when starting phone calls.
- 'Sacha Hartman would really like to see you.' Andréa presents the director's interest smoothly. The conditional aimerait expresses a polite wish.
- 'Would you be free tomorrow morning at the office?' She suggests a convenient meeting time. The conditional seriez-vous politely asks about availability.
- 'Perfect, see you tomorrow at eleven o'clock!' Andréa confirms the agreed time enthusiastically. The noun rendez-vous designates an appointment.
- 'You see, Sacha? It is settled.' Andréa shows him that his fears were unfounded. The phrase c'est réglé means it is taken care of.
- 'She really agreed to see me?' Sacha is pleasantly stunned by the outcome. The adverb vraiment emphasizes genuine surprise.
- 'Yes, she has been trying to reach you for two days.' Andréa reveals Isabelle's true interest. The preposition depuis denotes an action continuing from the past.
- 'Alright, then I will be there tomorrow.' Sacha commits to attending the appointment. The simple future serai expresses a firm promise.
