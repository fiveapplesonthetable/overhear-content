## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I received a message from Julien Doré.' Gabriel announces the unexpected text. The past tense ai reçu comes from the irregular verb recevoir.
- 'What does he say in his message?' Sofia inquires eagerly. The question phrase qu'est-ce qu'il dit asks what he is saying.
- 'He thinks that the songs are not suitable.' Gabriel shares the initial disappointment. The verb conviennent expresses appropriateness.
- 'That is a pity, I really liked this project.' Sofia expresses her regret. The expression c'est dommage means that is a pity.
- 'Wait, there is a second sentence!' Gabriel reads further with surprise. The ordinal number deuxième precedes the noun phrase.
- 'He wants you to act in his movie.' Gabriel announces the sudden proposal. The subjunctive form joues follows the expression of will.
- 'He wants me to be the lead actress?' Sofia is astonished by the opportunity. The subjunctive sois comes from the irregular verb être.
- 'Yes, and that you compose the music too.' Gabriel mentions the soundtrack request. The adverb aussi adds supplementary information.
- 'But Charlotte Le Bon was supposed to have this role!' Sofia remembers the original casting choice. The imperfect devait indicates prior intention.
- 'I will call him to check this proposal.' Gabriel seeks immediate clarification. The preposition pour expresses purpose before an infinitive.
- 'Julien confirms to me that the role is for you.' Gabriel delivers the definitive confirmation. The present tense me confirme shows direct affirmation.
- 'He says that you have immense talent.' Julien's praise is relayed enthusiastically. The adjective immense emphasizes great artistic skill.
- 'It is an incredible opportunity for my career.' Sofia realizes the significance of this break. The feminine noun opportunité takes the adjective incroyable.
- 'You worked hard to get here.' Gabriel acknowledges her dedication and perseverance. The compound past as travaillé indicates completed effort.
- 'You fully deserve this lead role.' Gabriel offers heartfelt encouragement. The adverb pleinement reinforces the verb mériter.
- 'Gabriel, we must celebrate this news!' Sofia is ready to celebrate the achievement. The verb fêter means to celebrate a happy event.
