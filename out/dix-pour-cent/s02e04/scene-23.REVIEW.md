## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Are you doing well, my dear Gabriel?' Arlette gently checks in on her troubled colleague. The friendly address mon cher expresses fond regard.
- 'It is great for Sofia's movie.' Gabriel acknowledges the artistic breakthrough for his partner. The preposition pour links the positive sentiment to the film.
- 'Since their meeting, Sofia is radiant.' Arlette notices the actress's vibrant artistic energy. The adjective rayonnante describes someone beaming with happiness.
- 'I think that I am jealous!' Gabriel candidly confesses his private insecurity to the veteran agent. The phrase je crois que introduces an honest admission.
- 'I am no longer so sure.' He second-guesses the wisdom of setting up the collaboration. The negative locution ne ... plus si sur expresses rising doubt.
- 'The script is a bit vulgar.' He rationalizes his personal jealousy by criticizing the screenplay. The phrase un peu softens the evaluative adjective vulgaire.
- 'You know this business very well.' Arlette reminds him of the realities of the film industry. The adverb tres bien modifies the verb connais.
- 'We sell desire in cinema.' The senior agent explains that dramatic art thrives on emotional chemistry. The verb vend comes from the regular verb vendre.
- 'Julien likes pretty women a lot.' Gabriel voices his anxiety about the charismatic musician's reputation. The adverbial expression aime beaucoup describes strong attraction.
- 'I do not want any trouble.' The protective agent fears emotional heartbreak in his personal life. The partitive d'histoire colloquially means trouble or drama.
- 'There must be desire between artists.' Arlette insists that creative passion requires genuine mutual attraction. The impersonal verb il faut states a necessary condition.
- 'Let her live her own life.' She counsels Gabriel to avoid suffocating his talented girlfriend. The imperative laisse-la urges him to give her space.
- 'Trust her above all.' The veteran agent offers timeless wisdom on loving an artist. The idiom faire confiance a someone means to place trust in them.
- 'She will love you even more that way.' Arlette reassures him that respecting Sofia's independence strengthens love. The simple future aimera expresses confident reassurance.
