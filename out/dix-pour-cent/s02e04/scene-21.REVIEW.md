## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Send in Miss Leopoldine Schneck!' Hicham calls in the job applicant for her interview. The imperative faites entrer means to show someone in.
- 'Hello, tell us a bit about yourself.' Gabriel invites the candidate to summarize her background. The expression parler de means to talk about.
- 'I studied cinema in Paris.' Leopoldine describes her academic credentials to the partners. The compound past ai etudie uses the auxiliary avoir.
- 'I worked for an events agency.' She shares her prior practical work experience in media. The adjective evenementielle describes event planning.
- 'Did you do a lot of music?' Hicham asks about her specific projects in the entertainment sector. The adverb beaucoup de expresses quantity.
- 'Yes, especially for concerts.' She confirms her experience with musical artists and live shows. The adverb surtout means especially or mostly.
- 'I organized circus performances.' Leopoldine explains another unusual facet of her professional background. The plural noun spectacles refers to live shows or performances.
- 'Do you know our actors well?' Gabriel inquires about her familiarity with agency clients. The irregular verb connaissez comes from connaitre.
- 'I love all of ASK's artists.' She expresses genuine enthusiasm for the talent agency roster. The verb adorer expresses strong affection or love.
- 'You have lots of positive energy.' Hicham praises her dynamic personality and lively presence. The noun energie agrees with the feminine adjective positive.
- 'Thank you very much for that nice compliment.' Leopoldine politely accepts the encouraging feedback. The adjective joli precedes the noun compliment.
- 'The assistant job is difficult.' Gabriel reminds her of the intense demands of the position. The preposition d' introduces the qualifying noun assistant.
- 'I am ready for this job.' She confidently affirms her readiness to tackle the challenges. The feminine adjective prete agrees with the female speaker.
- 'That is perfect, we will think about it.' Hicham concludes the meeting courteously. The near future allons reflechir uses aller followed by an infinitive.
