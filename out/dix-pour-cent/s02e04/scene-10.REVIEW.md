## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'She could not come. She apologizes.' Andréa conveys Isabelle's polite excuse. The adverb meaning 'not' sharpens the verb for 'has' here.

- 'As a child, I had fragile bones and I was often hospitalized.' Sacha recalls his painful youth. Notice how the verb for 'I had' leads straight into 'bones'.
- 'Do you know the loneliness of a sick child?' Sacha asks a poignant rhetorical question. The adjective meaning 'sick' agrees in gender and number with feminine noun 'loneliness'.
- 'No, I know the loneliness of a bored child.' Andréa replies with honest empathy. Andréa pairs the verb meaning 'know' with the noun for 'child'.
- 'That is how I discovered Isabelle Adjani.' Sacha recounts his cinematic revelation. The verb meaning 'I have' anchors the whole remark.
- 'She was everything to me. I wanted to make films for her.' Sacha describes his artistic inspiration. The key word here means 'was', paired with 'movies' to make the point clear.
- 'I wrote to her, but the reply was cold and stupid.' Sacha shares his painful childhood rejection. The adjectives 'cold' and 'stupid' describe the hurtful letter he received.
- 'I will never forgive her. That woman is dead to me.' Sacha declares his lasting grudge. The adverb meaning 'not' sharpens the verb for 'will forgive' here.
- 'She will think that she intimidates you.' Andréa skillfully challenges his masculine ego. The near future 'is going' introduces a second part of the sentence with the transitive verb 'intimidates'.

- 'You know what? Forget it.' Andréa pretends to abandon the negotiation. Listen for the expression meaning 'know', which leads naturally into 'fall'.
- 'Who cares what Adjani thinks?' Andréa dismisses the actress to provoke his competitive instinct. Andréa opens with the word for 'care' and lands on 'thinks' at the close.
- 'I am going to see her, we are going to clear things up.' Sacha resolves to confront the star directly. The idiomatic phrase 'to put' means to clarify a misunderstanding.
- 'Of course, you are the one who decides.' Andréa seals her psychological triumph smoothly. The adverb meaning 'well' sharpens the verb for 'decide' here.
