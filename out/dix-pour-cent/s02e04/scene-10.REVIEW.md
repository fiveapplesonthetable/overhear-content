## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'She could not come. She apologizes.' Andrea conveys Isabelle's polite excuse. The pronominal verb 's'en excuser' expresses regret or an apology for a situation.
- 'It is ridiculous. A legendary actress!' Sacha expresses his sharp frustration. The adjective 'legendaire' highlights the grand reputation of the movie star.
- 'Nobody knows better than me who Isabelle Adjani is.' Sacha asserts his deep childhood connection. The negative pronoun 'personne' combines with the comparative 'mieux que moi'.
- 'As a child, I had fragile bones and I was often hospitalized.' Sacha recalls his painful youth. The imperfect 'j'avais' describes an ongoing physiological condition during childhood.
- 'Do you know the loneliness of a sick child?' Sacha asks a poignant rhetorical question. The noun 'solitude' expresses isolation, qualified by the adjective 'malade'.
- 'No, I know the loneliness of a bored child.' Andrea replies with honest empathy. The relative clause 'qui s'ennuie' comes from the reflexive verb 's'ennuyer'.
- 'That is how I discovered Isabelle Adjani.' Sacha recounts his cinematic revelation. The idiomatic formula 'c'est comme ca que' introduces an explanatory narrative.
- 'She was everything to me. I wanted to make films for her.' Sacha describes his artistic inspiration. The pronoun 'tout' functions here as a predicate noun meaning everything in the world.
- 'I wrote to her, but the reply was cold and stupid.' Sacha shares his painful childhood rejection. The adjectives 'froide' and 'stupide' describe the hurtful letter he received.
- 'I will never forgive her. That woman is dead to me.' Sacha declares his lasting grudge. The future tense 'pardonnerai' is negated by 'ne ... jamais' with indirect pronoun 'lui'.
- 'She will think that she intimidates you.' Andrea skillfully challenges his masculine ego. The near future 'va penser' introduces a subordinate clause with the transitive verb 'intimider'.
- 'Nonsense. I am not afraid of her.' Sacha reacts defensively to the implication. The idiom 'avoir peur de' takes the stressed disjunctive pronoun 'elle'.
- 'You know what? Forget it.' Andrea pretends to abandon the negotiation. The imperative 'laissez tomber' is a common colloquial French phrase meaning drop it.
- 'Who cares what Adjani thinks?' Andrea dismisses the actress to provoke his competitive instinct. The colloquial expression 's'en ficher' means not to care at all.
- 'I am going to see her, we are going to clear things up.' Sacha resolves to confront the star directly. The idiomatic phrase 'mettre les choses au clair' means to clarify a misunderstanding.
- 'Of course, you are the one who decides.' Andrea seals her psychological triumph smoothly. The cleft sentence 'c'est vous qui' places the emphasis on the decision-maker.
