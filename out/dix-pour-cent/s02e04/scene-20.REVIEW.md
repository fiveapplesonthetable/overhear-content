## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello Andréa, it is time for our meeting.' Hicham enters Andréa's office unannounced. The noun phrase l'heure de indicates a scheduled appointment time.
- 'No, you are early. Come back later.' Andréa sets immediate boundaries. The idiom en avance means ahead of schedule.
- 'I love your office and your style.' Hicham attempts flattery. The verb adorer expresses high appreciation.
- 'Stop looking at me insistently.' Andréa calls out his inappropriate gaze directly. The imperative phrase arrêtez de means stop doing something.
- 'You are too suspicious with me.' Hicham brushes off her complaint. The adjective méfiante means wary or mistrustful.
- 'Listen, Hicham, let us be very clear.' Andréa initiates a serious clarification. The imperative soyons comes from the verb être.
- 'I prefer women, only women.' Andréa states her sexual orientation unambiguously. The adverb uniquement means exclusively or solely.
- 'I completely understand your point of view.' Hicham tries to sound understanding. The locution tout à fait means entirely or completely.
- 'No, I think that you do not understand.' Andréa cuts through his superficial agreement. The negation ne... pas surrounds the verb comprenez.
- 'For me, my personal life is definitive.' Andréa reaffirms her identity with conviction. The adjective définitive means final or settled.
- 'You will never change my decision.' Andréa leaves no room for doubt. The future tense changerez expresses certainty about the future.
- 'I only wanted to chat in a friendly way.' Hicham retreats to a polite pretext. The adverb amicalement means warmly or friendly.
- 'Now, leave my office, please.' Andréa orders him to leave firmly. The imperative sortez commands departure.
- 'Very well, Marteau, I leave you to work.' Hicham yields while using her annoying nickname. The phrase vous laisse means leave you.
- 'And stop calling me Marteau!' Andréa refuses the patronizing nickname. The infinitive appeler follows the preposition de.
- 'It is promised, I respect your space.' Hicham finally accepts the boundary and departs. The phrase c'est promis means that is a promise.
