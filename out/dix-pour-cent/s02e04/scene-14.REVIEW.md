## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello, Sacha Hartman.' The radio presenter introduces the celebrated guest. The formal greeting addresses the visiting filmmaker directly.
- 'Thank you for being with us.' The polite opening welcomes him to the studio broadcast. The prepositional phrase uses an infinitive of state.
- 'You speak perfect French.' The host compliments the visiting director's fluency. The adjective follows the masculine language noun.
- 'I attended the French school in Berlin.' Sacha explains his early language immersion. The compound past verb governs the direct object school.
- 'My parents have always loved France.' He attributes his bilingualism to family ties. The plural possessive introduces maternal and paternal affinity.
- 'Your first movie won a prize.' The journalist recalls his striking artistic debut. The ordinal adjective precedes the masculine noun.
- 'The Palme d'Or at Cannes!' The host highlights the highest festival accolade. The prestigious festival prize carries international prestige.
- 'An association is trying to ban your film.' The question addresses conservative censorship. The semi-auxiliary construction indicates active attempts to suppress.
- 'How do you react to these attacks?' The host invites his comment on public controversy. The interrogative adverb structures an inquiry into feelings.
- 'I do not want to give them a platform.' Sacha refuses to amplify his loudest critics. The negative auxiliary governs an infinitive with indirect object pronoun.
- 'Nor give them the slightest attention.' He dismisses the protesters entirely. The negative coordinating conjunction reinforces total indifference.
- 'They belong to the old world.' The filmmaker views the opposition as outdated reactionaries. The third-person plural verb signifies cultural divergence.
- 'The interview is over, Sacha.' Camille signals that their broadcast segment has wrapped up. The feminine past participle indicates completion of work.
- 'We must go now.' Camille urges him toward the door before crowds gather. The modal verb devoir indicates practical urgency to depart.
- 'All right, I am following you.' Sacha calmly prepares to leave the premises. The present-tense verb follows the agreement formula.
