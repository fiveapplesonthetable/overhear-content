## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Here I am, let me introduce myself to you.' The candidate opens his audition. The formal 'you' pronoun sets a courteous, professional distance around 'introduce'.
- 'I have no experience in this profession.' The candidate admits his amateur background. The verb meaning 'have' pairs naturally with the noun for 'experience' in everyday conversation.
- 'But I have always wanted to be an actor.' The candidate expresses his lifelong passion. The prepositional phrase 'depuis toujours' means since always or all along.
- 'I have a lot of natural charisma.' The candidate brags about his presence. The adverb 'beaucoup de' quantifies the masculine noun 'charisme'.
- 'Are we really seeing him for the role of Baptiste?' The casting assistant whispers in disbelief. The verb 'voir' refers to auditioning a candidate.
- 'I can tell you a funny story.' The candidate offers to entertain the room. The adjective 'drole' describes an amusing tale.
- 'Is this boy a joke?' The assistant questions whether the audition is serious. The feminine noun 'blague' denotes a joke or prank.
- 'I know, it is an idea from Gerard.' The colleague explains who suggested the candidate. The preposition 'de' indicates origin.
- 'What is your shoe size?' The assistant turns to wardrobe details. The noun 'pointure' specifically designates shoe size.
- 'My size is forty-two in France.' The candidate states his measurements. The cardinal number 'quarante-deux' indicates standard European shoe sizing.
- 'Please lower your arms.' The assistant gives posing directions. The polite formula 'veuillez' followed by the infinitive softens an order.
- 'I am taking your measurements quickly.' The assistant works efficiently with the measuring tape. The feminine plural noun 'mesures' denotes body dimensions.
- 'Do not suck in your stomach during the measurement.' The assistant instructs him on natural posture. The negative imperative 'ne rentrez pas' prevents inaccurate sizing.
- 'Thank you very much for your welcome.' The candidate concludes politely. The masculine noun 'accueil' refers to reception or hospitality.
