## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Above all, do not suck in your stomach!' The costumer gives practical advice during fitting. The negative imperative warns against unnatural posture.
- 'Afterwards, the trousers will be too tight.' She explains the physical consequence. The future tense verb predicts discomfort.
- 'I am not sucking in my stomach.' Gerard defends his natural physique. The present negative verb asserts honest measurement.
- 'Relax your muscles, please.' Emma gently insists on a relaxed stance. The polite imperative guides the client.
- 'Very good, it is perfect like that.' She completes the waist measurement. The approving adjective confirms accurate sizing.
- 'But that is truly ridiculous!' Mathias reacts with shock over the phone. The colloquial idiom denotes utter absurdity.
- 'The intended role was very modest.' Mathias recalls the original casting plan. The past participle describes a small character.
- 'He only had three lines!' He stresses how minor the part was supposed to be. The restrictive negation highlights minimal dialogue.
- 'It is a role way too big for him.' Mathias fears the audition is out of proportion. The comparative adverb emphasizes overwhelming difficulty.
- 'He has never done anything before.' He points out the newcomer's total lack of experience. The negative past construction underscores professional novelty.
- 'He is going to crash and burn completely!' Mathias worries about a devastating failure. The near future pronominal verb anticipates catastrophe.
- 'I am the one who introduced him to the director.' Mathias worries about his own reputation. The cleft sentence stresses personal professional responsibility.
- 'Call me back as soon as you have news.' He demands prompt updates on the audition results. The imperative verb requests immediate communication.
- 'Emma, can I see you for two minutes?' Gerard calls over the designer for a private word. The modal question politely requests attention.
- 'Yes, of course, Gerard, I am coming right away.' Emma readily agrees to step aside with the star. The present motion verb confirms prompt availability.
