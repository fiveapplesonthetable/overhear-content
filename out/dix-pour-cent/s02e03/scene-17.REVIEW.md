## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Catherine Barneville wants to see me?' Noémie asks in pure panic. The verb vouloir means to want.
- 'Tell her that Mathias went out.' Noémie desperately tries to avoid the encounter. The imperative dites-lui uses the indirect object pronoun lui.
- 'Say that I have urgent work.' She fabricates a quick excuse. The adjective urgent follows the noun travail.
- 'Mathias? It's Noémie.' She immediately dials Mathias's voicemail. The presentative c'est introduces herself on the phone.
- 'Your wife is here at the agency!' Noémie warns him frantically. The possessive determiner ta matches the feminine noun femme.
- 'Does she know about us?' She fears their secret affair is exposed. The idiom être au courant de means to be informed about or aware of.
- 'I don't know what to do, call me back!' She pleads into the receiver. The imperative rappelle-moi attaches the object pronoun moi with a hyphen.
- 'Hello, Noémie.' Catherine steps into the doorway calmly. The standard greeting introduces the tense confrontation.
- 'Hello Catherine. Sorry, I have to leave.' Noémie tries to slip past her. The semi-auxiliary devoir indicates obligation before the infinitive partir.
- 'Wait, it's about Mathias.' Catherine blocks her path politely. The prepositional phrase à propos de means regarding or concerning.
- 'I am running very late.' Noémie insists on her departure. The expression être en retard means to be late.
- 'I'm having lunch with Camille and her mother.' She blurts out her destination. The verb déjeuner means to eat lunch.
- 'Camille and her mother?' Catherine raises an eyebrow with curiosity. The possessive determiner sa indicates Camille's mother Annick.
- 'I need to eat a steak tartare.' Catherine decides she is hungry too. The idiom avoir besoin de expresses need.
- 'I'm coming with you!' Catherine invites herself along without hesitation. The verb venir expresses motion toward the listener.
- 'All right... let's go together.' Noémie realizes she is completely trapped. The imperative allons-y combines aller with adverbial pronoun y.
