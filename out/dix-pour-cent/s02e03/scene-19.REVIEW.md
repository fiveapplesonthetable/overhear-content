## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Why did you want Annick's number?' Mathias demands angrily. The interrogative adverb pourquoi asks for reasons.
- 'Is it forbidden for women to talk to each other?' Catherine deflects coolly. The adjective interdit means prohibited or forbidden.
- 'You're doing this just to annoy me!' Mathias sees right through her game. The verb embêter means to annoy or bother someone.
- 'Poor Camille, caught between two fires.' Mathias worries about his daughter. The idiom pris entre deux feux means caught in the crossfire.
- 'This situation is becoming completely toxic.' Mathias criticizes Catherine's maneuver. The adjective malsain means unhealthy or toxic.
- 'You dare talk to me about what is toxic?' Catherine turns on him fiercely. The verb oser means to dare.
- 'Selling our apartment behind my back?' She reveals his hidden scheme. The idiom dans le dos de quelqu'un means behind someone's back.
- 'It is my personal apartment!' Mathias shouts defensively. The possessive adjective mon indicates sole ownership.
- 'I financed the renovations five years ago.' Catherine counters with facts. The temporal expression il y a expresses ago.
- 'If it's a simple question of money...' Mathias attempts to reduce the dispute. The noun phrase une question d'argent means a financial matter.
- 'You are hiding your former mistress at the agency!' Catherine denounces his lies. The adjective ancienne placed before the noun means former.
- 'I simply wanted to protect our family.' Mathias insists on his good intentions. The verb protéger means to protect.
- 'Hippolyte told me you were collapsing.' Catherine mentions their son's worry. The reflexive verb s'effondrer means to break down or collapse.
- 'I am doing very well, as you see.' Mathias refuses to show vulnerability. The expression aller bien means to be doing well.
- 'We really can't talk to each other anymore.' Catherine recognizes the end of dialogue. The negative structure ne ... plus means no longer.
- 'Then let's settle everything with our lawyers.' Mathias surrenders to legal warfare. The imperative réglons comes from régler.
