## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello everyone!' Hicham greets the open office with typical swagger. The fixed expression tout le monde means everyone.
- 'Hello Hicham.' Camille replies politely to her new boss. The standard polite greeting opens their exchange.
- 'This is my son, Elvis.' Hicham introduces his visiting young boy. The presentation word voici introduces people directly into view.
- 'He is ten this weekend.' The upcoming milestone prompts a celebration. French expresses age using the verb avoir with the number of years.
- 'We are having a party in Paris.' Hicham plans festivities during their week together. The colloquial pronoun on acts as the subject for making a party.
- 'Elvis likes laser tag.' Hicham shares his son's chosen entertainment activity. The adverbial combination aimer bien indicates moderate liking.
- 'Can you arrange that, Camille?' Hicham immediately delegates the party logistics. The modal verb pouvoir expresses request in question form.
- 'Okay, leave it to me.' Camille readily takes charge of the task. The imperative phrase laisse-moi faire offers full handling.
- 'Two round-trip tickets for his friends.' Hicham orders transportation arrangements. The compound noun aller-retour designates return train or plane tickets.
- 'Thanks, Camille.' Hicham expresses brisk satisfaction with her help. The polite expression merci closes his set of requests.
- 'Are you crazy, Camille?' Mathias pulls his secret daughter aside in disbelief. The informal contraction t'es replaces tu es in everyday speech.
- 'Why do you say that?' Camille questions his sharp objection. The interrogative adverb pourquoi seeks clarification of his concern.
- 'You are playing the babysitter for him.' Mathias warns her against subservient duties. The idiom jouer les designates acting out an unhelpful role.
- 'He will not respect you.' Mathias cautions that flunky tasks undermine authority. The future tense of respecter warns of negative consequences.
- 'I want to be a junior agent.' Camille reaffirms her clear professional ambition. The verb expresses determination followed by an infinitive.
- 'So I am being careful, Mathias.' Camille insists she knows how to manage the boundary. The idiom faire attention signifies being vigilant and prudent.
