## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I am afraid he will fail his driving test.' Gabriel worries about his client's upcoming driving examination. The phrase avoir peur que introduces a clause about fear.
- 'Does he not know how to drive?' Herve questions the young internet celebrity's basic vehicle skills. The negative question expresses surprise.
- 'Yes, but he panics during the exam.' Gabriel clarifies that nerves are the real problem on the road. The affirmative word si contradicts a negative question.
- 'He cannot film without a license.' The prestigious film role requires him to drive legally on set. The preposition sans indicates lack or absence.
- 'I have something to relax.' Herve proposes an unconventional remedy to ease his tension. The reflexive infinitive se detendre means to unwind.
- 'He cannot smoke before the exam!' Gabriel immediately rejects any illicit substance before the test. The temporal preposition avant precedes the noun.
- 'Then, I have these very effective pills.' The assistant offers a legal pharmaceutical alternative instead. The plural demonstrative ces points to the tablets.
- 'Look at the side effects on the Internet!' Norman reads alarming medical warnings on his smartphone screen. The imperative regardez calls immediate attention.
- 'They always say total nonsense.' Herve dismisses online medical advice as wildly exaggerated. The phrase n'importe quoi means nonsense or rubbish.
- 'Every medication has undesirable effects.' Norman remains cautious about putting chemicals in his body. The indefinite determiner tout agrees with the singular noun.
- 'I do not need it at all.' The YouTuber insists that he can manage his stress naturally. The pronoun en replaces the prepositional phrase.
- 'I am very calm and relaxed.' He claims to feel completely peaceful despite his obvious anxiety. The adjective detendu describes a relaxed state.
- 'As you wish, my dear Norman.' Herve gracefully accepts his client's personal decision. The polite phrase comme vous voulez respects another person's choice.
- 'Wait, I forgot my mobile phone!' Norman panics at the last second as he prepares to depart. The past tense ai oublie indicates a recent realization.
