## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I found a solution for Nathalie Baye.' Noemie announces her scheduling breakthrough. The compound past 'j'ai trouve' expresses completed success with the feminine noun 'solution'.
- 'Ah, excellent. Show me that.' Mathias reacts with interest and demands to inspect the plan. The imperative 'montre-moi' pairs the verb with a hyphenated stressed pronoun.
- 'Here is Xavier Beauvois's schedule.' Noemie lays out the first production timetable. The presentative 'voila' introduces the masculine borrowed noun 'planning'.
- 'This one is Tonie Marshall's.' Noemie compares the two competing schedules. The demonstrative pronouns 'celui-ci' and 'celui de' replace masculine nouns elegantly.
- 'The shooting days are marked in red.' Noemie points out the color-coded days. The prepositional phrase 'en rouge' specifies the marking color of the schedule.
- 'Everything fits together perfectly here.' Noemie marvels at the puzzle pieces aligning. The reflexive verb 's'emboiter' means to interlock or fit snugly into place.
- 'Nathalie can do both movies.' Noemie concludes triumphantly. The modal verb 'peut' followed by the infinitive 'faire' expresses feasibility.
- 'No, I tell you that it does not work.' Mathias remains skeptical of the tight logistics. The idiom 'ca ne marche pas' denotes failure or impracticality in everyday French.
- 'Do you regret it, Mathias?' Noemie suddenly changes the subject to their secret past. The direct object pronoun 'le' refers back to their intimate encounter.
- 'That is not the topic, we are at work.' Mathias attempts to restore professional boundaries. The noun 'sujet' refers to the subject matter of conversation.
- 'Beauvois always exceeds the dates.' Mathias cites the director's notorious delays. The verb 'depasser' means to exceed or overrun allotted time frames.
- 'Nathalie needs two weeks of rest.' Mathias defends his star client's physical stamina. The expression 'avoir besoin de' expresses essential necessity.
- 'You said no, so you do not regret anything.' Noemie interprets his hesitation flirtatiously. The conjunction 'donc' introduces a logical deduction negated with 'ne ... rien'.
- 'Listen, Noemie, I prefer not to speak of it here.' Mathias deflects the personal banter. The pronoun 'en' replaces the unspoken topic before the infinitive 'parler'.
- 'In any case, do not worry, Mathias.' Noemie reassures him with confidence. The adverbial connector 'de toute facon' bridges the conversation gracefully.
- 'Leave it to me, it will fit.' Noemie concludes with breezy determination. The colloquial expression 'ca va rentrer' conveys making tight plans work.
