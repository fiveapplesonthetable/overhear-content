## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hi Julien! Are you doing well?' Gabriel greets the visiting artist in the party lounge. The informal greeting salut is used among friends and colleagues.
- 'Can you find me a taxi?' Julien urgently asks for a ride away from the venue. The verb trouver means to find or locate.
- 'Is there a problem?' The agent senses that something went wrong with the project. The phrase il y a introduces the existence of something.
- 'I argued with Charlotte.' The musician explains the artistic disagreement with his partner. The reflexive verb se disputer indicates a verbal argument.
- 'She does not understand the ending.' Julien feels misunderstood regarding the climax of his film. The negation ne ... pas surrounds the conjugated verb comprend.
- 'I must go back to Paris.' Frustrated by the dispute, he decides to cut his visit short. The modal verb dois expresses necessity or obligation.
- 'Sofia wants to do screen tests.' Gabriel seizes the opportunity to pitch his actress client. The noun essais refers to auditions or screen tests.
- 'We will see about that later.' Julien deflects the business pitch while in a hurry. The simple future verra comes from the irregular verb voir.
- 'You are too pushy, Gabriel!' Sofia reproaches her agent for bringing up work at an awkward moment. The adjective lourd idiomatically means pushy, clumsy, or annoying.
- 'He is not interested.' She feels discouraged and doubts the singer has any interest in her. The adjective interesse describes someone who is interested.
- 'Stop worrying like that.' Gabriel urges her not to let insecurity hold her back. The imperative arrete tells someone to stop an action.
- 'Julien is always very stressed.' The agent explains that creative people often experience intense anxiety. The adverb toujours indicates habitual behavior.
- 'You must give it a shot.' Gabriel encourages his client to take advantage of the opportunity. The idiomatic phrase tenter le coup means to give it a shot or take a chance.
- 'All right, I will talk to him.' Sofia finally agrees to approach the artist directly. The near future vais parler indicates an immediate intention.
