## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'We planned Elvis's birthday party.' Hervé announces proudly. The compound past a préparé comes from préparer.
- 'We booked two laser game sessions.' Camille presents the main activity. The numeral deux qualifies the feminine plural sessions.
- 'And a giant cake with candy!' Hervé adds with cheerful enthusiasm. The adjective géant means gigantic or giant.
- 'Do you really think that is enough?' Hicham scoffs disdainfully. The verb suffire means to suffice or be enough.
- 'It's fun for ten-year-old children.' Camille defends the kid-friendly plan. The adjective amusant means entertaining or fun.
- 'Elvis's mother is ruining my life!' Hicham vents his personal frustration. The colloquial verb pourrir means to rot or ruin.
- 'I want the best party in the world!' Hicham demands an extraordinary event. The superlative la meilleure fête means the best party.
- 'We forget the laser game and the candies.' He cancels their initial proposal instantly. The verb oublier means to forget or leave aside.
- 'I want a grandiose party, friends!' Hicham sets his extravagant vision. The adjective grandiose means magnificent or spectacular.
- 'A grandiose party? What kind of place?' Hervé asks cautiously. The noun phrase quel genre de means what sort of.
- 'I want a castle with a large park!' Hicham reveals his lavish ambition. The noun château means castle or grand manor.
- 'A castle? But that is huge!' Camille gasps at the scale. The adjective énorme means enormous or massive.
- 'Note everything: fountains and music.' Hicham rattles off extravagant features. The imperative notez directs them to take notes.
- 'And who are the invited guests?' Hervé checks the guest list. The feminine plural participle invitées functions as an adjective modifying personnes.
- 'The whole agency and big celebrities!' Hicham wants a star-studded spectacle. The noun célébrité means celebrity.
- 'All right, we will organize that.' Camille accepts the daunting challenge. The immediate future allons organiser combines aller with the infinitive.
