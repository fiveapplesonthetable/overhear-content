## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'What were you doing with Julien Dore?' Mathias demands an explanation about the meeting. The imperfect tense 'faisais' asks about an ongoing past activity.
- 'Julien is looking for a singer for his film.' Sofia clarifies the artistic opportunity. The verb 'chercher' takes a direct object without requiring any preposition.
- 'Do you want to be a singer or an actress?' Mathias forces a career choice. The disjunctive conjunction 'ou' presents two mutually exclusive vocational paths.
- 'I want to be an actress, of course.' Sofia affirms her primary dedication. The adverbial expression 'bien sur' adds confident certainty to her answer.
- 'But she sings very well too!' Gabriel enthusiastically praises her dual talent. The adverb 'aussi' highlights additional artistic capability.
- 'It is a chance to get myself known.' Sofia explains her practical promotional motive. The reflexive causative structure 'se faire connaitre' means to become known.
- 'Or to muddy the waters before starting.' Mathias counters with a pragmatic warning. The idiom 'brouiller les pistes' literally means to confuse the tracks or trail.
- 'This industry hates people who do too many things.' Mathias describes industry prejudice. The noun 'milieu' refers to the film and show business world.
- 'Gabriel is in love, he thinks you are capable of anything.' Mathias points out Gabriel's bias. The adjective 'capable de tout' describes unlimited belief in someone.
- 'Let us be realistic and avoid scattering our efforts.' Mathias calls for professional discipline. The first-person plural imperatives 'soyons' and 'evitons' rally common sense.
- 'Focus solely on acting.' Mathias gives direct advice on craft. The reflexive imperative 'concentre-toi sur' directs attention to dramatic performance.
- 'Mathias is a pragmatic agent who lacks imagination.' Gabriel dismisses Mathias's conservative caution. The relative clause 'qui manque d'imagination' critiques rigid predictability.
- 'He is good with contracts, but he has discovered nobody.' Gabriel questions Mathias's creative track record. The negative pronoun 'personne' follows the compound past auxiliary and participle.
- 'So let us stay curious, intelligent, open.' Gabriel champions artistic experimentation. The series of adjectives 'curieux, intelligents, ouverts' encourages bold exploration.
- 'And let us see what Julien has to offer.' Gabriel keeps the creative door open. The imperative 'voyons ce que' invites examining the upcoming proposal.
- 'All right, I am going to listen to him with care.' Sofia agrees to keep an open mind. The near future 'je vais l'ecouter' expresses immediate intention.
