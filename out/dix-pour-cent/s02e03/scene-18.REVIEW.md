## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Annick, I presume?' Catherine approaches the table with sharp confidence. The verb présumer means to presume or assume.
- 'I am Catherine Barneville.' She introduces herself formally to Mathias's former mistress. The subject pronoun je combines with the irregular present of être.
- 'Catherine? What are you doing here?' Mathias chokes on his surprise. The question phrase qu'est-ce que asks what.
- 'I came to have lunch with you all.' Catherine smiles icily. The verb venir expresses coming to a location.
- 'Noémie, please return to the office.' Mathias quickly orders his assistant away. The imperative retournez uses polite vous.
- 'Very well, I will leave you.' Noémie gladly seizes the chance to flee. The verb phrase laisser indicates leaving someone.
- 'This lunch was not planned this way.' Mathias tries to defend the arrangement. The past participle prévu comes from prévoir.
- 'It's nothing, I'll sit with you.' Catherine pulls out a chair deliberately. The reflexive verb s'asseoir means to sit down.
- 'Do you want to order something to eat?' Mathias asks nervously. The verb commander means to order in a restaurant.
- 'No, just a coffee, please.' Catherine declines food politely. The adverb juste restricts the noun café.
- 'Why did you come here exactly?' Mathias demands quietly. The compound past es venue agrees with feminine singular Catherine.
- 'I wanted to meet Annick.' Catherine explains her bold curiosity. The idiom faire la connaissance de means to make someone's acquaintance.
- 'Are you trying to provoke me?' Mathias suspects her hostile motives. The verbal locution chercher à means to seek or try to.
- 'Not at all, we are civilized.' Catherine feigns polite innocence. The adjective civilisé describes polite behavior.
- 'Then let's be calm in front of everyone.' Mathias pleads for discretion. The imperative soyons is the subjunctive imperative form of être.
- 'Perfect. Let's drink our coffee.' Catherine takes control of the table. The imperative buvons comes from boire.
