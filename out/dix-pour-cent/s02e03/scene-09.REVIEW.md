## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Here is rule number one here.' Andrea establishes the fundamental agency principle. The presentative 'voila' introduces 'la regle numero un' with authority.
- 'We never flirt with clients. Never.' Andrea states the ethical boundary emphatically. The pronoun 'on' stands for we, negated by 'ne ... jamais' around the slang verb 'draguer'.
- 'I was just offering a very warm welcome.' Gabriel minimizes his playful charm. The imperfect tense 'j'offrais' describes an ongoing polite intention.
- 'What harm can it do?' Gabriel questions the strict prohibition casually. The interrogative determiner 'quel' agrees with the masculine noun 'mal'.
- 'She is a magnificent raw talent.' Andrea defends her new client's artistic promise. The adjective 'brut' describes unpolished potential, paired with 'magnifique'.
- 'It took me months to get this meeting.' Andrea emphasizes her patient effort. The impersonal expression 'il m'a fallu' conveys the time required.
- 'Every agent in Paris wants her.' Andrea underscores the intense competition. The plural determiner 'tous les' precedes the noun 'agents'.
- 'I did all the work because I believe in her.' Andrea asserts her professional dedication. The verb 'croire en' means to believe in someone's potential.
- 'I do not want you to ruin everything.' Andrea warns against reckless interference. The verb 'vouloir que' triggers the subjunctive verb form 'tu fasses'.
- 'You flirt with your clients too, Andrea.' Gabriel deflects the criticism playfully. The adverb 'aussi' adds a touch of reciprocal accusation.
- 'Tell me you are not attracted to her.' Gabriel challenges her personal motives. The imperative 'dis-moi' introduces a negative subordinate clause.
- 'Of course I am, otherwise I would not want to represent her.' Andrea readily concedes. The affirmative response 'bien sur que si' contradicts a negative question.
- 'But I do not want to sleep with her.' Andrea draws a sharp line between attraction and action. The colloquial infinitive 'coucher avec' denotes sleeping with someone.
- 'That is the big difference between you and me.' Andrea summarizes her professional discipline. The preposition 'entre' connects the two stressed pronouns 'toi' and 'moi'.
- 'I am being careful, do not worry.' Gabriel attempts to reassure his colleague. The negative reflexive imperative 'ne t'inquiete pas' urges calm.
- 'Stay away from her, that is all I ask of you.' Andrea delivers her final firm instruction. The relative structure 'ce que' functions as the object of 'je te demande'.
