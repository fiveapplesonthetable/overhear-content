## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I truly love driving.' Norman expresses his newfound enthusiasm behind the steering wheel. The verb adorer indicates strong personal enjoyment.
- 'I could drive indefinitely.' He feels surprisingly relaxed and confident on the road. The conditional form pourrais expresses hypothetical ability.
- 'That is fine, but concentrate.' The driving examiner sternly reminds him to stay focused on traffic. The reflexive imperative concentrez-vous demands full attention.
- 'Here, we turn right.' The official gives the next directional instruction. The prepositional phrase a droite indicates turning to the right.
- 'Do not forget the turn signal!' Norman reminds himself aloud to signal properly before turning. The noun clignotant refers to the vehicle flashing indicator.
- 'Watch out for the pedestrian crossing.' The examiner points out an approaching danger on the street. The prepositional phrase attention au gives a warning.
- 'Let that lady cross first.' The candidate must yield right of way to walking pedestrians. The causative structure laissez passer means allow someone to pass.
- 'The road is completely clear.' Norman checks his mirrors and assesses the traffic situation. The feminine adjective degagee means unobstructed.
- 'Park the car along the sidewalk.' The final maneuver test is requested by the examiner. The prepositional locution le long du means along the side of the street.
- 'It went rather well.' The official hints at a surprisingly positive assessment. The adverbial phrase plutot bien indicates a favorable impression.
- 'You mean that I passed?' Norman hopes for confirmation that he finally earned his license. The idiom vouloir dire asks for intended meaning.
- 'I said absolutely nothing!' The examiner refuses to break official confidentiality rules. The negative construction n'ai rien dit expresses strict silence.
- 'Thank you very much, that is wonderful!' Norman rejoices at having overcome his driving exam anxiety. The adjective formidable expresses intense relief.
- 'Now, exit the vehicle.' The official dismisses the candidate at the end of the test. The imperative sortez orders him to step out.
