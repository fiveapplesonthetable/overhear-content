## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hurry up, we must leave quickly!' Mathias urges Isabelle to move fast. The imperative 'depechez-vous' commands prompt action.
- 'I am going ahead to open the door.' Mathias takes the lead through the studio hallways. The phrase 'passer devant' means to go in front or take the lead.
- 'So, how was my live interview?' Isabelle seeks immediate feedback on her appearance. The interrogative adverb 'comment' asks for an opinion or manner.
- 'Did it go well?' Isabelle wonders whether the broadcast ran smoothly. The imperfect form 'allait' describes past ongoing status.
- 'Was I good on the microphone tonight?' Isabelle focuses on her personal delivery. The adjective 'bien' acts adverbially here to mean effective or skilled.
- 'No doubt, you were very brilliant.' Mathias reassures her with quick praise. The phrase 'sans doute' means without doubt or probably.
- 'That is wonderful, Laure loved it.' Isabelle rejoices in the successful broadcast. The adjective 'formidable' means wonderful or tremendous.
- 'But now, the American shoot is waiting for us.' Mathias pivots back to their primary obligation. The noun 'tournage' denotes a film shoot.
- 'Director Gary is going to lose patience.' Mathias warns about tensions on the movie set. The idiom 'perdre patience' means to lose one's temper or become impatient.
- 'Do not panic, we have time.' Isabelle remains calm under pressure. The negative imperative 'ne paniquez pas' soothes someone's worry.
- 'The car is parked right downstairs.' Mathias guides her toward their transport. The past participle 'garee' means parked.
- 'Isabelle, get quickly into the back!' Mathias hurries her inside the vehicle. The verb meaning 'get in' pairs naturally with the noun for 'rear' in everyday conversation.
- 'Let us go, I am ready for Gary.' Isabelle embraces the next challenge of the night. The clause hinges on the everyday verb meaning 'let us go'.
