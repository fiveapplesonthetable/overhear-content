## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Jean, are you there?' Andrea checks on her reclusive star client. The direct address seeks his attention.
- 'You are very marked by your role.' Andrea gently observes his deep emotional exhaustion. The past participle indicates lasting psychological impact.
- 'I have never experienced this before, never!' Jean emphasizes the unprecedented intensity of the shoot. The repeated negative adverb highlights extreme feelings.
- 'They are waiting for you for the shoot.' Andrea reminds him of his upcoming film commitment. The transitive verb stresses professional obligation.
- 'You have to play a modern banker.' Gabriel describes the civilized character waiting for him. The modal verb introduces his next acting assignment.
- 'Honestly, right now, I cannot!' Jean bluntly rejects taking on another film immediately. The negative modal verb expresses total inability.
- 'I cannot follow on like that.' He insists on taking a break before another production. The verbal phrase refers to jumping between projects.
- 'I spent six months in the forest.' Jean recalls his prolonged survivalist isolation. The temporal noun phrase underscores extended hardship.
- 'I went down to bare human bone.' He describes stripping away civilized identity for his role. The prepositional phrase evokes primal survival.
- 'I refuse the noise and people!' Jean shrinks from modern urban life and film sets. The transitive verb expresses visceral revulsion.
- 'But have you not signed your contract?' Gabriel asks about legal paperwork for the banking movie. The negative inverted question probes formal commitment.
- 'Yes, I signed my contract!' Jean insists that he already put pen to paper. The assertive affirmative particle disputes Gabriel's doubt.
- 'You signed Emile Berthier!' Andrea reveals that he used his character's fictional name. The proper noun phrase reveals deep method confusion.
- 'That is your character, Jean.' Andrea tries to bring the disoriented actor back to reality. The predicate noun distinguishes fiction from reality.
- 'It is not you!' She reminds him of his own true identity. The emphatic negative copula enforces psychological separation.
