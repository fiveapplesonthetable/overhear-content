## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'There are actresses you see a lot.' Jean-Denis Gillières reflects on transient fame in cinema. The impersonal phrase 'there are' introduces a general observation.
- 'Then they work less, and the charm has vanished.' Jean-Denis laments how careers can decline. Notice how the verb for 'work' leads straight into 'charm'.
- 'You no longer feel like watching them.' Jean-Denis describes audience fatigue. The negative idiom 'no longer' expresses a loss of interest.
- 'But not Juliette. She is still blooming.' Jean-Denis sets Juliette Binoche apart from fading stars. The key word here means 'not', paired with 'still' to make the point clear.
- 'She opens up, like a wonderful flower.' Jean-Denis offers poetic praise for Juliette's charisma. Listen for the expression meaning 'opens', which leads naturally into 'magnificent'.
- 'Hello, I am Andréa Martel, who represents Juliette Binoche.' Andréa introduces herself to the festival guest. The relative pronoun 'who' introduces a defining clause for her agency role.
- 'Jean-Denis Gillières, a great friend of the festival.' Jean-Denis presents himself with pride. The adjective meaning 'great' agrees in gender and number with masculine noun 'friend'.
- 'Of course. Delighted.' Andréa responds with professional courtesy. Listen for the expression meaning 'of course', which leads naturally into 'delighted'.
- 'I am a huge fan of Juliette.' Jean-Denis shares his passionate admiration for the actress. The adjective meaning 'vast' agrees in gender and number with masculine noun 'fan'.
- 'I would really like to greet her, if possible.' Jean-Denis requests a personal meeting. The verb meaning 'would like' anchors the whole remark.
- 'Do you want me to tell her now?' Andréa clarifies the timing of the introduction. Listen for the expression meaning 'want', which leads naturally into 'now'.
- 'Yes, I am calling her right away.' Andréa takes out her phone to arrange the meeting. The adverb meaning 'yes' sharpens the verb for 'call' here.
- 'Agent... It is a strange profession, isn't it?' Jean-Denis ponders the nature of talent representation. Jean-Denis opens with the word for 'agent' and lands on 'no' at the close.
- 'Working in the shadows so that others can shine.' Jean-Denis describes an agent's self-effacing role. The key word here means 'to work', paired with 'shine' to make the point clear.
- 'Shoen... Did you say Schoenaerts?' Jean-Denis stumbles clumsily over Belgian actor names. The hesitation reflects his social awkwardness despite wealth.
- 'Andréa Martel! That is much simpler.' Andréa corrects him briskly with dry wit. The comparative 'much' contrasts her concise name.
