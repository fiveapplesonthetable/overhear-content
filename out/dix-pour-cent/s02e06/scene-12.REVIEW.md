## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'There are actresses you see a lot.' Jean-Denis Gillieres reflects on transient fame in cinema. The impersonal phrase 'il y a' introduces a general observation.
- 'Then they work less, and the charm has vanished.' Jean-Denis laments how careers can decline. The compound past tense 'a disparu' notes the loss of an actress's initial allure.
- 'You no longer feel like watching them.' Jean-Denis describes audience fatigue. The negative idiom 'ne plus avoir envie de' expresses a loss of interest.
- 'But not Juliette. She is still blooming.' Jean-Denis sets Juliette Binoche apart from fading stars. The reflexive verb 's'epanouir' conveys radiant vitality.
- 'She opens up, like a wonderful flower.' Jean-Denis offers poetic praise for Juliette's charisma. The comparative conjunction 'comme' introduces a floral simile.
- 'Hello, I am Andrea Martel, who represents Juliette Binoche.' Andrea introduces herself to the festival guest. The relative pronoun 'qui' introduces a defining clause for her agency role.
- 'Jean-Denis Gillieres, a great friend of the festival.' Jean-Denis presents himself with pride. The noun phrase 'un grand ami' signals influential patron status.
- 'Of course. Delighted.' Andrea responds with professional courtesy. The adjective 'enchantee' expresses formal pleasure upon meeting someone.
- 'I am a huge fan of Juliette.' Jean-Denis shares his passionate admiration for the actress. The adjective 'immense' emphasizes intense personal esteem.
- 'I would really like to greet her, if possible.' Jean-Denis requests a personal meeting. The conditional 'aimerais' frames a polite request.
- 'Do you want me to tell her now?' Andrea clarifies the timing of the introduction. The subjunctive construction 'que je lui dise' follows the verb of wanting.
- 'Yes, I am calling her right away.' Andrea takes out her phone to arrange the meeting. The direct object pronoun 'l'appelle' replaces the client.
- 'Agent... It is a strange profession, isn't it?' Jean-Denis ponders the nature of talent representation. The adjective 'drole' here means peculiar or unusual.
- 'Working in the shadows so that others can shine.' Jean-Denis describes an agent's self-effacing role. The conjunction 'pour que' introduces a purpose clause with subjunctive.
- 'Shoen... Did you say Schoenaerts?' Jean-Denis stumbles clumsily over Belgian actor names. The hesitation reflects his social awkwardness despite wealth.
- 'Andrea Martel! That is much simpler.' Andrea corrects him briskly with dry wit. The comparative 'beaucoup plus simple' contrasts her concise name.
