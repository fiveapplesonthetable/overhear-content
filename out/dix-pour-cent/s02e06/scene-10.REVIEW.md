## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I deduce that the Chopard jewelry has arrived. Hello.' Andrea remarks on the imposing security guard. The verb 'deduire' expresses logical deduction from visual evidence.
- 'Who are you, madam?' Renard demands formal identification. The interrogative pronoun 'qui' introduces a direct question.
- 'Andrea Martel, Juliette Binoche's agent.' Andrea introduces herself with authority. The noun 'agent' specifies her professional role representing the star.
- 'You must wait here, madam.' Renard blocks the entrance impassively. The modal verb 'devez attendre' conveys a strict administrative order.
- 'Aren't you the jewelry bodyguard?' Andrea quips about his defensive presence. The compound noun 'garde du corps' denotes a personal bodyguard.
- 'I protect the security of the room.' Renard defines his strict protective mission. The verb 'proteger' governs the direct object noun phrase 'la securite'.
- 'What then? Counter-terrorism?' Andrea teases his solemn demeanor. The technical noun 'antiterrorisme' humorously exaggerates the security level.
- 'Renard here. I have a certain Martel at room 403.' Renard radios his central command. The adjective 'certaine' conveys cool, detached impersonality.
- 'A certain Martel? I am her exclusive agent!' Andrea bristles at being treated like a stranger. The adjective 'exclusif' highlights her indispensable status.
- 'I am checking the authorizations.' Renard follows protocol without emotion. The verb 'verifier' denotes a routine security check.
- 'Do not put your big paws everywhere.' Andrea snaps into her phone while waiting. The colloquial noun 'pattes' refers metaphorically to clumsy hands.
- 'Not you, of course.' Andrea quickly clarifies her phone insult to the guard. The polite exception 'pas vous' prevents an awkward misunderstanding.
- 'Okay, I understand.' Renard receives confirmation on his radio. The phrase 'j'ai compris' signals receipt of clearance.
- 'It is all right, you can go in.' Renard steps aside to permit entry. The colloquial expression 'c'est bon' grants permission to enter.
- 'Thank you very much, Renard.' Andrea acknowledges the bodyguard with cool irony. The formal thanks are addressed directly using the guard's radio call sign.
- 'Have a good day, madam.' Renard concludes the exchange with professional courtesy. The courteous formula 'bonne journee' wraps up the gatekeeper encounter.
