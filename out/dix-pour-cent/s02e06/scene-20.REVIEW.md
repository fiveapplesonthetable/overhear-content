## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Listen to this ridiculous poem sent by Gillières.' Juliette is appalled by the message. The imperative écoute directs attention to the text.
- 'He writes that my feline beauty haunts him.' Juliette reads the pretentious line out loud. The feminine adjective féline means cat-like.
- 'This man really never drops the matter.' Andréa notes his relentless pursuit. The phrase lâcher l'affaire means to give up or drop it.
- 'He insists on inviting me onto his boat tomorrow.' Juliette conveys the unwanted invitation. The verb insister pour indicates persistent urging.
- 'I categorically refuse to get on that yacht.' Juliette takes a firm stand. The infinitive monter sur means to board or get on.
- 'The financial situation of your movie is delicate.' Andréa reminds her of the production stakes. The adjective délicate indicates vulnerability.
- 'What do you mean by that, Andréa?' Juliette senses hesitation in her agent. The phrase vouloir dire par là asks for clarification.
- 'Gillières can block the budget of your movie.' Andréa explains the billionaire's influence. The masculine noun budget designates production funds.
- 'I am not going to yield to a predator.' Juliette refuses to compromise her boundaries. The masculine noun prédateur designates an abusive person.
- 'I understand your anger and I support you.' Andréa validates her client's emotions. The feminine noun colère denotes righteous anger.
- 'Then find a solution to cancel this meeting.' Juliette charges her agent with finding a way out. The imperative trouve seeks a diplomatic escape.
- 'We can go there together with the team.' Andréa suggests a protective compromise. The adverb ensemble means together as a group.
- 'Do you promise not to leave me alone?' Juliette demands guaranteed accompaniment. The construction promettre de requires an infinitive.
- 'I will always be at your side on the boat.' Andréa makes an absolute pledge of protection. The locution à tes côtés means by your side.
- 'Alright, but we will leave after an hour.' Juliette imposes a strict time boundary. The phrase au bout de indicates an elapsed duration.
- 'It is promised, nobody will touch you!' Andréa seals the protective pact firmly. The indefinite pronoun personne with ne expresses nobody.
