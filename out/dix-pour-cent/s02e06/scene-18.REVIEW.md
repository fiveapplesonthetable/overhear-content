## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Catherine? What a surprise to see you in Cannes!' Mathias reacts with astonishment. The exclamative phrase quelle surprise expresses deep astonishment.
- 'I went to Mandelieu before coming.' Catherine reveals her unexpected trip. The compound past suis allée agrees with the feminine subject.
- 'Why did you go over there?' Mathias is alarmed by her journey. The question word pourquoi inquires about her motive.
- 'I saw Camille and her mother at the salon.' Catherine describes her visit calmly. The compound past ai vu comes from voir.
- 'It is in the past, stop torturing yourself.' Mathias attempts to avoid the painful topic. The reflexive imperative arrête de te torturer urges peace.
- 'It is not the past that worries me.' Catherine clarifies her real motivation. The relative clause qui me préoccupe emphasizes the true subject.
- 'It is Camille who is doing really badly.' Catherine expresses genuine concern for the young woman. The phrase va très mal describes suffering.
- 'She left the agency, it was better like that.' Mathias rationalizes his passivity. The adverb ainsi means in this manner or like that.
- 'Did you at least ask how she is doing?' Catherine demands accountability. The expression prendre des nouvelles means to check on someone.
- 'Not for several days, to protect you.' Mathias claims he acted out of consideration for his wife. The prepositional phrase pour te protéger indicates purpose.
- 'Stop hiding behind this pretext!' Catherine calls out his avoidance. The prepositional phrase derrière ce prétexte means behind this excuse.
- 'Camille needs her father, Mathias.' Catherine delivers a direct moral appeal. The verbal locution avoir besoin de means to need.
- 'I was so afraid of losing your love.' Mathias confesses his deep insecurity. The imperfect avais peur conveys his ongoing fear.
- 'I do not want a man who flees his duties.' Catherine demands integrity and responsibility. The verb fuit comes from fuir.
- 'You are right, I was cowardly with her.' Mathias finally recognizes his failings. The adjective lâche means cowardly or spineless.
- 'Call your daughter now and take on your role.' Catherine urges him to act honorably. The imperative assume commands taking responsibility.
