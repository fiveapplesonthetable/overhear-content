## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Is that Turkish film in the official competition?' Gabriel checks the festival program. The feminine noun compétition refers to film competition.
- 'No, it is screening at the Directors' Fortnight.' Arlette knows the festival categories well. The noun phrase Quinzaine des réalisateurs designates a parallel section.
- 'Is that the selection for first films?' Gabriel asks for clarification. The feminine noun sélection designates the chosen films.
- 'No, that is the Critics' Week.' Arlette explains the distinctions patiently. The proper noun Semaine de la Critique designates another section.
- 'I really do not understand anything at all.' Gabriel admits his total confusion. The negative phrase rien du tout means nothing at all.
- 'There is also a section called ACID.' Arlette mentions the independent cinema showcase. The acronym ACID stands for an association of independent cinema.
- 'That is quite a surprising name for a festival!' Gabriel smiles at the acronym. The adjective surprenant means surprising or unexpected.
- 'Wait, I just received a message.' Gabriel checks his buzzing phone. The construction viens de recevoir expresses recent receipt.
- 'An urgent problem with one of your actors?' Arlette notices his sudden focus. The adjective urgent describes pressing matters.
- 'No, it is Sofia who has just arrived in Cannes.' Gabriel admits the source of the message. The construction vient d'arriver indicates immediate arrival.
- 'That is wonderful! You should go meet her.' Arlette encourages his romance. The adjective merveilleux expresses great delight.
- 'I am a little afraid of mixing work and love.' Gabriel expresses his customary anxiety. The infinitive mélanger means to mix or blend.
- 'Enjoy life, Gabriel, we are in Cannes!' Arlette urges him to embrace joy. The imperative profite de advises taking advantage of good times.
- 'Look at the sun and the crowd on the Croisette.' Arlette gestures toward the bustling boulevard. The feminine noun foule refers to a large crowd.
- 'You are right, the atmosphere here is magical.' Gabriel begins to relax and enjoy the moment. The adjective magique describes an enchanting setting.
- 'Then go join Sofia and be happy!' Arlette sends him off with heartfelt warmth. The imperative sois comes from the verb être.
