## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Welcome to Cannes, Juliette.' Andrea arrives at the hotel with an affectionate greeting. The formula 'bienvenue a' takes a geographical destination without an article.
- 'Hello Andrea, how are you?' Juliette welcomes her agent warmly. The polite inquiry 'comment vas-tu' uses the informal 2nd person singular.
- 'We have a problem with the tuxedo.' Andrea delivers the diplomatic wardrobe dilemma. The masculine noun 'smoking' refers specifically to a formal tuxedo or dinner jacket.
- 'The Chinese actress Gong Li is wearing one for the opening ceremony.' Andrea explains the conflict. The adverbial pronoun 'en' replaces 'un smoking' with the verb porter.
- 'An Armani too?' Juliette checks the specific fashion house. The adverb 'aussi' queries whether both outfits share the same luxury designer.
- 'No, another designer. But it is still a tuxedo.' Andrea insists on the visual clash. The concessive locution 'quand meme' underscores the visual redundancy on the red carpet.
- 'We cannot have two women in the same outfit at the same time.' Andrea states the red-carpet protocol. The phrase 'en meme temps' emphasizes simultaneous public exposure.
- 'But all the men can dress the same!' Juliette points out the unfair double standard. The reflexive verb 's'habiller' is paired with the adverbial 'pareil'.
- 'I felt very good in that tuxedo.' Juliette expresses her disappointment. The imperfect 'je me sentais' describes her prior comfort and confidence.
- 'I will call her agent and we will discuss it.' Andrea offers to negotiate a resolution. The near future 'je vais appeler' promises immediate intervention.
- 'That will be useless.' Juliette realistically assesses the balance of power. The simple future 'servira a rien' comes from the idiom servir a rien.
- 'Gong Li is the icon of L'Oreal, the main partner of the festival.' Andrea admits the corporate clout. The noun 'icone' describes a global ambassador for a cosmetics brand.
- 'But we have good news. Your second choice has been repaired.' Andrea presents an alternative. The passive structure 'a ete repare' uses the compound past auxiliary.
- 'But it is a whole production! And I need fifteen-centimeter heels.' Juliette highlights the complexity. The expression 'toute une production' denotes an elaborate and exhausting undertaking.
- 'We could arrange a new fitting with three outfits.' Andrea proposes a quick styling session. The conditional 'on pourrait organiser' suggests a practical compromise.
- 'No, I think that will be very fine.' Juliette decides to make the best of the backup gown. The colloquial future 'ca ira tres bien' accepts the solution cheerfully.
