## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Good evening everyone, thank you for coming.' Juliette greets the imaginary audience on stage. The polite expression 'merci d'etre venus' uses the past infinitive to thank guests for having arrived.
- 'It is with immense pride that I welcome you here.' Juliette delivers an emotional opening line. The noun phrase 'une immense fierte' conveys deep personal honor.
- 'In the most beautiful place in the world: a movie theater.' Juliette praises the cinema hall. The superlative structure 'le plus bel endroit' highlights the prestige of the venue.
- 'I have your earpiece. Do you want to try it?' The technician interrupts to fit the prompting device. The noun 'oreillette' refers to the discreet wireless ear receiver.
- 'How does it work?' Juliette asks about the technical operation. The question 'comment ca fonctionne' asks for a functional demonstration.
- 'I can show you if you like.' The technician offers a direct demonstration. The modal verb 'peux' expresses polite capability and assistance.
- 'Do you mind if we speak informally?' The technician asks to drop formal address. The reflexive verb 'se tutoyer' means to address one another with informal pronouns.
- 'No, not at all.' Juliette warmly accepts the informal address. The emphatic negative 'pas du tout' provides an immediate and friendly reassurance.
- 'Look straight ahead, as if I were not here.' The technician guides Juliette's stage posture. The conditional conjunction 'comme si' sets up an imaginary scenario.
- 'I hear you perfectly. It works.' The technician checks the sound levels through his microphone. The adverb 'parfaitement' confirms clear audio transmission.
- 'Okay, I hear you too.' Juliette speaks back instinctively into the open microphone. The adverb 'aussi' indicates reciprocal reception.
- 'Do not answer. No one knows that you have an earpiece.' The technician reminds her that the earpiece must remain a secret. The negative imperative 'ne repondez pas' warns her against speaking aloud.
- 'It is just a voice in your head, like your own thoughts.' The technician coaches her mental approach. The adverb 'juste' minimizes the intrusion of the prompting audio.
- 'Oh, yes! I understand now.' Juliette catches on to the technique. The present tense verb 'comprends' signals immediate comprehension.
- 'Do you receive me? Take a step back.' The technician tests the wireless range. The imperative 'faites un pas en arriere' commands spatial repositioning.
- 'Very good, it is operational.' The technician concludes the audio test successfully. The adjective 'operationnelle' confirms full readiness for the ceremony.
