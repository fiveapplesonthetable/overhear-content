## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Good evening.' This greeting you switch to once it gets dark. Camille points out the time.
- 'It's really late.' To talk about the time, French uses 'it is', just like English.
- 'Andréa has already left.' Here's our first grammar point. Verbs of coming and going make their past with 'to be', not 'to have'. And the past form gets a silent extra letter because Andréa is a woman.
- 'Yes, sorry.' He apologises for the hour, but he doesn't leave.
- 'Andréa left a script here for me.' Compare: this verb, 'to leave something', takes 'to have' in the past. The little word for 'me' is squeezed in before the verb.
- 'No, she didn't leave anything.' The negative wraps around the helper verb, with the word for 'nothing' as the second half.
- 'See for yourself!' Camille gives a polite command. They both still use the formal 'you', even though they're about the same age.
- 'Thanks, Camille.' He has his script, and now he takes his time.
- 'Imagine there's an invisible line between us.' Another polite command, 'imagine'. The phrase in the middle, 'there is', is one you'll hear in every scene.
- 'It's a weird thing directors do.' The word for 'thing' here is the casual one, 'thingy'. He is showing off a film trick, framing her with his hands.
- 'When you watch films, love scenes...' He leaves the sentence hanging, on purpose.
- 'They really kiss.' The verb has a little extra word in front meaning 'each other'. That's our second grammar point: some verbs come with this small pronoun when two people do something to one another.
- 'No!' Camille doesn't believe a word of it.
- 'Yes, they do it for real.' To contradict a 'no', French has a special 'yes', different from the usual one. Then the expression 'for real'.
- 'They do it like that?' Camille asks a question made just with her voice going up. The words for 'like that' are worth learning as a pair.
- 'More or less.' And then, of course, they kiss. Meanwhile, down the corridor, Andréa is losing patience.
- 'What am I going to tell Françoise?' The near future: 'I am going' plus 'to tell'. If Gabor picks Line, Andréa has a big problem.
- 'I can't concentrate!' Literally 'I don't manage to concentrate'. The noise from the next room isn't helping, and she goes to see.
- 'I'm sorry.' Hippolyte, caught red-handed.
- 'Sorry.' Camille says the same word, but written with the feminine ending, because she is a woman. You can't hear the difference; you can only see it on the page.
