## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'No, Gabor, you're not going to die.' The action word 'to go' plus another action word is the near future; here it's negative, so the two little words for 'not' wrap around the action word 'to go'.
- 'I'll call you tomorrow.' French often uses the present for a near plan like this.
- 'Sorry. I'm here.' The word for 'sorry' ends with an extra letter because Andréa is a woman. She hangs up and joins the others.
- 'Thank you. Samuel didn't leave a will.' The negative changes a will into no will: the short article before will is the one French normally uses after a negated have or leave. Here will means the inheritance document, not a future action.
- 'So I inherit all his shares.' The word in the middle means 'so' or 'therefore'. Samuel's part of the agency now belongs to her.
- 'I don't want to work at the agency. I want to sell.' Hélène builds two sentences the same way: the word for 'want', then another action word in its basic form. First negative, then positive.
- 'To whom?' The first word is to, not at: it introduces the person who would buy the shares. The question asks for the buyer's identity, not the price.
- 'I don't know. To the quickest.' The middle word makes quick into the quickest. The final description stands for the quickest buyer, so Hélène need not repeat buyer itself.
- 'We can buy Samuel's shares.' Buy here means acquire the shares from their new owner, Hélène. The French action word can also mean buy back, but it does not require the buyer to have owned the thing before.
- 'What do you mean, we?' Literally 'what do you want to say'. That's how French asks what someone means.
- 'Us. The four of us.' French uses the same word for we before an action and us standing alone here. Mathias means the four partners: himself, Andréa, Gabriel and Arlette.
- 'Me, I have nothing. I rent my flat.' Starting with the strong word for 'me' puts the focus on herself. Andréa doesn't own a home, so she has nothing to put in.
- 'Money can be found.' Literally, money finds itself. That self pattern can express that something is possible or obtainable, without saying who does it. Mathias treats raising the money as a solvable problem.
- 'That's easy to say when you have some!' The small word just before the last verb means 'some of it', the money.
- 'You could earn more.' The ending on the verb makes it 'could'. Mathias thinks Andréa chooses her actors badly.
- 'With rubbish films? No, thank you.' The word for 'rubbish' is a very common way to say something is bad. Andréa would rather work with artists she believes in.
- 'Would you rather ASK disappeared?' Mathias addresses the partners as a group with the plural you. The French wording literally asks whether they prefer the agency to disappear. English would rather makes the same choice sound natural. Let's hear the whole meeting again.
