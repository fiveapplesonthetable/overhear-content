## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'All right, darling? You're late.' The word for 'darling' is feminine here, because she's talking to a woman. And Arlette uses the casual 'you' with her younger colleague.
- 'A coffee?' Damien, the waiter, knows them well.
- 'Yes, thanks, Damien.' Simple and friendly.
- 'I slept badly.' The everyday past tense: the verb 'have', then the action. The word for 'badly' sits in the middle.
- 'Were you out partying?' Literally 'did you make the party?'. That's the French way to say you went out and had fun.
- 'Yeah, it was wild!' The first word is the casual 'yes', and the last is a slangy word for 'crazy'. She's being ironic, of course.
- 'I spent the night on my bank's website.' In French it's 'the site of my bank': the owner comes last.
- 'And you?' The strong form of the casual 'you', used after 'and'.
- 'Me too, I did my accounts.' Arlette has been counting her money as well.
- 'I might as well spend what I have on the agency.' The first word gives the idea 'might as well'. Arlette has made up her mind.
- 'Your life insurance? Are you sure?' The word for 'sure' takes an extra letter, because Arlette is a woman.
- 'I have no children, apart from Jean Gabin.' After a negative, the word for 'some' shrinks to a tiny linking word before the noun. And Jean Gabin, named after a famous French actor, is her dog.
- 'So, what's the point?' A short, fixed expression: literally 'to what good?'.
- 'You're all my family.' Here the formal 'you' is really the plural one: she means all of them at the agency.
- 'All of you, and my actors.' The agency is her whole life.
- 'Thank you, Arlette.' Andréa is touched.
- 'Do you need to pee, Jean Gabin?' Literally 'do you want to do pee-pee?', the way you'd talk to a child, or a dog.
- 'Wait, he's peeing!' Too late. The waiter's command uses the formal 'you', the polite form a waiter uses with customers. So the tender moment ends with a puddle on the terrace. Now the whole breakfast once more, in French. Let's listen to the whole scene once more without interruption.
