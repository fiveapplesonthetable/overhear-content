## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello, Line.' He uses her first name, but listen: all through lunch they'll use the polite 'you' with each other. That's the first thing to notice today. Then he thanks her.
- 'It's really kind of you to come.' The last word has a feminine ending, because the person who came is a woman. Line answers with a smile.
- 'I'm really happy to be here.' Literally 'I'm really pleased', and again the feminine ending. Gabriel apologises.
- 'Sorry, it's a bit last-minute.' The last word looks like 'improvised', and it means exactly that. Line waves it away.
- 'Not at all. It was a good idea.' 'It was' is a past form you'll hear constantly. And she adds something gracious.
- 'It's lovely to see you.' Literally, seeing you gives pleasure. This is an ordinary warm greeting when you are pleased to meet someone again, not a formal promise to do something for them.
- 'Our Samuel... It's hard to believe.' Our makes the name affectionate, marking Samuel as someone dear to both of them. Hard to believe describes news that is difficult to accept, even though they know it is true.
- 'He was your mentor, wasn't he?' The little tag at the end works like 'isn't it' or 'wasn't he' in English, and it never changes. Gabriel answers sadly.
- 'Yes, we're all orphans now.' Literally all orphans: Gabriel compares the agency's loss of Samuel to children losing a parent. All has a final s sound here because it stands for all of us, rather than sitting before the word for days as in every day.
- 'Two months ago, we had lunch here together.' The opening three words before two months place the lunch in the past. Together at the end underlines that Line and Samuel shared the meal; here refers to this restaurant.
- 'What a good friend. What a gentleman!' This same little exclamation word twice: it means 'what a'. The last word is borrowed straight from English. Then a surprise.
- 'I felt one foot slip towards the grave.' Felt is followed by the foot and what it did: slip. Towards gives a direction, not an arrival. She describes feeling close to death herself when she heard the news, then gives the image a comic twist.
- 'But I quickly pulled it back.' The little word for 'it', meaning the foot, sits in front of the helper verb. Gabriel can't help smiling.
- 'You're not the melancholy type.' The negative wraps around the verb 'are', and the formal 'you' again. Line agrees cheerfully.
- 'Melancholy? No, no!' She repeats his description as a question and then firmly rejects it. The word has the same ending when describing a man or a woman, so her repetition does not need a different ending.
- 'And the rumours from Rio, are they true?' French can name the topic first and then ask is that true. The singular that treats the whole story as one claim, even though rumours is plural. Gabriel sets the record straight.
- 'No, I swear, he really did swallow a wasp.' The past once more: 'has' plus 'swallowed'. As strange as it sounds, it's true. And Line has the last word.
- 'What an idiot!' This same exclamation word as before. She says it with love, the way you'd talk about a dear old friend. Here's the whole lunch one more time.
