## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'What are you playing at with my son?' Literally 'at what are you playing?'. He uses the casual 'you', as a boss to a young assistant, and as a father, though only she knows that.
- 'I didn't know he was your son.' Two past forms here, for states that lasted: 'I knew' and 'it was'. With the 'not' around the first one.
- 'He didn't tell me.' A short sentence packed with small words: the word for 'me' and the word for 'it' both sit before the verb, inside the 'not'.
- 'You're completely irresponsible!' The long word in the middle means 'completely'. That ending often works like English 'ly'.
- 'You're the one who lies to everybody!' Literally 'it's you who lies'. That's how French puts weight on one person. And 'everybody' is literally 'all the world'.
- 'You're a coward!' Camille finally says what she thinks of him.
- 'You never told the truth about us!' Here 'never' is two little words around the helper verb, just like 'not'. 'Us' means Camille and her mother.
- 'Stay away from my family.' A casual command. He has no idea how close to home that is.
- 'I'm part of your family too!' Literally 'I make part of your family'. The last two words, 'me too', add strong emphasis on who she is.
- 'Have you got a light, Noémie?' Later, alone outside with a cigarette, Camille asks Mathias's assistant. Literally 'do you have some fire?'
- 'No, I don't smoke.' A plain 'not' sentence: the two little words around the verb 'to smoke'.
- 'But wait... There you go.' Noémie, who has never been very friendly, finds a lighter and lights it for her anyway.
- 'Thanks.' The shortest way to say it, and you'll use it a hundred times a day in France.
- 'It's nothing.' Spoken French often drops the first half of the 'not', so this is how people really say it.
- 'Sorrows, men, it all goes up in smoke.' The verb means 'leaves', so it's literally 'everything leaves in smoke'. A sad, kind little piece of wisdom to end the day.
