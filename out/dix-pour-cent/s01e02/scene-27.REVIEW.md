## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Why not take them both?' Notice the shape: 'why' plus 'not' plus an action word in its basic form. It's a neat way to suggest an idea. Gabor can't believe his ears.
- 'Both of them?' Literally 'the two'. It's the same phrase as at the end of Andréa's question.
- 'Bonnie and Clyde at eighty, with two women!' The famous couple of robbers, but old, and both played by women. The number eighty in French is literally 'four twenties', an old way of counting.
- 'That makes it modern.' The French wording literally says that becomes modern. That points to their revised story: two women playing the couple of robbers. Become describes a change, rather than a permanent quality.
- 'You mean a real couple of women?' Literally 'you want to say'. That's how French says 'you mean'. Gabor uses the polite 'you' with the agents: this is business.
- 'Yes. Line Renaud and Françoise Fabian.' Andréa's yes confirms Gabor's understanding. She then joins the two full names with and, presenting the actresses as a pair rather than two competing choices.
- 'We've just seen them.' Come followed by the short linking word and see means have just seen. Them stands for Line and Françoise and sits before see. Andréa points to the tribute they have all watched.
- 'Each one is a star.' The first word is the feminine 'each one', because he's talking about two women.
- 'But together…' But turns away from considering each actress separately. Together invites Gabor to imagine the two in the same film. Andréa leaves the thought unfinished to make the combination sound intriguing.
- 'Did you see that?' The French past uses have plus seen. That points back to the actresses' joint appearance at the tribute. The short question asks Gabor to share Andréa's reaction to what they watched.
- 'That's not bad.' The two negative words surround is. In ordinary speech the first is often dropped, as in the original scene. Not bad can offer cautious praise, rather than merely denying that something is terrible.
- 'It's totally unexpected.' Totally strengthens unexpected: Gabriel sells the combination as completely surprising. Unexpected describes the proposal itself, rather than either individual actress.
- 'Everyone will talk about it.' Literally 'all the world'. The verb is in the future: the ending tells you it hasn't happened yet. Andréa seizes the moment.
- 'And you get two stars!' The French wording says you have two stars. Have presents the casting as part of the proposed package; English get captures what the director gains by agreeing.
- 'We'll give you a good price.' Literally, we make you a good price. Good describes a price favourable to the person paying. The short you comes before make and identifies the person receiving the offer.
- 'Outjet!' Gabor raises a toast. This is the way the subtitles represent his exclamation; it is not a standard French expression to learn for ordering a drink. The agents follow his lead.
- 'Er, yes, outjet!' The hesitation comes before yes. Andréa then echoes the unfamiliar toast, using repetition to join in without asking what it means.
- 'Well then, outjet!' The opening well then can introduce a response or mark acceptance. Gabriel follows it by repeating the toast too. Let's hear the whole exchange once more.
