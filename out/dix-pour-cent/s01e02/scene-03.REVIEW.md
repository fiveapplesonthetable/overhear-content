## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'It bothers me to see you sitting there.' The little word for 'me' comes before the verb 'to bother'. And here Andréa and Mathias use the polite 'you' with each other, a sign of how cold things are between them.
- 'It's a chair.' Mathias plays innocent.
- 'No, it's Samuel's place.' To say whose something is, French says 'the place of Samuel'.
- 'He died six days ago.' The phrase that usually means 'there is' also means 'ago' when it comes before a length of time. Someone suggests another seat; Mathias says that one is Camille's, isn't it, Camille? And Camille can only say yes.
- 'Let's not waste time on details.' A 'let's' command, made negative: the two negative words go around the verb.
- 'We have urgent problems.' The adjective comes after the noun, as most French adjectives do.
- 'Starting with Samuel's clients.' Samuel's stars bring in a big share of the agency's money.
- 'If word gets out, they'll leave.' The first part literally means 'if it becomes known'. Then the near future: 'they are going to leave'.
- 'Here's what I suggest.' The first word is how you present something: 'here is'. He hands out a document.
- 'A spreadsheet with what each person earns?' Andréa can't believe it: he has divided up Samuel's clients in a table, with everyone's earnings in it.
- 'Exactly.' Same word as in English, said with complete calm.
- 'You want Françoise Fabian?' Andréa decides to tell them about her morning.
- 'I saw her this morning.' The little word for 'her' sits before the verb, and loses its vowel. And because 'her' comes first, the past form of 'to see' takes an extra e on paper. You can't hear it.
- 'You approached her?' Mathias suspects her of stealing a client behind everyone's back.
- 'No! She's the one who asked to see me.' The pattern 'it's her who' puts the stress on 'her'. In the show Andréa also mocks him with a fake German army title.
- 'She wants you?' Mathias can't quite believe it.
- 'Yes. Well, it's complicated.' The second word here is a little hesitation, like 'well' or 'I mean'.
- 'She says she doesn't want to do Gabor's film.' Andréa leaves out the part where she insulted her.
- 'I'll call her.' The near future once more, with the little word for 'her' just before the verb.
- 'No, Mathias, I'm handling it!' Literally 'it's me who manages'. The same pattern as before, to say 'me, not you'.
- 'Sort it out in twenty-four hours or I step in!' A polite command, but a threat all the same. As the meeting breaks up, a colleague tells Andréa she's a legend for what she said to Françoise. A legend of embarrassment, she replies.
