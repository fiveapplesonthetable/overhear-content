## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Samuel was an excellent agent.' The verb is 'to be' in the past form used for describing how things were. Then she gets to the point.
- 'But big parts are rare for a woman my age.' Literally 'of my age'. She's in her eighties, and she knows the business.
- 'I want movement, action, energy!' Notice the little words in front of each noun. They mean 'some': French puts them in where English puts nothing. The form changes with the noun: masculine, or shortened before a vowel.
- 'Find me something powerful!' A casual command, with 'me' stuck on after a hyphen. And after the word for 'something', an adjective needs a small word meaning 'of' in front of it.
- 'Do you follow me?' Line uses the casual 'you' with Gabriel; they've known each other for years. Gabriel nods, and starts making things up.
- 'Yes. Line, we have a great project right now.' The little word for 'we' in everyday French is the same one that can mean 'people' in general.
- 'I thought of you.' And here's something to notice: Gabriel says the polite 'you' to her. She calls him the casual way, he stays respectful. A younger agent with a legend, that feels right.
- 'Really?' She's flattered, and curious.
- 'It's a film by Gabor Rajevski, with Michel Piccoli.' A famous director's name and a famous actor's name. Line lights up at the second one.
- 'I adore Michel!' The word for 'I' loses its vowel and sticks to the verb, because the verb starts with a vowel: a very common French sound.
- 'It's the story of an old couple who do hold-ups.' Gabriel describes it as Bonnie and Clyde in their eighties. The word for 'old' comes before the noun, as it often does with short, common adjectives.
- 'Wonderful!' An easy one: it sounds just like the English word 'magnificent'.
- 'Why did you think of me?' The past of 'think' is made with 'have' plus a form of the verb, just like 'I have thought'. Gabriel has to think fast.
- 'Because the part is powerful, full of life!' He simply hands Line's own words back to her. Clever, or desperate.
- 'And shooting starts in three weeks.' The little word before 'three weeks' means 'in', counting forward from now.
- 'Three weeks? Why so soon?' Line isn't a beginner; she can smell a story.
- 'At first, Françoise Fabian was supposed to take the part.' The verb 'must' in this past form means 'was supposed to'. Now the truth is out, or half of it.
- 'She was just a bit tired.' The word for 'tired' has a feminine ending for Françoise. It's a polite little lie.
- 'Line Renaud in a Gabor Rajevski film?' She tries out the idea, saying her own name like a poster.
- 'I think that sounds really good.' Literally 'I find that it sounds'. Using 'find' for 'think' is very common in opinions.
- 'Why not?' Two little words, and Gabriel has a star. Now he only has to tell Andréa.
