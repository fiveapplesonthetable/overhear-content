## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'What is wrong with her suddenly?' Colette wonders why baby Flora is crying softly. Notice how 'has' leads directly into the noun meaning 'blow'.
- 'These are tears of relief.' Andrea understands that the baby feels the tension releasing. The key noun here means 'crying', anchored by its matching French article.
- 'Do not worry, it will be fine.' Colette offers calm reassurance. The two-part French negation brackets the verb for 'worry' on both sides.
- 'I was very angry with you.' Andrea admits her initial resentment after the breakup. Notice how 'was' leads directly into the noun meaning 'anger'.
- 'I understand your initial anger.' Colette acknowledges the pain she caused. The verb meaning 'understand' pairs naturally with the noun for 'anger' in everyday conversation.
- 'You had no other choice.' Andrea recognizes that leaving was necessary. Notice how 'had' leads directly into the noun meaning 'choice'.
- 'That forced me to reflect.' Andrea explains how the shock made her look at herself. This conversational past tense combines a helper verb with the past verb form for 'forced'.
- 'To reflect on our shared future?' Colette asks gently about Andrea's thoughts. Notice how 'reflect' leads directly into the noun meaning 'future'.
- 'I cannot change who I am.' Andrea accepts her nature as a dedicated agent. The clause hinges on the everyday verb meaning 'can'.
- 'I love you, but let us be honest.' The clause hinges on the everyday verb meaning 'love'.
- 'It is better that we stop there.' Andrea makes the painful decision to end their relationship. The clause hinges on the everyday verb meaning 'is worth'.
- 'It is undoubtedly the best decision.' Colette nods in quiet agreement. The key noun here means 'doubt', anchored by its matching French article.
- 'We will take care of Flora together.' Andrea confirms their lifelong parental commitment. French forms the near future by pairing the present tense of 'to go' directly with 'take care'.
- 'Yes, we will always be a family.' Colette confirms that their bond as co-parents remains unbroken. Notice how 'will be' leads directly into the noun meaning 'family'.
