## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'He died in his sleep.' The vet assures her it was peaceful. The verb meaning 'to be' pairs naturally with the noun for 'sleep' in everyday conversation.
- 'It is the best death.' He tries to make her feel better. In spoken French, the little word before 'the next word' drops its final vowel and attaches with an apostrophe.
- 'And for us too.' He adds that it is ideal for anyone. The adverb meaning 'also' gives the reply its conversational emphasis.
- 'It is true, thank you.' Arlette agrees sadly. A short, idiomatic conversational reply that keeps the exchange moving naturally.
- 'I reserved a place at the cemetery.' He took care of the arrangements. The verb meaning 'to have' pairs naturally with the noun for 'seat' in everyday conversation.
- 'They will come pick him up.' He explains the next step. The clause hinges on the everyday verb meaning 'to come'.
- 'Can I do anything else?' He offers further assistance. In everyday spoken French, rising intonation on 'can' at the end is enough to turn the sentence into a question.
- 'It is fine, thank you.' She declines politely. A short, idiomatic conversational reply that keeps the exchange moving naturally.
- 'Can you leave us?' She asks for some privacy. Inverting the verb and pronoun around 'can you' creates a crisp, polished question.

- 'He is really gone.' She realizes the finality of it. This conversational past tense combines a helper verb with the past participle for 'gone'.
- 'Goodbye, my dog.' She says a heartfelt farewell. Notice how 'to see again' leads directly into the noun meaning 'dog'.
