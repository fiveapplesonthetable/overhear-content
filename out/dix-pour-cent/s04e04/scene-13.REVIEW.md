## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Camille, Gabriel and Sarah are arguing!' Sofia eagerly shares the latest hallway drama. The reciprocal verb 'se disputer' means to argue or quarrel.
- 'What? That is not my problem.' Camille brushes aside the gossip to focus on business. The key noun here means 'problem', anchored by its matching French article.
- 'Did you wear that for the audition?' Camille points in disbelief at Sofia's extravagant golden dress. The verb meaning 'have' pairs naturally with the noun for 'audition' in everyday conversation.
- 'No, I did not go.' Sofia casually reveals that she skipped the meeting with the director. In the past tense with 'etre', the participle 'allee' agrees with a feminine speaker.
- 'Excuse me? You missed the appointment?' Camille can hardly believe what she is hearing. The verb 'manquer' means to miss an event or appointment.
- 'There is a screening soon.' Sofia claims she will meet the director casually at a movie screening instead. The feminine noun 'projection' refers to a film screening.
- 'It is much better like that.' Sofia defends her spontaneous strategy. The adverb 'mieux' is the comparative form meaning better.
- 'I worked hard for this role!' Camille reminds Sofia how much effort went into securing the casting call. Employed as an adverb, 'dur' means hard or intensely.
- 'Wait, are those my new shoes?' Camille suddenly notices her own designer heels on Sofia's feet. The descriptive adjective for 'new' agrees in gender and number with its noun.
- 'I took them for the photo.' Sofia confesses that she borrowed the shoes from Camille's office without asking. The verb meaning 'have' pairs naturally with the noun for 'photo' in everyday conversation.
- 'You do not want to work, Sofia!' Camille loses her temper at her client's unprofessional attitude. The clause hinges on the everyday verb meaning 'want'.
- 'You do not want to be an actress.' Camille accuses Sofia of preferring celebrity glamour over real acting. In French, professions after 'etre' omit the indefinite article.
- 'Now, take back your things and get out!' Camille demands her shoes back and orders Sofia to leave. The imperative 'sors' comes from the verb 'sortir'.
- 'There, I give you back your shoes!' Sofia angrily kicks off the heels and storms out of the room. The verb 'rendre' means to return or give back.
