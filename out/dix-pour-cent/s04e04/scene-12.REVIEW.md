## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Do not worry about Sandrine.' Sarah tries to soothe Gabriel's anxiety over his famous client. The negative imperative 'ne t'inquiete pas' means do not worry.
- 'She just wants a change of air.' Sarah believes the actress merely craves novelty. The idiom 'changer d'air' means to get a change of scenery.
- 'But she wants to quit cinema!' Gabriel fears losing his star client to the stage forever. The verb 'quitter' means to leave or abandon.
- 'She is going to have fun for five minutes.' Sarah predicts that the comedy experiment will be brief. The reflexive verb 's'amuser' means to have fun or enjoy oneself.
- 'Then she will quickly come back.' Sarah is confident that Sandrine will return to film. The conjunction 'puis' means then or next.
- 'Come back to what, in your opinion?' Gabriel half-heartedly follows her train of thought. The phrase 'a ton avis' means in your opinion.
- 'To her true love, of course!' Sarah speaks poetically about the actress's lifelong vocation. The expression 'grand amour' translates as great love or true love.
- 'And what is her true love?' Gabriel stares at his screen while asking the question. The informal question 'c'est quoi' asks what something is.
- 'It is cinema, my dear Gabriel.' Sarah states the obvious answer with gentle irony. The adjective 'cher' means dear.
- 'But you are not even listening to me!' Sarah realizes that Gabriel is ignoring her completely. The adverb 'meme' adds the emphasis even.
- 'All right, I am leaving now.' Fed up with his inattention, Sarah turns toward the door. The idiomatic verb 's'en aller' means to go away or leave.
- 'Yes I am, I am truly listening to you!' Gabriel looks up in a panic and denies ignoring her. In French, 'si' is used instead of 'oui' to contradict a negative statement.
- 'Do you want to come with me tonight?' Gabriel tries to make amends by inviting her out. The preposition 'avec' means with.
- 'No, you only think about work.' Sarah rejects his offer and walks down the hallway. The adverb 'seulement' restricts the meaning to only.
