## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'For example, what do you want?' Christophe leans back and tests Andrea's skills. The question uses the standard inversion with what.
- 'I want you to call François.' Andrea gets straight to her objective. Notice that the verb to want takes the subjunctive form of to call.
- 'Look at how I do it.' She sits closer and grins mischievously.
- 'I slide my hand in here and take your phone.' Demonstrating her move step by step, she narrates her action. The verb to slide is followed by the direct object.
- 'I slide my fingers across the screen.' She wakes up the screen with a light touch.
- 'I finish by sending it. Result!' With a quick tap, she announces her triumph. Notice the present participle construction introduced by the preposition in.
- 'What did you do?' Realizing what just happened, Christophe gasps. The question is in the conversational past tense with the auxiliary to have.
- 'You sent a message to Berléand!' He stares at his phone in total disbelief.
- 'It is not true!' Panicking at how quickly things moved, he shouts in shock.
- 'You told me to wait, and you did not come home.' The next morning in the apartment, Colette confronts her. The past participle agrees in the feminine with Andrea.
- 'It was strictly professional, you know that.' Andrea defends herself. The adverb ends with the classic suffix for adverbs of manner.
- 'We do what I want this evening.' Colette lays down the law. The pronoun one is used colloquially for we.
- 'We meet my friends.' She adds her firm plan. The verb to find or meet up takes a direct object.
