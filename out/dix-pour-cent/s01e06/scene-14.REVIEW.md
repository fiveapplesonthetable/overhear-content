## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'For example, what do I want?' The opening means 'for example'. The later question asks 'what do I want?', with Andréa setting up her own demonstration.
- 'I want you to call François.' French links wanting and someone else's action with a word meaning 'that'. Literally she wants 'that you call François'; English simply says 'you to call'.
- 'Look at how I do it.' The first word commands him to look. The question word after it means 'how', directing attention to the method she is about to demonstrate.
- 'I slide my hand in here and take your phone.' Two actions are linked by 'and': sliding her hand and taking the phone. The word for 'your' identifies the phone as Christophe's.
- 'I slide my fingers across the screen.' The two actions mean 'make slide', describing her moving her fingers. The last words place that movement across the phone's screen.
- 'I finish by sending it. Result!' The shortened object attached to the sending action means 'it', the message. The opening says she finishes by doing that final action.
- 'What did you do?' The opening frame asks 'what'. The completed action at the end means 'done', so Christophe is asking what she has just done.
- 'You sent a message to Berléand!' The completed action means 'sent'. The small word before the recipient's name means 'to', identifying who receives the message.
- 'It is not true!' The denial literally says 'it is not true'. Here it is a reaction of disbelief, like 'I cannot believe it!'.
- 'You told me to wait, and you did not come home.' We are now at home with Colette. The first attached object means 'to me'; the feminine ending on 'returned' refers to Andréa, who did not come home.
- 'It was strictly professional, you know that.' The opening looks back: 'it was'. The final little object before 'know' means 'that', pointing to her claim about the dinner being professional.
- 'We do what I want this evening.' The opening means 'we do'. The middle words mean 'what I want', making Colette's preference the rule for this evening.
- 'We meet my friends.' The action means meeting up with people. The possessive is plural because it belongs with her friends; the written ending shows these friends are women.
