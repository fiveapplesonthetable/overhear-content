## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'He would not pay in cash!' The opening means 'he would not pay'. The last two words mean 'in cash', rather than paying with liquid of any kind.
- 'Yes he would! The father-in-law owns real estate in Paris.' The first word answers yes to a negative claim. The hyphenated family word means father-in-law; the final words name property in Paris.
- 'When buyers purchase property, they pay in cash. That is well known.' The buyers and their action share the same word root. The following 'they' refers back to those buyers; the last sentence claims this is well known.
- 'So it is cash.' The first word draws a conclusion: 'so'. The following cash word is being used as a thing, not to describe a drink.
- 'That is how Mathias is going to become our boss.' The opening points to 'that way'. The later 'going to' introduces a change: Mathias is going to become their boss.
- 'And I will become his right-hand woman.' The action looks ahead: 'I will become'. The final body words literally mean 'right arm', but together describe a trusted deputy.
- 'Then he will ask you to marry him.' The small word before 'ask' means 'you'. Asking someone 'in marriage' is the usual way to say proposing marriage.
- 'You think so? No! I am going to take a look.' The first question means 'you think so?' The final expression for looking literally mentions throwing a glance, meaning taking a quick look.
- 'No, Noémie! Are you crazy? Be careful!' The feminine word for crazy describes Noémie. The last two words form a warning: 'be careful', rather than a request to manufacture attention.
- 'Mathias knows that everything I do is for his own good.' The words meaning 'everything that I do' introduce her defense. The final word is 'good': she claims she acts for Mathias's benefit.
- 'So he will reward my loyalty. Keep watch, okay?' The action 'will reward' looks to the future. The command after it tells Hervé to keep watch while she searches the bag.
- 'Jean Gabin! What? Jean Gabin is ruining the money!' The office dog is named Jean Gabin. The middle sequence means an action is happening right now: he is damaging the money.
- 'Give it back, Jean Gabin! I cannot believe it!' The command to give back has 'it' attached after a hyphen. The next exclamation literally says 'it is not true', expressing disbelief.
- 'Noémie, get out of there!' The command means 'get out'. The final word points to the place she must leave: 'there'. Hervé now wants Noémie out of the office.
