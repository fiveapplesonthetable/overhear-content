## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I was on holiday with my parents in Spain.' The opening looks back with 'I was'. The holiday word is plural in French, and the final place expression puts the family in Spain.
- 'The hotel had a pool. I went there with my brother.' The first action describes what the hotel had. The attached place word in the second sentence means 'there': the pool he entered with his brother.
- 'We played a stupid game.' The opening 'we' is followed by a completed action: played. The short word after the action means 'at', linking playing with the game.
- 'We tried to stay sitting on the bottom.' The first action describes what they were trying to do repeatedly. The final position means at the bottom, with the pool understood.
- 'But one day, the pump trapped my swimsuit.' The words for 'one day' introduce a particular event. The pump is the subject of the completed action: it trapped his swimsuit.
- 'I was very scared. I thought I was going to die.' French expresses being scared as having fear. The second sentence looks back at what he believed would happen: he was going to die.
- 'And my brother saved me.' The object attached to the completed action means 'me'. His brother is the person who saved him; François is the person saved.
- 'After that, I never went back into a pool.' The opening places this after the accident. The two negative words mean 'never', denying any return to a swimming pool afterwards.
- 'It's in the past now.' The final word means 'now'. The past is treated as a thing here, so the statement places that experience behind him.
- 'But it is magnificent! That is Dom Juan!' François now gives his interpretation of the director's story. The first description means magnificent; the next sentence links the experience to the play's character.
- 'His fear of death, desire, pleasure!' The possessive means 'his', attached to fear. The other two items name desire and pleasure, the themes Christophe hears in the actor's experience.
- 'No, it was a childhood game that went wrong.' The first word rejects that interpretation. The final action literally means 'turned badly', the French way of saying a situation went wrong.
- 'That's exactly it: Dom Juan doesn't know how to swim!' The word before 'that' means 'exactly'. The following negative says the play's character does not know how to swim either.
- 'You see! You can't throw him in the pool.' The opening asks whether Andréa's point is clear: 'you see'. The short object before throwing means 'him', François or his character.
- 'That's why there's the duck!' The opening means 'that is why'. The later three words mean 'there is'; the thing they introduce is the duck that makes the staging possible.
- 'Not the duck again! No!' The word after 'not' means 'again' here. François rejects the return of the duck proposal, even after the explanation of his fear.
