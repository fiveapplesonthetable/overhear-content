## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'And Philippe, do you know who I mean?' The first name introduces someone from Camille's family. The final words literally mean 'who I want to say', the French way of saying who she means.
- 'Mom's brother.' The family word means brother. The last word is Mum; in this reply Mathias identifies Philippe through Camille's mother.
- 'Yes, Philippe. He had a little movie shop.' The action looks back at what Philippe had. The last words describe a shop for films, simplifying the source's video-rental shop for this retelling.
- 'I was thirteen or fourteen, I went there all the time.' French gives childhood age with 'had'. The attached place word in the second half means 'there'; the final words say she went all the time.
- 'That's where I discovered cinema.' The location word is 'there'. The completed action means 'discovered', making that film shop the place where her interest in cinema began.
- 'Aren't you eating?' The two negative words surround 'eat'. With question intonation, this asks why the person is not eating rather than telling them not to eat.
- 'I'm not very hungry.' French expresses hunger with 'have'. The negative includes 'very', so Mathias says he does not have much hunger: he is not very hungry.
- 'Does your stomach hurt? Have some star-anise tea.' The first question asks about a stomach ache. The next command means 'take' or 'have', followed by the name of the star-anise tea she recommends.
- 'They're little stars that smell very nice.' The plural opening means 'they are'. The little stars are described as smelling very good; the final word describes their smell, not their moral character.
- 'Maybe it's hard to find at the hotel.' The beginning means 'perhaps'. The final word gives the place where finding this tea might be difficult: the hotel.
- 'The place is quite fancy. They surely have everything.' The opening names this place. The word before 'chic' means 'rather' or 'quite'; the second sentence guesses that such a hotel has everything.
- 'By the way, did you call Dominique Besnehard?' The opening two words introduce a new point, like 'by the way'. The completed action asks whether Camille has called the producer yet.
- 'No, not yet. Why?' The reply combines 'not' with 'yet'. The following one-word question asks why Mathias wants to know.
- 'I think Andréa prefers that you stay.' The first action is 'think' or 'believe'. The rest reports Andréa's preference: she would like Camille to stay at the agency.
- 'Really? Good night.' The first word asks for confirmation: 'really?' The next two words are the standard nighttime farewell, wishing a good night.
- 'Good night, Camille.' The farewell is returned with Camille's name. The feminine shape of 'good' matches the feminine word for night.
