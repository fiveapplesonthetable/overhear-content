## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'What? Why not?' Both opening words are questions: 'what?' and 'why not?' The second asks for a reason to reject the proposed compromise.
- 'It is completely absurd!' The description ends with 'completely absurd'. The long word before 'absurd' strengthens the judgment, rather than naming another problem.
- 'You wanted Berléand, you have him.' The first action looks back at what he wanted. The short object before 'have' means 'him': he wanted Berléand and now has him.
- 'When I was little, I was seven years old.' French gives age with 'have'. The final words literally say 'I had seven years', meaning he was seven years old.
- 'My father left, he left my mother.' Two completed actions tell the same loss. First his father went away; then the next action specifies that he left the boy's mother.
- 'I was in the swimming pool, with my inflatable duck.' The first half places him in the swimming pool. The final word describes the duck as inflatable, something that can be filled with air.
- 'Do you understand why I need this duck?' The first question asks whether Andréa understands. The middle words literally mean 'I have need', the French way to express needing something.
- 'Yes. But Dom Juan will not be in the water.' The negative surrounds 'will be', placing Dom Juan outside the water. Andréa acknowledges the story without accepting its staging demand.
- 'I could fire him!' The opening action means 'I could', leaving it hypothetical. The short object before the firing action means 'him', referring to François.
- 'With one week to go before opening night?' The time words count down to opening night. The word for 'first' names the premiere here, so the question means 'with only a week left?'.
- 'I do not care!' This whole expression means 'I do not care'. Its two short attached words belong together; translating each literally would miss his dismissive reaction.
- 'I know the role by heart.' The final two words mean 'by heart'. He is saying he has memorized the role, rather than learned it using his heart.
- 'I am not afraid to go on stage!' The middle word is fear. French says 'have fear' where English says 'be afraid'. The final action means going onto the stage.
- 'I am sure of it.' The shortened word after 'I' means 'of it' here. The final feminine ending matches Andréa, who is saying she is sure.
- 'Except it will be in a warehouse in the suburbs.' The first two words mean 'except that'. The setting is then a warehouse in the suburbs; this is where Andréa says his replacement plan would lead.
- 'Where you were acting before Berléand.' The first word means 'there', followed by 'where'. The final 'before' compares his old working life with his career before Berléand.
- 'Are you threatening me?' The little word before 'threaten' means 'me'. With statement order and a rising tone, he asks whether Andréa is threatening him.
- 'Not at all.' These three words form the complete reply 'not at all'. The final word means 'all', strengthening her denial.
- 'Do not force the theatre director to choose.' The negative command tells him not to force a choice. The final action means 'choose'; Andréa warns that the theatre's director has a decision to make.
