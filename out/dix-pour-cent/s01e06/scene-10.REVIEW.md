## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Do something.' The director demands immediate action from Andrea. Faites is from the verb faire.
- 'Otherwise I will have to sue you, I have no choice.' Sinon means otherwise, and le choix means choice.
- 'Yes, of course. You can count on me.' Compter sur means to rely or count on someone.
- 'It is a disaster for the agency.' Une catastrophe is a feminine noun meaning a disaster.
- 'Is it that bad? It is only theatre, after all.' Quand meme is a common phrase meaning after all.
- 'Do you know what this could cost?' Pouvoir means can or could, and couter means to cost.
- 'We cannot let this punch story get out.' Un coup de poing is the French term for a punch.
- 'We must stifle this right now.' Il faut is followed by an infinitive meaning one must or we must.
- 'When my mother has an unhappy customer, she gives her friends a discount.' Un geste means a concession.
- 'She does not lose any customers.' Perdre means to lose, and la clientele means the customer base.
- 'So we lower the rates for all our stars?' Baisser les tarifs means to lower the prices or rates.
- 'Call them yourself, then!' Andrea uses an imperative with the emphatic pronoun vous-meme.
- 'Hello, Hippolyte? Hello, this is Camille from ASK.' Camille makes the phone call to Mathias's son.
- 'Andrea asked me to call you about your contracts.' Au sujet de means on the subject of or about.
- 'Can you come by to check them and sign them?' Passer means to stop by, and signer means to sign.
