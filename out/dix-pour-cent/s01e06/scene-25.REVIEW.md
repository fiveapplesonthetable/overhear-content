## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Refusing to swim is refusing the norm!' An infinitive verb can serve directly as a sentence subject.
- 'Dom Juan\'s thirst for life is right there.' Colette points to the passage in her book that captures Dom Juan's character.
- 'Everything is allowed, except the norm of monogamy.' She explains that Dom Juan defies every social rule except his fear of commitment.
- 'He is so afraid of this norm.' Colette is really talking about Andréa's terror of settling down with one person.
- 'He takes refuge with anything wearing a skirt.' She adds bitterly that he chases every woman in a skirt to avoid real intimacy.
- 'It isn't his desire that stops him being faithful.' The construction 'empecher de' means 'to prevent from'.
- 'It\'s his fear!' Colette insists that fear, not courage, drives his constant conquests.
- 'Pardon? Can you repeat that?' Christophe is fascinated by her theory and asks Colette to repeat what she just said.
- 'About the fear of the norm?' Christophe wants to hear more about this fear of monogamy for his staging.
- 'I am going to give you my book.' The near future uses 'aller' plus infinitive.
- 'You will understand.' Colette hands him her book and promises it will all make sense when he reads it.
- 'I am going to sign it.' Colette offers to sign the copy for him before leaving.
- 'Donjuanism or The Sickness of the Bond.' Colette recites the formal title of her book.
- 'Shall I put my phone number too?' Christophe flirts with Colette by offering to write his phone number inside.
- 'No. The dedication is enough.' Colette firmly turns down Christophe's advance.
- 'Just the dedication?' Christophe accepts the rejection with mild disappointment.
- 'Bravo! Well done, director!' Francois mocks the young director from the stage.
