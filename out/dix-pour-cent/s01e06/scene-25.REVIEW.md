## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Refusing to swim is refusing the norm!' An infinitive verb can serve directly as a sentence subject.
- 'Dom Juan\'s thirst for life is right there.' The adverb 'la' points out the core of his personality.
- 'Everything is allowed, except the norm of monogamy.' The preposition 'sauf' marks an exception.
- 'He is so afraid of this norm.' The idiom 'avoir peur de' expresses fear of something.
- 'He takes refuge with anything wearing a skirt.' The relative pronoun 'qui' serves as subject of the clause.
- 'It isn't his desire that stops him being faithful.' The construction 'empecher de' means 'to prevent from'.
- 'It\'s his fear!' The demonstrative 'c\'est' puts sharp rhetorical focus on fear.
- 'Pardon? Can you repeat that?' The modal 'pouvez' asks a polite request.
- 'About the fear of the norm?' The preposition 'sur' indicates the topic in question.
- 'I am going to give you my book.' The near future uses 'aller' plus infinitive.
- 'You will understand.' The simple future 'comprendrez' promises future clarity.
- 'I am going to sign it.' The direct object pronoun 'le' precedes the infinitive.
- 'Donjuanism or The Sickness of the Bond.' Colette recites the formal title of her book.
- 'Shall I put my phone number too?' The pronoun 'y' refers back to inside the book.
- 'No. The dedication is enough.' The verb 'suffire' firmly declines Christophe's advance.
- 'Just the dedication?' The adverb 'juste' expresses mild disappointment.
- 'Bravo! Well done, director!' Francois mocks the young director from the stage.
