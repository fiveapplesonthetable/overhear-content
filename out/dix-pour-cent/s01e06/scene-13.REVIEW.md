## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Christophe, you cannot say no.' Andrea corners the stubborn young theatre director over the phone. The verb can in the negative takes standard French negation.
- 'My pride won't allow it.' She insists that she will not take no for an answer. The compound noun for self-respect or pride is joined with a hyphen.
- 'I have lots of things to tell you.' She promises an exciting conversation. The adjective for full is used colloquially with of to mean tons of or lots of.
- 'No, I have to go home.' In Andrea's apartment, Colette gets ready to leave to finish her work.
- 'I still have a big part of my report to write.' She explains the crunch on her tax audit. Notice the formal verb for drafting or writing a report.
- 'No, stay, please!' Andrea pulls her back. The imperative form of stay uses the informal second-person ending.
- 'You dress like that for a work dinner?' Looking at Andrea's chic outfit, Colette asks suspiciously. The reflexive verb for getting dressed takes the preposition for like this.
- 'Tonight, I play it sober and classy.' Andrea explains her strategy for charming the young director.
- 'It is a present for you.' To make Colette feel at home, Andrea hands her a small package containing a spare toothbrush.
- 'That way you won't need to bring your own anymore.' Andrea smiles warmly. The conjunction for that way is followed by the future tense.
- 'I celebrate my birthday tomorrow evening at the theatre.' Later at the restaurant, Christophe brings up his plans.
- 'I would really like you to come.' He looks across the table and adds warmly. The conditional form of to like is followed by the subjunctive form of to come.
- 'As a director, you are gifted at getting what you want.' Andrea flatters him with a sharp observation. The phrase literally means in the role of.
- 'I am not bad.' Christophe smirks with false modesty. The negation wraps around the verb to be.
