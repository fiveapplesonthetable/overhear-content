## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Did your wife and your father-in-law know about this tax audit?' The opening possessives mean 'your', addressed formally to Mathias. The middle expression literally means being aware, asking whether his family knew about the audit.
- 'Yes.' One word confirms that they knew. No extra explanation is offered with this yes.
- 'Won't that affect their intention to buy out my shares?' The opening negative question asks about an effect on their intention. The last words name buying her ownership shares, rather than physical parts of an object.
- 'I don't know, Hélène.' The two negative words surround 'know'. The final name identifies the person Mathias is answering: the agency owner's widow.
- 'It is unbelievable! How am I going to sell them?' The first sentence expresses disbelief. The following question asks how she will sell them; the small object before 'sell' refers to her shares.
- 'Without fresh money, the agency will close after this audit.' The opening says 'without fresh money'. The later 'going to' predicts closure, with the audit identifying the event after which the danger will become real.
- 'Do you all understand that?' The word for 'all' belongs to the people being addressed. Hélène wants the whole room to understand the risk, rather than just Mathias.
- 'What then? Are we going bankrupt?' Mathias asks what happens next. The final two words literally mean 'make bankruptcy', the French expression for going bankrupt.
- 'I am asking you the question!' Hélène insists she is the one asking the question. The little object before 'ask' means 'to you', addressing the room's decision-makers.
- 'What do you mean, you do not know?' Gabriel now challenges Mathias's uncertainty. The opening means 'what do you mean', followed by the claim that he does not know.
- 'I don't know, Gabriel!' Mathias repeats the same short negative answer. Adding Gabriel's name makes clear whom his frustrated reply is aimed at.
- 'You always know everything!' The stressed 'you' at the end reinforces the person being criticized. The two words before it mean 'always' and 'everything': Gabriel says Mathias normally knows it all.
- 'Call your wife now. We have to know.' The first command tells him to call his wife now. The following impersonal statement means 'we must', followed by the action of finding out.
- 'It will be useless. She will not talk to me.' The first sentence predicts that it will be useless. The future action in the second sentence means 'will talk', negated because his wife will not speak to him.
- 'It looks like a divorce.' The first action means 'resembles' or 'looks like'. Mathias uses the image of a divorce to describe what is happening to his marriage.
