## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Hello, everyone.' The greeting is followed by words meaning 'to everyone'. The final word refers to the whole group she is addressing.
- 'Hello.' Mathias returns the greeting. This is the usual daytime hello, equally suitable when a formal meeting begins.
- 'You have lost your boss. I am sorry.' The opening looks back at losing their boss. The feminine word for sorry describes Colette, who acknowledges their loss before giving her findings.
- 'But the law is the law.' The same word for law appears on both sides of 'is'. This repeated statement insists that the rules still apply despite personal circumstances.
- 'A company is not a family. There are laws.' The first negative says a business is not a family. The second sentence introduces the existence of laws with the French equivalent of 'there are'.
- 'You confuse private life and professional life.' The action means confusing or mixing up two things. Each half names a kind of life: private and professional, the boundary Colette says they have crossed.
- 'You are going to pay a big fine in court.' The opening predicts payment. The thing to be paid is a fine, described as large. The last word names the court involved.
- 'Why the court?' The first word asks 'why'. By repeating only the court, Mathias focuses on that part of the announcement without restating the whole sentence.
- 'The company paid for a private hotel room for ten years.' The completed action means 'paid'. The final time words count ten years, describing how long company money paid for a private hotel room.
- 'The agency was paying for his holidays.' The action looks back at ongoing payments. The plural word for holidays follows a possessive meaning 'his'; those holidays were paid by the agency.
- 'Some people even bought socks with the company's money!' The first word means 'some people'. The completed action is buying; the last words identify the money used as belonging to the company.
- 'That's different. It was a festival in the Alps, my feet were freezing!' Gabriel first calls his purchase different. The time words locate a festival in the mountains; French describes his feet as frozen using 'I had'.
- 'It's an offense. Sorry. Goodbye.' The final legal noun calls it an offense. Colette then apologizes briefly and gives a formal goodbye, ending the meeting.
