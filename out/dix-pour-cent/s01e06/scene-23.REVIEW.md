## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'What's your name? Cheers!' The first question asks someone's name, literally how they call themselves. The last word is a toast: 'cheers', literally 'health'.
- 'Colette, wait! I swear, it was for work!' The opening is a command to wait. The little object before 'swear' means 'to you'; Andréa insists the incident was connected with work.
- 'Colette, I'm sorry, forgive me.' The feminine word for sorry describes Andréa. The last hyphenated command asks Colette to forgive her, with 'me' attached after the action.
- 'You're right to be angry. That was wrong.' French says 'have reason' to mean being right. The final description calls what happened wrong; Andréa acknowledges why Colette is angry.
- 'But I saw Bel and Berléand together, I was happy.' The completed action means 'saw'. The final words say 'I was happy', with a feminine ending because Andréa is describing herself.
- 'You kissed her in front of everyone!' The shortened object before the completed kiss means 'her'. The final three words mean 'everyone', so Colette stresses that the kiss was public.
- 'Yes, it's true. Be fair, I'm telling the truth!' The command in the middle means 'be honest' or 'be fair'. The final words mean 'the truth': Andréa insists she is telling it.
- 'Is it going to happen like this at every party, Andréa?' The opening predicts what is going to happen. The time words mean 'at every party', turning one incident into a question about a repeated pattern.
- 'But it's not important! It was nothing!' The first negative denies importance. The next sentence describes it as nothing; Andréa is minimizing what Colette has seen.
- 'A kiss at a party, that's all.' The first word names a small kiss. The final three words mean 'that is all', dismissing the act as something limited.
- 'What matters is us!' The opening means 'what matters'. The last word is the stressed 'us', which gives their relationship the emphasis.
- 'I knew it, anyway. It's going to end like this.' The first action looks back at knowing already. The middle expression means 'anyway'; the final statement anticipates how the relationship will end.
- 'No, wait! It can't end like this.' The first command asks her to wait. The negative surrounds 'can', denying that this is how their relationship must end.
- 'All you know how to give is your body. Go home!' The opening names everything Andréa knows how to give. The body at the end is contrasted with emotional commitment; Colette then commands her to go home.
- 'Colette, get into the taxi!' The command means 'get in'. The next little word means 'in', and the final word names the taxi. Andréa is urging Colette to enter it.
