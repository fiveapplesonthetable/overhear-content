## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Do you dance?' The verb means 'dance'. Statement order can become a question simply by raising the voice at the end.
- 'Why? Are you asking me out?' The first question word means 'why'. The shortened object attached to 'invite' means 'me'; 'go out' suggests a date here.
- 'No, better than that. An audition for a film!' The comparison means 'better than that'. The borrowed word after it means an audition in French, rather than the whole cast.
- 'What is it?' The last word asks 'what'. This short spoken question means the same as the longer question frame you have heard elsewhere.
- 'I say Luc, I say Besson, I say Luc Besson!' The same two opening words mean 'I say', repeated to build suspense before Gabriel gives the filmmaker's full name.
- 'I thought of you.' The completed action means 'thought'. The final words mean 'of you': Sofia is the person he had in mind.
- 'For real?' Take these three words together as 'for real?' Sofia wants confirmation that he is serious.
- 'Yes! The supporting role, with a huge paycheck!' The word that literally means 'second' describes a supporting role. The actor's payment is the fee; Gabriel says it is excellent.
- 'A gorgeous girl, full of energy... And who dances hip-hop!' The description begins with a girl and gives her qualities. The final part adds the ability required for this role: dancing hip-hop.
- 'She dances hip-hop?' The opening 'she' refers to the character in the film. Sofia repeats the dance requirement as a question because it worries her.
- 'Yes! That is you, isn't it?' The middle words point to Sofia: 'that is you'. The last word asks for agreement, like an English 'right?'.
- 'Yes, yes... That is completely me, yes.' The words meaning 'entirely' or 'completely' strengthen her agreement. Her hesitant repeated yeses conceal the problem: she cannot do the dance.
- 'Go see Hervé for the music. But keep the secret!' The first word is a command to go. The second instruction says to keep the secret; the little word before 'secret' means 'the'.
