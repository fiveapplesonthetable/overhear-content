## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'François, it is me. I am here, as planned.' The opening identifies Andréa as the visitor. The words after 'I am here' mean 'as planned', referring to their agreed visit.
- 'That is enough! Fox, quiet!' The first statement means 'that is enough'. The final two words ask for calm, directed at François's dog Fox.
- 'I am having a swimming pool dug.' The two actions work together: 'I make' plus 'dig'. They mean he is having a pool dug by someone else, rather than digging it himself.
- 'Seriously?' The two words literally mean 'without a joke'. As a surprised question, they mean 'seriously?' or 'you are not joking?'.
- 'I cannot take the shame of not knowing how to swim anymore.' The negative around 'bear' ends with 'anymore'. The shame is not knowing how to swim; the second negative applies specifically to that ability.
- 'I have turned down so many films.' The completed action means 'refused'. The following amount word means 'so many', emphasizing the number of films he has turned down.
- 'Which ones? Titanic?' The opening asks 'which ones?' and is plural because it refers to the films. Andréa then makes a joke by suggesting the famous shipwreck film.
- 'I am proud of you, François.' The feminine word for proud describes Andréa. The final short words mean 'of you', identifying François as the person she is proud of.
- 'I am really sorry about Dom Juan.' The word between 'am' and 'sorry' means 'really'. The feminine ending of sorry belongs to Andréa, the speaker.
- 'I know that Christophe Bel has vision.' The opening says 'I know'. The following statement credits Christophe Bel with vision; the little word before vision introduces a quality he possesses.
- 'Are you going to do it, then?' The opening points to what he is going to do. The short object before 'do' means 'it', referring to taking on the production again.
- 'No, it is too late now.' The small amount word before 'late' means 'too'. The final word places that judgment in the present: it is too late now.
- 'Christophe regrets it too. He admires you, you know that!' The first sentence says Christophe regrets it 'too'. The object attached to 'admires' means 'you'; the later object before 'know' means 'that'.
- 'Then why does he not call me directly?' The first question word means 'why'. The shortened object before 'calls' means 'me'. The last word means 'directly', without Andréa as an intermediary.
- 'Christophe? It is Andréa Martel.' We have moved to Andréa phoning Christophe. She checks his name and then gives her full name, a common concise way to identify a caller.
- 'I hope you are free tonight, I am inviting you to dinner.' The first action means 'hope'. The time words mean 'tonight'. The little object attached to 'invite' means 'you', followed by the invitation to dine.
