## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Francois, it is me. I am here, as planned.' Andrea announces her arrival at the country house.
- 'That is enough! Fox, quiet!' Francois calms his barking terrier dog Fox. Du calme means quiet.
- 'I am having a swimming pool dug.' Faire creuser means to have dug, using the causative.
- 'Seriously?' Sans blague is a colloquial exclamation meaning no joke or seriously.
- 'I cannot take the shame of not knowing how to swim anymore.' Ne plus means no longer.
- 'I have turned down so many films.' Refuser means to refuse or turn down, and tellement means so many.
- 'Which ones? Titanic?' Andrea teases him with humor. Lesquels means which ones.
- 'I am proud of you, Francois.' Etre fier de means to be proud of someone.
- 'I am really sorry about Dom Juan.' Andrea expresses genuine empathy, and vraiment means truly.
- 'I know that Christophe Bel has vision.' Avoir de la vision means to have creative vision.
- 'Are you going to do it, then?' Andrea encourages him to reconsider the role.
- 'No, it is too late now.' Francois hesitates out of wounded pride. Trop tard means too late.
- 'Christophe regrets it too. He admires you, you know that!' Regretter means to regret, and admirer means to admire.
- 'Then why does he not call me directly?' Directement is an adverb meaning directly or in person.
- 'Christophe? It is Andrea Martel.' Andrea takes initiative right away and dials his number.
- 'I hope you are free tonight, I am inviting you to dinner.' Esperer means to hope, and etre libre means to be free.
- 'All right, with pleasure.' Christophe accepts Andrea's dinner invitation with avec plaisir.
