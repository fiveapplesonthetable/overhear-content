## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Yes?' Sofia answers the door with a brief question.
- 'Sofia? Sorry, am I disturbing you?' Gabriel uses the informal pronoun 'te' with the verb 'deranger'.
- 'No, you aren't bothering me.' The standard negation 'ne... pas' wraps around the pronoun and verb.
- 'I had to see you.' The imperfect tense 'devais' expresses past necessity.
- 'I feel really bad about earlier.' The fixed phrase 'tout a l'heure' refers to earlier that evening.
- 'What I said was horrible.' The relative phrase 'ce que' means 'what' or 'that which'.
- 'No, I should have told you.' The conditional past 'aurais du' expresses regret.
- 'Do you want to drink something?' The conjugated verb 'vouloir' is followed directly by an infinitive.
- 'Yes, gladly.' The polite adverb 'volontiers' accepts an invitation with pleasure.
- 'I have some port.' The partitive article 'du' indicates an unspecified quantity of drink.
- 'I love port.' French verbs of preference take a definite article.
- 'Gabriel! Open up!' Andrea pounds frantically on Gabriel's apartment door late at night.
- 'Andrea? Are you hurt?' The feminine participle 'blessee' agrees with Andrea.
- 'I had a pool problem!' Andrea explains her drenched clothes with frantic absurdity.
- 'Gabriel, marry me!' The imperative joins the stressed pronoun 'moi' with a hyphen.
- 'Protect me, save me!' Two urgent imperatives plead for emotional rescue.
- 'Help me, Gabriel...' Andrea whispers her final desperate plea in the hallway.
