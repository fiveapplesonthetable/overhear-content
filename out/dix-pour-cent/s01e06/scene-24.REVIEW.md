## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Yes?' The one-word question means 'yes?' Gabriel is answering his door and asking what the visitor wants.
- 'Sofia?' Gabriel says Sofia's name as a surprised question. A name by itself can ask someone to confirm their identity.
- 'Sorry, am I disturbing you?' Sofia first apologizes, with 'me' attached to the command. The short object before 'disturb' means 'you': she asks whether her visit is a nuisance.
- 'No, you aren't bothering me.' The negative surrounds 'bother'. The little object before the action means 'me', so Gabriel reassures her that she is not disturbing him.
- 'I had to see you.' The action before 'see' means 'had to'. The little word between the two actions means 'you', the person Sofia needed to see.
- 'I feel really bad about earlier.' The short word after 'I' belongs to the action 'feel'. The last few words refer to earlier today, the time of their argument.
- 'What I said was horrible.' Now Gabriel takes responsibility. The opening words mean 'what I said'. The final description calls his words horrible, rather than Sofia herself.
- 'No, I should have told you.' Sofia says what she should have done. Two little objects before the telling action mean 'to you' and 'it': she should have told him she could not dance.
- 'Do you want to drink something?' Gabriel offers a drink. The words after the drinking action literally mean 'some thing', so the offer leaves the drink unspecified.
- 'Yes, gladly.' The final word means willingly or gladly. It adds a warm acceptance beyond a simple yes.
- 'I have some port.' The little word before port means 'some'. Gabriel says he has this drink available, offering it without naming a quantity.
- 'I love port.' The opening is a strong 'I love'. Sofia names port as the drink she enjoys; she accepts the specific choice enthusiastically.
- 'Gabriel!' Andréa now calls out Gabriel's name from outside. The name alone gets his attention before she explains why she has come.
- 'Andréa? Are you hurt?' The final feminine word means injured or hurt. Its ending matches Andréa, whose appearance makes Gabriel worry about her.
- 'I had a pool problem!' The completed action uses 'have' to describe having had a problem. The final word identifies the source of this problem: a swimming pool.
- 'Gabriel, marry me!' The command means 'marry', with 'me' attached by a hyphen. This is a direct plea for marriage, addressed to Gabriel.
- 'Protect me, save me!' Both commands have 'me' attached. They mean 'protect me' and 'save me', making Andréa's wish for safety explicit.
- 'Help me, Gabriel...' The final command again attaches 'me' after the action 'help'. She repeats Gabriel's name as a personal appeal.
