## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Did you bring a present?' The completed action means 'brought'. The final word is a gift, so Andréa is checking whether François brought a birthday present.
- 'Very good. Very funny. I am glad that amuses you.' Two short judgments begin the reply: 'very good' and 'very funny'. The later little object attached to 'amuses' means 'you'.
- 'I have to take care of the music. Enjoy the atmosphere.' The first action means 'have to'. It is followed by taking care of something: the music. The following command tells François to enjoy the atmosphere.
- 'He isn't talking to me! He invites me, and he ignores me!' Three little objects refer to François: 'to me', then inviting 'me', then ignoring 'me'. His repeated complaints show the contrast between invitation and welcome.
- 'Wait, he's going to come back.' The opening is a command to wait. The later 'going to' introduces a return: Andréa says Christophe will come back.
- 'It's his party, he's stressed. Give him time.' The possessive before party means 'his'. The later command has 'to him' attached after a hyphen: she asks François to give him time.
- 'No, I'm leaving.' The shortened middle sequence belongs to a common expression for leaving. Translate the whole statement as 'I am leaving', rather than word by word.
- 'What? No, stay! François!' The command for 'stay' follows the refusal 'no'. Andréa addresses François by name as she tries to stop him leaving.
- 'Christophe, you are made to work together.' The 'you' here is plural: Andréa means the director and the actor together. The final word means 'together', the partnership she is urging.
- 'Give me one good reason.' The command to give has 'me' attached. The request is for one good reason, so the feminine describing word matches the word for reason.
- 'He's a great actor!' The description places 'great' before the actor. Here it means an excellent actor, rather than necessarily describing his height.
- 'He has no respect! I refuse to work with him!' The word after the negative means 'no' or 'any' with a negative: no respect. The second sentence refuses working with him.
- 'It's not that he doesn't want to!' This is a negative explanation of a negative: she denies that not wanting is the reason. The issue is inability, which she is about to reveal.
- 'Then what is the problem? He can't?' The first question asks what the problem is. The shorter second one asks whether he cannot do it, with the action left understood.
- 'You must not tell anyone. He does not know how to swim.' The first sentence prohibits telling anyone. The second uses 'know' followed by another action to describe a skill he lacks: swimming.
- 'What?' This one-word question asks 'what?' Christophe has heard something unexpected and wants it repeated.
- 'He doesn't know how to swim!' The negative surrounds 'knows'. With the next action, the complete meaning is 'does not know how to swim', not merely lacking information about swimming.
