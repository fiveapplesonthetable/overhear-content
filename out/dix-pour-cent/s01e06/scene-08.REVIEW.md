## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Will you be okay?' This reassurance question uses 'go' twice: the near future followed by the basic action. As a whole it asks whether things will be okay.
- 'What do you think about it?' The shortened opening joins informal 'you' and 'about it'. The final question word means 'what': Mathias asks what Camille thinks.
- 'And the agency? The buyout of the agency?' The first word moves on to another subject: 'and'. The repeated agency is followed by a noun meaning a buyout or purchase.
- 'After what happened with your wife, does that change anything?' The opening looks back at what happened. The last two words literally mean 'some thing'; in this question they amount to 'anything'.
- 'Listen, I am handling it, okay?' The first word asks her to listen. The action after 'I' means 'manage' or 'handle'; the final check asks her to accept his answer.
- 'Are you going to stay standing there for a long time?' The opening predicts what the other person is going to do. The final word means 'for a long time', so she is asking about the duration.
- 'I do not know.' The two negative words surround 'know'. This is a short complete answer: he does not know how long.
- 'If I had not moved to Paris,' The beginning sets up something that did not happen: 'if I had not moved'. The location is Paris, where Camille now regrets moving.
- 'And if I had not come to see you here,' A second 'if' adds coming to see him. The feminine ending of the completed arrival word belongs to Camille, the person speaking.
- 'You would buy the agency and your family would be happy.' Both actions describe an imagined result, 'would buy' and 'would be'. The final feminine word for happy describes the family.
- 'As for me, I would still be selling clothes in the South.' The detached 'me' emphasizes Camille's own alternative. The word before 'selling' means 'still': her old job would have continued.
- 'What do you want me to tell you?' The question begins 'what do you want'. It then adds 'that I tell you', the everyday French way to ask what he can possibly say.
- 'That is just how it is.' The last two words mean 'like that'. The complete reply accepts the situation as it is, rather than explaining why it happened.
- 'Who was that producer looking for an assistant?' The opening asks 'who was it'. The word for 'looking for' describes an ongoing search in the past; the job was an assistant.
- 'Who, Dominique Besnehard?' The two short opening words ask 'who, exactly?' Mathias then supplies Dominique Besnehard's name as a possible answer.
- 'I am going to call him.' The short object before 'call' means 'him'. She says she is going to call the producer, indicating her next step.
- 'In case he is still looking.' The opening expression means 'in case'. The final word means 'still' here: he might still be looking for an assistant.
