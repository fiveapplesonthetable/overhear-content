## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'I mean.' He is correcting himself. The second word literally means 'my intention', with a possessive ending that makes it his own intention. Here the whole phrase simply introduces what he meant to say.
- 'They need a monthly allowance.' The ending tells us that several people need something. The word meaning 'monthly' comes after the allowance it describes. It is built from the word for 'month'.
- 'It's called a salary.' Literally, 'its name is a salary'. The word for 'name' has a little ending that points back to the allowance. Her correction is pointed: these are employees' wages, rather than pocket money.
- 'I'm responsible for all the people outside.' Arabic places the first-person pronoun directly before the adjective meaning 'responsible'. This present statement needs no separate helping verb. The short linking word introduces a description of those people. Here, 'outside' means beyond the meeting room.
