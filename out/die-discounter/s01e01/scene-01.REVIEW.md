## Walk-through narration (English only, no target-language words quoted)

- 'Yes, I'm Titus.' This is a direct self-introduction. The two words before his name identify who he is. Keep that opening and replace the name with your own when introducing yourself. The first little word simply adds an affirmative yes; it is not part of his name.
- 'Today is my first day here.' German can put the time expression first. The verb still takes the second position, before the subject phrase. Notice the ending on the word for first: it belongs with the masculine noun for day. The possessive tells us whose first day this is.
- 'I don't know the others very well yet.' The opening points to the other employees. Knowing people means being familiar with them; knowing a fact would call for a different German word. The two words expressing not yet leave room for that familiarity to grow. His final two words soften the description: he does not know them all that well.
- 'But I'm not here to make friends.' The opening marks a contrast with what we might expect from someone starting a new job. After the comma, he names a purpose and immediately rejects it. Literally he says finding friends; natural English usually says making friends. The plural refers to friends in general, rather than one particular friend.
- 'I'm here to earn money.' Listen for the purpose construction again. Its opening word introduces his goal; the little marker just before the final action word completes it. The money comes between those two markers. Both parts concern the same person: Titus is here, and Titus intends to earn the money. This sentence gives the practical reason for his new job.
